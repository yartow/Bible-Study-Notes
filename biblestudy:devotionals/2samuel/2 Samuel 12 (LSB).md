# 2 Samuel 12 (LSB)
> ### Yahweh Sends Nathan to David
> **2Sam. 12:1**   Then Yahweh sent Nathan to David. And he came to him and said, 

David did not send Nathan to him, but **Yahweh sent Nathan**. The prophet needed no permission from the king to speak to him. Sure, the king could still kill him (examples of Ahab?) but if Yahweh wanted him to speak, to live or to die, it would happen. In the same way Jonah went to Nineveh and spoke there, most likely more than just the seven words described in [[Jonah 3 (LSB)|Jonah 3:4]], for otherwise they would not have known which God it was they should turn to and from which acts they should repent.

> **2Sam. 12:5**   Then David’s anger burned greatly against the man, and he said to Nathan, “As Yahweh lives, surely the man who has done this deserves to die. **6** “And he must make restitution for the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing and had no compassion.”

It is obvious to see that to David it was not clear that Nathan's story was an analogy or a hypothetical story. David showed genuine **anger** which showed that he had not yet lost his moral values despite his *temporary* suppression of his conscience. It might feel strange how anyone—especially someone who was as loyal to God as David—could commit such crimes as he had done, a first degree (premeditated) murder (even though he let Joab do the work). It needs to be understood here that David was a killer and had killed animals at a young age (#todo/opzoeken/reference  bears and lions[^1]). At a still young age, he had slain Goliath and even chopped off his head, which was probably the first time he had killed a man, but the Bible does not speak of any hesitation on David's part. This is also why God told David why he was not allowed to build the temple because of the blood on his hands (1 Chronicles 22:8). 
In Dan Simmons' #tags/people/authors book #tags/books *Darwin's Blade* the protagonist used to be a sniper in the Army or in the Marines, but started working as an analyst and consultant at accidents that were unexplainable. After twenty years of not having killed a man he had to kill one again, and directly afterwards had to vomit because the thought of killing a man is unnatural to man and his spirit. 
Though this is just a novel, I have heard many things like this. Everyone knows how soldiers come back disoriented from the battle field because they cannot deal with all of the people they have killed. Some get used to this and deal with it with a "clear" conscience, for example when they have done all of it in the name of justice. In the same David was used to killing, and the killing of an innocent person, especially in a fight, was only a small step for him. Also, after having at least seven wives and ten concubines, it was only a relatively small step for him to add an extra wife or go to bed with another woman. 
For this reason God wanted someone without blood on his hands, but unfortunately David commanded his son, as one of the first actions of his reign, to kill off anyone—even his own half-brother—who opposed him or his son's reign. #biblestudy/discovery

> **2Sam. 12:7**   Nathan then said to David, “You are the man! Thus says Yahweh, the God of Israel, ‘It is I who anointed you king over Israel and it is I who delivered you from the hand of Saul. **8** ‘I also gave you your master’s house and your master’s wives into your care, and I gave you the house of Israel and Judah; and if *that had been* too little, I would have added to you many more things like these!

Note that God did not say he would give David more wives. 

The **house of Israel and Judah** are the people whom David ruled over. His **master's house and [...] wives** was the house or family of Saul including his wives. The people of Israel and Judah would have added to David's power, but what benefit would the latter have been? It would have been something like God entrusting Saul's elderly parents to him. That would only have been a nuisance and extra worry. 
Of course it would or could establish more respect from the people. For David's own son slept with his father's concubines and Rehoboam did the same with some of his father's wives (#todo/opzoeken/reference ), and Adonijah tried to do this to Solomon to show the people he was the new king, to show that they were the new king now. Perhaps this was also a benefit for David to influence those in the kingdom—and the number might not have been little—who still preferred the house of Saul, such as Shimei and Sheba (2 Samuel 16:5, 20:1-2). #biblestudy/discovery

> **9** ‘Why have you despised the word of Yahweh by doing evil in His sight? You have struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword, have taken his wife to be your wife, and have killed him with the sword of the sons of Ammon.

Notice that Nathan mentions three sins here even though the first and the last seem to be the same. In #tags/culture/ane  the Ancient Near Eastern culture death was not so much a problem as who it was who killed you. It was for this reason that Saul killed himself lest he be killed by the heathens (1 Samuel 31:4). Uriah died in battle, which was honorable, but David *let* him be **killed with the sword of the sons of Ammon**. 

> **10** ‘So now, the sword shall never depart from your house, because you have despised Me and have taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.’ 

There were many more sins that David had committed (see notes on the first verses of [[2 Samuel 11 (LSB)|2 Samuel 11]]), some of which are not explicitly mentioned. Letting Joab do the work and putting blood on his hands as well, is one of these sins, but the worst sin with the worst punishment deriving from it, is that David **despised** God. 
It is hard to explain what *despise* means in this context. An easy explanation of despising someone is when someone is so bad that you will not even lift one finger to help him. Suppose there is a man who has beaten his wife and does not regret it, and he is now on the streets begging for food. You might despise him and not even give him a piece of your bread. 
In the context of two people who are friends, suppose there one friend has to go abroad and asks his friend to take care of his wife. He promises to, but during his absence he seduces her. Upon return the first friend will say, "Do you despise me that much that you take my own wife." Of course the second friend will say, "No, I do not despise you at all, it just happened." The despise #questions/language/english/spelling noun  is in the fact that he *let* this happen. If he truly loved his friend and respected him, he would not have done anything to hurt her or his friendship. It would not even come across his mind. From the moment it *did* come across his mind, a true friend would have stayed away from the wife, not meeting with her separately—doing anything to prevent any possible progress in the wrong direction.
In the same way David despised God and did not respect Him during his time of sinning. He may have thought, "God will not take any action on me, for He is not present here." The despise #questions/language/english/spelling noun is even greater for God had given him everything he wanted in terms of relations, power and materials, yet he paid God back in this way. #biblestudy/discovery
It is noticeable how David could at one time be so grateful to God (see the Psalms and last chapters of this book) while at other times completely forget God. We understand this about ourselves, because most of us have never been at the place where David was, with the same kind of relationship with and a view of God as David had. So our volatility (the *amplitude*, if you will) in both directions is smaller. The truth is, however, that our inclination *downwards* is just as bad and most likely even worse. Even though most Christians have not killed anyone, we have done many other things that are much worse (see also notes on 2 Samuel 12:6 for an explanation of why David's sin can be seen as relatively "less bad" than we think) or many small things that combined are much worse. So the question actually becomes, why is it that we go extremely downward, but not upwards in the same way? The picture below illustrates this graphically. If man were inclined to behave symmetrically, then the dotted line shows that man normally lives on a lower level than David did.

> **11** “Thus says Yahweh, ‘Behold, I will raise up evil against you from your own household; I will even take your wives from before your sight and give *them* to your companion, and he will lie with your wives in the sight of this sun. **12** ‘Indeed you did it secretly, but I will do this thing before all Israel, and before the sun.’”

The fact that David had more than one wife was never approved of by God, but God had not punished it either, at least not in the way we would have expected this. God knew what would happen, such as the violation of his daughter Tamar by his son Amnon and the events that caused this. This by itself would be a punishment to David and a clear sign to the readers of God's Word of the consequences of polygamy. 
It may seem here that these event were consequences of David's sin against Uriah, but God's retribution is never so simple that it can be retrieved to a single sin. God's wrath is a culmination of His great patience which encompassed the forgiveness and #questions/language/english/spelling kwijtschelding forfeiture or forfeiting of many sins through his unlimited mercy. 

> **13** Then David said to Nathan, “I have sinned against Yahweh.” And Nathan said to David, “Yahweh also has taken away your sin; you shall not die.

If David had not repented, he surely would have died. The question comes to mind why this punishment was not given to Saul or any of the kings of Israel and Judah? Perhaps the principle that greater responsibility is given to those with greater power. David was one with much more wisdom and constraint and the knowledge of God—knowledge here meaning more than just knowing *about* God but hearing from God as well. As far as we know Solomon had only heard twice from God, once after the succession of the throne, when God asked him for anything, and once (or twice? #todo/opzoeken/reference ) to warn him about the wrong way he was going to. Solomon also did not write about an intense relationship with God as David had done in his book of Psalms, but briefly *mentions* God in Ecclesiastes and Proverbs as an entity that exists and must be respected. As for Saul, he had not even heard *once* from God directly #todo/opzoeken/reference but only through Samuel who served as his prophet. #biblestudy/discovery

Where people could have glorified God for His Law, the king He appointed and the godly family, now all of these are seen as nothing more than human actions. This is the reason why people think that Christian marriages are just as bad as secular ones, and that the Christian way of governing and the Christian (or Judaic Law, which is the same) lead to no better result than other policies. Solomon could have made the country rich, but he squandered the money that he was responsible for by building palaces for each of his 400 wives #todo/opzoeken/reference . 1 Kings xxx By David's **deed [he has] given occasion to the enemies of Yahweh to blaspheme**, and this does not only apply to him, but to every wrong deed Christians do as well. 

David received as punishment that the sword would never leave his house and that his son would die. The former he would experience in person in a few years (or had already experienced, depending on whether the events were written chronologically). Still, these were punishments where other parties were mostly hurt. His newborn son suffered death, his daughter Tamar suffered from rape, etc. Of course it is great suffering for a father to see his children behave in this way, but perhaps it can be said that God was very mild on David. Later on when David becomes proud and sins through his census (2 Samuel 24) it is again the people who die, 70.000 of them, and not David. Perhaps God had done it in this way because it is best way for David to suffer. David could have suffered from running and hiding from others, or from physical pain, but he might have suffered most from the pains of others, which is possibly why God hurt him there, in his heart, to stimulate his repentance. However, do note that after three days of seeing his people die, David offered himself and his family to God so that his people might live (2 Samuel 24:17, but see also notes 2 Samuel 24:25).

From this part of the text itself we cannot infer that David was truly repentant, for he could have done it for others to see him (cf. David had earlier shown this kind of sorrow when Amnon died, which lasted for days in which he cried every day—or perhaps even weeks—even though he was evil and had raped his daughter. David even showed the same kind of sorrow for Absalom who took over the throne and tried to kill him. It can truly be said that David was a person who cared more about others than about himself.

> ### Joab Fights Ammon
> **2Sam. 12:26**   Then Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon and captured the royal city. **27** And Joab sent messengers to David and said, “I have fought against Rabbah, I have even captured the city of waters. **28** “So now, gather the rest of the people together and camp against the city and capture it, lest I capture the city myself and it be named after me.” **29** So David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah, fought against it and captured it. **30** Then he took the crown of their king from his head; and its weight *was* a talent of gold, and *in it* *was* a precious stone; and it was *placed* on David’s head. And he brought out the spoil of the city *in* a very great amount. **31** He also brought out the people who were in it and set *them* up with saws, sharp iron instruments, and iron axes, and made them pass through the brickkiln. And thus he used to do to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned *to* Jerusalem.

This seems like a strange time, but in reality, if this was written chronologically, more than a year had gone by since the death of his newborn son, for Bathsheba had had her postpartum (the period after having giving birth to her first son) which in most cases takes at least three months, plus the nine months of pregnancy of Solomon. 
Not only the timing, but also the subject of this pericope seems to stand out in this chapter. The background story that embeds the arc of David and Bathsheba is the story of the sons of Ammon who had started or at least incited the war, #todo/opzoeken/reference it was during this war that David took a rest and took Bathsheba from Uriah, and it were the sons of Ammon who had killed Uriah. Now, with the destruction of the **cities of the sons of Ammon** this arc as well as as the percussions of David's immoral actions had come to an end. #biblestudy/discovery Do notice God's timing here: David destroyed the city only after he was right with God. Saul, too, was to offer to God *before* his fight with the Philistines (1 Samuel 13:9). Both serve as examples that we need to be right with God before He will or can give us the things we need[^4].
