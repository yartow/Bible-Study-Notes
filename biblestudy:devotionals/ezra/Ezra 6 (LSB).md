# Ezra 6 (LSB)
### The Scroll with Cyrus’ Decree is Found
> **Ezra 6:1**   Then King Darius issued a decree, and a asearch was made in the 1archives, where the treasures were deposited in Babylon. 

Even though there was an archive, we cannot assume this decree would have been found. At all times enemies of the Israelites have existed in all ranks of the government, and these could have destroyed any document that benefited the Israelites. However, in this case, as well as in the case of Esther, the fate of the Israelites was sealed by the finding of archived documents. Here, it was the governor who asked questions to Zerubbabel, which ultimately led to another blessing, namely that the people who lived in the land—which also included the non-Israelites—were to contribute to the house of God. So aside from the materials funded by Cyrus and the Israelites in the Empire, now those living in Israel had to contribute as well through their taxes (v.8). Moreover, opposition would possibly decline as well because of Artaxerxes' edict and threats to anyone who refused to cooperate.

> **2** And in 1Ecbatana in the fortress, which is ain the province of Media, a scroll was found and there was written in it as follows: 

Most likely the scribes did not leave the scrolls in the palace where the king lived, but gathered them and transported them to a safe place, in this case in the **fortress**. These storage facilities must have had some kind of ordering system, for otherwise scrolls of decades ago could not be found anymore. In this case we are talking about 17 years ago. It is quite possible that these scrolls were also categorized, so that edicts and decrees, and that for specific minorities perhaps, could be found as well. Still, I can imagine that all of this would have taken a long time. The letter from the governor had to be delivered to the king. It would take some time before the king would have time to read the letter. After the king's command to look for the scroll, this command would have to be sent to Ecbatana, and there this search request would need to be processed, if there are was not a line of other requests already that needed to be processed first. If this request was successful, then this scroll needed to be delivered to the king again (assuming no copy needed to be made of the scroll) and there too another process would have to be started. 
We do not know about the specifics of these processes, but in all this took a long time and it was a test for the Jews in Israel—because they did not know the outcome of Darius' decree. On the other hand, it was time where they could continue their work on the temple, undisturbed by the governor.

Note that not only Cyrus, but Artaxerxes as well, call the temple **the house of God** and not "the house of the god *of Israel*". It would seem that they think of God as the greatest god, but it needs to be shown that they did away with their own gods, if even for their own generation. 

> **Ezra 6:6**    “Now aTattenai, governor of *the province* beyond the 1River, Shethar-bozenai and their colleagues, the officials of *the provinces* beyond the River, 2keep away from there. **7**  		“Leave that work on the house of God alone; let the governor of the Jews and the elders of the Jews rebuild that house of God in its *designated* place. **8**  “Moreover, aI issue a decree concerning what you shall do for those elders of Judah in the rebuilding of that house of God: the full cost is to be given to those people from the royal treasury out of the tribute of *the provinces* beyond the River, and that without negligence. **9**  “Whatever is needed, both young bulls, rams, and lambs for a burnt offering to the God of heaven, and wheat, salt, wine, and anointing oil, according to what the priests in Jerusalem say, *it* is to be given to them daily without negligence, **10**  that they may bring sweet-smelling sacrifices near to the God of heaven and apray for the life of the king and his sons.

Though this may sound self-seeking, it actually is a sign of the fear Artaxerxes has for God. If he were to let another people worship their god and *not* ask for a blessing from their god, it would mean he does not need it or that he does not believe in that god's power. But since Artaxerxes actually ask the Jewish people to **pray for the life of the king and his sons**, and seemingly shows his fear for God and knowledge that his success depends on Him, he does ask for God's blessing.

> **11**  “And I issued a decree that aany man who violates this edict, a timber shall be pulled out from his house and he shall be impaled on it and bhis house shall be made a refuse heap on account of this.

Note that the above decrees are in the present tense but that this decree was **issued** in the past tense. This could imply that he had already issued this decree *before* he sent out this letter and a precursory decree, warning everyone not to stop hamper the Jews. This percursory decree was easier to send out because it did not contain many details, but the decree from v.6 on required a calculation of the treasury before such measurements could be stated.

Darius pronounced a curse here to **any king or people**. Later on, the Ottomans did take possession of Jerusalem, but did not **destroy [the] house of God**. The curse is in that case more meant for the Greeks and the Romans, for the Greeks defiled the Temple in the time of the Maccabees #todo/opzoeken  and the Romans destroyed in 70 AD #todo/opzoeken . Both empires, and all empires afterwards, even the British one, were overthrown later on, so this does not necessarily show the effectuation of Darius' curse. However, do we see any difference between these two empires and the others? Both cultures, the Greek and the Roman, have disappeared, while the Ottoman and the Arabian ones still exist. The Greco-Roman cultures were revived during the Renaissance, but only the part that concerned art.

### The Dedication of the House of God
> **Ezra 6:13**   Then aTattenai, the governor of *the province* beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and their colleagues carried out*the decree* with all diligence, just as King Darius had sent. **14** And athe elders of the Jews were building and succeeding through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo. 

How did the elders build and succeed **through the prophesying**? In the same way Nehemiah was attacked or threatened from many sides so Zerubbabel too had many things to take care of including the continuous encouraging of people who were under stress. The prophesying words of the prophets gave admonishing words when they stopped and encouragement when they were tired #todo/opzoeken references in Haggai and Zechariah. 

> So they built and completed*it* according to the decree of the God of Israel and the decree bof Cyrus, cDarius, and dArtaxerxes king of Persia. **15** And this house was brought to completion 1on the third day of the amonth Adar; it was the sixth year of the reign of King Darius.

*MacSBNnkjv* writes that Ezra 4:8—6:18 and 7:12–26 are written in Aramaic. It is very likely then that these parts were originally part of historic documentation meant for the archives of the Persian Empire, as Ezra was working as scribe for them. Keeping that in mind, Ezra had to be careful not to write anything that displeased the King, Artaxerxes in this case. Rather yet, he had to make the king look good or even better. I would guess that for this reason "**by the decree of [...] Artaxerxes king of Persia**" has been added to v.14, despite the fact that Artaxerxes had done nothing to complete the house of God and had probably not even been born #todo/opzoeken then.
The first part after Ezra 4:8 describes Artaxerxes in a negative way, because he stopped the Israelites from continuing to build the wall. This part was written in Aramaic, not because Artaxerxes was supposed to read it, but rather because it was a copy of a letter that was written in Aramaic, which he himself had decreed. A few verses that were not quoted from his decree  occur outside of the context of the letter, such as v.23, are written in Aramaic instead of Hebrew. It would probably have made more sense if Ezra kept these in Aramaic, instead of switching back and forth between Hebrew and Aramaic at every change from the narrator to the first person perspective. 

> **Ezra 6:16**   And the sons of Israel, the priests, the Levites and the rest of the 1exiles, ==acelebrated== the dedication of this house of God with joy. **17** They brought near for the dedication of this house of God 100 bulls, 200 rams, 400 lambs, and as a sin offering for all Israel a12 male goats, corresponding to the number of the tribes of Israel. **18** ==Then== they appointed the priests to atheir sections and the Levites in btheir divisions for the service of God 1in Jerusalem, cas it is written in the book of Moses.

It was only after the physical work on the rebuilding of the temple that the **priests** and **Levites** were **appointed**. First of all, if there is no temple there is no work for them to do, but second of all, the priests and Levites, no matter their elevated position as being the only tribe to serve God in this way, had to do their share of the work as well. A few years later these would have to work again #questions/language/english/spelling handen uit de mouwen steken, when Nehemiah ordered the walls to be rebuilt. 
Thirdly, physical work shows character, both for men and women. In tough situations where it is very hot or cold, when food is not present in abundance and where there are all kinds of other limiting factors, one's effort shows the discipline one has. It is these fruits that let one qualify for the work of a priest or Levite. Perhaps it was after having observed these results that Zerubbabel and the other elders would know how to appoint each person for the right **service of God**.

> **22** And athey celebrated the Feast of Unleavened Bread seven days with gladness, for Yahweh had caused them to be glad and bhad turned the heart of cthe king of ==Assyria== toward them to 1strengthen them in the work of the house of God, the God of Israel.

The principal guidelines for this Passover meal can be applied to the Holy Supper, because both meals remember past events for a specific and separated people. The remembrance is of what God has done for His people, but the difference between the two meals is that the first meal remembers the first Pesach, where there was no suffering, except that of the lamb, and thus no cleansing of sin, while the second meal remembers Jesus' death at the cross, where there *was* suffering and atonement for our sins. 
Needless to say, and all Christian denominations would agree here, these meals are for those who have **separated themselves** and who remember these events because they have either seen or experienced it. In the first case these are the Israelites and in the second case it is the Christians. Everyone who does not indulge in **the uncleanness of the nations** is welcome to join. No restriction is being made here for Israelites or Christians who have not reconciled with their fellowmen.
