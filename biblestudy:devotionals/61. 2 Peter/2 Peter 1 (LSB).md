# 2 Peter 1 (LSB)

> **2Pet. 1:1**   Simeon Peter, a slave and apostle of Jesus Christ,
> 
> ### Precious and Magnificent Promises
>   To those who have received the same kind of faith as ours, by the righteousness of our God and Savior, Jesus Christ: **2** Grace and peace be multiplied to you in the full knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord;

With this blessing Peter "wishes" them **grace and peace [...] multiplied**. This comes through the condition or premise that this occurs *by* **the full knowledge of God and of Jesus**. The **in** should be read as a preposition that provides the condition. If *knowledge* were something concrete, such as a relationship, it would be clear when Peter said that "something will be multiplied *in* the relationship", just like knowledge of one's spouse will be multiplied in marriage. Here, however, this knowledge is not so concrete that one can be *in* or *out* of knowledge—but one can be in or out of a marriage. But when one has the full knowledge of God and of Jesus, only then grace and peace are multiplied. 

> **3** seeing that His divine power has granted to us ==everything== pertaining to ==life and godliness==, 

Everything we need to **pertaining to ==life and godliness==** we have received from Him. Jesus has come to give man the abundant life (John 10:10), that is the *real* life with real peace, joy and love to experience. Anyone who has the Holy Spirit cannot say he does not have what he needs to live a fruitful life. Christians have all the gifts they need to accomplish their ministry and purpose (#todo/opzoeken/reference  1 Peter 4, gifts from the Spirit), and the fruit of the Spirit is in them (Galatians 5:22) with which they have this love, joy and peace and even more. All of this is obtained and multiplied through the **knowledge of Him**. 
Note that this knowledge is not a knowledge *about* God, but rather *of* God. 

> through the full ==knowledge of Him== who called us by His own glory and excellence. 

God can call not only with His voice, but also by **His own glory and excellence**. These are shown through nature itself (cf. Romans 1 #todo/opzoeken/reference ) but even more than that, they are also seen in his behavior towards us. Everything item listed in v. 5 is also part of God's excellence, for God has moral excellencce (by definition), knowledge and self-control. If He were not self-controlled, man would have been obliterated thousands of years ago. There are many events in the Bible where God (almost) obliterated all of mankind (e.g. during the Flood) or parts of it (peoples like the Amorites and Assyrians, even the Israelites). Many of these events have not happened because of God's patience, and many more would have happened if God did not the virtues listed here and elsewhere in the Bible. 

> **4** For by these He has granted to us His precious and magnificent promises, ==so that== by them you may become ==partakers== of *the* divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world by lust. 

**[H]aving escaped** is written in the past tense, meaning that corruption does not go together with the divine nature, or in other words, this is written to Christians.
How do Christians explain that fact that there is still corruption in their ways? There is no Christian who does not sin and is perfect in his ways. The simple way of explaining this is by viewing the Christian as still being on the right path and having strayed away from it. He may fall, or walk in a slightly wrong direction, but he is not too late yet to return. So having escaped this does not mean that one cannot return to his captors, as mentioned in the next chapter (2 Peter 2:22). 
Of course this statements has implications for those who believe in the doctrine of *Once Saved, Always Saved* #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/osas and that God will keep on working in His children until they are saved. #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/perseverance The truth, however, is that it the human eye cannot distinguish whether a person is saved or not. When a person seems to have been walking on the right path, worshing God with his lips, but later goes off the right path, who knows then whether this person had been a son of God? He may never have given himself completely over to the Lordship of Christ #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/osas/lordship, or perhaps he has and the opposite is true, namely that it is possible to choose to leave God, even after having committed oneself to Christ. 

> **5** Now for this very reason also, applying all diligence, in your faith supply moral excellence, and in *your* moral excellence, knowledge, **6** and in *your* knowledge, self-control, and in *your* self-control, perseverance, and in *your* perseverance, godliness, **7** and in *your* godliness, brotherly kindness, and in *your* brotherly kindness, love. 

Every virtue listed here must be supplied by another virtue. Many discussions about whether a virtue is good or not ignore the context of a virtue, by which I do not mean the situation in which he virtue is used, but the presence of other virtues. For example, justice is not good when there is no mercy; Patience is not good, if there is no righteousness, and so forth.

> **8** For if these things are yours and are increasing, they render you ==neither useless nor unfruitful== in the full knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Anyone who practices the virtues mentioned above will *always* be fruitful and useful. The world might not have use for you, like we see in so many fairy tales, such as Cinderella #tags/movies/animation, but the reward at the end is the greatest for those who persevere. 
This usefulness specifically applies to the **full knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ**. One can have knowledge of Christ but be **unfruitful**, which means one does not have the fruit of the spirit but rather the works of the flesh (Galatians 5:19-22). But when one practices these virtues in the Kingdom of God he will never be **useless**, and though he will never be perfect and still stumble (v. 10), the fruit will still be present. Just like a tree has a certain amount of resilience, but still bears fruit despite harsh weather, bugs and birds eating of the fruit, broken branches and impure water.

In doing good, or attempting to do so, the Christian will never stumble. Of course, he will never be perfect, but every attempt is accepted and every mistake is forgiven.

This is the part where Peter assures his readers that **in this way** they will have **entrance into the eternal kingdom**, the subtheme of this book and the reason for doing everything commanded in this chapter.

> **2Pet. 1:19**   And we have as more sure the prophetic word, to which you do well to pay attention as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star arises in your hearts.

Peter makes a stronger argument here by stating that they have the **prophetic word**. Which of course needs to be backed up, which he sort of does in the next verse where he claims that a true **prophecy is not the will of man, but [by] men moved by the Holy Spirit.**

> **20** Know this first of all, that no prophecy of Scripture comes by one’s own interpretation. **21** For no prophecy was ever made by the will of man, but men being moved by the Holy Spirit spoke from God.

Peter means here two things. First, that prophecies are given by God and do not originate from the subconsciousness of man. Second, if there is a prophecy, and assuming the text has been passed on literally, the interpretation thereof is not subject to man's own creativity, but only to what the Holy Spirit *meant*, which can be *verified* by the Word of God and may never contradict with it.
