# Mark 15 (LSB)
> ### Jesus Before Pilate
> **Mark 15:1**   And early in the morning the chief priests with the elders and scribes and the whole Sanhedrin, immediately held council; and binding Jesus, led Him away and delivered *Him* to Pilate. **2** And Pilate questioned Him, “Are You the King of the Jews?” And He answered him and said, “You yourself say *it*.” **3** And the chief priests *began to* accuse Him of many things. **4** Then Pilate was questioning Him again, saying, “Do You not answer? See how many accusations they bring against You!” **5** But Jesus made no further answer; so Pilate marveled.

It is amazing when a person can stay silent when so many accusations are thrown at him. Moreover, it would be very uncommon for a person who is so disciplined and meek—not one who repents and admits his mistakes, but one who rejects the lie and only admits to saying He is the King and that He is God—to stay silent throughout these accusations. Pilate felt it in his intuition that Jesus was innocent and that there was more to the story than the priests told him (v. 10). What the priests accused him of were not even crimes which deserved the death penalty within Roman Law. So for Pilate this must all have seemed very suspicious. How many accusations could there have been? Jesus said He would rebuild the Temple in three days, He is the Messiah and the Son of Man as prophesied by Daniel and therefore God as well. It seems ridiculous to a Roman to punish a man for these things. He had not even stirred up the crowd or caused unrest. 

> **Mark 15:6**   Now at the feast he used to release for them *any* one prisoner whom they requested. **7** And the man named Barabbas had been imprisoned with the insurrectionists who had committed murder in the insurrection. **8** And the crowd went up and began asking him *to do* as he had been accustomed to do for them. **9** And Pilate answered them, saying, “Do you want me to release for you the King of the Jews?” **10** For he was aware that the chief priests had delivered Him over because of envy. **11** But the chief priests stirred up the crowd *to ask* him to release Barabbas for them instead. 

Pilate had followed the whole interaction here of how **the chief priests stirred up the crowd**. He knew the crowd was blindly following the priests, but he allowed it. He knew Jesus was not guilty (v. 14) but he wished **to satisfy the crowd** (v. 15) and that was the gravest mistake he made, which cost him his Earthly career and his Heavenly resting place. 

> **12** And answering again, Pilate was saying to them, “Then what shall I do with Him whom you call the King of the Jews?” **13** And they shouted again, “Crucify Him!” **14** But Pilate was saying to them, “Why? What evil did He do?” But they shouted all the more, “Crucify Him!” **15** And wishing to satisfy the crowd, Pilate released Barabbas for them, and after having Jesus scourged, he delivered Him over to be crucified.

#todo/opzoeken What is not mentioned here is that Pilate hoped that the crowd would allow him to release Jesus and that the scourging would have been enough (Luke 23:20). This, however, would still have been a grave sin by Pilate, for the scourging itself would already have been deadly, and in Jesus' case most certainly was. Even if he had not carried the cross and hung on it, he would have died, perhaps in a matter of hours due to blood loss. So releasing Him would only have gained him a few extra hours of life. 

Note that the order of proceedings seems to be different in the Gospel of Luke, where the scourging is not explicitly mentioned, but it seems like Pilate scourged Jesus in order to satisfy the crowd, hoping that they refrain from requesting the crucifixion. Here, however, the crowd wanted Barabbas to be free *and* they wanted Jesus dead. It seems as if Jesus was to be cruficied, but Pilate had Him scourged anyway. 
Also, it was *after* the scourging that he was brought to Herod, while in Luke this seems to be the other way around. #biblestudy/apologetics/bible/contradictions

> ### Jesus Is Mocked
> **Mark 15:16**   So the soldiers took Him away into the palace (that is, the Praetorium), and they called together the whole *Roman* cohort. **17** And they dressed Him up in purple, and after twisting a crown of thorns, they put it on Him; **18** and they began to greet Him, “Hail, King of the Jews!” **19** And they kept beating His head with a reed, and spitting on Him; and kneeling, they were bowing down before Him. 

The others who were crucified were not mocked like Jesus had been, even though their crimes were much greater. Why would the soldiers not have been overcome with pity? If they did not care about non-Roman people hanging on the cross, and would mock a person for the mere crime of claiming to be the Son of God, why would they not mock criminals with more serious crimes even more? That would have made sense. 
Perhaps one of the reasons is that the soldiers did not get the opportunity to mock the #questions/language/english/spelling accused? Here, however, because Jesus was led to Herod's **palace**, they had the liberty to do all of these things, and perhaps Herod enjoyed it as well. For the soldiers would not have had access to an expensive **purple robe** unless someone from the palace allowed it. 

> **20** And after they had mocked Him, they took the purple robe off Him and put His *own* garments on Him. And they led Him out to crucify Him.

Why would Pilate be friends with Herod if he did not want Jesus to be crucified, perhaps even reluctantly scourged him—not only because of His innocence and the ridiculous accusations, but perhaps also because his wife told him she had dreamt of Jesus? It is especially strange that Pilate was enemies with Herod but became friends with him that very day (see Luke 23:12). Perhaps Herod had something in common with Pilate, but what would that have been then? Pilate did not mock Jesus, but Herod did and allowed his guard to brutally treat him. If anything, Pilate would have despised Herod even more because of his cruelty. 
There are several explanations for this. One would be that Pilate was not as morally righteous as he is thought of or depicted in movies. Pilate perhaps only had a short moment of doubt due to his conscience, but might have been just as cruel and megalomane as Herod Antipas and his father (Antipas? #todo/opzoeken ). There is a reason, after all, why he was dishonorably discharged from his position #todo/opzoeken #tags/history#. 
In any case, what we can be certain of is that Pilate and Herod had more in common than most think. Herod killed—supposedly reluctantly—John the Baptist, and Pilate killed—also supposedly reluctantly—Jesus Christ. Both because of their own benefit and to save their own reputation.

> **Mark 15:21**   And they pressed into service a passer-by coming from the countryside, Simon of Cyrene (the father of Alexander and Rufus), to carry His cross.

Though playing only a small part here, **Simon of Cyrene**'s son, Rufus, was known by Mark (cf. Romans 16:13), which is why he is mentioned here.

> ### The Crucifixion
> **Mark 15:22**   Then they brought Him to the place Golgotha, which is translated, Place of a Skull. **23** And they tried to give Him wine mixed with myrrh; but He did not take it.

This **wine mixed with myrrh** would have an anaesthetic effect, but Jesus **did not take it** because he wanted to fully experience the pain that was due Him, even though He was already at the limit of His suffering. 

> **24** And they crucified Him, and *divided up His garments among themselves, casting lots for them *to decide* who should take what. **25** Now it was the third hour, and they crucified Him.

This was at 3 p.m. #questions/language/english/spelling , Mark uses the #todo/opzoeken Roman? way of keeping time, where counting is done from 12 p.m. . Compare with John and other gospels. This influences the interpretation of some stories, such as that of the Samaritan woman at the well, see notes on John 5 #todo/opzoeken/reference . 
The significance of **the third hour** is that it was at that time traditionally the Jews would say their prayers and offer evening sacrifices[^1]. They did not know, however, that the true sacrifice was a couple of kilometers away from them.

> **Mark 15:27**   And they crucified two robbers with Him, one on His right and one on His left. **28** [And the Scripture was fulfilled which says, “And He was numbered with transgressors.”] **29** And those passing by were blaspheming Him, shaking their heads, and saying, “Ha! You who *are going to* destroy the sanctuary and rebuild it in three days, **30** save Yourself by coming down from the cross!”

These were ones who had probably never met Jesus and not seen any of the miracles Jesus had done or heard his sermons. They had heard and knew *about* Jesus, but they did not actually know Him. 
It is also clear from their behavior that these were not people who had repented of their sin and were not open to the Gospel, for they were **blaspheming** Him, a man suffering to death on a cross. No sane person with any sense of righteousness and feelings for another person would do this, not even to a criminal, let alone to one whose crimes were not even clear.

> **34** And at the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, “ELOI, ELOI, LAMA SABACHTHANI?” which is translated, “MY GOD, MY GOD, WHY HAVE YOU FORSAKEN ME?” **35** And when some of the bystanders heard it, they *began* saying, “Look, He is calling for Elijah.”

Why would the people think that Jesus was calling **Elijah** if He was calling out to God? The answer to this is simple and can be found in the first part of the verse. I have always wondered why they added the Aramaic text here, but it is clear to me now that the author wanted to let his readers know what Jesus said, which is "**Eloi**" which sound a lot like **Elijah**. 

Another question is why someone would suddenly run and give Jesus to **drink** when He mentioned—or so they thought—the name of Elijah? The answer is found when combining the texts of the other gospels with this one. There is a part in between vv. 35,36 where Jesus says, "I am thirsty", and that is the reason why someone gave Him to drink. Furthermore, however, there are a number of prophecies fulfilled in this act, but perhaps the most simple answer is because Jesus wanted to utter His final statement (John 19:30)[^2]. 

> **36** And someone ran and filled a sponge with sour wine, put it on a reed, and gave Him a drink, saying, “Let us see whether Elijah will come to take Him down.” **37** And Jesus, uttering a loud cry, breathed His last. **38** And the veil of the sanctuary was torn in two from top to bottom. **39** And when the centurion, who was standing right in front of Him, saw the way He breathed His last, he said, “Truly this man was God’s Son!”

Perhaps something was shown in Jesus' breath. This is a mere speculation, but it could have been His Spirit. 

> **Mark 15:40**   And there were also *some* women looking on from a distance, among whom *were* Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James the Less and Joses, and Salome, **41** who, when He was in Galilee, were following Him and serving Him; and *there were* many other women who came up with Him to Jerusalem.

> ### Jesus Is Buried
> **Mark 15:42**   And when evening had already come, because it was Preparation day, that is, the day before the Sabbath, **43** Joseph of Arimathea came, a prominent Council member, who himself was waiting for the kingdom of God; and he gathered up ==courage== and went in before Pilate, and asked for the body of Jesus. **44** And Pilate wondered if He had died by this time, and summoning the centurion, he questioned him as to whether He already died. **45** And ascertaining this from the centurion, he granted the body to Joseph. **46** And when Joseph had bought a linen cloth and took Him down, he wrapped Him in the linen cloth and laid Him in a tomb which had been hewn out in the rock. And he rolled a stone against the entrance of the tomb. **47** And Mary Magdalene and Mary the *mother* of Joses were looking on *to see* where He had been laid.

These women, among others, were faithful servants. They could have felt as if they had been cheated #questions/language/english/spelling bedrogen, beter woord, by a man who claimed to be the Messiah, but then could not fulfill his promises. We can only guess what was on their minds and on that of the disciples, but instead of abandoning him, they took care of Him, even though He was dead. They had heard of the Resurrection and they knew He was with the Father, in Heaven, but they did not understand His prophecy concerning the fact that He would *actually* rise from the dead. They knew and believed He was the Son of God and God Himself, but their feelings towards this man—a human being—could not be included in this knowledge.
