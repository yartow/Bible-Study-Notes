# 1 John 1 (LSB)
> ### The Word of Life
> **1John 1:1**   What was from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we beheld and touched with our hands, concerning the Word of Life— **2** and the life was manifested, and we have seen and bear witness and proclaim to you the eternal life, which was with the Father and was manifested to us— **3** what we have seen and heard we proclaim to you also, so that you may also have fellowship with us; and indeed our fellowship is with the Father, and with His Son Jesus Christ. 
> **4** And these things we are writing, so that our joy may be made complete.

John writes four times in this letter the reason why he is **writing these things**, being 
1. so that his joy may be complete (1 John 1:4)
2. so that that you may not sin (1 John 2:1)
   1. to the little children, fathers and young men (in the church he is writing to) he also writes for another reason (1 John 2:12-14)
3. so that the reader is not deceived (1 John 2:26)
4. so that the reader may know he has eternal life (1 John 5:13)

#### Essay. The homogeneity of recipients of Biblical epistles. 
A topic of discussion is often who are the recipients are of a book. This is part of deducing the theme of the book and helps in determining the interpretation of doctrines. An example of this is found in Hebrews #todo/nogaftemaken  where depending on who the recipients are the text could either say that believers will always keep their faith or can actually lose it. See [[Hebrews 6 (LSB)/Warning Against Falling Away]]. 
The discussion on the topic of recipients of this specific book is that the letter is written either for (1) believers, (2) unbelievers or (3) both, where specific sections are for believers, and other sections are for unbelievers. There is, however, a fourth possibility that is not seen in literature. This is my own finding which I have come to through many years of analysis and reiteration in my mind, which is: (4) both, where a specific message is meant for believers in general, but not disregarding the fact that some of those "believers" are only so in name and not in action. 
A clear example of this type of recipient can be found in the case of 1 Corinthians 5, where a member of the congregation slept with his stepmother. It is impossible to fathom the possibility that this man was a Christian but made a "mistake" or "did not know about this particular sin", as he was involved in a sin even the pagans perceived as going too far #questions/language/english/spelling crossing the line? mooiere verwoording. Still, Paul calls the Corinthians "believers", but despite this we should not conclude from this that all Corinthians were believers, for Paul himself calls some "so-called" believers (1 Corinthians 5:11).
Of course, a distinction between "believers" and "Christians" can be made, where the former is someone who only believes in Christ and the latter is someone who has given Christ control of his life as well, but this distinction is often not necessary and is probably not what Paul or the other authors have meant, as this only makes the discussion more complex. 


> ### God Is Light
> **1John 1:5**   And this is the message we have heard from Him and declare to you, that God is Light, and in Him there is no darkness at all. **6** If we say that we have fellowship with Him and *yet* walk in the darkness, we lie and do not do the truth; **7** but if we walk in the Light as He Himself is in the Light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus His Son cleanses us from all sin.

This walking **in the Light** is more than just keeping the Laws of God. The Pharisees did that as well, but the blood of Jesus did not cleanse them from their sin. We need to walk **as He Himself is in the Light**. This seems contradictory, for no one can walk in the light—that is, live a sinless life—as Jesus did, and even if one could, why would His blood be necessary to **[cleanse] us from all sin**, which we would not even have? Perhaps it should say, 
> "If we do our best to walk in the Light as He Himself is in the Light, then we—Christians among each other—have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus His Son cleanses us from all sin." #composed/bible/1john

#### Were the recipients of 1 John Christians? 
The hina-clause (Gr: ἵνα) in vv. 3-4, translated as "so that" indicates that the present situation the recipients did not have fellowship with John and did not have complete joy. This could indicate that the recipients were unbelievers or (less likely) that this part of the letter was only meant for the part of the congregation that had not fully accepted Christ as their savior yet. In the remainder of the letter (e.g. 2:12-14, 4:13,16-17), it is clear that John speaks to believers. This can be harmonized by understanding that v. 7 indicates that the recipients, or part thereof, did *not* **walk in the Light**, for this is the *only* condition to **have fellowship with one another**. Thus it is most likely that the recipients were Christians who were at this point in time not walking in the light and abiding by God's commands, for they were under the influence of a false teacher, Diotrephes (3 John 9). 

> **8** If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us. **9** If we confess our sins, He is faithful and righteous to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. **10** If we say that we have not sinned, we make Him a liar and His word is not in us.

This is a fine example of a literal hamburger where v. 9 is emphasized by being sandwiched in between two verses. The main key to becoming a believer, regardless of one’s view of election #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/predestination/election, is to **confess our sins**. This is the first step in the Gospel, namely that we *confess* that we are sinners, which leads to the next step of the need for salvation. 
**If we say that we have no sin**, we implicate not only ourselves, but also God as **liar**.
