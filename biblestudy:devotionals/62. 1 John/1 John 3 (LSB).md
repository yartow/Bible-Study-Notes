# 1 John 3 (LSB)
> ### Children of God, Love One Another
> **1John 3:1**   See how great a love the Father has given to us, that we would be called children of God; and we are. For this reason the world does not know us, because it did not know Him. **2** Beloved, now we are children of God, and it has not been manifested as yet what we will be. We know that when He is manifested, we will be like Him, because we will see Him just as He is. 

This can have two interpretations. We will be like Him because we will see Him... Meaning that those who get to see Him as He is, will become like Him. Or it could mean: we already know that we will see Him just as He is, from other verses in the Bible (perhaps the end of 1 Corinthians 13). We also know that no man, human that is, sees God and lives (tdr Jacob's words, but also those of Isaiah?). Hence, we know that we will be like Him, that is, not necessarily like gods, but like Him as in His purity, because we will see Him and still live. 

> **3** And everyone who has this hope *fixed* on Him purifies himself, just as He is pure.

So everyone who has this hope of seeing Him and also becoming like Him, that is, once again, as pure as He is, purifies himself right now already in preparation for the ultimate purification and the responsibilities that come along with it. 

Example. Woman who prepares to be a queen.

> **1John 3:4**   Everyone who does sin also does lawlessness; and sin is lawlessness. **5** And you know that He was manifested in order to take away sins, and in Him there is no sin. **6** ==No one who abides in Him sins; no one who sins has seen Him or has come to know Him.== 



> **7** Little children, let no one deceive you. The one who does righteousness is righteous, just as He is righteous. **8** ==The one who does sin== is of the devil, because the devil sins from the beginning. The Son of God was manifested for this purpose, to destroy the works of the devil. **9** ==Everyone who has been born of God does not sin, because His seed abides in him; and he cannot sin, because he has been born of God.== **10** By this the children of God and the children of the devil are manifested: everyone who does not do righteousness is not of God, as well as the one who does not love his brother.

It would be redundant to say "real children of God", for one either is of God, or is not and is of the devil. Children of God can be recognized by two *works*, by *doing* righteousness and *loving* one's neighbor. 
I have emphasized *works* because of v.7. No one should be deceived by what one *claims* to be, but what one **does**, i.e. one's deeds or fruit, you shall recognize them #todo/opzoeken/reference . 

Note also that it requires *both* righteousness and love in order to be **manifested** as children. This is a very important aspect, which is highlighted throughout this book and the whole Bible. Especially in this age where many churches overemphasize love and miss out on doing righteousness, Christians need to show that only the presence of both works represents God accurately. 

> **1John 3:11**   For this is the message which you have heard from the beginning, that we should love one another; **12** not as Cain, *who* was of the evil one and slew his brother. And for what reason did he slay him? Because his deeds were evil, and his brother’s were righteous.

Cain slayed Abel not only because he himself was evil, but also because his brother's were righteous. The darkness cannot stand the light, it also blinds those in the dark, it exposes them as worse than they looked, it makes them change if they stay long enough in it. Therefore the only way to escape this is to escape to darkness or to extinguish the light, which is what Cain did. 

> **1John 3:13**   Do not marvel, brothers, if the world hates you. **14** We know that we have passed out of death into life, because we love the brothers. The one who does not love abides in death.

It is as if this means that **the one who does not love** resides in a bath of death, so much so that even his head is underwater. Love is what one needs in order to stay afloat, but as soon as a person does not have this floating device anymore, he will go under. 
A question would then be, is this water death that kills him, or is it water that influences him to become like death to anything it comes into contact with? The first is definitely true, eventually, and the second as well. A person who has no love will easily kill others and influence others to be just like he is.

> and you know that no murderer has ==eternal life== abiding in him.

The **eternal life** mentioned here is the present part of the eternal life that God gives us. See notes on John 17:3. It does not say, "no murderer will have eternal life", but it clearly says that he does not have it *now*, and it not *abiding in him*.
Is it true then that murderers do not have the characteristics of the Christian eternal life? Do they not have any peace, joy, happiness, patience or all the other characteristics of the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22)? The answer is "no". Murderers might seem really controlled, and they might have a double life where they can have a family, whom they actually love, but their happiness is only superficial. > **16** By this we have known love, that He laid down His life for us; and we ought to lay down our lives for the brothers.

This is one of the most famous and impactful verses next to the verse with the almost same reference, John 3:16. None of us have known what love is until Jesus Christ laid down His life for us. We *thought* we knew what love was, but it was---and is---only after Jesus died for us (even though that was about 2,000 years ago) and the realization thereof that we *truly knew* what love was. That is what it took for God to let us know His love. 
Now, however, He wants us to love the brothers and sisters and to make *them* realize this. For this we need to **lay down our lives for the brothers**. 
The next verses do not explain how we should do that, for it is on the one hand so simple that it does not require an explanation. Laying down one's life simply just that---to die for and instead of another. 
**Example.** A simple example of this happened in the concentration camps in Germany during the Second World War. The prisoners were lined up and one person was going to die that day. A young Jew was picked from the line by the guard. An older person, however---a Christian, perhaps a pastor, I do not remember---offered his own life. The Jew asked him why he did that, upon which the pastor answered that he was old, but that the Jew still had a wife and a family waiting for him. 
Giving one's life is not easy, but the principle is as easy as that. John thus gives us instructions on how to implement not this, but the giving of *less than one's life* in practice, which turns out to be much more difficult. 
This is usually the case and perhaps for these three reasons:
1. When someone is clearly dying, e.g. drowning, we can easily see that, but when someone is struggling, we do not see a need for help. 
2. Even if we do see this, we will not help these persons immediately, because if we do so, we cannot help those who are really in need. A lifeguard at the beach for example, cannot help people with small matters all the time, because he needs to look out for those in real danger at sea. 
3. We do not *want* to spend our time on small matters. Our gifts were given us for greater matters and problems. There is no glory in saving the small ones. For example, in the book #tags/books *The Deceivers* by Kristen Simmons, a young girl who lives in the suburbs of #todo/opzoeken is struggling to leave her poverty-stricken neighborhood #questions/language/english/spelling achterstandswijk, ghetto etc., but is determined to do so, even if she has to go to night school to get a degree. Now, night schools in America have given many people great opportunities, but there is not much honor in being a teacher there. These teachers have given up a (potential) career and do not get any glory for this. Just think of the character of Walter White in *Breaking Bad*, played by #todo/opzoeken #tags/people/actors Bryan Cranston? who not only had a doctorate in Chemistry but had founded a big company and had left it all, and then taught Chemistry in a small-town high school.
4. Lastly, we are not prepared for these kinds of things. If a soldier is trained for battle on some kind of ground, such as water, he will lose on those terrains he has not been trained for. The famous Chinese war strategist, Sun Tzu's Number One rule is therefore: "Know the type of ground you will be waging war at." #todo/opzoeken This is why John trains us for every situation, great and small, in the following verses.


> **17** But whoever has the world’s goods, and sees his brother in need and closes his heart against him, how does the love of God abide in him?

These are three conditions which show how merciful God is, if indeed we may interpret it this way. Does it mean that any who do not fulfill these three conditions will not be judged? No, they *will* be judged, but in a different way. This verse is not about judgment but it is a statement that the love of God does not abide in those who *do* fulfill these conditions. 
However, we need to realize that it is not difficult to fulfill these conditions. For who does not have the world's goods? Everyone has a little bit of money or other materials which he can give away or sell. Everyone has some access to seeing brothers in need, either through the internet or on TV. And while one of us does not close their heart in some way against his brother. 

> **18** Little children, let us not love with word or with tongue, but in deed and truth. 

How does one **love [...] in [...] truth** and who are **of the truth**? It is those who are God's children and whose **heart does not condemn** them. Whatever is asked by these person is received by them. This is how we can see they are of the truth. 
Notice that I added an extra condition aside from being God's children. Even when we are children of God, John still urges strongly that we should love one another (cf. 1 John 4:4,7) and Paul does that many times in his letters as well, e.g. in his epistles to the Galatians and Corinthians.
There most certainly exists a love that is not of the truth, and I think that even genuine Christians exercise this. An example of this is the love that Christians have for immigrants and for those in their country who do not accept the Gospel, perhaps even up to the point of loving the LGBT+-community in the wrong way. Saddleback Church is an example of this, which is unfortunately mirroring what is going on in many evangelical churches in the United States and in Europe. The people there genuinely want to help others and sometimes cut a few corners to do so. Unfortunately it is the Bible they are cutting in, sometimes unbeknownst. 

> **19** And by this we will know that we are of the truth, and will assure our heart before Him **20** in whatever our heart condemns us; for God is greater than our heart and knows all things. 

If we are of the truth, no matter how great our sin is, that is **in whatever our heart condemns us**, God will always give us assurance that our heart belongs to Him. See also the note on the previous verse. Christians can be of the truth and still sin, but if they have the truth they will see their own sin. 

> **21** Beloved, if our heart ==does not condemn== us, we have confidence before God; **22** and whatever we ask we receive from Him, because we keep His commandments and do the things that are pleasing in His sight.

What would be greater than not only *being* a child of God but also having this **confidence before God**? Notice that this is not a confidence before men. A Christian who lives a holy life can be very confident in front of people less holy than he. But when he is front of holier people, he will become quiet and his confidence will shrink, let alone when he is in front of the Holiest of Holies. 
A question has been asked how or when we ever obtain this state wherein we receive whatever we ask from Him? Jesus had this state, for His Father always listened to His requests, but do Christians have this as well? The well-known missionaries have many stories to tell about miracles in their lives where God listened to their prayer. Hudson Taylor #tags/people/christian/missionaries, for example, left for China #tags/country/china with almost no money. In some occasions God provided a way for him, such as a ship that would go to China, while in other occasions he took away things, such as his money. Did God not listen to Taylor in the latter case? No. God gave him exactly what he wanted. While we do not know exactly what Taylor prayed for, he probably asked that he could be nearer to God (which every Christian would ask for, just like in the well-known hymn [*Nearer my God, to Thee*](https://hymnary.org/text/nearer_my_god_to_thee_nearer_to_thee_een)) and be dependent on God alone.
This is not a prerogative for missionaries alone, but for everyone who is anointed with the Holy Spirit as long as we abide in Him, that is **we keep His commandments and do the things that are pleasing in His sight** (cf. 1 John 4:15-16).
