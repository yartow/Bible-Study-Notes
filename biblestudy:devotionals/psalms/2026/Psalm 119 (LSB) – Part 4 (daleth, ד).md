# Psalm 119 (LSB) – Part 4 (daleth, ד)
#biblestudy/devotionals/psalms/2026

Even though this psalm is not about the stages of life of the believer, but I have interpreted this as such, because of the resemblance with it. v. 25 resembles the stage where a person is hopeless and realizes he needs God revival. But the believer needs to recount his sins and his guilt, and repent of those. God will answer the one who repents (v. 26a).
The true believer is open to God's teachings and even longs for this (v. 26b–27). It is only then that the young believer will see God's **wondrous deeds**, which were formerly unknown to him. 
Even true children of God will suffer loss and grieve, but they will be raised up and encouraged by God (v. 28). Sometimes this suffering is even caused by the believer's own ways. But whether this is the cause or not, he will ask to be sanctified and to receive and know God's law (v. 29). 
After a certain period things should go better in the Christian's life. He is maturing and not only turns from his false ways to seeks God's law and actually *go* the **faithful way** and accept God's **judgments before** him. He knows that he, too, despite being a child of God now, is prone to judgment.
Further on, on the road to maturity, he finds out that the only thing in life he can count on, is God's **testimonies**, that is, His Law, but also the things that God has shown him. He almost literally *clings* on to it, like a person clinging on to a rope from a mountain. he lets everything go, and holds on to one thing only. His hope is then truly set on this single thing. 
In the end, the Christian does not walk God's law anymore, but he *runs*. Just like when a heart is physically enlarged and capable of pumping more blood—and therefore more oxygen—to the muscles, when it is trained, the Christian can now run on it, naturally, without being exhaused (cf. Isaiah 40:31). This, of course, is not what the psalmist had in mind, but I am merely using it as an example. The psalmist was probably thinking of more peace and gladness when he used the enlarged heart. 

> **25** My soul clings to the dust; Revive me according to Your word.

The psalmist is mixing two sayings #questions/language/english/saying the literal term for this? like a dead person whose body literally **clings to the dust** of the Earth, so a spiritually dead person's **soul** clings to this world. Only God's **word** can revive Him. Here, both the capital and small letter "w" are correct, although the psalmist meant the latter given the context, because it is God Who needs to speak in order for us to come alive, both in the physical case at conception as well as during our conversion, when He spoke into our hearts through His Holy Spirit. In the context of this psalm, however, the psalmist is not an unbeliever, but he describes his feeling in an exaggerated way, which is called *hyperbole* in literature. He describes his distance from God as so distant as if he is dead and needs to be revived. However, because he is not actually dead, but grieving (v. 28) he needs comfort, which he can only get from God's **Word** which will then **revive** him.

> **26** I have recounted my ways, and You have answered me; Teach me Your statutes.

In [[Psalm 73 (LSB)]] v. 15 the psalmist, Asaph, said a similar thing, but then decided not to vent his anger and doubts *publicly* because this would betray the generation for which he was responsible. Privately, however, in one's personal prayer to God, it is allowed to speak out one's thoughts, respectfully, to God. 


> **27** Make me understand the way of Your precepts, 
> So I will muse on Your wondrous deeds.

The key word here is **make**, for man cannot understand by himself. He needs the Holy Spirit for that Who works this out in man (cf. Philippians 2:12–13). This is as paradoxical as the man who met Jesus and asked for his daughter to be healed. See Mark 9:24. That man said he believed, but asked Jesus to help him believe. Man knows the law, most of us do at least, but most do not actually **understand** it. 
As soon as man understood, he would **muse on [God's] wondrous deeds**. It is impossible to understand the intricacies of God's Law and not see the beauty of it. 
**Example.** Those who study English or another language have compulsory courses on poetry and literature. Part of those courses is to analyze these in very fine details, to know about the theme and motifs of stories, the background of authors, and the techniques with which they write. By understanding all of these details they see how difficult the process is of writing and they see the beauty of the written work. I have never seen a person before who understood all of these things and still thought there was no beauty in literature.
The same goes for other fields such as biology. There are no biologists who understand the smallest of details in the body and do not find it fascinating. 
Now one might say that I am biased, and that this person became a biologist because he was fascinated about biology. But this is not true. Take for example those who are forced into a studies—which happens in China all the time, due to a shortage of places at the university students apply for and therefore have to enroll in a studies they did not choose—such as a car mechanic. Some people dropped out of school and started working at a car shop. Those who really got into the details of how cars work, are fascinated by it—every single time. 

> **28** My soul weeps because of grief; 
> 	Raise me up according to Your word.

No believer is forever harmed from any **grief**. Pain and death are what show that the Gospel is reality. They are caused by sin and the only solution to escape from these is to escape from sin, which is not possible. 
**Example.** Sin is like a forever chemical, like PFAS, the only thing that can be done to resolve the problem, is by taking it and putting it somewhere *else*. In the same way sin can only be resolved by taking this sin that lies on man, and putting it one someone else—on *Jesus*. 
Now when we said that escaping from sin was *impossible*, I meant that this is impossible for *man*—and with this I mean for anyone except Jesus. Jesus is able to actually *dissolve* this sin, namely by paying for it. Whenever sin is paid for, sin has no power anymore (cf. Romans 6:7ff,14).
Jesus Christ paid for all sin, once for all (1 Peter 3:18). He is not waiting for another person to repent so that He can *then* pay the punishment for that person. No, He *already* paid for it when He was on the cross, for all those who have repented in the past or will repent during their lifetime and accept Jesus. Why then does sin still have power over those will believe in the future and even over those who have *already* accepted Christ? The answer for the former group is that this power is on lease, if you will, but the end of the lease term is when these people accept Christ. As for the latter group—the believers—sin does have power, but it has no legal power. Whenever the Christian is bothered by sin, it is because he let it in himself (cf. James 1:14–15), but even when that happens, it cannot convict of us death, because Christ already paid for the *wages* of sin (Romans 6:23) by His death.
So, when we are knocked down, we can still stand up, because we are not dead, but because we *live with him* (Romans 6:8) we can ask Him to **[raise] us up according to His Word**.

> **29** Remove the false way from me, 
> 	And graciously grant me Your law.

It is not by coincidence that this verse comes right after v. 28 about grievance. Not always, but *often* grief comes because one follows a **false way**. Take for example someone who was in a relationship with a woman and later on realized she was not the right one and has now broken up with her. He realizes he followed a false way and now needs the way of Gods **law** and asks God to **grant** this to him. 
Even though the Law is written in the Bible, the Bible is a living word. A law could be meaningless to someone who does not have the Holy Spirit to make him understand it. The Word could say that one should not lie and a person who is lying could read that and still feel nothing and undertake no action at all. He might even think that message was not meant for him. 
Another reason why the Law needs to be granted is because the Word of God contains so much information it is impossible to remember everything. It takes wisdom for the Holy Spirit to remember a specific law or command and to know that it applies to a specific situation. There are so many examples of people in the Bible that one *needs* God's wisdom to filter out that specific verse that He meant for you to read *today*.

> **30** I have chosen the faithful way; 
> 	I have placed Your judgments before me.

Opposite of the false way, the psalmist has chosen **the faithful way** now. In both cases one will always be faced with judgment, sooner or later, but when one chooses the faithful way, the judgments will be placed **before** him, right now. He will see all day long what God's judgments are. The fear of God is real and the believer should ever realize this. As Spurgeon #tags/people/christian once said #quotes/church  #todo/opzoeken/reference "the believer has looked into hell and runs away from it towards God". #todo verify if it was he who said this.

> **31** I cling to Your testimonies; 
> 	O Yahweh, do not put me to shame!

A person who clings to a truth has much more to lose than someone who does not. For example, a person who has always believed that his father was a righteous man, and even boasts about this among his friends, will be utterly shattered when he finds out that his father was a liar and an unrighteous man, much more so than the one who had a normal father and never boasted about him. Believers should always take the position of the first man, **cling to [God's] testimonies** and not be afraid to be **put [...] to shame**, for this will never happen. God always comes at the right time. Even in the case of William Tyndale #tags/people/christian/martyrs, who was saved many times from death during his life—being persecuted from translating the Bible into English, which was forbidden by the Church of England—but who still died eventually at the burning stake, God fulfilled His testimony at the right time, even though it was *after* Tyndale's death. To man this would seem as too late, as *not* having saved or not having been able to save one's servant, but this was never God's intention nor was this Tyndale's request, for his last words were #quotes/church 
> “Lord, open the King of England's eyes." 
And God, indeed, did open the King James' eyes who would later in life allow the publication of the eponymous King James Bible.

> **32** I shall run the way of Your commandments, 
> 	For You will enlarge my heart.

Depending on how one "divides" this psalm, the last part of this psalm can be read in a chiastic way. The division in this case is not by verses, but as shown below, where each line relates to the opposite line, centered around v. 30b. 

> **28** My soul weeps because of grief; Raise me up according to Your word.
> 	**29** Remove the false way from me, 
> 		And graciously grant me Your law.
> 			**30** I have chosen the faithful way; 
> 				I have placed Your judgments *before me*.
> 			**31** I cling to Your testimonies; 
> 		O Yahweh, do not put me to shame!
> 	**32** I shall run the way of Your commandments, 
> For You will enlarge my heart.

**My soul weeps**, but God **will enlarge my heart**. 
**Remove the false way from me**, and **I shall run the way of Your commandments**. 
**[Grant] me Your law** and **do not put me to shame!** Let the world know that if anyone follows Your Way he will be safe. 
**I have chosen the faithful way** and this means that **I cling to Your testimonies** and will never let go. 
**I have placed your judgments before me**, You and You alone will judge, Your Law and judgment I will gladly accept (v. 30b). #biblestudy/discovery
