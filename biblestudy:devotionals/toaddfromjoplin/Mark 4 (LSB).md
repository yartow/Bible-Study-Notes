> ### The Parable of the Sower
> **Mark 4:1**   [^a]And He began to teach again [^b]by the sea. And such a very large crowd gathered to Him that [^c]He got into a boat in the sea and sat down; and the whole crowd was by the sea on the land. **[^2]** And He was teaching them many things in [^a]parables, and was saying to them in His teaching, **[^3]** “Listen *to this*! Behold, the sower went out to sow; **[^4]** and it happened that as he was sowing, some *seed* fell beside the road, and the birds came and ate it up. 

The fact that **it happened** indicates that the farmer did not do this on purpose. Moreover, the fact that this parable is an allegory does not mean that we can allegorize everything. It can be said that this world is like a field, containing rocks, under and on the soil, and having soil that seems fertile but actually is far from it, or will only let seed grow for a short while. 
In this world, too, there are places where the seed, which is the Word of God, plants and grows better. Ultimately though, this field is compared with our hearts and not the place we live in. The same can be said about the planting of the seed in us. Even if we grow up in a place where the seed is not well received, such as in the West where atheism is rising, or in a specific family in the Nigeria or the province of Wen Zhou, China, #tags/country/china where Christianity is currently thriving, ultimately the decision to choose Christ is dependent on the type of soil of our heart. 

See also source, R.C. Sproul, sermon. #todo/opzoeken/reference 

> **[^5]** “And other *seed* fell on the rocky *ground* where it did not have much soil; and immediately it sprang up because it had no depth of soil.

The first explanation of why the rocky ground—which is not literally ground with rocks, but a bottom full of rocks or a ground that fully consists of rock, like the Grand Canyon, with a little bit of soil on top of that #todo/nogaftemaken  Zie #biblestudy/devotionals/toaddfromjoplin for a more precise explanation—allows the seed to grow faster is often used. However, it is not explained how this spiritually works. Of course, it is clear why truly rocky ground without any soil would not allow any seed to grow, just like the road does not, either because of the birds or because of the lack of minerals and moist, but what would be the difference between deep soil or superficial? Aside from the fact that the superficial soil is more easily warmed up, it is also thrown loose by the wind so that the seed can easily root into the soil, much more so than in the deep soil which is packed because of its weight and thus more dense and more difficult for the seed to root in.
This seems like the third type of soil, where the seed is choked by the weed and thorns and thistles and deprived of sunlight by these. ==The difference between the different types of soil is the *phase* in which the seed is stopped from progressing.== In the first type of soil the seed has not even been planted yet, in the second it can only grow when circumstances are easy enough, while in the third one the seed passes the test of the deep and cold and packed soil sufficiently, but is choked in its growth by circumstances which it encounters later in life.

Jesus explains that the second type of soil will **fall away when persecution or affliction arises because of the word**. He also says that they **immediately receive [the Word] with joy**. This does not mean, however, that the third one does not receive the Word with joy, but it just means that in the explanation of the second type of soil the emphasis is put on the swiftness of receiving the Word and falling away, while in the explanation of the third type of soil the emphasis is placed on the reasons why it falls away. 

Note that the second type of soil's reason for falling away is not only its young phase, but also *persecution and affliction*. Does this mean then that the third type of soil does not walk the same path of tribulation through persecution, but walks one of tribulation through the *temptations* or **worries of the world**? No, I do not think this is so. Some might say that in the West that most of the Christians who fall away from the faith have not experienced persecution and thus fall in the third category of soil. However, every true Christian who accepts the Word with joy, does the Word and spreads it to others *will face* persecution. There are those who have spread the Word door-to-door, like #tags/people/critics/formerchristians Michael Shermer, who have enthusiastically embraced it and even spent their life on it, like Bart Ehrman, but due to the worries of life, perhaps worries about the scientific truth of the Bible or other things that did not rhyme with the image of God *they* had, which caused them to fall away. These two clearly have the third type of soil.
What about all those other Christians who have never been enthusiastic about the faith nor have spread the Gospel either door-to-door or on the streets nor spent a significant amount of their ministry in the mission? These are actually of the type of soil between the first and second type. The Word has not been taken away from them, but it also has never gained enough root to firmly grow. These people also do have worries and care about material things, but these do not choke them off. Therefore they do not belong to the third category. It is not because of these things that they fall off the faith. As a matter of fact, they still go to church, despite their worries, but the faith has never rooted in their hearts. It is *because* of this, that they have never *actively propagated* or *defended* their faith and therefore never were persecuted. 
It still seems strange, however, that those who were willing to defend their faith to persecutors, whether they were physical or philosophical persecutors, become victims of *seemingly lesser* enemies of the faith, which are **the worries of the world, and the deceitfulness of riches, and the desires for anything else**. However, as Jesus already says about the riches, these are *deceitful*, and these worries are just the same. A worry may seem a small thing not in line with the faith and with the eternal life that comes with it, but we are warned against it (James 1:6) and even *commanded* to *always* pray against it (Philippians 4:6). 
Furthermore, it is not only worries and materials, but the desire for *anything else*, which is *everything* in this world! Man may have problems with money, sex and power, but women have another set of temptations and weaknesses. Every person has its weak points, and most would fall off of their faith because of this. Even the most loyal servants of the Colombian government, for example, were bribed by Pablo Escobar #tags/people#, who threatened to kill their wives and children if they did not allow his cargo to pass through. This is how he built his empire, because he knew the weaknesses of people. 

> **[^6]** “And after the sun rose, it was scorched; and because it had no root, it withered away. **[^7]** “And other *seed* fell among the thorns, and the thorns came up and choked it, and it yielded no [^1]crop.

The distinctive feature of the soil is that it bears **no crop**. Jesus mentioned four types of soil and perhaps that was not meant as an exhaustive depiction of the types of openness that people have towards the Word. There are in this parable only two outcomes, those with crop and those without crop. These are easy to distinguish from each other, especially when Jesus says that the former **were yielding crop and produced thirty [...] fold** at the very least. A tenfold crop is very profitable already, so what would a thirtyfold look like? It would be like an apple tree with only big apples and where all the apples are sweet, have no holes in them and where there are no rotten ones. This is almost unimaginable, especially to those who have grown fruit or vegetables such as lettuce, which will always be full of snails, other bugs and holes caused by these. 
So in other words, there are either Christians or there are no Christians, and if some are Christians, they are recognizable. 

**Example.** I once worked at a Christian company. I call it a Christian company because the CEO of that company who founded it, is a Christian, but of course this does not mean that all of the employees nor its management are Christian. Many professed to be Christian, however, and if any solicited to work there it would be mentioned that it is a Christian company where one is also expected to behave as such and respect the expression of the religion, e.g. when there is a prayer or when there are special meetings.
Needless to say, there were many colleagues who professed to be Christian but did not behave as such. I was very much troubled by this, because I did not want this new colleague of mine, who was not a Christian, to think that that is what Christians look like. He told me: "Don't about it. I can see the difference between true Christians and those who only profess they are." I did not need to explain the difference between these two, even though he had not read the Bible. 
On the one hand I was relieved to hear that the Gospel was not tainted by these bad testimonies, but on the other hand these colleagues who say they are Christians are in for quite a shock if they were to receive an anonymous letter saying that e.g. "60% of your colleagues do not think you are a true Christian". 
If you were to receive such a letter, what would your response be? Your colleagues could be wrong, perhaps they do not know you very well, because they have only seen one side of you at work, or perhaps they just happened to see that ugly side of yours which appeared above the surface once. Perhaps you do not want to say you are not a Christian, could you honestly state that you are not on the side of the thirtyfold Christian?

>  **[^8]** “And other *seeds* fell into the good soil, and as they grew up and increased, they were yielding a [^1]crop and produced thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold.” **[^9]** And He was saying, “[^a]He who has ears to hear, [^1]let him hear.”

A question that is often asked, is, "Which type of soil are you?" An even better question, however, is, "Which type of soil do you want to be, honestly?" A person with the good type of soil must die of himself (this example was used by Jesus of the seed or kernel which had to die first in order to become fruitful), he must be broken and ploughed—everything in his life will be turned upside down! But even when one is willing to all of that, another question arises, "Is it possible to change the type of soil you have?" The answer to that is "No", but even though you cannot, the good thing is that God *can*!
Jesus is thus in fact saying with **let him hear** that we should *listen*, as is often said colloquially, "you are hearing, but you are not listening", or the other way around. Those who have read this passage or heard a sermon about this have heard the Gospel and heard Jesus' explanation on what needs to be changed in our hearts, but have we listened? If we have, we will *act* accordingly, knowing that we have a responsibility to respond to the word. 

**Example.** There was a person whom I wanted to convert to Christ and I read Revelation 3:20 with him about Jesus knocking on the door of your heart. So I told him, "Jesus wants to enter your heart, you just have to let Him in" to which he responded, "I would open the door if I heard anyone knocking, but I don't." I said, "Well, the fact that I'm telling you right now already means you're hearing His call, right?"

Are we waiting for a call before we get into action? If so, what kind of call do we expect then? Is not the Word itself, or a sermon preached from the Word, enough of a call? #tags/theology/gospel
