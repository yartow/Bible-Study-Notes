# # 2 John (LSB) 
> ### Walk in Truth and Love
> **2 John 1**   The elder to the elect lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all who know the truth, **2** for the sake of the truth which abides in us and will be with us forever: **3** Grace, mercy, *and* peace will be with us, from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love.
> 
> **2 John 4**   I rejoiced greatly to find *some* of your children walking in truth, just as we received commandment from the Father. **5** Now I ask you, lady, not as though *I were* writing to you a new commandment, but the one which we have had from the beginning, that we love one another. **6** And this is love, that we walk according to His commandments. This is the commandment, just as you have heard from the beginning, that you should walk in it.

There is a circular reasoning here. The commandment is to **love another**, and love is to **walk according to His commandments**. #todo/nogaftemaken 

> **2 John 7**   For many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who do not confess Jesus Christ *as* coming in the flesh. This is the deceiver and the antichrist. **8** See to yourselves, that you do not lose what we accomplished, but that you may receive a full reward. **9** Anyone who goes too far and does not abide in the teaching of Christ, does not have God. The one who abides in the teaching, he has both the Father and the Son. 

**[T]oo far** means here that in the field of not **[abiding] in the teaching of Christ**. It is hard to see how "too far" would refer to "**not confessing Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh**" for that would not be something anyone can do partially, rather it is binary #questions/language/english/spelling#, that is one either confesses or he does not confess that Jesus Christ is God and has now incarnated in the flesh. 
Every teacher makes mistakes in his teachings and does not abide in Him on a daily basis. Christians make mistakes, but there are levels of sin which the Christian does, and there is the intention, frequency and seriousness of the sin that matter as well. It is hard to know where to draw the line, but the person who is led by the Spirit (1 John 2:27, Galatians 5:16,25) will know at the right time when a teacher **goes too far**.

> **10** ==If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching, do not receive him into *your* house, and do not give him a greeting, **11** for the one who gives him a greeting participates in his evil deeds.==

This verse refers to the teacher who has gone too far. At such a point, when the Christian realizes that no discussion is possible with the teacher because of his stubbornness or any other shortcoming to realize the errors in his teaching, the Christian, should let the elders take over this discussion. But when the teacher does not even listen to the elders, the church members should not try to discuss with him individually. The danger is that the church member will be led astray by the teacher. Rather and better yet, they should stay away from him and treat him as an unbeliever (cf. 1 Corinthians 5 #todo/opzoeken/reference ). 
Why should this teacher not be treated then? Assuming that this teacher is not evil socially, and does not have intentions to do, but is simply mistaken in his beliefs, why should we treat him so harshly so as to not even greet him when encountering him, for example, on the street? The greeting mentioned here is in the Ancient Near East context #tags/culture/ane#.  This means this is not a simple hello and goodbye, such as is done in the West today. Rather yet, it entails an invitation to one to have at least a cup of water and, if suited, some food. Paul, however, has explicitly mentioned in 1 Corinthians 5, based on Jesus' commands in Matthew #todo/opzoeken/reference that the excommunication must be put in practice to let the sinner realize his sin through the lack of fellowship. This is what John means by not greeting the sinner. So of course one can still say " Hello" on the streets and have a short talk, but it must be clear to the sinner that no more than that is allowed. 
This is why it is so important that the command for excommunication must be done publicly so that the whole church and community will know about the sin on this person, this new rule, and so that the community will keep each other in check. 
**Example.** A church member and friend of mine divorced his wife because of immorality on his spouse's side. The leaders of the congregation officially and publicly condemned her and her sin, but her family did not obey the leadership. Instead, they kept inviting her to birthday parties and other events, making it awkward for her former husband, but also did she not miss any of her previous fellowship and social contact. Moreover, even worse, she did not realize her sin nor was she reminded of it every time she came into contact with one of the church members. The worst part was that even though the leadership had made this decision, they did not reprimand her nor her family for disobeying the rules. 
This of course leads to a total depravity of the social structure and can easily lead to more sin in the church, because there is no fear and it is obvious that the leadership does not implement any of the consequences it warned of.
