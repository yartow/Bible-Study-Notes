> ### Bear One Another’s Burdens
> **Gal. 6:1**   Brothers, even if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are spiritual, restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, *each of you* looking to yourself, so that you too will not be tempted. 

The difficulty of behaving as a Christian is in reproving those who are **caught in any transgression** while at the same time realizing our sin and the *gentleness* with which we have been treated by Jesus, and the *mercy* the Father has shown us when we were still enemies of Him (cf. Romans 5:6-11). More than that, we *still* are sinners. This does not mean that we can never reprove a person for the sins he committed, but we must keep our own sinful nature in mind, and *humble* ourselves, so that we can restore this person **in a spirit of gentleness**.

> **2** Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ. **3** For if anyone thinks he is something when he is nothing, he deceives himself. 

The following part I wrote when on an evening where I led a group of people of our fellowship in a Bible study. My way of leading was not appreciated.
*I thought I was something, I thought I was a good teacher, someone who could control the flow of the evening, but I was wrong. If I had been a good group leader, I would have felt that the group was used to a different kind of leadership, the soft kind of leadership, the not-so-punctual kind of leadership where programs start and end later, where people are not interrupted even when they are over time and where people can freely speak off-topic.* 

> **4** But each one must examine his own work, and then he will have *reason for* boasting in regard to himself alone, and not in regard to another. **5** For each one will bear his own load.

If anyone boasts, he should do so compared to an absolute standard, not relative to other people. All sports activities are based on comparisons between people of all times, which is not good for two reasons. The first one being the argument Paul uses here, namely that none of us can say we are good—either in sport or morally—if we compare ourselves to others. In sports alone man can be defeated by any animal on almost every field[^1], whether it is in the height or distance of jumping, the speed with which we run or the strength with which we lift up things. An ape alone could beat man in all these things. Just look at the difficulties with which people climb mountains or boulders, while any ape or monkey lands or ascends[^2] these within seconds, sometimes not even having to climb these because of their mere swinging and jumping power.
As for our **own work**, if this is evaluated at its own, not meaning that one should compare one's own work with what he had done before—for one should still not boast if he is now better than before—but rather compared to Jesus' works, then he can boast. So in other words, he can never boast and Christians should never boast in their work. 

> **Gal. 6:6**   And the one who is instructed in the word is to share in all good things with the one who instructs *him*. 

Christians should be humble and realize that everything they have is given them by God's grace. Even on a human level, everything they know, has been taught them by others. Not only humility is needed but gratefulness as well to **the one who instructs**. In many non-Western cultures students as well as parents of students are grateful to the teacher, because it is a low-paying job, with little benefits. The real enjoyment of a teacher is in handing over knowledge and passion to his students, but at many times the student does not appreciate the effort a teacher puts in his students until many years later when he has finally grown up and has spiritually become more mature. A few examples of where these things are shown are the movie #tags/movies *Mr. Holland's Opus* for which Richard Dreyfuss received an Academy Award nomination, where the appreciation for the teacher's comes is shown decades after students have graduated, and the animation series #tags/movies/series/animation *Great Teacher Onizuka*, which depicts the lack of passion of most teachers and who see teaching merely as a job, instead of as a *responsibility* of these students.
Christians need to exemplary in this, both as teachers as well as as students, and not only as students in seminary or in school, but also as receivers of the Word, when we listen our weekly sermons or when we attend seminars or conferences. 
This is why I am of the opinion that conference and seminar organizers should not have to ask Christian attendees for a fee, but rather, Christians should be instructed and know that they have to give out of their own hearts. 
The organization *Open Doors*, founded by Brother Andrew (Anne van der Bijl) #tags/people/christian/missionaries, which helps persecuted Christians all over the world, has yearly conferences. At a certain point in time they decided to rent a bigger place for their conferences, because these would accommodate more people and would be more centrally located so that people from places further away, such as Belgium #tags/country/belgium —which has a very low number of Bible-believing (Protestant) Christians—could also attend. The costs of this place (the *Jaarbeurs* in Utrecht) were incredibly high, but the voluntary donations where much higher than the costs. Every year God showed His goodness for the faith the organization put in Him. 
However, during COVID #tags/history/corona they feared a very low attendance and moved back to their former, smaller and farther located, conference hall, forgetting the many times how God had helped throughout great financial risks. In fact, the conference hall was so small that they could only house half of the people, and had to hold the same conference program (music, sermons and testimonies) twice on the same day. I am not certain, but the total attendance was much less than it was before in Utrecht, and even though they saved costs with this location, it is not sure whether the net sum was greater. In any case, to me it seems their faith in God was less and their faith in the calculation of risk was greater.

> **7** Do not be deceived, God is not mocked, for whatever a man sows, this he will also reap. **8** For the one who sows to his own flesh will from the flesh reap corruption, but the one who sows to the Spirit will from the Spirit reap eternal life.

It seems as if v. 7 suddenly appears out of nothing, but in the context of v. 6 it could mean that whatever good we share with the one who has instructed us, must be genuinely out of one's heart. We may be able to deceive people, by supposedly giving a great amount of money to the church, but in fact doing this for our own benefit, since it could be tax deductible or have other forms of benefits. It could also benefit one's reputation and in some churches even one's decision power. 
**Example.** In my personal life I have, without realizing at the time, deceived myself. I once gave a donation to a Bible translating organization of about a hundred euros. For this, however, I received a study Bible which was worth 80 euros, and I received a tax cut of about 40 euros. I do not know if I was mocking God, I would have if I had boasted to others about my supposed generosity, but I did feel like I was doing these organizations a favor. 
**Example.** An example of mocking God is found in a guy named Rob, about whom Mike Winger #tags/people/christian did a video on YouTube because he claimed that according to the Bible Christian men should be polygamists. For this he twisted God's commands such as "Be fruitful and multiply" and in doing so mocked God. It is hard to believe that he did this unknowingly, as if he has been tricked by the devil, or been taught by a false teacher. 

But **God is not mocked**, if any man does these things, he will not receive a reward from God (cf. Matthew 6:2,5), but they will receive a reward from another source (cf. Romans 6:23). v. 8 even says that their reward will be **corruption**, not in sense of bribery, but in the sense that everything they have will ultimately be destroyed (Matthew 6:19). 
**Example.** In many movies this same concept is depicted in wicked—and especially rich—people, who have gone over many dead bodies in order to gain the wealth they currently have, but what they have built up—their empire—is as weak as the weakest link. They built an empire, not on trust but on greed. In the TV series #tags/movies/series *Tulsa King* for example, one family within the Mafia of New York finds its empire collapsing as soon as the son of the leader #questions/language/english/spelling betrays his own father and his father's former best friend (Sylvester Stallone #tags/people/actors). Within a period of mere months he loses the trust of all his workers #questions/language/english/spelling and that of the heads of other families.

> **9** And let us not lose heart in doing good, for in due time we will reap if we do not grow weary. **10** So then, while we have opportunity, let us do good to all people, and especially to those who are of the household of the faith.

There many who have **[sown] to the Spirit** and done good things, but all of it is worthless[^3] if we grow weary. Just like in a race, when one gives up before he has reached the end, all of it is worthless. But if we **do not grow weary**, **we will reap**. We have to continue until the end. The last remark is there to remind Christians of their *duty* to help fellow Christians, just like *Open Doors* does, and in the same way Christians individually should do that as well. 

It is easy to **lose heart in doing good**, because there are so many factors that discourage us. In Dutch there is this saying, "Wie goed doet, ontmoet goed", meaning that those who do good things, will meet good things. However, we know that as a believer in God this very often not the case. For sure, there are many blessings God gives to those who follow Him and love Him (cf. Romans 8:28, Psalm 121:7), but none of these are guarantees in the sense of *our interpretation* of blessings. Daniel was thrown in the lion's den and Job lost everything, including his children. In both cases these people gained big rewards afterwards, but this does not apply to all people—especially martyrs, who gained nothing, except possibly a legacy or remembrance, because they lost their lives. All of these people received the greatest reward in Heaven, and the blessing and protection they received on Earth was that their faith was kept until the end, which is the fulfillment of 1 Corinthians 10:13 (cf. Philippians 1:6). Now most Christians are in-between the martyr—who was left with nothing—or Job—who was doubly blessed—but the fact remains that there is a great possibility that they receive next to nothing for all the good work they have done. Yet, they are prepared for *good works* (Ephesians 2:10) and this is their purpose. #biblestudy/discovery
The Christian will **grow weary** when he keeps doing this all on his own. At the very least, he needs other Christians to pray for him. Robert Jermain Thomas #tags/people/christian/martyrs/missionaries is one of the few martyrs within *the Torchlighter*-series #tags/movies/series/animation/christian who worked all alone, without so much as a wife to support him when he came home. But few are like him—as gifted or called in the way like as he was for a mission as extraordinary as his. "Normal" Christians need other Christians to be with in the mission, to share their lives with and to pray for and to be accountable to (cf. James 5:16). This is what will prevent them from being weary (#todo/opzoeken/reference Isaiah 14:21).

> **Gal. 6:11**   See with what large letters I am writing to you with my own hand! **12** As many as are wanting to make a good showing in the flesh, these are trying to compel you to be circumcised, simply so that they will not be persecuted for the cross of Christ. **13** For those who are circumcised do not even keep the Law themselves, but they want to have you circumcised so that they may ==boast in your flesh==. 

These false teachers were afraid of persecution themselves. I do not know whether they were circumcised before they were persecuted. If they were Jews, then they would be. Because of the threat of persecution it seems like these people were Jews who had seemingly "followed" Christ temporarily. But because of pressure from the Jews from Israel, they reverted to Judaism—by people like Paul used to be—they decided **to compel** their converts—in this case the people from Galatia—**to be circumcised** and follow the Mosaic Law again, *deny Jesus Christ* (perhaps Jude 4 refers to this as well), and through this reach salvation. 
However, it is clear that they do not believe in Judaism, because they **do not even keep the Law themselves**, rather yet, by having more believers being circumcised, they seem to have more followers and they can boast in the size of their faction.

> **14** But may it never be that I would boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world. 

I understand that Paul would boast in his lord, just like children boast in their father. Children actually do not claim anything about themselves, when they boast about their own father, but they are praising him for what he is or can do. This is what Paul means, he will never boast in himself, and even though he says "**except**", there is no exception, for his "exception" is that he boasts **in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ**, which still is not a boast by definition per se, but rather an appraisal of Jesus' strength, willpower and works on the cross.

> **15** For neither is circumcision anything, nor uncircumcision, but a new creation. **16** And those who will walk in step with this rule, peace and mercy *be* upon them, and upon the Israel of God.

What is this **rule**? The rule is that those who *do not* look at outer appearances such as the circumcision. This principle is of course applied in examples such as the following or belief in a certain doctrine, and as an extension of that, being of a certain denomination. The only thing that matters is that they are **a new creation** and are now **of the household of the faith** (v. 10). If we **walk in step with this rule, peace and mercy** is upon us. 
Israel as a country—nor Israelites individually—has never walked in step with this rule. They have never seen Christians as members of the house of God but always pushed for the need of circumcision. Still, they are obviously protected by God, which is only because of His mercy and patience, and not because they walked in step with this rule. This is possibility part of the reason why the **peace** is still not **upon them**, not in the spiritual way, but also not in the geopolitical way. 

> **Gal. 6:17**   From now on let no one cause trouble for me, for I bear on my body the marks of Jesus.

This is an usual ending, for Paul, for his time, but also for our time. It is quite abrupt, first of all, but more than that it gives the impression of a tiresome sigh. Paul seems to be tired here, not having even the energy to write any greetings from the brothers or to inquire. It could be that he did not have much time to write when he wrote this from prison #todo/opzoeken (zat hij in de gevangenis?) or that he wanted this letter to be sent as soon as possible, because of its importance in protecting the churches in Galatia from false teachings. 
It is remarkable to see that Paul sees this issue as causing **trouble for [him]**. I do not know in what way Paul was involved, other than having founded at least a few of these churches. But technically speaking—I suppose—no one asked him to be involved, so it did not cause *him* any trouble. It would have been more like that Paul *heard* of what happened in Galatia and decided to become involved himself. However, we know that within Christianity things are not isolated. The pain of any brother or sister is felt by the whole body, whether one's help is requested or not, it is trouble for every member of the Body. 
In this particular case it would be even more clear why it would cause Paul trouble, namely because of his reputation. People want to uphold a place to preserve the reputation of the one who built it. 
**Example.** In Gordon Ramsay's #tags/people *Hell's Kitchen* series, there was one Mexican restaurant in Hollywood, owned by the son of the founder, who had passed away. #todo/nogaftemaken 
Paul is not writing any of this to uphold his own reputation or to keep his work afloat. Perhaps he even knew that the church of Ephesus would become corrupt as well as about other churches' ultimate ending #questions/language/english/spelling and that there was nothing that could be done about it. Paul does all this work in order to bring Jesus glory—not just for His reputation, but also to save as many souls as possible in the time that these churches have left before their demise.
