Jacob spent 17 years in Egypt and saw how God's promises were fulfilled. First, God had kept Joseph. God had taken care of his son, so that he would have *twelve* sons with their own land in the Holy Land that would be theirs later—no sons were lost, and he would even gain two more, Joseph's own two sons. Furthermore, he would see how God blessed Joseph as the prime minister #questions/language/english/spelling werkelijk woord voor Jozefs bediening of Egypt and the wisdom and #questions/language/english/spelling voorspoed that God brought upon him and the kingdom. Lastly, he would see that no one of his family was to die of famine. God provided, and even though Jacob had to go through much hardship (even the supposed loss of Benjamin and the travel to Egypt), he now was in a place where his flock could fare well (47:6) and his people were nourished (Genesis 50:21). 
This number, seventeen, is one that does not often occur. I think this is the sum of ten and seven, where ten and seven are the number of human and godly completeness, respectively. Jacob had seen his life being fulfilled and all the pieces of the puzzle were now being put in place. The reason for his hardship was now known, and even more so, not so much his faith in God but his knowledge of God's provision were now fulfilled ([[Genesis 48:15]]). The first part is the human part, the ten years, and the second the seven years.
