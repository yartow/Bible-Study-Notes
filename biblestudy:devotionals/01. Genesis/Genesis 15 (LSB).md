# Genesis 15 (LSB) 

> ### Yahweh Cuts a Covenant with Abram
> **Gen. 15:1**   After these things the word of Yahweh came to Abram in a vision, saying, 
> 	“Do not fear, Abram, 
> 	I am a shield to you; 
> 	Your reward shall be very great.”
> 
> **Gen. 15:2**   And Abram said, “O Lord Yahweh, what will You give me, as I go on being childless, and the heir of my house is Eliezer of Damascus?” **3** And Abram said, “Since You have given no seed to me, behold, one born in my house is my heir.” **4** Then behold, the word of Yahweh came to him, saying, “This one will not be your heir; but one who will come forth from your own body, he shall be your heir.” **5** And He brought him outside and said, “Now look toward the heavens, and number the stars, if you are able to number them.” And He said to him, “So shall your seed be.” **6** Then he believed in Yahweh; and He counted it to him as righteousness. 

This verse is often quoted to indicate the righteousness that is supposedly bestowed on as Christians. The main emphasis is that this righteousness is not earned but given by grace. While this point concerning grace instead of merit is correct, the word *given* is not, especially not the past participle that indicates that it has *already been given*. The more correct way of saying this is that righteousness *will be given* to Christians, either when they die here on Earth or when Jesus passes the final judgment. However, because it is a *certainty*[^1] that the true Christian perseveres (Philippians 1:6, Romans 8:29-30) until the Day of Judgment, God *treats* Christians  *in advance* as if they are righteous—even though they are not, for no one is (Psalm 14:3). This treatment is phrased as it being **counted [...] as righteousness**. Another term that is used (in the NIV) is that it was *credited to him*. Just like where a person with a creditcard can use money *as if* he possesses it and where the accountability is at the end of the month, so Christians can live a life of righteousness as if they have already obtained, but where the judgment still needs to come—of which Christians already know the outcome (cf. Philippians 3:12f). I could not have said it better than the [GotQuestions page](https://www.gotquestions.org/imputed-righteousness.html) on this topic:

> By having the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, we can be seen as sinless, as Jesus is sinless. This is amazing grace! We are not righteous in ourselves; rather, we possess Christ’s righteousness applied to our account. It is not our perfection but Christ’s that God sees when He brings us into fellowship with Himself. We are still sinners *in practice*, but the grace of God has declared us to have righteous standing before the law.
