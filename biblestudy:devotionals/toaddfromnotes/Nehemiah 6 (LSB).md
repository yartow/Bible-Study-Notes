> ### The Wall Is Finished
> **Neh. 6:15**   So the wall was finished on the twenty-fifth of *the month* Elul, in fifty-two days. **16** Now it happened that when all our enemies heard *of it*, and all the nations surrounding us saw *it*, their confidence fell. And they knew that it was from our God that this work had been accomplished.

The best way to to psychologically defeat one's enemies is to gain victory, especially an unexpected victory under difficult circumstances. This was the case with the Jews. Despite the financial hardships due to their nobles' usury, the low morale because of the ongoing threats, the deteriorating physical conditions and stamina because of the continuous work day and night, the people finished the building of the wall in a #questions/language/english/spelling whopping **fifty-two days**! Given the current technology of that time as well as initial state the walls were—they did not only build these almost as good as from scratch, but had to remove older and broken pieces of wall as well, and also all the delivered material, such as timber, probably needed to be processed before these were ready for use—this was an amazing feat.

[^1]: There is a common misconception that *goodness* refers to how *good* a person is, in the sense of being morally *good and bad*. However, *good* in the Biblical context of this noun (goodness) refers to how much goods one gives to another. We do not need to discuss whether God is morally good, because He is by definition. Everything He does is good, and everything anyone does differently is not good *by definition*. Whenever the Bible mentions that God is *good*, it is meant that He is a great giver, giving generously, giving rain to both those who love him as well as those who hate him. Not only a giver of material things (rain is something material as well) but also of immaterial things, namely of his mercy, love and justice. All of His characteristics are morally good, but God *is also* good because He *gives* of those things. So, once again, God already is good by nature and by definition, but when His goodness is mentioned, the *showing* of His characteristics is meant.
