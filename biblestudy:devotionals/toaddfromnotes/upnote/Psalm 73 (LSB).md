# Psalm 73 (LSB)

> [1] Surely God is good to Israel, To those who are pure in heart! 

I have never doubted the goodness of God. I have been “blessed”—if you will—with a particular kind of naivité, which allows me to wholeheartedly believe in people's good words and fall and for their traps, to believe advertisements, and easily get mugged in certain places. On the other hand, it allows to believe in the goodness of God, without ever doubting His intentions, righteousness and the execution of justice. 
Matthew 5 also says, #todo/opzoeken/reference Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God. 
Now I am far from holy, and I dare not say I am pure of heart, but being able to see Him is like seeing the lighthouse at the beach, in dark stormy seas. I have always had hope in God, for Him to do miracles and save me in times of trouble. 

> [2] But as for me, my feet had almost stumbled, My steps had almost slipped. [3] For I was envious of the boastful, I saw the peace of the wicked.

This is so true about my life. In fact, the whole psalm seems to describe me and my struggles with the faith.
It is only because of God's faithfulness that I am still a faithful believer in Christ. All my life I have seen people more successful than I, in all kinds of fields—in academics, sports, looks and relationships. All I wanted was to be better than they. These people were not necessarily wicked, but I was, for I was willing to put God aside to achieve my goals to become like the people I envied. 
My steps had already slipped, for I had sinned many a time, spending money on clothes and on girls, but most of all, relying on myself instead of on God and ignoring His voice. It was all due to my pride. I saw others being boastful, and I wanted to be able to boast as well, not knowing that the only thing worth boasting in, is in Jesus Christ.

>  [4] For there are no pains in their death, And their body is fat. [5] They are not in trouble as other men, And they are not stricken along with the rest of mankind. 

All I saw in others is that they had **no pains**. Their lives seemed to be perfect. Of course I realized later on that things were not as perfect as they seemed. My classmates' parents divorced, others did not graduate, and all lived immoral lives, including myself. In those days everyone was not only proud, but also arrogant and lofty, thinking they were better than others, yet at the same time unconfident #questions/language/english/word about themselves..

> **6** Therefore lofty pride is their necklace; The garment of violence covers them.
> **7** Their eye bulges from fatness; The delusions of their heart overflow.
> **8** They scoff and wickedly speak of oppression; They speak from on high.

The times have changed, but the world and its underlying principles have not. All throughout history affluence and wealth were seen in the **fatness** of people. This is the reason why women in Greek and Roman sculptures and in paintings, all the way from the medieval times, were fat, rather than slender, because it portrayed the ideal image of affluent women. 
In these days there are other fashion trends by which affluence is shown, but not only affluence is important, but also individuality. Therefore fashion trends today show the expression of who one is, through tattoos, custom made jewelry and clothes. It strongly surpasses the limits of modest clothing, indicating one's bravery and uniqueness—until another person does the same things, after which the bar is set higher.
The **pride** of man has not changed, and it is the basis of all of their expression. **[Violence]** was shown as a sign of power. The Mafia and other gangsters gain a lot of respect, and many young men want to be like them, because of it. 

> **9** They have set their mouth against the heavens, 
> And their tongue goes through the earth.

Some of the people who curse God or damn themselves through curses invoking God do not know that they have **set their mouth against the heavens**, others are fully aware of this. Regardless, whether they believe in the existence of God or not, this is what they are doing, and not only that, they are *proclaiming* this—especially these days, this is done on the internet and the message goes **through the earth**. Perhaps some people like Bart Ehrman #tags/people/critics/formerchristians, who said in his debate with Dinesh D'Souza #tags/people/christian about the topic #todo/opzoeken/reference whether God is good, that his goal was not to turn believers into agnostics or atheists, but to make them critical in their search for the truth. This intention seems to be a good one, but at the same time he gave arguments that cast shadows on the goodness of God by asking rhetorical questions on a supposedly good God Who still chooses to let people suffer, not only through the consequences of other people, but also by nature itself. Now, if he really did not know the answer to these questions, I could understand his position. But even though he claims not to have the answers to these questions, he still has *concluded* that God is not good—if He were to exist—and propagated this message as if he were hoping that he could convince others to this position as well. This is a clear example of his *pride* #questions/language/english/word hoogmoed, to come to a *conclusion* despite the fact that he does not have the answers, and a lack of *humility* to pretend that *he does* have the answer. Years before this debate, Rev. James D. Kennedy already mentioned on his show #todo/opzoeken/reference as well as in the first of his sermon series *Truths that Transform* (also available as book #tags/books/christian/theology) that Rabbi Kushner used the same argumentation, in a manner as if he found out something new, where he posited that a good God does want people to suffer, but since people do suffer, God is either not all powerful or not good. Despite the fact that there is not an answer to the question when it is posited in this way, Rabbi Kushner chose the former, namely that God is not all powerful. Ehrman chose his way out by claiming that no such God exists Who is both all-good and all-powerful. 
Both are wrong in their assertion that God does not *want* people to suffer. The first mistake is that both mix up the verbs *want* and *will* and do not distinguish between the two. What God *wants* means what God *would like* for things to be, and what God *wills* means that God has decided that certain things should *happen* and that they should happen in *His specific way*. The second thing is that the verb *to want* is applicable in two levels. The first is what God longs, namely that man repents of his sin and turns his heart toward God. On a higher level, however, he wants certain people to suffer, because it leads to a certain outcome that He has planned. Then, God *wills* the latter into existence.

This is worse than atheists who deny there is a God, or genuine agnosts who do not know whether there is one. But these are people who *know* there is a god or—in this culture—believe that there are many gods, and they despise the god of Israel. 
Why would anyone knowingly despise a god of whom one knows he is greater than one's own god? The person who does that knows he will be destroyed by this god, and so will his own god. 
The answer is that this lies in multiple facts. The first one is that they are *blinded* by their own lusts, their evilness and wickedness. Drug dealers and other criminals continuously defy the government and special task forces, such as Pablo Escobar did, the fictional ... from *Scarface* #tags/movies with Al Pacino ... and the Sundance Kid #todo/opzoeken/reference (with Robert Redford and Paul Newman). By *captivity* I do not mean a life in prison, but a life according to God's rules—that is, a moral life, with freedom only bound by God's Law. 
The second fact is that they know they will not go to Heaven, if they even believe in such a thing. They know that what they are doing is bad. Even the less wicked people, who do their best to live a good life, know that their past deeds cannot be simply erased.

But Jesus did say, literally, that "it is finished" because His atonement was sufficient for all the people in the whole world, both past, present and future.
The only thing left for these people, is either to atone for their sins, or to continue on this rampage and take what they can before they die, and to drag everyone to hell along with them.

> **12** Behold, these are the wicked; And always at ease, they have increased in wealth.

The more the wicked are **at ease**, the more envious I become. I have always asked myself why the wicked are not worried, as if they have no problems in life. If they even increase **in wealth** this makes me doubt the decisions I have made in life. I will then wonder whether I should have taken another career or taken chosen differently. But all of this is vain thinking. It was through the protection of God that I have been withheld from making these wrong choices and chosen the better. Every good choice I have made was due to His protection, and every bad choice is due to my own fault. The vanity and arrogance of my thoughts lie in the short-term thinking of pleasure and wealth, instead of of pleasing God and the long-term rewards that has for us. Of such bad character and such small stature is man that he even needs a reward in order to be motivated to do what is good. For he thinks, "What good is it to do good, if one does not gain anything by it?", for the prospect of being with God is not sufficient. He needs to have Heaven as well, in the afterlife, and the kingdom of Heaven while on Earth, with peace and hope. 
Forgive me, Lord, for not having considered Your presence as sufficient, unlike Horatio G. Spafford #music/composers wrote in his song, *It is well with my soul* #tags/music.

> **14** For I have been stricken all day long And reproved every morning.

> **15** If I had said, “I will recount thus,” Behold, I would have betrayed the generation of Your children.

If recounting has the same meaning as the word used in Psalm 119:13,26, then this does not refer to getting even with one's enemy, but rather to bring this up in one's active memory and even say it out loud. In other words, David says here that if he intended to remember all of the times he had **been stricken [and] reproved [, he] would have betrayed [God's] children**. Such is the character of man that he would **recount** when he was *struck*, but not when he was *blessed*. If he had done so, his betrayal would have been the lie he spread about God's goodness, or rather the lack thereof. Speaking negatively about someone, and highlighting his bad points, without mentioning his good points, is slander. And when this is recounted to a **generation**, who naively believes this message, it has been **betrayed** and tricked into believing the lie. 
This is the case with President Donald J. Trump #tags/people who is constantly negatively portrayed by the CNN, while his accomplishments are almost never told about in the news. Now God does not have any points, but He is still portrayed negatively by many in this world. Those people spread lies about God and about His people, the Israelites (or Jews, as they are called today), and His children, the Christians. Only the negative things they do are told, but not the good things, and the fact that the whole success of the West is due to Jews and Christians. #biblestudy/discovery

> **18** Surely You set them in slippery places; You cause them to fall to destruction.

The enemies of God will **fall to destruction** eventually, though this will not always happen in this life. But even worse, God **[sets] them in slippery places**, even here on Earth. Now it is true that *God tempts no one* (James 1:13), so this verse must be understood in the right way, namely that it is not God's cause that they slip or fall, if they do, but that it is because they decided to go near these slippery places themselves. 

> **19** How they become desolate in a moment! They are completely swept away by terrors!

The wicked and powerful ones in this world seem indestructible, which is why so many people in the world want to be like them. They do not realize, however, that the powerful are mere people as well, with the same weaknesses and fears that all humans have. Most movies do not show this, but some of the more realistic movies show that even the bad guys have fears, due to an attachment to people they love or simply because they might lose power.

> **20** Like a dream when one awakes, O Lord, when aroused, You will despise their form.

Just like one's fear is immediately gone when one realizes it was just a nightmare **when [he] awakes**, the wicked realizes the opposite, namely that all of his power and wealth cannot help him against his wicked deeds and that judgment is coming for him. #biblestudy/discovery

The people who are upright and believe in God can still be **senseless and arrogant [and] like an animal** when they are hurt. I have experienced this first hand, and but also second hand in others. 
**Example.** At the moment my wife is ill as a cause of Topical Steroid Withdrawal Syndrome. It is the third year she is dealing with this and every year it comes back in a lighter form. The first year, however, it was unbearable for her and she had hardly slept in a period of three months, her skin was dry, itching as a cause of flare-ups while it was being scratched away because she could not bear the itch and pain.
In those months she could not feel God near her either. It was almost as if—or perhaps she truly had—she had lost all hope of ever healing, and more importantly, the hope in the goodness of God.

> **24** With Your counsel You will lead me, And afterward take me in glory.

God leads us through His **counsel**. This might seem logical, but if you think about it, there are many ways in which He could lead. He could lead by *example*, which He did when He was on Earth, but only to those who knew Him at the time. He could lead through punishment and judgment, which has done at specific times. In Solomon's life, for example, it seems like God was mostly not present, and only when Solomon crossed the lines, did He intervene. Of course, this is only what is written. In reality, however, most likely, He led with His counsel. He gave Solomon wisdom—as He has given to all of us—and with that we can know His law.
