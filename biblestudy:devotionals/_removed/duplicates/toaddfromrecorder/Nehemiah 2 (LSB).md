Nehemiah was afraid because he was now to speak out the truth. His longing was for the city and its walls to be restored to the beauty it had before. Any city which would restore its fortresses, however, or rebuild its army, police or any militia, was deemed as a threat to the empire. For the same reason the Egyptians were very much afraid of the Israelites, because they were growing too vast in number, and in China there is the same kind of fear of the number of Christians which is steadily growing *despite*—China thinks, but actually it is *because of*—persecution. Nehemiah had gained the trust of the king in so much as to even be his personal cupbearer, and the king liked him so much that he even wanted him to return (v.6). A request such as this, which Nehemiah could no longer withhold, as he could not lie to the king or hide his longings from him, *could* have caused a breach in the king's trust in him, as if Nehemiah had staged all of his past years of work for the king only to have permission to return to Jerusalem, rebuild the city walls and regroup the people into forming a new rebellion against the kingdom. 
Fortunately, though, the king believed Nehemiah's genuine explanation for his love for the city of his father's and his God.

Somewhere in the story Nehemiah must have written to the king and explained the situation after which the king let him continue his repairs. 
In any case, it was not because of Nehemiah's plea nor of his good relationship with the king, but it was because **the good hand of [...] God was on [Nehemiah]** (v.7) and because He worked through Artaxerxes that the king relented and decided to help the Jews.

Two things are noticeable here, the first being that the correlation between the city that **lies waste**—or rather its walls—and their status of being a  **reproach** to the nations, and the second being the fact that it is their *status* that motivates them to build. Of course, they also needed confirmation of how **the hand of God** was on Nehemiah and that the king was with them, but eventually it was the way of how other people would perceive them which Nehemiah mentioned first, most likely because this motivated them most. Now this could mean that Nehemiah used any way possible to motivate them, but it could also mean that the reproach was not for themselves but for the people of Israel. It is just like when someone rises for the sake of his people, just like Japanese people commit suicide, the so-called seppuku, in order to show honor to whatever affiliation they are associated with.
