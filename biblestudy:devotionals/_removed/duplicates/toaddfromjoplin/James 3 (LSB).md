> Behold how great a forest is set aflame by such a small fire! **6** And the tongue is a fire, the *very* world of unrighteousness; the tongue is set among our members as that which defiles the entire body, and sets on fire the course of *our* existence, and is set on fire by hell.

The tongue is not merely an *object*, it is a ***world* of unrighteousness**. What does unrighteousness have to do with the sins of the tongue, such as gossiping, lying and slandering or spreading and teaching false things? And how can an object be a whole *world*?
As for **unrighteousness**, righteousness has to do with *giving* the person what he deserves[^1]. Of course, no one deserves to be gossiped about, but even if they did, this is not the *giving* in the context of being righteous. Righteousness is about deserving something due to merit or payment. A person who is evicted from her house for not having paid rent, for example, as hard as it may sound, is treated righteously. 
How then should we perceive the unrighteousness caused by the tongue? Of course, every  kind of unrighteousness has in some way been communicated by language, either handwritten or orally. The moral flaws of a dictator like Hitler, however, are not necessarily those relating to the tongue in a wider sense, but are more in the categories of pride, envy and self-aggrandizement. The sins of the tongue—as could be noted intuitively in this chapter—relate to those of gossip, lies and slander and other forms of directly hurting people. Those three sins can ruin a person. The saying, "It takes on a life of its own", clearly expresses the impact of these sins. Or in other words, it takes on a *world* of its own, for it ruins the whole world of this person. 
**Example.** In recent years, about 2022 #todo/opzoeken/reference  there was a scandal coined *Varsity Blues* #tags/history#, which was filmed by Netflix into a motion picture named *Operation Varsity Blues* #tags/movies starring Matthew Modine #tags/people/actors , about rich people who bribed college admission boards to let their children enter prestigious colleges. Of course the lives of these students and their parents were ruined, and righteously so, but for two people this was done unrighteously, one of whom being John Wilson, former CEO of Staples Europe—a company where I worked and met him. He spent 10 million dollars on legal fees, six months under house arrest and was not able to work for six years. Aside from the material damage, he and his family have suffered much greater *immaterial* damage and he has since—even until now—been doing everything to restore his family's reputation. 
In this sense unrighteousness is done, through the tongue, to one's whole world. 

> **7** For every kind of beasts and birds, of reptiles and creatures of the sea, is tamed and has been tamed by mankind. **8** But no one can tame the tongue; *it is* a restless evil *and* full of deadly poison. **9** With it we bless *our* Lord and Father, and with it we curse men, who have been made in the likeness of God. **10** From the same mouth come blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to be so. **11** Does a fountain pour forth from the same opening fresh and bitter *water*? **12** Can a fig tree, my brothers, produce olives, or a vine produce figs? Nor *can* saltwater produce fresh.

James is not saying that the same mouth cannot speak out blessing and cursing, but that not **ought to be so**, just like in nature this does not occur. Man, however, has often corrupted the natural ways, for example by homosexual relations, by equalizing women with men and eradicating the difference between them through feminism, and in many other ways. Our mouths have cursed, either unknowingly or when we were not Christians yet. So that is the proof that both are possible from the same mouth, but we *should* not do so.
I would make a exception for judges, who speak out judgments and perhaps curses over criminal offenders, and who praise God with the same mouth. But that is because in cursing the enemies of God they also give glory to God, when they do this righteously.

> **14** But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your heart, do not be arrogant and *so* lie against the truth.

Logically—as in "according to logic", but also according to the words of Jesus—one would expect a statement like, "Those who are understanding let them do good things, and those who are foolish, let them do bad things", but in the second part the advice is still to **not be arrogant**. The reason for this is that even those who have *bitter jealousy and selfish ambitions* have the wisdom that is from above, and that is **the truth**. No one can deny the truth and say that he did not know selfishness was a bad thing. There are those, like Christopher Hitchens #tags/people/critics , who claim that the moral values are agreed upon by society. He claimed that there is no right or wrong because there would need to be an absolute and objective standard for that, and there is not, because this standard would have to be set by a person outside of our world, our Creator, but he does not exist according to Hitchens. 

> **15** This wisdom is not coming down from above, but is earthly, natural, demonic.

Wisdom from below is **earthly, natural [*and*] demonic**. Perhaps one might think I am being too harsh here. How can I consider all wisdom from Earth demonic? Some wisdom  sayings of, say, Aristotle, Socrates and Confusius or even the Buddha are similar to that of the Bible. The Golden Rule in Buddhism is the same as that of the Bible, namely to not treat another as you would want to be treated. The fact that something is true, does not exclude the possibility that it is demonic. *Demonic* means that it is from demons. The Satan and his demons have said words that were true, if only half true. Their intention, however, is to mislead one into doing things that are really bad, like the Satan tried to mislead our Lord Jesus by showing Him Bible verses, but actually wanting Him to do what the Satan wanted. 
In the same way the devil has given the philosophers of this world some wisdom from above but a lot of wisdom from himself, in order to trick the people into following the philosopher and ultimately leaving God and following him. 


> **16** For where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there is disorder and every evil practice. **17** But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, considerate, submissive, full of mercy and good fruits, without doubting, without hypocrisy. **18** And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.
