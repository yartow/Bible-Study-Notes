> ### Wives and Husbands
> **1Pet. 3:1**   In the same way, you wives, be subject to your own husbands so that even if any *of them* are disobedient to the word, they may be won without a word by the conduct of their wives, 

These **husbands** are unbelievers and for a variety of reasons these **wives** could have married them, perhaps due to societal reasons. In those days a single woman was not able to sustain herself and "get a job". Perhaps she was given in marriage or perhaps she was not Christian yet when she married. 
Either way, the command—not advice—that Peter gives here is according to the theme of this letter, namely that action follows obedience, or in other words, the wives should not only talk but *act* upon the Word by **[being] subject to [their] own husbands** and having good **conduct** in their lives. 

> **2** as they observe your pure conduct with fear.

It is not that these husbands fear, but they **observe** the **fear** of their wives have in their **conduct**.

> **3** Your adornment must not be *merely* external—braiding the hair, and wearing gold jewelry, or putting on garments; 

Why would God have given mankind beauty in their outer appearance, that is **external** beauty, so that one becomes more beautiful when adorning oneself? Why did he not make people just like animals who all look beautiful even without adornment? There are no dogs with glasses and all dogs and horses and other animals look beautiful.[^1] Even insects, although some would disagree, are amazing looking creatures and not one looks better or worse than the other. It is even hard to distinguish one from the other. 
Unfortunately we do see differences with domesticated animals, dogs which are bred into ugly looking Mexican dogs, for example, or animals that are too fat, such as certain chicken, but cats and dogs as well. It is because of man's sin that there is so much difference. People like smaller dogs and breed them to form just the dog they want. People like having much meat and fat on the bones of the animals they eat and so they breed fat chicken. 
In the same way we have caused our own bodies to deteriorate because of sin. Perhaps we all were healthy and beautiful—as Adam and Eve most definitely were—most likely the people in the generations after that were still very beautiful as well. If everyone was (just as) beautiful there would be no distinction and people would not distinguish on the basis of external appearance, such as through the braiding of hair, jewelry and clothes. 
So the answer to the question I began with, is that God has given mankind beauty, because He is beauty Himself and He can only create beautiful things. But God never wanted anything to become ugly. When ugliness showed its face through sin, people saw the differences in beauty and people became envious with all due consequences. 
If God has made everyone and everything ugly we would not have had envy, perhaps, but we would not have enjoyed our view on this world, that is of nature, of animals and of people, as much as we do now when we see beauty.

> **20** who once were disobedient, when the patience of God kept waiting in the days of Noah, during the construction of the ark, in which a few, that is, eight persons, were brought safely through *the* water.

God was patient to the Amorites and waited 400 years before letting the Israelites take their land (Genesis 15:16). It is not sure how clear the warning was for the Amorites to repent from their sin or what their fate would be, but for the people **in the days of Noah** this was definitely clear. Though not explicitly stated in Genesis 6-7, Noah, as a preacher of righteousness (2 Peter 5), warned the people about God's wrath and the oncoming flood. The people near him surely must have seen him building this ark and asked him. Also, right before the flood many animals had come to the ark. So these people had a much bigger responsibility to respond to Noah's message of salvation. Perhaps it is because of this reason that Jesus proclaimed His victory to them, because they were the ones who did not believe, while they were—of all generations—the ones who had most proof of this truth. 

> **21** Corresponding to that, baptism now saves you—not the removal of dirt from the flesh, but an appeal of a good conscience to God—through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, 

A contrast is given here between the water of the Flood and of salvation. Though the first water killed the people, Noah and his family **were brought safely through the water**. Analogously, Christians too are saved by **baptism** (of course, not only by this, but also by our repentence and rebirth). 
Peter emphasizes here that the baptism is not a removal of dirt—perhaps referring to the Essenes, but also to the general population which participated in the ritual bathing in baths (*mikvah*) before entering the synagogue[^3]—but it is **an appeal of good conscience to God**. To explain this, take the example of the wedding. It is not the act of the ceremony that renders a couple to be married, that is merely a ritual and in some cultures a signed contract, but it is the stating of vows *in good conscience*, promising to stay with and take care of the other until death do them part. With the words a Christians says in good conscience during his baptism, he confirms that he will follow God for the rest of his life and accept Him as Lord and Savior. 

**Example.** A friend who lived together with her boyfriend told me once that there was no added benefit to marrying, after I explained the difference to her and had told her that a marriage includes vows and witnesses (although they have changed these laws again and removed the compulsory vow and the phrase "until death..."). Though that may be true, it is still better to be with someone who has promised you and of whom you believe he did so with a *good conscience*, than to be with someone who even refuses to say these words. 

Of course, this appeal by itself is not good enough. Exodus 22 #todo/opzoeken/reference). This is why it is so important to add the words **through the resurrection of Jesus Christ**, because everything has been paid for by Jesus' death on the cross.
