> **6** For this reason I remind you to kindle afresh the gift of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands. 

Paul shows his authority as an apostle of Christ when he says, "**through the laying on of my hands**" (v. 6), "**for which I was appointed [...] an apostle**" (v. 11) and "**Hold to the standard of sound words which you have heard from me**" (v. 13). 

> **7** For God has not given us a spirit of timidity, but of power and love and self-discipline.

The question that rises in me when reading this is whether Timothy's passion to follow Christ, or to live according to His Word, had subsided or that he had become prone to shame because of Christ or Paul—because he was in prison—because Paul asked him to **kindle afresh** the "**gift of God**". It seems even that Timothy was either timid or that Paul feared Timothy would be timid and therefore **ashamed** (see also v. 16) and hence encourages him with these words. Or, it could be to *prevent* him from becoming like those who left him (v. 15). The latter seems most likely. In the next chapter Paul exhorts Timothy to be like a soldier, not reprimanding him in any way, but preparing him for the way the Christian must walk.
Note that the shame in 2 Timothy 2:15 is the good kind of shame. Christian should not be ashamed for men when they do good, but they should be ashamed for God when they do bad. 

> **2Tim. 1:8**   Therefore ==do not be ashamed of either the witness about our Lord or me His prisoner,== but join with *me* in suffering for the gospel according to the power of God, **9** who has saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works, but according to His own purpose and grace which was given to us in Christ Jesus from all eternity, **10** but now has been manifested by the appearing of our Savior Christ Jesus, who abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel, **11** for which I was appointed a preacher and an apostle and a teacher. **12** For this reason I also suffer these things, but I am not ashamed; for I know whom I have believed and I am convinced that He is able to guard what I have entrusted to Him until that day. **13** Hold to the standard of sound words which you have heard from me, in the faith and love which are in Christ Jesus. **14** Guard, through the Holy Spirit who dwells in us, the treasure which has been entrusted to *you*.

Vv. 8-14 are better understood when the parentheses are noted, which are indented below for emphasis. When we discard these parenthetical clauses we see that Paul lists four main point, shown in the reformatted text below, at the first indented level. The first two of those points are: **do not be ashamed** and **I am not ashamed**; The last three points urge the Christian to hold on to God's Word and explain why. 
It should be noted that the first point is about as long as the last three points combined. While the length of a statement does not necessarily indicate the importance, in this context it would be fair to say it does. Aside from that, this literal structure provides a beautiful balance between the two points, as if the two points could be placed left and right on a weighing scale. The "weight" then of the amount of text is rightly balanced for literal purposes, but spiritually seen the weight is also equally balanced, with on the left side God's work and His **grace**, and along with these other gifts of **life and immortality**. On the right side of the scale then comes our "work", which is that we entrust our lives to God and hold to sound teaching, **in faith and love**. #biblestudy/discovery

One of the things that might cause confusion is the word "but", which is used two times. The second "but" refers to the fact that God was only *witnessed* before **the appearing of [...] Jesus**, **but now [He] has been manifested**.

The last three points form a hamburger-structure in which the first and third point refer to the entrusting of a treasure, through which the middle verse is emphasized, which is to hold on to the standard of the soundness of Paul's teachings. 

What, then, are those two things that are entrusted? The first is Paul's life. Paul entrusted his life to God, which is the first and only thing Jesus requires when a person wants to follow Him. The second treasure is what we received from God, some call it "salvation" but actually it is "the possibility of salvation", for salvation happens as soon as we leave our bodies on Earth. This gift is of course the gift of Jesus, or more specific, the fact that Jesus came to Earth to die for our sins.
The fact that the middle verse calls for sound teaching—and by implication the living of a doctrinally sound life—seems to imply that the two precious things will be wasted if one does not hold on to Paul's teachings. Both the life one gives to God is wasted (#todo/opzoeken/reference  ...Christians are most pitiful of all) as well as Jesus' blood. 

#### Essay. The shame of a Christian
Shame is in essence not the feeling of knowing you have done something wrong—that would be guilt—but knowing that *others* know you have done, or are doing, something wrong. This thing you have done does not have to be *morally* wrong, but it is the *feeling* that one is wrong and that it *should not be done* in a certain way. 
**Example.** An example of this when one goes to a party and sees that everything wears the same clothes except you. It might not even be deliberately, but a certain sense of shame would arise in any normal person. 
It would be very normal for Timothy to feel shame for having a friend in prison. Especially in the Middle-Eastern *shame culture* #tags/culture #todo nog wat meer uitleggen hierover, elders. it is not uncommon that people are socially isolated and ignored when they have been in prison. These days, especially in the black community, these values have been turned around through, for example, hip hop music, where the message is propagated that one is not truly *gangster* when he has not killed someone or at least spent time in jail. #tags/culture/african
But in *normal* societies this is not the case. Paul even says that **all who are in Asia turned away from** him (v. 15), and although he does not provide the reason, he does urge Timothy *not to be ashamed*, and commends **Onesiphorus** for not being **ashamed** of Paul's imprisonment (v. 16). 
**Example.** I can personally testify of shame when it concerns knowing someone who has been in prison. It is not easy to say that I have a friend in prison, and even harder to say why he is in prison, and still harder to say that he is a Christian, even though he made mistakes. I can understand it is easier to say that one has been in prison for a good reason, such as persecution for righteousness' sake (cf. Matthew 5 #todo/opzoeken/reference ), but stupid mistakes are inexcusable.

> **2Tim. 1:15**   You are aware of this: that all who are in Asia turned away from me, among whom are Phygelus and Hermogenes. **16** The Lord give mercy to the house of Onesiphorus, for he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chains, **17** but when he was in Rome, he eagerly searched for me and found me— **18** the Lord grant to him to find mercy from the Lord on that day—and you know very well what services he rendered at Ephesus.

Onesiphorus was not only **not ashamed of** the fact that Paul was in prison, he came all the way from Asia Minor and **eagerly searched for** Paul. Perhaps the prison was not easily found or accessible. Especially if Paul was imprisoned in a house, it would have been difficult to find Paul's location. But this time—the second time he was imprisoned, #todo/opzoeken —he was imprisoned in a dungeon under the ground. The problem would probably have been to find the *right* authorities and convince them to let him see Paul. Onesiphorus' determination is shown here by the fact that he travelled all the way here and spent his time, just to take care of Paul. He must have had his responsibilities too and had to depend on God for taking care of his needs and the needs of those he needed to take care of, such as his parents or other family members. 
All that Paul asks of God as a "reward" for Onesiphorus' deeds is nothing material, and not even an immaterial blessing, but simply that **the Lord grant to him to find mercy [...] on that day**, which is the Day of Judgment #tags/theology/eschatology/judgment#, which is also all that the Christian can ask and hope for. No deed, no matter how great, is worth any reward from God, for every deed done was done by the power through the grace of God *alone*. 

Onesiphorus' self-sacrificial attitude inspired by Paul's, who humbly followed God's commands and spread the Gospel of Christ, all the while being persecuted, sometimes to the brink of death (cf. 2 Timothy 2:10).
