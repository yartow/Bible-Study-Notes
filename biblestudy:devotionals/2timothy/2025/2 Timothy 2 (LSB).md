# 2 Timothy 2 (LSB)
> REMEMBER JESUS CHRIST
> 
> **2Tim. 2:1**   You therefore, my child, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus. **2** And the things which you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses, entrust these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also. 

Timothy's first task—as every Christian's—is to entrust the knowledge of the Word to others. It is also to be efficient, which is done by teaching those who are **able to teach others** in the first place. 
Billy Graham #tags/people/christian said his greatest regret was not to have had a small number of disciples like Jesus had, and passed on to them his knowledge of the Word. If he had done so, in the 99 years he had lived before he passed away, there would have been twelve disciples who would taught others for over sixty years, who together would have taught many more people than Graham alone has done in seventy years. 

Paul proceeds to tell Timothy there three things he needs to do with examples for each of them: (1) suffer hardship, (2) abide by the rules, and (3) work hard. 
 
> **3** Suffer hardship with *me*, as a good soldier of Christ Jesus. 

The Christian *always* suffers persecution *if* he desires to live godly in Christ Jesus (2 Timothy 3:12), but even when he himself is not suffering, 
he suffers along with the persecuted and suffering Christians. Aside from that, he should not *avoid* suffering, for it is part of the Christian way. Soldiers, too, do not decide whether they go to war or not, but simply obey commands and do not find ways to avoid these. 
Some would even say—though not many—that they long for war, for this is what they have been training for. The American soldiers posted in Afghanistan #tags/history/war/afghanistan #todo/opzoeken/reference when? are often without any duties and only waiting to be dispatched. As a soldier this is discouraging. 

> **4** No soldier in active service entangles himself in the affairs of everyday life, so that he may please the one who enlisted him as a soldier.

As long as the soldier is in active duty, he is not allowed to concern himself with **everyday life**. Of course he will write the occassional letter, but this is in the evenings, when "work" is over—even though he is still on duty and standing-by, in case of war—and only because he has permission to do so. The Christian must be able to see when he is on active duty, which is not only in church or on the streets, when he is preaching or evangelizing, and not only when others are looking for him. It is an active call from God. 
**Example.** When I wake up, I have an active duty right away, even before I receive anything from God, which is to pray, and then to read the Word and meditate on it. This period of "Quiet Time" or "Silent Time" does not end as soon as a set amount of time is over, such as 15 or 30 minutes—which is often the case for young believers, who have "assignments" to read the Bible every day for this set amount of time—but it is over when one hears from God that he can now stop reading, meditating or praying. 
I am not claiming this hearing is a *supernatural* thing, as if everyone should have the gift of prophecy. So do not take these words literally. My point is that there is a time for everything, but the times are set *by God*. 

> **5** And also if anyone competes as an athlete, he is not crowned unless he competes according to the rules. 

The second thing Timothy needs to do is to **[compete] according to the rules**. The Christian always **competes** in an unfair game, for other contestants do not play by the rules. Also, the crowd usually cheers for the other party. Fortunately, though, the referee is righteous, but it is only at the *end of the race* when the result is made known—who are disqualified and who have won.

**Example.** A good example of this was given by the Chinese race walker Wang Liping, in the women's 20 km walk in the 2000 Sydney Olympics. She was disadvantaged by her coach, who only reluctantly coached her because of pressure from her father. Then, in the race the whole crowd was cheering for her Chinese teammate, who was their favorite. In the end the situation seemed hopeless, for there were multiple people in front of her, which meant would not get a medal anymore. But it turned out that one of them was disqualified but refused to leave the match, while another, an Australian, was disqualified just a few meters before the finish. Even so, when she passed the finish first, no one cheered for her, either because they actually preferred her teammate to win or, more likely, because they expected the Australian—who was also on her home turf and the Australian audience's favorite—to win.

We can learn from this example that in this world Christians—who are not on their home turf—are at a disadvantage and do not receive honor and glory where these are due. The cheering is almost always for other side. Aside from that, they are also persecuted in many ways. #tags/evangelism/persecution In the West it is much harder for Christian students to enter a good university when they were homeschooled. Even when the scores of their SAT or other entrance exams were passed well, many universities still hold on to the prejudice that Christian students cannot be good scientists because they believe in Creation rather than Evolution. In politics, Christians are seen as bigots and mysoginists by many, who are proponents of white supremacy and who suppress minorities. They argue this from history even though actual historical facts show the opposite is true. 

> **6** The hard-working farmer ought to be the first to receive his share of the crops. 

#todo/tothiergebleven 
A prerequisite for Christians is that they work hard. This is required in every aspect of life and in every role. Take for example the role of priests and Levites and the number of tasks they need to perform, or the work Moses and Aaron had to do during their ministry, all the way up until their deaths, which would be hard even *without* the complaints of the Israelites. In the New Testament as well, the roles for leaders such as elders, evangelists and fathers are far from easy. However, each deserves his rewards (see 1 Timothy 5:17-18, 1 Corinthians 9:7-14), not only in monetary terms, but even more so in terms of respect. 

> **7** Understand what I say, for the Lord will give you insight in everything.

This verse could refer to the part preceding it or the part below. Even when one does not understand why the Christian *needs* to suffer (and even if he does not suffer, that he needs to suffer *with* other Christians) or work hard, the **Lord will give [...] insight in everything**.

> **2Tim. 2:8**   Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my gospel, 

Timothy was aware of course of the **gospel** that Paul proclaimed, but Paul emphasized this anyway, perhaps for others who would read this letter as well. This statement cannot be repeated enough. It is a statement that tells the truth to Jews #tags/religion/judaism and that Jesus is the Messiah—which "**of the seed of David**" refers to—and at the same time it appeals to Muslims #tags/religion/islam for it claims that Jesus has **risen from the dead**. #tags/theology/gospel

> **9** for which I endure hardship even to chains as a criminal. But the word of God has not been chained. 

Paul uses his literary skills #tags/literaryskills and contrasts his **chains** to the unchained **word of God** #questions/language/english/spelling opposite the verb "contrasts". Wherever man is weak, God is strong, which is an ongoing literary motif in Scripture (see for example Matthew 20:16, 2 Corinthians 12:9-11). 

> **10** For this reason I endure all things for the sake of the elect, so that they also may obtain the salvation, which is in Christ Jesus, with eternal glory. 

Again, Paul shows his self-sacrificing attitude, this is the main point, even though Paul is not trying to boast here. He is showing the Christians should follow his example in eduring for a greater cause, namely those who have not been saved yet, but will be saved, because they have been *elected*. #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/predestination/election Without going too far into doctrinal matters, his statement shows that even some of the elect have not been saved yet *and* that **salvation** involves human intervention—i.e. in this case, Paul's works and the fact that he **[endures] all things**.
The **elect** do not **obtain the salvation** by default. Christians need to **endure all things**—spreading the Gospel, donating money and time, showing Christ-like character, to name but a few—**so that** the elect may obtain salvation.
Because no one knows who is part of the elect, Christians should spread the Gospel to everyone. 

> **11** It is a trustworthy saying:

Statement like these are put forward without proof, probably because Paul knows that his audience is God-fearing. That this **saying [is] trustworthy** does not need to be argumented or proven. Those who know God, know that this is true and most likely have experienced this themselves.

> 	For if we died with Him, we will also live with Him;

Those who died with Him, are those who gave their lives to Christ (cf. Galatians 2:20). These personally experienced what it meant to die to themselves and **live with Him**, living the *true life* (cf. John 10:10). 
One of the reasons why this does not have to be proven, is because if the Christian life was really this dreadful and hopeless, without joy or any fun, why would there be such a big number of believers and why has this number only been growing? In the West it may *seem* as if Christianity is declining and that churches are running empty, but that it only because these church-going people now show their true colors, namely that they were not Christians at all. The true Christian will bear fruit and this will affect others into believing, repenting and converting to Christianity. Even the missionaries who died as martyrs #tags/people/christian/martyrs/missionaries such as Adoniram Judson, who seemingly converted only converted a small number of people, or not even one, would have converted later on, even after their deaths, many people to Christianity. Christians are like flowers—take any flower—there is no flower that prospers and bears fruit without any seeds—there must be at least one seed and almost always it is many more than one. In the same way Christians can not come to faith and leave no legacy behind. For every Christian who dies, many more stand up, for each Christian has a purpose in life (cf. Ephesians 2:10) and God uses all of these people to build up the Church (cf. Ephesians 4:11-12, 2 Timothy 2:2) and spread the Gospel to the world.

> **12** 	If we endure, we will also reign with Him;
> 	If we will deny Him, He also will deny us;
> **13** 	If we are faithless, He remains faithful, for He cannot deny Himself.

How can God **remain faithful** and deny us at the same time? Remaining faithful means that he will not cheat on someone when he has made a commitment to this person before. So in marriage it means that one spouse will never marry another person. In work-relations it means that one will never work for another boss. As for God, even if a so-called believer serves another god, God will stick to the promises He made. These promises, however, do not entail that God will keep taking care of his people at all times. God clearly promised Abraham #todo/opzoeken/reference  that He would stay their God as long as the people obeyed or as soon as they repented. But when the people do not serve God, God will deny them as well. At that point in time—temporarily, or as long as the people persist in their stubbornness—God denies the people as His children. 

> ACCURATELY HANDLE THE WORD OF TRUTH
> 
> **2Tim. 2:14**   Remind *them* of these things, solemnly charging *them* in the presence of God not to dispute about words, which is useless *and leads* to the ruin of the hearers. **15** ==Be diligent to present yourself approved to God as a workman who does not need to be ashamed, accurately handling the word of truth.==  #biblestudy/memorization **16** But avoid godless *and* empty chatter, for it will lead to further ungodliness, **17** and their word will spread like gangrene. 

#tags/literaryskills Another *literary hamburger* is used here where v. 15 #questions/language/english/spelling moet voorstellen, maar niet "is supposed to be", the "meat" and vv. 14, 16-17a are the "buns". The emphasis is on the *uselessness* of a **dispute about words** and what it leads to. One would *think* that this leads to division, as do all arguments—especially those about useless things—but Paul does not even mention that, because a division is not the worst that could happen. Granted, disputes have caused most, if not all, of the divisions in church denominations, but many of those denominations still exist and hold to the *Five Solas[^1]*. The worst that could happen for individuals is **the ruin of the hearers** of this dispute and, as a result thereof, **further ungodliness**; what is even worse for the Christian community is a **spread like gangrene** of these words and of the resulting behavior as well.
So again, Timothy is called to work hard, in Paul's words, to **[be] diligent** in **accurately handling the word of truth**. This is the basis of all defense or attack against false teachings. The Christian should always have the right attitude and never deviate from the teachings of Scripture. Granted, there are examples where one must choose between two evils—and then, of course, it is best to choose the lesser evil—but there is never a situation where one has to deviate from Scripture. 
Take for example the famous Buddhist story[^2] where a Buddha told of his former life as a bodhisattva[^3] where he realized a certain man was going to kill a five hundred people. He thought about whether to kill this man, which would let him end up ==in hell== (#todo not hell, because Buddhists have a different concept for that) for doing bad, but save five hundred lives *and save the murderer from acquiring horrific karma*, or not do any bad, and let these lives be lost. The answer was clear, according to Buddha, namely that he would take the life of this one murderer, which would be better and cause less pain overall, even though he would be disadvantaged himself. 
This story is wrong for the following reasons. It is based on reasoning on how to maximize credits, called *karma* in Buddhism. #todo/opzoeken ook in Hinduism? Buddha's intent was good, and he did not want the man to incur negative karma and therefore killed him. The Buddha was supposed to have incurred negative karma, but because of his good intentions, his bad act eventually gave him good karma. Buddha's point here is that one's intentions determine whether the karma he obtains, is positive or negative. There are no absolutes in Buddhism, even up to the point that theBuddhists do not even make statements about what is right or wrong, or that other religions such as Christianity or Islam are right or wrong, they simply state that it is a matter of perception[^4]. 
In Christianity, however, there are absolutes. There is a right and a wrong, defined by God and written in His Word. God also looks at intention and one's knowledge of the Word, but sin exists nonetheless, but is merely not counted when one is unaware of it (Romans 5: #todo/opzoeken/reference ). And in the story above, killing someone preventively *is bad*. Defending [was dit nou zelfverdediging, of preventief doden?]
This principle is based on the premise that karma is obtained only through one's deeds. God's principles are much more subtle #questions/language/english/spelling dieper, gelaagd, and He looks at the heart.

> Among them are Hymenaeus and Philetus, **18** who have gone astray from the truth saying that the resurrection has already taken place, and they upset the faith of some. **19** Nevertheless, the firm foundation of God stands, having this seal, “THE LORD KNOWS THOSE WHO ARE HIS,” and, “EVERYONE WHO NAMES THE NAME OF THE LORD is to DEPART FROM WICKEDNESS.”

Sometimes people are on the right track and they have faith in God, a little bit, not enough to endure hardships, just like the second and third types of soil in the parable of the four types of soil #todo/opzoeken/reference . This is perhaps not the strongest argument against *Once Saved, Always Saved* #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/osas, but whether these people were saved or reborn, or not, right now they do not have faith anymore.  These are the people Paul speaks of in v. 18. False teachers can **upset the faith of some** and this will disappoint or anger the Christian, especially those who had been discipling the ones who now left the faith. In v. 19, however, Paul explains that there is a **firm foundation of God**, which every Christian knows, which is that God's will will prevail? #questions/language/english/spelling Gods wil zal gebeuren. If anyone leaves the faith, it is only because it was not in God's plan[^5], for the **Lord knows those who are His**, and apparently these were not His. We can reason by implication that those who left the faith were also committing ungodly (v. 16) acts. 
This explains the second quote, "**Everyone who names the Name of the Lord is to depart from wickedness**." Note that the phrase "**is to**" indicates an imperative. It is not advisory, but a command. Conversely #questions/language/english/spelling reversely?, everyone who does *not* depart from wickedness is not to name the Name of the Lord.
Whoever calls Yahweh his God, admits that he is his worshipper. If he calls Him *Lord* or *Master*, or *Father*, he is His slave (cf. v. 24) or His son, respectively, and he will not do anything that is wicked to his god, master or father. If he does, he does not worship God, and he is neither his servant nor son. 
This provides another comfort for the discipler of him who left the faith. He who departed from God, was not His, because His children would not partake in the wicked.

> **2Tim. 2:20**   Now in a large house there are not only gold and silver vessels, but also vessels of wood and of clay, and some to honor and some to dishonor. **21** Therefore, if anyone cleanses himself from these *things*, he will be a vessel for honor, sanctified, useful to the Master, having been prepared for every good work.

There is a difference between the propositions in **to honor** and **for honor**. The phrase "**there are [...] vessels [...] to**" means that these vessels were *created* for this purpose. Some vessels are created for the purpose of **dishonor**, which may sound cruel, but man cannot fathom God's way of thinking (see notes on #todo/opzoeken/reference Romans or Isaiah). But v. 21. does speak about how one can become **a vessel for honor, [...] useful to the Master**, namely by cleaning **himself** so that **he will be [...] sanctified**. 
Again, Paul is probably not stating anything about the deterministic character of the doctine of election #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/predestination/election here, neither is he claiming that one can change the status of his election, i.e. whether he has been elected or not simply by cleansing himself. If anything, these verses can be interpreted two ways, including one according to the doctrine of *Irresistable Grace* #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/irresistiblegrace, namely that the one who has been elected **cleanses himself** and the one who is totally deprave #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/totaldepravity is not even able to do so (see also notes on v. 25). For this reason I refrain from speculating (cf. v. 23), because the arguments that can be made from these texts are neither definitive nor strong enough. 

> **22** Now flee from youthful lusts and pursue righteousness, faith, love, *and* peace, ==with those== who call on the Lord from a pure heart. 

The fleeing must go along **with those who call on the Lord**. Conquering temptation, seduction and addiction is rarely ever possible without other Christians. God has placed other Christians on Earth to support and reprimand each other, to teach and admonish (Colossians 3:16), to confess sins to and pray for one another (James 5:16), in order to equip each other (Ephesians 4:12, 2 Timothy 3:16). This goes against the modern movement of self-help, which is engrained in most modern philosophies and is dissipated all throughout music and television. #todo/nogaftemaken nog voorbeelden bedenken van films waarin dit thema aanwezig is.

> **23** But refuse foolish and ignorant speculations, knowing that they produce quarrels.

These **foolish and ignorant speculations** are not on the level of those concerning Calvinistic doctrines (see e.g. notes on v. 21) but on whether the Resurrection has already taken place, which are but *trivial* matters in the sense that every person who has *read* the Bible—I am not even talking about *studying* these—would know that certain matters need to take place before the Resurrection would take place, among which Jesus' Return on the clouds and that every eye would see him. #tags/theology/eschatology/SecondComing Other speculations are concerning the time of *when* Jesus would come. The answer is simply that no one knows. If this could be deducted from the Bible, Jesus would already have known this while He was on Earth, but He did not. 
So *when* Jesus returns no one knows, but when He *has returned* everyone will know. #biblestudy/discovery

>  **24** And the Lord’s slave must not be quarrelsome, but be kind to all, able to teach, patient when wronged, **25** with gentleness correcting those who are in opposition, if perhaps God may give them repentance leading to the full knowledge of the truth, **26** and they may come to their senses *and escape* from the snare of the devil, having been held captive by him to do his will.

There is general consensus among Christians about the order of the chain of salvation. The Christian must first realize that he is sinful, then repent of his sins, commit his life to Christ, only after which he receives the Holy Spirit. The discussion between Christians is about *when* the Holy Spirit is involved. The doctrine of Total Depravity states that a person is not able to do any of the three steps, and cannot even realize he is sinful unless the Holy Spirit works through him. It follows then that the process of salvation is God's provision only—and *not* man's responsibility—for it is *God* Who sends the Holy Spirit to whom He chooses. The greatest part #todo/opzoeken within Calvinistic denominations will admit that man does need to do something, namely repent and change, because, after all, they do feel they have sinned. Regardless of whether it was the Holy Spirit who made them conscious of that or themselves—and they believe it is the former—they now need to *act* on it. So in practice there is no difference between these Calvinists and other Christians. However, there are two groups within Calvinism that take the doctrines of Calvin too literally, in the branch #questions/language/english/word juiste woord? named *hyper-Calvinism* #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/hyper. 
Let me start with the first group. These are the denominations on far right side of the spectrum within the Dutch Reformed denominations, such as *Oud-Gereformeerd* (English: Old-Reformed) and *Gereformeerde Gemeente in Nederland en Noord-Amerika*, often abbreviated as "Gereformeerde Gemeente" (English: Reformed Congregation of the Netherlands and North-America). Within these denominations there are two groups of people: those who want to follow God and those who are indifferent. They *do* act on their guilt and sense of sin and repent thereof, and they have committed their lives to Christ, but they do believe they have become children of God after that. This is because they have been taught that *something else* still needs to come, which is a confirmation of God, shown in a miraculous way. Only those who *experienced* God are saved and are allowed to participate in the Holy Supper[^6]. This leads to much depression among those people, who long for something they will never get and cannot work for. All of this is part of the doctrine of limited atonement #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/limitedatonement
 #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/predestination/election, although these people go further than what the doctrine states. 
As a side note, those who are indifferent do not care about salvation themselves, but they do see those around them getting depressed, working hard and abiding by the rules, and they do not understand why the importance of those rules, because they do not believe. Naturally, they rebel against these rules and we see that in those villages in the *Bible Belt* drugs and substance abuse #tags/addiction/substance is the highest in the Netherlands. 
On the other side of the spectrum there are those who also believe in God's responsibility and therefore think they do not have to do anything at all.
