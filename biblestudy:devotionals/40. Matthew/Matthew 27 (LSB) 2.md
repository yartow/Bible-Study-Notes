> ### Judas’ Remorse
> **Matt. 27:1**   Now when morning came, all the chief priests and the elders of the people took counsel together against Jesus to put Him to death; **2** and they bound Him, and led Him away and delivered Him to Pilate the governor.

The fake trial and the torturing of Jesus' through beating took place since about 2 AM—which is when roosters start crowing, although it is not certain when the second or third time is—all until **morning**! 
**[All] the chief priests and the elders of the people took counsel together against Jesus**—with the exception of Nicodemus and Joseph of Arimathea (if they were elders #todo/opzoeken ), of course. This was not a small conspiracy, but one made with all the leaders together. We know that these were all of the Sadducees (which #questions/language/english/word bekleedden, the seats of the Sanhedrin and the priesthood) and Pharisees (the Scribes were among the Pharisees). 
How is it possible that there is a group of people that *systematically* hates Jesus? Just like it is very hard for rich men to enter the kingdom (#todo/opzoeken/reference ), in the same way it is very hard for powerful men as well. This is why Jesus says at the start of His Sermon on the Mount that the poor in Spirit are blessed (#todo/opzoeken/reference Matthew 5:2). This is also why, in this world, it is not exceptional when people hate Jesus. There are some who *specifically* hate Jesus because they are either committing a sin which Jesus forbids, such as homosexual practice, or because they have had a bad experience with someone from "the Church", such as abuse—even though most likely these were not real Christians—but in this world people hate Jesus systematically because the whole system is focused on getting rich or powerful, and hence everyone *in* this system is by definition not in the Kingdom—one can only be in one place at a time. #biblestudy/discovery 
That is why it is important to realize in which system we live. Do we live as Heavenly citizens in the worldly system, or do we actually *belong* to the system? #biblestudy/devotionals/questions

> **Matt. 27:3**   Then when Judas, who had betrayed Him, saw that He had been condemned, he felt remorse and returned the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests and elders, 

His **remorse** came as soon as he **saw that [Jesus] had been condemned**. Judas too needed to see to believe. He already knew that Jesus was no ordinary man, perhaps he even believed He was the Messiah and the Son of God. However, he did not care. All he cared about was himself. He also *knew* that Jesus would be condemned and put to death, because this is what Jesus foretold the disciples, but it was only after he *saw* this happening, that he felt his remorse. 
This kind of behavior appears more often and may seem genuine, but true remorse is shown through actual change. Many men who physically abuse their wives show some kind of remorse, with actual tears—especially when the wife decides to leave—and promises to change, but will beat up their wives as soon as things have calmed down a bit. #biblestudy/discovery Judas was no different than this. 

> **4** saying, “I have sinned by betraying innocent blood.” But they said, “What is that to us? See *to that* yourself!” **5** And he threw the pieces of silver into the sanctuary and departed; and he went away and hanged himself.

There are different narratives of what happened exactly to Judas and who purchased the land (#todo/opzoeken/reference Acts), but it is certain that Judas did not own the money anymore, because **he threw [it] in into the sanctuary**. If by **sanctuary** was meant the Temple, Judas just have stood in front of it and have thrown it inside. 
It is amazing to see that the priests were sinful even in front of the most holy place in the world. 

> **6** And the chief priests took the pieces of silver and said, “It is not lawful to put them into the temple treasury, since it is the price of blood.”

It is not certain whether the thirty pieces of silver were standard pieces of (Roman) money or temple money #questions/language/english/word. 

> **7** And taking counsel together, they bought with the money the Potter’s Field as a burial place for strangers. **8** For this reason that field has been called the Field of Blood to this day.

If the name of this **field** was commonly known, even to **this day**, which would have been about thirty years later, then the stories about how the priests had set up Jesus through Judas with **blood** money would have been commonly known as well.

> **9** Then that which was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet was fulfilled, saying, “AND THEY TOOK THE THIRTY PIECES OF SILVER, THE PRICE OF THE ONE WHOSE PRICE HAD BEEN SET by the sons of Israel; **10** AND THEY GAVE THEM FOR THE POTTER’S FIELD, AS THE LORD DIRECTED ME.”

Even if it had been a high amount, what Judas had done would have been treacherous, but now this was a mere small amount. Adding insult to injury, **the thirty pieces of silver** was the exact amount that was paid for slaves. So in other words, this was what Judas—but the priests as well—regarded Him as. 

Note that this amount is often mentioned as worth about 400 euros, but there are a few things to note here. If these were Roman denarii the amount of silver would have been about 28 percent of that, so about 100 euros. secondly, and this is what most people forget, the purchasing power parity equivalent of this amount of money is equivalent to a year's wage of a laborer, which is 10,000-12,000 euros. 

> ### Jesus Before Pilate
> **Matt. 27:11**   Now Jesus stood before the governor, and the governor questioned Him, saying, “Are You the King of the Jews?” And Jesus said to him, “You yourself say *it*.” **12** And while He was being accused by the chief priests and elders, He did not answer. **13** Then Pilate said to Him, “Do You not hear how many things they testify against You?” **14** And He did not answer him with regard to even a *single* charge, so the governor marveled greatly.

See notes on #todo/opzoeken/reference the same passage in Mark or Luke. Jesus answered only to those questions pertaining to His mission and His identity, which is the Son of God. 

> **Matt. 27:15**   Now at the feast the governor was accustomed to release for the crowd *any* one prisoner whom they wanted. **16** And at that time they were holding a notorious prisoner, called Barabbas. **17** So when the people gathered together, Pilate said to them, “Whom do you want me to release for you? Barabbas, or Jesus who is called Christ?” 

This was early in the morning (see v. 1), perhaps even at a time when people were still busy doing their own things. The only plausible explanation that many **people gathered together** at *this time* in the morning is that these had been actively brought together by the priests, perhaps even bribed. 

> **18** For he knew that because of envy they had delivered Him over.

Even Pilate, who did not know the priests, saw their true intentions through this (presumably) short interrogation of Christ. So great was their **envy** that they could not even hide it with their great skills of hypocrisy.

> **Matt. 27:19**   Now while he was sitting on the judgment seat, his wife sent him *a message*, saying, “Have nothing to do with that righteous Man; for last night I suffered greatly in a dream because of Him.” **20** But the chief priests and the elders persuaded the crowds to ask for Barabbas and to put Jesus to death. **21** But the governor answered and said to them, “Which of the two do you want me to release for you?” And they said, “Barabbas.” **22** Pilate said to them, “Then, what shall I do with Jesus who is called Christ?” They all said, “Let Him be crucified!” **23** And he said, “Why, what evil did He do?” But they were crying out all the more, saying, “Let Him be crucified!”

Pilate has done many attempts to save Christ from the death penalty. He might be commended for that, but ultimately he still gave in to the crowd, for fear of his own position, instead of considering what is righteous. This is what Pilate would be punished for by God, later in life #todo/opzoeken  uitleggen wat er daarna met Pilatus en zijn carrière gebeurde. In this paragraph we see the literary skills of Matthew—even though he did not use paragraphs and line breaks—where Pilate stood up for Jesus three times, but the people insisted three times that Jesus **be crucified**, signifying that their measure was full. 

> **Matt. 27:24**   Now when Pilate saw that he was accomplishing nothing, but rather that a riot was starting, he took water and washed his hands in front of the crowd, saying, “I am innocent of this man’s blood; see *to that* yourselves.” **25** And all the people answered and said, “His blood be on us and on our children!” **26** Then he released Barabbas for them; but after having Jesus scourged, he delivered Him over to be crucified.

> **Matt. 27:45**   Now from the sixth hour darkness fell upon all the land until the ninth hour. **46** And about the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, saying, “ELI, ELI, LAMA SABACHTHANI?” that is, “MY GOD, MY GOD, WHY HAVE YOU FORSAKEN ME?” **47** And some of those who were standing there, when they heard it, *began* saying, “This man is calling for Elijah.” **48** And immediately one of them ran, and taking a sponge, he filled it with sour wine and put it on a reed, and gave Him a drink. **49** But the rest *of them* were saying, “Let us see whether Elijah will come to save Him.” **50** And Jesus cried out again with a loud voice, and yielded up His spirit. 

David Pawson #tags/people/christian said that Matthew wrote in a style that paces through the events and he uses the word **immediately** much more often than any other writer of the Bible. However, this fast pace seems to lead to a point where things slow down, starting in v. 51, which emphasizes those events. #biblestudy/discovery

> **51** And behold, the veil of the sanctuary was torn in two from top to bottom; and the earth shook and the rocks were split. **52** And the tombs were opened, and many bodies of the saints who had fallen asleep were raised; **53** and coming out of the tombs after His resurrection they entered the holy city and appeared to many. **54** Now the centurion, and those who were with him keeping guard over Jesus, when they saw the earthquake and the things that were happening, became very frightened and said, “Truly this was God’s Son!”

> **Matt. 27:55**   And many women were there looking on from a distance, who had followed Jesus from Galilee while ministering to Him. **56** Among them was Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James and Joseph, and the mother of the sons of Zebedee.

## 3. Isaiah 28:14–15 — “Scoffers who rule this people”

**“Therefore hear the word of the LORD, you scoffers,
who rule this people in Jerusalem.
Because you have said, ‘We have made a covenant with death,
and with Sheol we have an agreement…’”**
*(Isaiah 28:14–15)*

✅ **Why it matters:**
* This is a direct **rebuke of the rulers of Jerusalem**.
* Early Christian interpreters (e.g. in the Apostolic Fathers and Church Fathers) applied this to the **religious leadership** who opposed the Messiah.
* “Covenant with death” is seen allegorically as **rejecting God’s covenant** and aligning with earthly powers (e.g., Rome).
