#### Essay. On divorce and remarriage
Jesus is not saying anything new here. If the Pharisees had paid attention to these literal words in Scripture, they would have realized that marriage is something that **God has joined together**, and which man *cannot* separate. It is not that man *should not* separate it, but it *cannot* do so. In the same way God has created a certain child to be born of certain parents. It is God who joined these three persons in this way and regardless of what man does, be it a change of name or separation at birth, the relationship between the three always exists. 
In the same way a man can separate from his wife but what he does on Earth is not bound in heaven, which is where the marriage was registered, if you will. By human law, all subsequent marriages are invalid, if the first one was not annulled for a valid reason and in the right way—which is through a higher authority. It does not matter whether the marriage was instituted by a church authority, government or village chief, or even when the two were forced to marry each other—the marriage is valid if the ceremony was performed in the presence of a higher and universally (or locally) recognized authority, and witnesses to testify of this.
The separation, however, is not simply done with these same elements. Just like two parents cannot agree to have their child killed, without it being a murder. In the same way, a divorce is always unvalid, and it is adultery as soon as one spouse marries another person. 

What about Jesus' exception of **sexual immorality** (v. 9), is that not a legal reason for divorce and remarriage? This depends on the interpretation of "sexual immorality". David Pawson #tags/people/christian/minister and John Piper's view is that this term refers to a person engaged to another, but sleeps with a third person *before* marriage has taken place, just like what Joseph thought of Mary when he found out she was pregnant (#todo/opzoeken/reference ). Mike Winger #tags/people/christian/minister , however—who also addressed the view of the aforementioned people in [his lengthy video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N2pC6ZikbYo&pp=ygUTbWlrZSB3aW5nZXIgZGl2b3JjZQ%3D%3D) on divorce and remarriage—interprets this as a general term that covers all sexual acts outside of marriage.
In this case (this is my interpretation, not Winger's), the adulterer has been defiled, and it would be "bad" if the man defiles himself by sleeping with her again. This defilement is only removed when adulterer repents of his sin and asks for forgiveness to his spouse and to God. However, if the adulterer does not repent, it is impossible for the two persons to reconcile and become like a married couple. No marriage can consist, or should persist, when a third party is involved. Hence, the divorce is therefore granted by Jesus.
Both Winger and MacArthur mention that a granted divorce also indicates that remarriage is possible. In my opinion this is the weakest part of their arguments. They state it in this way, "Why else would a divorce be granted, other than to marry another person?" Granted (no pun intended), it is a valid question—why else? Perhaps it is to live separately from one's spouse? But a divorce (or a certificate thereof) was not necessary for that, the certificate was only needed for a woman to show the next spouse that she had not been unfaithful. Still, I find the arguments of this case to be based more on inference than on clear reason. #questions/language/english or deductions?  #todo/opzoeken nogmaals beluisteren Winger's argumenten.

The real answer is probably in the form of a decision tree, with so many contingencies that man is incapable of comprehension. But despite the fact that we do not know the answer for sure, when someone remarries—whether it is allowed or not—if he does this with a clear conscience, he will not be continually living in sin, even though the act of remarriage will be sin, but will not be counted as such (Romans 5:13). Mike Winger cited a quote by David Pawson saying the opposite, but I still need to look up whether that was taken out of context. #todo/opzoeken perhaps Pawson was referring to a man who cheated on his wife, divorced her and then married another woman. See also notes on v. 9, this is not the kind of situation Pawson referred to, but in the extreme situation described there he would be right. So, I would conclude that both Winger and Pawson are right, dependent on the person's conscience and awareness of sin.

> **7** They *said to Him, “Why then did Moses command to GIVE HER A CERTIFICATE OF DIVORCE AND SEND *her* AWAY?” **8** He *said to them, “Because of your hardness of heart Moses permitted you to divorce your wives; but from the beginning it has not been this way.

When something is permitted, even by a prophet, this does not mean it is allowed in Heaven. It may be the best solution in this world, but it is the best out two bad options. The actual best solution would be to never divorce and never sin, but this is impossible. 
People change and can fall in love with others outside of their marriage and therefore cheat on their spouse. Even if there is no adultery at play, or violence for that matter, due to a lack of love the marriage could be horrendous one or both of the two spouses and it would be better for them to divorce. Still, though this is a valid reason to divorce, in my opinion, this reason alone does not grant the right to remarry. 
Intuitively (although it is not always good practice to trust on intuition in ethical matters) I would say that if in a marriage Spouse A does her best to love her husband and respect him, but Spouse B does not love her nor respects her, up until a situation that is unbearable for Spouse A, she has the right to divorce him. Of course a certain threshold needs to have been passed and the husband should not repent of his sin, otherwise she should still give him another chance. If Spouse A still longs to be married, she would have a right to do so. 
Again, though, this is the intuitive line of thinking. Jesus warns us of this and tells us that only sexual immorality damages the relationship sufficiently, in such a way that it is very hard to restore. 
There are cases where the spouse has forgiven the other spouse's adultery or other forms of sexual immorality, but those are rare, and it is the mercy of God to allow for divorce, if this is what is meant (but see [[/Essay. On divorce and remarriage|notes on v. 6]]).

> **Matt. 19:10**   The disciples *said to Him, “If the relationship of the man with his wife is like this, it is better not to marry.” **11** But He said to them, “Not all men *can* accept this statement, but *only* those to whom it has been given. **12** For there are eunuchs who were born that way from their mother’s womb; and there are eunuchs who were made eunuchs by men; and there are *also* eunuchs who made themselves eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. He who is able to accept *this*, let him accept *it*.”

The general consensus on the meaning of **eunuchs** in these verses is that Jesus is referring first to people who have no desire to marry, then to people who were forced not to marry, such as slaves or perhaps even Catholic priests, and then those who do have a desire to marry, but an even larger one to serve the Kingdom and willingly choose to stay single. 
Jesus then tells us to **accept *this***, where *this* could refer to this fact—namely that there are only three types of eunuchs—or to the fact that it is **better not to marry**. 
**Example.** I once spoke with a woman someone about this fact, that it is *better not to marry*, and she simply could not accept this fact. I told her that given her state, which is that was neither of the three types of eunuchs, it was *good* for her to marry—even the *best* choice, better than to burn with desire, as Paul mentioned (1 Corinthians 7:9)—but it would have been better if she had been a eunuch of Type 1 or 3. 
Again, she could not accept this. Her problem was perhaps in the fact that she could not accept that God created people in a unique way, but made some better than others. It would be just like some who received the gift of apostleship or prophecy, while others a mere and lesser gift of speaking in tongues or hospitality (1 Corinthians 12:31). These gifts are better, there is no discussion about it, and their ministry is "greater", if you will, and has greater impact. But this does not mean that the person is necessarily greater because of this. Remember that it is a *gift*—*given* by God—and not  that the person earned or worked for. The person who is *given* the state of not desiring marriage is not greater, but he can do a greater work for the Kingdom because he can give all of his time to God.

> ### JESUS BLESSES CHILDREN
> 
> **Matt. 19:13**   Then *some* children were brought to Him so that He might lay His hands on them and pray; and the disciples rebuked them. **14** But Jesus said, “Let the children alone, and do not hinder them from coming to Me; for the kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these.” **15** And after laying His hands on them, He departed from there.

**Matthew 19:13-30.** See also [[20250524 Verjaardag Sandra. Family Time]] for a Family Time suggestion.

The belief of the children's parent that the blessing of Jesus would come when He **[laid] His hands on them and [prayed] for them** is genuine, but not necessary. Receiving the blessing of Jesus does not require physical touch or even vicinity near Jesus. Just like in the case of people who were touching Jesus' shadow and were healed by faith. They were not healed by His shadow, but by faith that Jesus was able to heal them. The faith of these parents was in that Jesus' prayer would be heard by the Father. 
In the same way the dedication of children is done in church by faith that the Father will bring these toward Him. 
Personally I do have faith that God will hear a parent's prayer for God's blessing over the child and that the child will *always* come to faith. Many parents, though, see this as a ritual, to let the pastor say a prayer, hoping that the pastor's prayer will be heard by God. However, that is not how the dedication of children is intended. It is not a free pass for parents to lay back and be exempt from teaching their children to walk in the way of God. The dedication is meant for parents who genuinely want their children to be part of God's family and who do everything in their power to keep the child in the way of the Law.
I do have to say that I have seen multiple exceptions where parents had given their lives to God, working full-time for the ministry, but where the children had not come to faith. All of these children had become "good citizens", so to speak, none of them were abusing substances or had addictions or we're involved with criminality, but they did not go to church or believed in Christ as their savior. I used to think that it was because the parents were so busy with their ministry that they paid too little attention to the children, but I have seen cases where that did not apply either. 
So I do not have any proof for this hypothesis, but it is a promise of God that He will bring the children to Him if the parents take up their responsibility.

The difference in the LSB with the KJV is that in the LSB Jesus refers to the thing that is good, but the KJV writes both "Good Master" and "**good thing**". The subsequent question is consistent with the interpretation, so in the LSB this is translated as "....**about what is good**" and in the KJV as "Why callest thou me good?" As for the LSB's interpretation, Jesus means to say that there is no such thing as a **good thing**, because only God is good. Also, there is no good thing that can be done in order to have eternal life. 
This is not in contrast with Jesus' words that the young man must **keep the commandments** if he wishes **to enter into life**. The explanation is that there is no one good thing that one can do to have eternal life or to be saved, but when one keeps the commandments, then he can enter into life. > **Matt. 19:23**   And Jesus said to His disciples, “Truly I say to you, it is hard for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven. 

Of course riches are not the only things that are hard to give up, but it is one of the most tempting things. Jesus warns more often about money than about lust. It is easier to be poor and enter the kingdom of heaven and then become rich, then to become rich first and then try to enter, for to enter, the gate is narrow and everything needs to given up, but everything gained within the kingdom of heaven may be kept. 
Now I am not saying that the riches that one obtains after he comes to faith will go with him to heaven. No, we have to distinguish between two realms here, the physical and spiritual. The physical does not pass through to the spiritual or the other way around. It can be seen as follows. In life there are one or two spiritual gates man passes through: the gate that comes after *physical* death where one goes to Heaven or Hell, and *during* life (not before it) the spiritual gate which only some go through after spiritual death, which is the Christian rebirth. In both cases nothing can be brought along which is not part of the realm one goes to. So in the case of physical death, one will go from the physical to the spiritual realm and no physical materials can be brought with him, but his spiritual materials—the gold, silver and even hay and wood with which he built (all of which refer to the character he obtained during life)—these he will bring with him and only these will remain in the afterlife, i.e. Heaven or Hell (there is no Purgatory #tags/religion/christianity/catholicism ). 
In the case of spiritual rebirth—which can only take place while a person is living—there is the realm of the kingdom of God *within* the spiritual realm where only those things that are pure and holy can be brought into.

It **is impossible** for people to give up everything for God. There have been rich men of course who became Christians and kept their wealth, but not without being willing to give it up first.
