> ### The Genealogy of Jesus Christ
> **Matt. 1:1**   The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham:

Matthew starts his geneology with Abraham, because his main audience are the Jews and one of the purposes of providing this geneology is to show that Jesus was a Jew, as prophesized by Moses (#todo/opzoeken/reference  one from your brothers will rise up).

In these four verses four women are mentioned—three by name (Bathsheba is the fourth)—of whom one was a prostitute (Rahab) and one acted like one (Tamar, for a good reason, see Genesis #todo/opzoeken/reference ). Three of these were gentiles (Tamar was #todo/opzoeken/bible, Rahab was a Canaanite from Jericho, Ruth a Moabite) and two of them had children in an unconventional way (Tamar and Bathsheba). 
This is exceptional in Ancient Middle Eastern texts #tags/culture/ane as women and children were deemed irrelevant. This shows again that the Bible was revolutionary for its time in the inclusion of women. Also, it shows that Jesus is not ashamed of showing the world who His forefathers and -mothers were. Likewise, as Christians we should never be ashamed of who we are and of who our family is. Full transparency is a characteristic trait of followers of Christ.

Again, Matthew targets his Jewish readers by showing Jesus' geneology through His Earthly father, **Joseph**, as the Jews held a male family line #questions/language/english/word high. 
According to the Talmud the Jewish line is carried forth from the woman, meaning that a person is a Jew, even when the mother is Jewish but the father is not. However, this is only a rule to determine whether one is Jewish and applies only to mixed marriages. As for the rest, however, the male geneology is of higher importance.

> **Matt. 1:17**   Therefore all the generations from Abraham to David are fourteen generations; and from David to the deportation to Babylon, fourteen generations; and from the deportation to Babylon to the Christ, fourteen generations.

These are not literally **fourteen generations** because Matthew has skipped a few names from the list (#todo/opzoeken/bible which names? ) . But this is perhaps done for ease of remembering and symmetry, also to show the God's timing in the birth Christ. Fourteen is two time seven. Two is the number of the relationship between man (or man and woman, one marriage consists of two people) and seven stands for the fullness of God. This means that when man has interacted with each other in a way that is sufficient according to God, an intervention happens (David, Babylon and Christ). This happened *three* times because three is the number of the Trinity, indicating that the third time has to do with Yahweh Himself[^1]. 

> ### The Conception and Birth of Jesus
> **Matt. 1:18**   Now the birth of Jesus Christ was as follows: when His mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together she was found to be with child by the Holy Spirit. **19** And Joseph her husband, being a righteous man and not wanting to disgrace her, planned to send her away secretly. **20** But when he had considered this, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying, “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife; for the One who has been conceived in her is of the Holy Spirit. 

Despite his incorrect assumption that Mary had committed adultery, he still loved her. This is a show of true love, which will not disappear as soon as bad things happen #questions/language/english/word better way of saying this. Not only did he love her, he was also a righteous man. Now it would have been righteous to send her away, because she had (supposedly) committed *sexual immorality* #todo/opzoeken/reference reference to Jesus' words (not *adultery* because they were not married). But righteous also means that a person does not get punished more than he deserves. In this case, Mary, whom he had always known as a chaste and #questions/language/english/word woman, did *not deserve* the shame a woman would have gotten when the news were to spread out.

As for Joseph not wanting to marry Mary anymore, perhaps this was because of the shame that he would take upon himself of marrying her, that he was **afraid to take Mary as [his] wife**, or because he thought she loved someone else, or was not as mature of character as he thought she was.

Mary and Joseph both lived in Nazareth according to Luke 1:26.
Mary became pregnant around the sixth month of pregnancy of John the Baptist's mother, Elizabeth. Mary visited her after she had become pregnant and stayed there for three months, and must have witnessed the birth of John the Baptist.
After that, perhaps visibly pregnant with a four-month old child, Joseph married her. So this must have been clear to the people in the town, but to them it could either be that she had committed adultery or that they had sex before marriage.

Much attention is given to Mary and even other women in the Catholic Church #tags/religion/christianity/catholicism and almost none to Joseph, but both of them were Jesus' parents here on Earth (and both should not be revered or idolized).
