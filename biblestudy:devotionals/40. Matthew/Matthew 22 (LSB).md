> ### PARABLE OF THE WEDDING FEAST
> 
> **Matt. 22:1**   And Jesus answered and spoke to them again in parables, saying, **2** “The kingdom of heaven may be compared to a king who gave a wedding feast for his son. **3** And he sent out his slaves to call those who had been called to the wedding feast, and they were unwilling to come. 

**Example.** The organization I am currently working for (Immigration Services) will soon have a feast. The highest chief will be present—whom we have never seen because she is so high at the top—and she organized a feast for the whole organization with free food, music and games. A lot of money has been spent to make all of this possible.
I am one of those who are usually **unwilling to come**, because I have no joy in meeting her or any of the other leaders, nor do I have any emotional attachment to the organization. 
Jesus, however, is not referring to a distant king in His parable, but One Who made everything possible and Who was even more close to the people than David was. Also, do not forget that rejecting an invitation in the Near Ancient Middle-East #tags/culture/ane is very different than it is now. In the Chinese culture, for eeven when one rejects an invitation for a wedding, one is culturally obligated #questions/language/english to at least give a small gift to compensate the bridal couple for the expenses they made. #tags/culture/chinese Thus, we can expect that the people were expected to be present or at the least have a good reason and message for the king.

> **4** Again he sent out other slaves saying, ‘Tell those who have been called, “Behold, I have prepared my dinner; my oxen and my fattened livestock are *all* butchered and everything is ready; come to the wedding feast.”’ **5** But they paid no attention and went their way, one to his own farm, another to his business, **6** and the rest seized his slaves and mistreated them and killed them. 

It is an incredible honor when a king invites one not only once, but twice, and especially when one did not even reply the first time. Reversely, the loss of face is even bigger for the king when he is rejected twice. Still, the invitees were callous and even cruel to those who sent the message, indicating their absolute disregard of the king's love. 

> **7** But the king was enraged, and he sent his armies and destroyed those murderers and set their city on fire.

The destruction of the **murderers and [...] their city** was not because of their rejection of the king's invitation, but because of their deeds. In the same way, those who reject God end up in hell because of their own deeds. Of course, one could say that he rejects the king, but does not murder anyone, but in the spiritual realm this is not possible. One cannot reject God, but still do good deeds, for all that is good, is from God. One cannot reject God, but live according to the Law that He created. 
**Example.** Suppose there is a person who lives perfectly and without sin, there will inevitably be one day where he gets into trouble and where he will ask himself—or someone else will ask him—what he will do about it. As soon as this person thinks that he should trust in himself instead of in God, he has committed the sin of pride. Or take another situation where he has children and he teaches them about how the world was created. As soon as he teaches them that there is no God and that the world came into existence by itself through evolution, he has denied the existence of God and committed sin. There is simply no way for man to reject God and keep His Law and do good deeds at the same time. 

>  **8** Then he *said to his slaves, ‘The wedding is ready, but those who were called were not worthy. **9** Go therefore to the main highways, and as many as you find *there*, call to the wedding feast.’ **10** And those slaves went out into the streets and gathered together all they found, both evil and good; and the wedding hall was filled with dinner guests.

The feast is attractive to many, just like Christianity is, when there are less restrictions. Generally speaking, people are attracted to Christian elements, such as the beauty and serenity of churches, the peace and harmony of Christian communities, and the integrity and character of Christians. It is the *restrictions* they do not like, i.e. the Law—although many are hypocritical about that, in the best case, they are naive.

> **Matt. 22:11**   “But when the king came in to look over the dinner guests, he saw a man there who was not dressed in wedding clothes, **12** and he *said to him, ‘Friend, how did you come in here without wedding clothes?’ And the man was speechless. 

Either it was customary in the culture that the host provided clothes or (from *MacSBNnkjv*) it was not expected that guests "rounded up hastily from 'the highways' [...] could be expected to come with proper attire." See also notes on [[Exodus 34 (LSB)]].

> **13** Then the king said to the servants, ‘Bind him hand and foot, and throw him into the outer darkness; in that place there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.’ **14** For many are called, but few *are* chosen.”

Why was the man speechless? He had a clear answer. Clothes were offered to him[^1], but he refused to wear these, because he preferred his own clothes, which were inferior to the clothes the king had offered him. A similar question can be asked to any person who enters a church and says he is a Christian, "Why do you commit this and that sin? Why haven't you taken off that sin and cleaned up your life?" The simple answer is that he did not want to, because—even though he knew these sins were not part of the Christian life—he enjoyed doing these sins—and this is something difficult to admit. 

The king's command seems harsh and anti-climactic. Whereas the feast had finally started after so many did not show up, all of sudden the party is crashed and a guest is bound **hand and foot, and [thrown] into the outer darkness**. Why would the king not simply eject him from the party, or offer him a second chance to change his clothes? 
In the world, this could be very likely, but in the Kingdom of God, rules are different. When the King has spoken, it will happen (cf. Isaiah 14:24,27), and whether He is merciful at that moment is up to Him.

> ### THE SADDUCEES AND THE RESURRECTION
> 
> **Matt. 22:23**   On that day *some* Sadducees (who say there is no resurrection) came to Jesus and asked Him a question, **24** saying, “Teacher, Moses said, ‘IF A MAN DIES HAVING NO CHILDREN, HIS BROTHER AS NEXT OF KIN SHALL MARRY HIS WIFE AND RAISE UP A SEED FOR HIS BROTHER.’ **25** Now there were seven brothers with us; and the first married and died, and having no seed, he left his wife to his brother; **26** so also the second, and the third, down to the seventh. **27** And last of all, the woman died. **28** In the resurrection, therefore, whose wife of the seven will she be? 

**[Seven]** is the number of godly fullness or completion. For the difficulty of the question the number would not have mattered, even a mere two husbands would have made their point. They could also have used the example of a woman who married a second husband after her first one had died. So I am not sure why the Sadducees used this specific example where they even had to use and quote the levirate law from the Torah. #biblestudy/questions/matthew
Unbeknownst to the Sadducees, there is slight reference to Jesus, for Jesus would not have been born had this law not existed. > For they all had *married* her.”
