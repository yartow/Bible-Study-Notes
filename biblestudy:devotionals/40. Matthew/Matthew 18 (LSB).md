> ### Who Is Greatest in Heaven?
> **Matt. 18:1**   At that time the disciples came to Jesus and said, “Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?”

The answer to this question is given three verses later. Jesus points out not only the *prerequisite* of entering the kingdom of heaven, but also who is the **greatest**. As expected, the rules in world of the Kingdom of Heaven are reversed to our world's. Not the greatest, but the least enter heaven. What is also different from our world is that everyone is the greatest. The very least requirement needed to enter heaven makes one the greatest. This is unheard of. In this world everyone who is the best, of his high school class for example, faces another challenge as soon as they enter university. And then, later on, when the graduate summa cum laude, valedictorian or best of their year, at work they will encounter another situation where they will not be the best. In Chinese there is a proverb that goes, "there are always people who are better than others, there are even heavens higher than heavens." Though impossible, in Heaven everyone is the greatest, which implies something about equality in Heaven. #biblestudy/discovery #tags/theology/eschatology/newworld

Almost all children **humble** themselves. No matter how tough or mature they are, they realize they are not adults. They miss many of the skills and strength to do things that adults can.

Children want to *receive* love, while parents want to *give* love.
Adults lose this sense of humbleness when they become independent. Most people do, but some always appreciate the presence of an older person who instructs them in life. Everyone who wants to enter the **kingdom of heaven** needs to become humble **like children** and realize there is someone they need to depend on and receive love from. #biblestudy/discovery

#### Essay. Can a believer fall from his faith?
v. 13 is not proof that a believer can fall away #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/osas, this is merely a parable intended to show how the Father *prevents* His **little ones** from straying from the faith. He does put effort in it—a lot!—and His joy is greater than that over those who **have not gone astrary**, and lastly, it is **not [His] will** that they **perish**. 
"Will" must be understood here as His "longing" or "desire". So yes, people *can* fall away, despite the Father's efforts to find the lost, for He always gives people free choice. To be fair, a real Christian would never *want* to stray. He may have wandered off, even out of free will and because of the wrong choices had made, but deep inside he wants to be saved, for this is the same reason why he accepted Jesus and committed his life. The only argument that can be brought up against that is that this person *has changed*. But the counterargument against that is that Jesus would have known that this person would change and eventually reject Him. Was this person "reborn" then, if only for a little while? 
There are two ways to answer this. The first answer is that he was indeed reborn and that all of his sins are paid for. If Jesus says that he has been made righteous and all of his sins are gone, even though this righteousness will be *credited* to him *on the last day*, this still means that Jesus has already paid for his sins two thousand years ago. What happens then after he rejects Jesus? If he ends up in hell again, what needs to be paid then to the devil, for Jesus has already paid for his sins and suffered the wrath on the cross, and how does he suffer in hell? This would violate the principle of #questions/language/english righteous retribution #todo/opzoeken , such as the principle of an eye for an eye, or *double jeopardy*. The same sin of a person only needs to be paid for *once*. It cannot be that both Jesus and the man pay for the same sins. 
#todo/tothiergebleven  #todo/nogaftemaken  

The second answer is that Jesus did not pay for this man's sins. This man was thus never truly reborn and only *thought* his sins were forgiven. This is the argument used by Calvininists and written in the doctrine of Limited Atonement #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/limitedatonement.

There is a third answer, which I have never heard before, and that is that this man is truly reborn, and his sins have not yet been forgiven, but as good as forgiven, because Jesus promises that his sins are forgiven and Jesus' promise is as good as true. Jesus promise, however, is that if you hold on to my commands, you will be forgiven. This extra condition is not written explicitly in the Gospels but it is clear in, among others, 1 John 4. Even if we assume they have been forgiven, this does not mean that the debt is *repaid*, which is not the same. This is why the king in the parable below, despite having forgiven the slave (Matthew 18:27,32), still demanded payment. Even if the slave had not made the mistake of unforgiveness to his fellow slave, his debt would have been unpaid, but that would have eventually been covered by the king's money.

What makes it difficult is that the king in the parable was only human and trusted the slave to have changed all of his ways. If the king had known what would happen a short time later, he would never have forgiven him. Can we say then that Jesus actually forgives people when He knows they are not sincere?
I would say, as with all things, that there is a middle ground. If a person sincerely pleads and repents—as the slave may have done—it would be harsh to tell the person, "I know you are sincere right now, but I also know that in the future you will turn your ways." This would never motivate the person to do his best anymore, and instead of turning his ways in the future, this might cause a so-called "self-fulfilling prophecy" in the sense that his bad ways will now start earlier than they would have. 
So, what Jesus perhaps does, is that He *actually* forgives this person and proves this by giving him the Holy Spirit. When this person definitely turns his ways from Jesus, the Father takes the Holy Spirit from this person. 
Thus, the answer to the question of whether he was truly reborn depends on the definition of "rebirth". Is that an actual repentance and commitment to Jesus Christ with everything one has, or the same but with a commitment lasting his whole life? 

The most difficult part with the first and third theories is that the believer can freely choose to leave God even though he has the Holy Spirit. One would think that the Holy Spirit would influence the believer.
