> ### JESUS BEFORE PILATE
> 
> **Matt. 27:11**   Now Jesus stood before the governor, and the governor questioned Him, saying, “Are You the King of the Jews?” And Jesus said to him, “You yourself say *it*.” **12** And while He was being accused by the chief priests and elders, He did not answer. **13** Then Pilate *said to Him, “Do You not hear how many things they testify against You?” **14** And He did not answer him with regard to even a *single* charge, so the governor marveled greatly.

Jesus clearly did speak to Pilate (v. 11), but **He did not answer [...] with regard to [the charges]**. This is what marveled Pilate. It can be assumed that Pilate was experienced in handling these kind of trials and that he knew the difference between a guilty and an innocent man. 

> **Matt. 27:15**   Now at the feast the governor was accustomed to release for the crowd *any* one prisoner whom they wanted. **16** And at that time they were holding a notorious prisoner, called Barabbas. **17** So when the people gathered together, Pilate said to them, “Whom do you want me to release for you? Barabbas, or Jesus who is called Christ?” **18** For he knew that because of envy they had delivered Him over.

Pilate wanted Jesus to be free for three reasons: Jesus was innocent and not worthy of death, he knew that the Jews were envious of him, and his wife warned him not to touch Him. Pilate helped in putting the worst man possible next to Jesus, one who deserved death, hoping that the great contrast would change the people's mind. Unfortunately, this did not change the mind of the people.

>  **20** But the chief priests and the elders persuaded the crowds to ask for Barabbas and to put Jesus to death. **21** But the governor answered and said to them, “Which of the two do you want me to release for you?” And they said, “Barabbas.” **22** Pilate *said to them, “Then, what shall I do with Jesus who is called Christ?” They all *said, “Let Him be crucified!” **23** And he said, “Why, what evil did He do?” But they were crying out all the more, saying, “Let Him be crucified!”

To his credit, Pilate did several things to save Jesus from the crucifixion: He used the worst criminal, **Barabbas**, he confirmed to the crowds what to do with Jesus (v. 22) to give the crowds a second chance to let Him go, and asked for a reason (v. 23) for his crucifixion. 
The Pharisees are the most guilty of all. They were responsible for the responses to Pilate's tries. They stirred up the crowds to choose Barabbas, even though this person would never have been chosen, if it were not for Jesus. Furthermore, they suggested that Jesus be **crucified**. Also, they deliberately did not answer Pilate, but stirred up the crowds to shout even more, to such a way that **a riot was starting**, of which they knew Pilate was afraid. 

> **Matt. 27:24**   Now when Pilate saw that he was accomplishing nothing, but rather that a riot was starting, he took water and washed his hands in front of the crowd, saying, “I am innocent of this man’s blood; see *to that* yourselves.”

Pilate thought he could get away (from universal guilt) with a symbolic gesture and a few words, however, it is not sufficient to say to God that he had tried, but they would not listen. In the end, Jesus' death was still (under) his responsibility.

> **25** And all the people answered and said, “His blood be on us and on our children!” 

It cannot be that **the people answered** all in the same manner. It must have been one person—a Pharisees—who started the chant upon which the rest repeated. It cannot be said that the Pharisees and the people did not know what they were saying here, for this was a clear form of cursing oneself, or at least taking upon oneself the guilt. Ironically, it was Jesus Who took the guilt of the world upon Himself, and was willing to take theirs as well.

> **26** Then he released Barabbas for them; but after having Jesus scourged, he delivered Him over to be crucified.

Here, Pilate made his second mistake. The scourging had not been necessary. In the movie #tags/movies *The Passion of the Christ*, where Jesus is portrayed by Jim Caviezel #tags/people/christian/actors#, the director, Mel Gibson, used this scene to put Pilate in a more merciful light, by giving the crowd one more chance to let Jesus free, by flogging Him instead of crucifying Him, after which the crowd still did not relent. However, the flogging itself would have killed Jesus already in a manner of hours, due to the blood loss, or could have killed Him directly due to shock. > **Matt. 27:38**   At that time two robbers *were crucified with Him, one on the right and one on the left. **39** And those passing by were blaspheming Him, shaking their heads **40** and saying, “You who *are going to* destroy the sanctuary and rebuild it in three days, save Yourself!
