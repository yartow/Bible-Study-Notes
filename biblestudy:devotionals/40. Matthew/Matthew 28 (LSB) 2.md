> ### Jesus Is Risen!
> **Matt. 28:1**   Now after the Sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first *day* of the week, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary came to look at the grave. 

Concerning the issue of on what day Jesus was crucified—the answer depends on the interpretation of "three days and three nights", and which day was the Sabbath mentioned in ... (for the next day was a Sabbath) #todo/opzoeken/reference , but also on when Jesus had risen. The High Sabbaths were the first and last days of the seven days of Pesach. If the first High Sabbath did not coincide with the regular Sabbath, which started on Friday evening and ended 24 hours later, then, if we take the "three days and three nights" literally, there are two possibilities for when Jesus was crucified: (1) Jesus was crucified on Tuesday and lay in the grave from Tuesday evening to Friday evening. He was risen then, on Friday evening, contrary to the conventional #questions/language/english/word (iets wat altijd gedacht wordt, maar niet perse waar hoeft te zijn) traditional Sunday morning, but no one knew about this. Because it was still **Sabbath** then, **Mary Magdalene and the other Mary** waited until Sunday morning **to look at the grave**. However, this raises the question why they did not visit him on Wednesday morning. (2) The second possibility is that He was crucified on Wednesday, but this raises the same question. 
This only leaves Thursday and Friday. If the crucifixion took place on Thursday Jesus' time in the grave would effectively be three days and two nights, and if it took place on Friday, this would be two nights and one day. Because of Hebrew (or Aramaic or more broadly, Middle-Eastern #tags/culture/ane) idioms #questions/language/english/word "three day and three nights" can mean a variety of things, unlike the stricter Western definition of one day meaning 24 hours, which renders these two possibilities plausible as well. 
Concluding, the restrictions of a Sabbath on Friday and the duration of these "three days and three nights" are lifted up by the existence of the High Sabbath and the Hebrew used of idioms, which renders all days include Monday to Friday possible. However, Thursday and Friday seem to be most plausible due to the assumption that the two Marys would visit Jesus' grave as soon as possible. 

> **2** And behold, there was a great earthquake, for an angel of the Lord descended from heaven and came and rolled away the stone and sat upon it. **3** And his appearance was like lightning, and his clothing as white as snow. **4** And the guards quaked from fear of him and became like dead men. **5** And the angel answered and said to the women, “Do not be afraid; for I know that you are looking for Jesus who has been crucified. **6** “He is not here, for He has risen, just as He said. Come, see the place where He was lying. **7** “And go quickly and tell His disciples that He has risen from the dead; and behold, He is going ahead of you into Galilee, there you will see Him; behold, I have told you.”

The word **behold** is not only used to *show* something, such as, "See, this is what I have made for you," but it can also show future things which cannot be seen with the eyes. God used this word many times to show the forefathers the promise of their seed #todo/opzoeken/reference . Even though in some cases they had something visible to see, like the stars in the sky (Genesis 15:5), in most cases they did not see anything and had to go fully by faith. This is why Abraham was commended for his faith (Hebrews 11:8–12). Even so were these women who believed—despite the fact they were surprised to see that Jesus had risen, because they did not fully understand. #biblestudy/discovery

> **Matt. 28:8**   And they left the tomb quickly with fear and great joy and ran to report it to His disciples. **9** And behold, Jesus met them and said, “Greetings!” And they came up and took hold of His feet and worshiped Him. **10** Then Jesus said to them, “Do not be afraid; go and report to My brothers to leave for Galilee, and there they will see Me.”

See the other Gospels to see which parts have been skipped here. The speed and brevity with which these events have been noted leads up to another emphasis in v. 11. Like in Matthew 27:51 this slow-down of the pace is to show what happened **behind** the scenes. 

> **Matt. 28:11**   Now while they were on their way, behold, some of the guard came into the city and reported to the chief priests all that had happened. **12** And when they had assembled with the elders and took counsel together, they gave a large sum of money to the soldiers, **13** and said, “You are to say, ‘His disciples came by night and stole Him away while we were asleep.’ **14** “And if this is heard before the governor, we will win him over and keep you out of trouble.” **15** And they took the money and did as they had been instructed; and this story was widely spread among the Jews, *and is* to this day.

The Gospel of Matthew was written about thirty years after Jesus' death, so **this day** means that the rumors concerning Jesus' resurrection still survived decades later.

> ### The Great Commission
> **Matt. 28:16**   But the eleven disciples proceeded to Galilee, to the mountain which Jesus had designated. **17** And when they saw Him, they worshiped *Him*; but some doubted. **18** And Jesus came up and spoke to them, saying, “All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth. **19** “Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, **20** teaching them to keep all that I commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age.”

The fast pace with which this is written and the sudden ending seems like Matthew was in a hurry to write down the most important events. This makes it very likely that Jesus said many more words and many more events happened during the Ascension.
