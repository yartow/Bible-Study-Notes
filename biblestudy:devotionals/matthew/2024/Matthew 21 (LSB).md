#### Essay. Does God always provide?
#tags/theology/attributesofgod/lovingkindness/provision
This is not something that anyone can willekeurig #questions/language/english/word say, but the principle is true. If **the Lord has need of something** He will have it. If He needs a prophet, He can raise one from the dust. This emphasizes the need for us to trust in Him with all of our hearts (Proverbs 3:5-6) and not to worry about material things (Matthew 6:25ff). When we *actually* need something, not because we want it or even need it for ourselves, but because we need it to further God's Kingdom, God knows it is a need and not a wish, and if He needs it, He will provide for it Himself. When we do not obtain something, it means we did not need it. 
Of course there is always the argument that one did not pray hard enough or did not have enough faith, and indeed, there are those kinds of situations, but these situations as well are those where God *needed* you not to obtain what you needed, in order for you to learn the lesson of praying or having more faith. 
Note that there are two levels of needing and two levels of wanting, so four levels in total. The lowest level is that of human want. This is what man wants to have which is necessarily important. These could be sinful things but also good things which we want to further the Kingdom. However, they are not necessary. An example of this is money or other materials, or help from people. These can be beneficial to the Kingdom of God, but they are not always needed for all situations. In a specific they could be needed, which brings us to the second level, which is that of human need. 
Human need is that which man cannot do without. For human lives these are food, water and air, but for the Kingdom these are the Bible, a Bible teacher and the Church, among others. 
We then get to the third level which is God's want. What He wants, is almost always less than our Level 2 needs, for we tend to think that we need a specific thing in order to praise, worship or serve God, which in God's eyes is not needed (cf. Psalm 51:16-17). 

In the final level, Level 4, only those things are present that God cannot do without to fulfill His will—if such a thing can be said without being blasphemous. 
**Example.** In the trivial case there is a good thing and God wants you to have this good thing (Level 3) and He has this in His plan (i.e. His will, Level 4). This is where Christians pray for things which they know of that God wants, e.g. "Let not my will be done, but Yours" #todo/opzoeken/reference. These are not examples of the healing of a sick person—for we do not know God's will concerning these—but more like the spread of the Gospel in a certain area and that people may come to know Christ, which are #todo betere voorbeelden bedenken. 
But suppose there is a situation where—as mentioned above—a person *needs* to learn to persist and pray more, to fast and to fully surrender to God, then this need can only be fulfilled by *withholding* him from that which he is praying for, even if it is something that he needs for the Kingdom (Level 2) or which God wants (Level 3). In this case it is God's needs that apply, which is the *lack* of provision. 
Another example is that of the current war between Israel and Iran #tags/history/war/israel/2025/iran where many people die. It is logical that God does not *want* people[^1] to die—perhaps with exception of the terrorists[^2]—let alone that Christians die, but  there is something higher than God's want, which is God's *will*. There could be multiple scenarios where God has chosen one specific to happen—this scenario is called His will. In order to establish this will, certain needs need to be fulfilled. 

In vv. 2-3 Jesus shows that God provides whatever is needed. From our essay above we establish that even when we do not get what *we* need, God has still provided what *His* need is for us. #biblestudy/discovery/godshiddenplans

The reason Jesus rode on a donkey instead of on a horse, was because the horse is a symbol of war. His kingship was not shown by the two animals, but by the disciples who **laid their garments on them**, and the **crowd [who] spread their garments in the road**. Of course, most of these people probably did not know that He was not the kind of king they expected, and that He was God as well, but the point here is that His splendor is not shown by His material possessions.

> ### THE BARREN FIG TREE
> 
> **Matt. 21:18**   Now in the morning, when He was returning to the city, He became hungry. **19** And seeing a lone fig tree by the road, He came to it and found nothing on it except leaves only; and He *said to it, “No longer shall there ever be *any* fruit from you.” And at once the fig tree withered.

This again shows Jesus' limited knowledge of events. Even though He was fully God at that time, His knowledge was freely and deliberately limited so that Jesus would have genuine feelings and responses, not to show to us or to the world, but for His own "experience". With this I do not mean that Jesus wanted to experience what it is to be human, but that this life on Earth was something He had to go through. So, applying this to the situation above, we see that Jesus did not know the fig tree did not have any fruit, for otherwise He would not have **[come] to it and found nothing**. If Jesus had known about this, He might have not stopped by the tree. Of course, it is possible that He would have stopped at the tree despite His knowledge of the tree, for the simple reason to teach His disciples this lesson about faith and prayer. However, the description that Matthew and Mark (Mark 11:13) uses clearly indicates that Jesus did not know whether there were figs, because He was misled by its leaves (see notes on Mark 11:13). #tags/theology/christology/godhood/fullknowledge 

> **Matt. 21:20**   And seeing *this*, the disciples marveled, saying, “How did the fig tree wither *all* at once?” **21** And Jesus answered and said to them, “Truly I say to you, if you have faith and do not doubt, you will not only do what was done to the fig tree, but even if you say to this mountain, ‘Be taken up and cast into the sea,’ it will happen. **22** And all things you ask in prayer, believing, you will receive.”

There is a small difference between the translations of the LSB and ESV here. The word **believing** here is a gerund, referring to prayer, while in the ESV this is phrased in the form of an extra clause, "if you have faith". Of course the emphasis is not on only having faith when praying, but rather on the *continuing* character of faith. #biblestudy/discovery
An important instruction here is also that one **will receive** only when things are asked when onen believes. Note that it does not say that the Christian is required to believe that what he asks for must happen, but the Christian must believe *in Christ*. The faith of a Christian should always be in that Jesus Christ listens to our prayers if these are according to the Father's will.

> ### PARABLE OF THE VINE-GROWERS
> 
> **Matt. 21:33**   “Listen to another parable. There was a landowner who PLANTED A VINEYARD AND PUT A WALL AROUND IT AND DUG A WINE PRESS IN IT, AND BUILT A TOWER, and rented it out to vine-growers and went on a journey. 

The word **landowner** literally means, "man, head of a household", which is exactly what God is. 

Jesus quotes from Isaiah 5:1–2, where at the end of v. 2 the climax shows that all the grapes were worthless, regardless of how much effort God had put in it. The reason these grapes—which refer to Israel—were worthless is because they were not planted by streams of waters (cf. Psalm 1:3). The tree itself can bear fruit, provided it gets sufficient nutrients and water. God had provided everything already, so it was the tree who refused to take from the source of water, or in this case, to drink from the source of living water.
Here, Jesus refers to the leaders of Israel who were supposed to provide for the people. 

> **34** Now when the harvest time approached, he sent his slaves to the vine-growers to receive his fruit. **35** And the vine-growers took his slaves and beat one, and killed another, and stoned a third. **36** Again he sent another group of slaves larger than the first; and they did the same thing to them. **37** But afterward he sent his son to them, saying, ‘They will respect my son.’ **38** But when the vine-growers saw the son, they said among themselves, ‘This is the heir; come, let us kill him and seize his inheritance.’ 

The difference between the *murder* (not a killing, because it was premeditated, cf. Exodus 20 #todo/opzoeken/reference ) of this son was that in the former situations the vine-growers did not want to lose the fruit which was due to the landowner. Here, however, they wanted to **seize his inheritance** as well. Killing the son would not make them the new heirs, but they thought that after the son was dead, the landowner would not send any more servants to collect the money and as long as there were collectors, the land was as good as theirs. 
This is an image of the leaders of the Jewish community—the Sadducees and the Pharisees—who killed the prophets, and ultimately the Son as well, in order to keep the fruit of the people of God, which were not even theirs, just like the vineyard was not  the vine-growers', for the landowner had **rented it out**. The Jewish leaders postponed this payment by killing the prophets and they thought that they could keep God away forever, and God stayed silent for four centuries, and the Jewish sects thought that the people of God were now theirs.
