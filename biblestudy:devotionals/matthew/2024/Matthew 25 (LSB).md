> ### Parable of Ten Virgins
> **Matt. 25:1**   “Then the kingdom of heaven may be compared to ten virgins, who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. **2** “Now five of them were foolish, and five were prudent. **3** “For when the foolish took their lamps, they took no oil with them, **4** but the prudent took oil in flasks along with their lamps. **5** “Now while the bridegroom was delaying, they all got drowsy and *began to* sleep. **6** “But at midnight there was a shout, ‘Behold, the bridegroom! Come out to meet him.’ **7** “Then all those virgins rose and trimmed their lamps. **8** “And the foolish said to the prudent, ‘Give us some of your oil, for our lamps are going out.’ **9** “But the prudent answered, saying, ‘No, there will not be enough for us and you *too*; go instead to the dealers and buy *some* for yourselves.’ **10** “And while they were going away to make the purchase, the bridegroom came, and those who were ready went in with him to the wedding feast; and the door was shut. **11** “And later the other virgins also came, saying, ‘Lord, lord, open up for us.’ **12** “But he answered and said, ‘Truly I say to you, I do not know you.’ **13** “Therefore, stay awake, for you do not know the day nor the hour.

**Delayed...midnight.** The Lord may be "**delayed**" but he will come in time. It is just like when the prophet Samuel told King Saul that he would come on the seventh day, to make a sacrifice to the Lord before Saul would start his attack on an enemy king. Saul waited until the seventh day but did not see him and became anxious, and started offering a cow himself, despite the explicit rule that it was forbidden for anyone to offer anything except for the priest. It was after the fight, perhaps at the end of the seventh day, but still the seventh, that Samuel came and became furious for ignoring God's command. 
In the same way, Jesus will return and His return will be unexpected. Those who already know that Jesus will come back in the seventh year will become excited, but they will restlessly wait in the seventh year when they see Jesus has not yet come. Regardless of when Jesus comes, be it at the very last minute of the seventh year, or even much later than that, He is right in coming whenever He wants. It is the Father's prerogative.

> ### Parable of the Talents
> **Matt. 25:14**   “For *it is* just like a man *about* to go on a journey, who called his own slaves and handed over his possessions to them. **15** “And to one he gave five talents, to another, two, and to another, one, each according to his own ability; and he went on his journey. **16** “Immediately the one who had received the five talents went and traded with them, and gained five more talents. **17** “In the same manner the one who *had received* the two *talents* gained two more. **18** “But he who received the one *talent* went away, and dug *a hole* in the ground and hid his master’s money.

The first two slaves **[immediately...] went and traded with them**, but the last slave simply **went away**. His lack of eagerness to use these talents shows his worthlessness. Anyone with a bit of sense of business would at least *try* to use the money, or have thoughts of how to invest it. Even the safest way, to put it in the bank, would be an option. But those who rather put it **in the ground** are too lazy to even think about any opportunities for the Kingdom of God.
Does this mean then that those without any sense of business are not worthy to enter the Kingdom? No. It is not because of any sense or talent[^1] that we are imbued with—for these are given to us by God Himself—and salvation comes solely by the work of Jesus Christ. However, *every* Christian must have a sense of urgency to spread the Gospel and to be powerful and effective, be it through prayer (James 5:16) or through a multitude of gifts[^2] (1 Corinthians 11-12 #todo/opzoeken/reference  ), and *willing* to use it (cf. the joy of Peter and Paul/John #todo/opzoeken/reference  after having been flogged and their urge to *immediately* continue spreading the Gospel. #biblestudy/discovery

The slave lied that he was **afraid**, for there were so many other things he could have done with the talent. In the same way, if we interpret talents as those talents with which God has endowed us, and we are afraid to use it, say, having the talent of singing but being afraid to sing in public, instead of hiding it and not using it, one could still use it for himself and sing in his own room when no one is looking. There is not excuse for not using one's talents.

> **Matt. 25:29**   “For to everyone who has, *more* shall be given, and he will have an abundance; but from the one who does not have, even what he does have shall be taken away. **30** “And throw out the worthless slave into the outer darkness; in that place there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

This would sound very cruel if this were said to people in general, for the rich would become richer then and the poor poorer, but we know God is not like that. Contrary, He is meek and graceful to the poor (cf. #todo/opzoeken/reference Nehemiah, see also God's warnings to the rich who oppress the poor in Micha). This message is given, however, to Christians—that is, **everyone who has**—and that these will receive more, purely by grace. Those **who [do] not have** refers to the unbeliever, and more specifically to those who do not want to believe and receive the Gospel. In the end—that is, when they end up in hell—everything shall be taken away from them, for that which they had on Earth cannot be brought to the afterlife. But those who had the faith here, though they too are not able to bring their materials to Heaven, they will have much more—they will *know* God as He is, instead of only believing in Him, alongside the many other blessings mentioned in Revelation 21 #todo/opzoeken/reference. > ### The Final Judgment
> **Matt. 25:31**   “But when the Son of Man comes in His glory, and all the angels with Him, then He will sit on His glorious throne. **32** “And all the nations will be gathered before Him; and He will separate them from one another, as the shepherd separates the sheep from the goats; **33** and He will put the sheep on His right, and the goats on the left.

The division between **left** and **right** will be based on works. This does not mean that salvation is based on the works of *man*, for it is by Christ's work only that we have been saved, but it is by our works that we *show* that we have been saved. It is not possible for one to be saved and now show works in his life. James calls it a dead faith #todo/opzoeken/reference but it simply means that one is in fact not saved (or depending on terminology, *will* not be saved as soon as he dies, or when Jesus returns before that). 

> **Matt. 25:34**   “Then the King will say to those on His right, ‘Come, you who are blessed of My Father, inherit the kingdom, which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world.

This verse is related to the election of the saints, but the main point here is that God created a kingdom for His children. It is *for us* for whom this is created. Perhaps this was created even before Earth was created, for He already knew what would happen. 

> **35** ‘For I was hungry, and you gave Me *something* to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave Me *something* to drink; I was a stranger, and you invited Me in; **36** naked, and you clothed Me; I was sick, and you visited Me; I was in prison, and you came to Me.’ **37** “Then the righteous will answer Him, saying, ‘Lord, when did we see You hungry, and feed You, or thirsty, and give You *something* to drink? **38** ‘And when did we see You a stranger, and invite You in, or naked, and clothe You? **39** ‘And when did we see You sick, or in prison, and come to You?’ **40** “And the King will answer and say to them, ‘Truly I say to you, to the extent that you did it to one of these brothers of Mine, *even* the least *of them*, you did it to Me.’

Whenever we serve **one of these brothers of [Jesus]** we have served His body and hence Jesus Christ Himself as well. It is important to serve the world as well, but it is more important to serve our own family first.

> **Matt. 25:41**   “Then He will also say to those on His left, ‘Depart from Me, accursed ones, into the eternal fire which has been prepared for the devil and his angels; **42** for I was hungry, and you gave Me *nothing* to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave Me nothing to drink; **43** I was a stranger, and you did not invite Me in; naked, and you did not clothe Me; sick, and in prison, and you did not visit Me.’ **44** “Then they themselves also will answer, saying, ‘Lord, when did we see You hungry, or thirsty, or a stranger, or naked, or sick, or in prison, and did not take care of You?’ **45** “Then He will answer them, saying, ‘Truly I say to you, to the extent that you did not do it to one of the least of these, you did not do it to Me.’ **46** “And these will go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous into eternal life.”

Can you imagine people who call themselves Christians but refuse to help other Christians? Jehova's Witnesses (and Mormons as well #todo/opzoeken) call themselves Christians, but they do not help other Christians. They are very strict in letting their members join other societies and social groups. #todo/opzoeken their own sports and book clubs etc. However, even in the Christian world there are denominations, especially in the Netherlands, where they have their own schools and theological seminaries (such as the Theological Seminary at Kampen) which are required to be able to preach in one of their churches. They even have their own missionary organization. This might not sound strange until you realize that there are *two* Reformed missionary organizations, just because one differs a little bit from the other. However, these are not the best examples of Christians who are not helping other brothers. A better example would be the Chinese MSI church, who claims to be Christian, but does not allow (or discourages) its members to attend gatherings organized by other churches, nor allows them to help these groups. 
Some of these kind of churches have false teaching regarding the Gospel, but even if it is true, if they do not show this by their works, Jesus will not know them. 

[^1]: The parable is about *talents* in the sense of money, where one *talent* is equivalent to 15 years wage of a laborer, perhaps 450.000 today's terms. 
[^2]: The difference between *talents* (its modern meaning) and *gifts* is that talents are the skills accrued by a person and gifts are the potential (of the speed with which one learns and the maximum (#questions/language/english/word maximal?) obtainable level) thereof given af birth. So a person might have a gift of playing the piano, but if he never comes in touch with a piano, he does not have any skill at it, and thus no talent. Another person might not have this gift of playing the piano, but might be just as talented, but purely through hard work.
