For this reason the Pharisees served as examples for the people of what the Bible said and how a follower of God ought to behave, especially to those who had not read the Bible. 
They had taken the role of dictating and interpreting the Law upon themselves, they had not been chosen by God, like David or Moses and the other prophets were. However, this consequently meant that they #questions/language/english functie bekleden functions in which they had to be examples of Law-abiding citizens. 
In this world people can work in the field of the Judiciary, Executive or Justice branch—for example as judge, Senator or President—without an exemplary and unreproached life (cf. 1 Timothy 3 #todo/opzoeken/reference )—which in God's Kingdom cannot be any further from the truth. To Christians integrity is one of the most important character traits: the way we are in church must be the same as how we are at work or anywhere else. Unfortunately many (Dutch) Reformed Protestant Christian churches have become like the world by having a *Reverend* (in Dutch, *Dominee*, meaning "lord") as the leader of their church. In the appointment process the church looks with scrutiny at his credentials or experience in leading or his knowledge of the Bible, but not using the same standards for his lifestyle—e.g. eating, drinking, how his children behave and how he treats his wife, or whether the fear and the joy of the Lord are in him. 

> **3** therefore all that they tell you, do and keep, but do not do according to their deeds; for they say *things* and do not do *them*. **4** And they tie up heavy burdens and lay them on men’s shoulders, but they themselves are unwilling to move them with *so much as* a finger. 

In my opinion this is the second most harmful thing the scribes and the Pharisees are guilty of. Their hypocrisy is what most people already know, but the fact that do not help others with the burdend they lay on them, is a great sin. So even if they were not hypocrites and held themselves to the same standard, and even if they did not **do all their deeds to be noticed by men**, this is still a sin, in and of itself. A Christian always has to help others with the burden of their sin, or he himself is in sin. A true believer cannot tell others what is sin and leave him without so much as a word of advice or a listening ear. This is what distinguishes Christianity from other religions.
Had it been better then to not tell them of their sin? Suppose a righteous person meets a prostitute and he only has one minute left to talk to her. > **5** But they do all their deeds to be noticed by men; for they broaden their phylacteries and lengthen the tassels *of their garments*. **6** And they love the place of honor at banquets and the best seats in the synagogues, **7** and respectful greetings in the marketplaces, and being called Rabbi by men. **8** But do not be called Rabbi; for One is your Teacher, and you are all brothers.

Jesus' meaning here is not that no one should **be called Rabbi**, but that everyone should always realize that Christian **are all brothers**. This does not contradict with Paul's statement that effective elders are worth a double portion (1 Timothy 5:17). This is meant purely in the context of merit. One who works harder should earn more, and one who's gifts are more rare, should also earn more, either in honor or in appreciation. When someone is able to translate from a not often-spoken language or can speak multiple languages, it not wrong to compliment him and praise him for his gifts or talents[^1]. However, regardless of his capabilities, he should still be treated as a brother. Any brother who does wrong, should be reprimanded, and no brother should have the better seats in church or at the dining table, for those things have nothing to do with one's capabilities. Rather yet, the better seats in church are for those who have bad eyesight, who cannot walk well or are older, so that they can see the preacher and hear him better. Everything serves a function, and there is no respect of persons (Colossians 3:25). 

> **9** And do not call *anyone* on earth your father; for One is your Father, He who is in heaven. 

In the Catholic Church leaders of the church are addressed as *Father*—perhaps even with a capital letter when written! #todo/opzoeken —instead of as brother. The other titles Jesus mentions here are used elsewhere, such as **instructors** in the army or **Teacher** in school, but **Father** is never used unless it is one's own father, which is the only legitimate use of this title. So the Catholic Church is obviously overstepping its bounds here and explicitly assuming authority that is not given it. #tags/religion/christianity/catholicism

> **10** Do not be called instructors; for One is your Instructor, *that is*, Christ. **11** But the greatest among you shall be your servant. **12** And whoever exalts himself shall be humbled; and whoever humbles himself shall be exalted.

There are, of course, humble Catholic priests and other leaders, but in general these priests enjoy a high position, power and status in the eyes of the people, because they have studied and know how to explain the Bible, while others do not—especially in medieval times. > ### WOES TO THE SCRIBES AND PHARISEES
> 
> **Matt. 23:13**   “But woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you shut off the kingdom of heaven from people; for you do not enter in yourselves, nor do you allow those who are entering to go in.

The **woe** is a curse, it is not merely advise for the readers to beware. This woe emphasizes not the fact that the Pharisees doe not **enter in [...] the kingdom of heaven**, but that they do **[not] allow those who are entering to go in**. The Bible makes it clear in several parts that there is one thing more severe than one's sin, which is to let others sin. #todo/nogaftemaken examples, woe to those who let one of these children stumble. Not only do they proclaim a false gospel, but they also use their God-given authority within society to put pressure on those who do want to listen to and follow the Gospel.

> **Matt. 23:15**   “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you travel around on sea and land to make one proselyte; and when he becomes one, you make him twice as much a son of hell as yourselves.

Proselyte are converts which in the small sense of the word could refer to a conversion from one sect—e.g. the Sadducee or Zealot sects—to theirs. In a broader sense this refers to a conversion from heathen religions to Judaism. The Pharisees were making proselytes to help them in their cause, not to build people up spiritually, let alone to convert pagans.
The proselytes become figuratively speaking **twice as [bad]** as the Pharisees. Whereas older generations still have some common sense and adhere to some moral values and social pressure, in following generations these become less and less. Today's Democrats and Republicans are completely the opposite of each other, almost mutually exclusive in their positions, while a generation ago—or even less—many positions were shared by both parties. Even former Present Biden was *for* the Second Amendment in the 90s (or at least he promised he would not take people's guns away, which he reneged on a few decades later, in order to win votes for his presidential candidacy) and former President Obama claimed in his first term that a marriage was between a man and a woman, not between two people of the same sex. Perhaps these are the wrong examples, because these Democratic presidents only said those things to gain popularity, but when looking at Democratic presidents before Reagan, such as John F. Kennedy, it can be seen that they were much more conservate. #tags/politics 
The older generation of Pharisees, such as Gamaliel (see Acts #todo/opzoeken/reference ) —Paul's teacher—was the one who calmed down the commotion among Pharisees with the wisdom of former generation, but the younger generation of Pharisees do not care about peace and are only looking for their own interest. And so the Pharisees' students were taught by a younger generation of Pharisees who were prone on dividing the people, and the students were taught to do so even more.

> **Matt. 23:16**   “Woe to you, blind guides, who say, ‘Whoever swears by the sanctuary, *that* is nothing; but whoever swears by the gold of the sanctuary is obligated.’ **17** You fools and blind men! For which is more important, the gold or the sanctuary that sanctified the gold? **18** And, ‘Whoever swears by the altar, *that* is nothing, but whoever swears by the offering on it, he is obligated.’ **19** You blind men, which is more important, the offering, or the altar that sanctifies the offering?

This is an obvious statement, but still the Pharisees were guilty of this. Perhaps they did not do these things literally—that is, swearing on the gold or on the altar—but they did not understand the holiness of swearing on the sanctuary. Let me explain. In those days there was a law named the *korban* which stated that if anyone swore (or pledged) to give a certain amount to the temple, he was committed to it. And so there were young men who had vowed this *korban* but for some reason they did not have enough money to take care of their parents anymore. (This could be because of the Pharisees' influence who stimulated them to make this decision (impulsively, without thinking it through), or their arguments that supporting the sanctuary was even more important than supporting one's parents.) #todo/opzoeken/reference #todo/opzoeken wat was de korban precies? 
So in a sense these young men had vowed on the sanctuary and they could not escape their vow, but the Law—which the sanctuary also stands for—they could supposedly escape, even though the Fifth Commandment—not just any law but one of the Ten Commandments!—is "Obey your parents."
One of the hallmarks of Western society is the abandonment of the care for one's parents. The elderly are too easily put in homes for the elderly #questions/language/english/word and when put there, they are hardly visited. 
Of course, I have to realize that I myself am guilty of that as well. I have too often put my work or even church activities before the care of my parents. I have parents whom I only visit a few times a year, even though they live only an hour away, or only call once every few months. Therefore my judgment is first pointed towards myself.

> **20** Therefore, whoever swears by the altar, swears *both* by the altar and by everything on it. **21** And whoever swears by the sanctuary, swears *both* by the sanctuary and by Him who dwells within it. **22** And whoever swears by heaven, swears *both* by the throne of God and by Him who sits upon it.

Even though swearing is not something the Christian should do—his "yes" should be a "yes", and his "no" and "no" #todo/opzoeken/reference  —*if* he does it, is should by a logical thing. How is it possible for anyone to swear on that thing that is on the altar? Swearing in fact means, "If I do not do that thing that I promised to do, you may take thing away from which I swore on." So if someone swore on his mother, he offers up his mother to be taken away from him—that is to be put to death—if he does not fulfill his promise. And swearing on an altar means that the greatest thing there is—the temple or the altar or God—can be taken away from him, or put otherwise, he can be taken away from the sanctuary of God and never be allowed to enter the Temple again or even be part of the community of believers. This, of course, is something that one should never swear—which is the reason why Jesus commanded this #todo/opzoeken/reference. The main point, however, is that one cannot swear on a small thing, such as on the offer he put on the altar. For what use is it to swear on the gift he paid for and put on the altar? If this is taken from him, it only means that he needs to buy a new offer to sacrifice.

> **Matt. 23:23**   “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you tithe mint and dill and cumin, and have neglected the weightier provisions of the Law: justice and mercy and faithfulness; but these are the things you should have done without neglecting the others. **24** You blind guides, who strain out a gnat but swallow a camel!

Even the lightest of herbs, up to the milligrams, the Pharisees tithed, but the most important thing about the Law is not the tithing, but it is mercy and faithfulness. Micah 6:8 summarizes the Law to three things: justice, lovingkindness and humbleness—all three which the Pharisees do not have.
Why would the Pharisees even bother to uphold the small things? Weighing the cumin most certainly is not an easy thing. These days people leave the faith and do not hold to anything of the Law. The answer is that the Pharisees did this purely  to gain honor from the people. Perhaps they actually tithed their salary, but it could just as well be that they lied about this, seeing how easily they lied and tricked people, and even bribed them[^2]. To humans it is always the difficult things that with which #English  people gain honor. In the eyes of man honor is gained with superficially difficult things—with things that can be seen on the outside. If the Pharisees wanted to do actual difficult things, these would have been to love their enemies and pray for them. Even these would have been seen by others, but these were, apparently, too difficult for them to do. 

> **Matt. 23:25**   “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you clean the outside of the cup and of the dish, but inside they are full of robbery and self-indulgence. **26** You blind Pharisee, first clean the inside of the cup and of the dish, so that the outside of it may become clean also.

The inside does not have anything to do with the outside, but Jesus still says "**so that the outside of it may become clean also**". If it were the outside that was dirty, I understand that the inside cannot become clean, for one has to hold the outside in order to clean the inside. However, when one cleans the inside with hands that have been dirtied by the outside of the dish, the inside will never become clean. In the kingdom things work the other way around, as mentioned before: the rules of the kingdom are  almost always the opposite of those of the world. A person with a dirty inside, that is their heart, can never become clean on the outside, because everything he does, even the good things, are done with the wrong intention and these dirty the outside works. 

> **Matt. 23:27**   “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you are like whitewashed tombs which on the outside appear beautiful, but inside they are full of dead men’s bones and all uncleanness. **28** In this way, you also outwardly appear righteous to men, but inwardly you are full of hypocrisy and lawlessness.

Why would Jesus state another woe which is almost similar to the previous one of hypocrisy. If woes are curses, does this need to be cursed two times?
The difference between the two is that the former woe concerns what the Pharisees do and the second woe what the Pharisees are. Not only do they teach others on what to do, and they teach the wrong things, but also do they show a bad example of how a follower of God should behave. 
**Example.** I have known Christians who genuinely believed in the doctrine of hypergrace, but who otherwise led an exemplary life. They did not teach people to sin but rather not to sin, but they overemphasized the aspect grace within the gospel. One of them told me about a person he knew who was living in sin but he knew that the person would eventually go to Heaven because he had once said the sinner's prayer. These are two theological errors. The first one is that if one deliberately lives in sin, and continuous to do so, he cannot be a Christian. Either he was never reborn or he was, but has fallen from the faith, assuming that is possible. 

> **Matt. 23:29**   “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you build the tombs of the prophets and adorn the monuments of the righteous, **30** and say, ‘If we had been *living* in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partners with them in *shedding* the blood of the prophets.’ **31** So you bear witness against yourselves, that you are sons of those who murdered the prophets. **32** Fill up, then, the measure *of the guilt* of your fathers. **33** You serpents, you brood of vipers, how will you escape the sentence of hell?

The woe here is again pointed at the Pharisees' hypocrisy, but this time it is for covering up the murders they have committed. They not only kill **the prophets [...] and the righteous** but they also built monuments for them as if they were truly sad about their deaths. Their ancestry alone would not have made them guilty, for in the same way Rahab's and Ruth's ancestors (i.e. the Jericho #questions/language/english/word and the Moabites) had killed the Israelites, but they  themselves were not condemned. However, it were their acts that condemned them. And on top that, their fake act of mourning and giving honor to these prophets led to this curse of Jesus (see v. 36) which they would ultimately experience by the persecution of the Romans in 60 AD after the revolt of the Jews and the persecution by the Romans.

> **Matt. 23:34**   “On account of this, behold, I am sending you prophets and wise men and scribes; some of them you will kill and crucify, and some of them you will flog in your synagogues, and persecute from city to city, **35** so that upon you may fall *the guilt of* all the righteous blood shed on earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, whom you murdered between the sanctuary and the altar. **36** Truly I say to you, all these things will come upon this generation.

God waited for the Ammonites for four hundred years before their guilt was full. God was merciful enough to give them a chance to repent and to wait until they had crossed the line of God's patience. After these four hundred years the measure was full and God let the Israelites destroy the Ammonite people in Canaan. The guilt of the Pharisees on the other hand had not been made full yet. The Pharisees' fathers were still under the mercy of God, for even though they had murdered all of His prophets (according to Stephen, see Acts #todo/opzoeken/reference) God still had not sent them into exile again, as He had done before. They were in the post-Exilic period and had received a new chance. The Israelites had been dominated by all major kingdoms, from the Assyrian to the Roman one, with exception of the Greek, and the only reason for that was the mercy of God. But now, Jesus challenges them to **fill up [...] the measure of the guilt** so that God *would* act out His wrath on them. This was a warning from Jesus as well, that if they continued on this path, they would be destroyed. However, the Pharisees neglected this warning went through with plotting the murder of Jesus. #biblestudy/discovery/godshiddenplans

Not only will they be persecuted but also will they not **escape the sentence of hell**. This is a statement only God can make, and when Jesus does, it also indicates that He is God, for no one knows the fate of any man but God. #biblestudy/discovery #tags/theology/christology/godhood 
 
When Jesus uses the phrase "**from [...] Abel to [...] Zechariah**", this is in hyperbole form, but He is not exaggerating. #biblestudy/grammar He uses the names of the first and last martyrs of the Old Testament (according to the Jewish canonical order) (or perhaps even prophets #todo/opzoeken Able as prophet) and says that the guilt of all persecutions between those two falls on them. He is not saying that the Pharisees are *literally* guilty of all persecutions of *all* prophets, but if the Pharisees *falsely* claim they would not have made the mistake of their forefathers—and Jesus says that they would have and have already proven this by their current behavior and their persecution of Him—they will incur all the guilt of their forefathers upon them, even including that of Kain. 
Jesus is merciful to the humble, but He punishes those who are proud. This, too, reflects the law of the Kingdom of God: the biggest sinners who humble themselves and admit their sins will be forgiven, but the smallest sinners who do not admit even their slightest sins, will be punished in the greatest way. That is why those who *think that* they were first shall be last, and vice versa. #todo/opzoeken/reference
