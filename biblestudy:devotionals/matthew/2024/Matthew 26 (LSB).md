> ### The Plot to Kill Jesus
> **Matt. 26:1**   Now it happened that when Jesus had finished all these words, He said to His disciples, **2** “You know that after two days the Passover is coming, and the Son of Man is *to be* delivered over for crucifixion.”

Jesus' prediction of His death was incredibly precise. Even hours before His arrest, in the garden of Gethsemane, He let His disciples know what would happen to Him in the next few minutes. The disciples, however, were still shocked even after the arrest, because they did not truly realize what Jesus meant and were unprepared for the actual event. 


> **Matt. 26:3**   Then the chief priests and the elders of the people were gathered together in the court of the high priest, named Caiaphas; **4** and they plotted together to seize Jesus by stealth and kill Him. **5** But they were saying, “Not during the festival, lest a riot occur among the people.”

This and the previous paragraph shows what happens in the front and at the back. Jesus is preparing to be arrested, and the chief priests and elders are preparing to **kill Him**. Even the timing of Jesus' prediction is exact. 
Jesus mentioned that **after two days Passover is coming**. An outsider would think, "What does that have to do with His death?" but this is for two reasons. First of all, it let the disciples know about the timing of His arrest. Since the priests did not want to arrest Him **during the festival**, they had to do this after the festival or before it. It was obvious that they wanted this to be done as quickly as possible. 

Second of all, and more importantly, it has a theological meaning, namely that it is a fulfillment of the meaning of Passover. Passover is not merely a remembrance of the first Passover when the Israelites escaped from Egypt. No, both the yearly Passover as well as the original one are pointing forwards to what to expect: Just like the Israelites escaped from the clutches of the Egyptian Empire, even more so everyone who believes in Jesus will escape the grasp of Satan. 

**stealth....lest...**. The priests knew all too well that what they did was wrong. Even if they erroneously believed that Jesus was a false prophet, a true leader would convince his people of the wrong person they were following and exploit the faults in his message. They would not have done this with stealth, being afraid to lose the favor of their own people.

#### Essay. Determining the exact day of Jesus' crucifixion
Note that this part took place before the start of Pesach, which is also called **Unleavened Bread**. The Hebrew Pesach lasts seven days, unlike the Western version, and included two Sabbaths (just like national holidays, but compulsory) on the first and last day of the week. These Sabbaths are called High Sabbaths to distinguish them from regular Sabbaths which start on Friday evening. This is important to know, because Jesus was crucified and taken down just before the High Sabbath. This could have been on a Friday, but is not necessarily so. ==See notes on the determination of the dates of Jesus' birth and death.== #todo/opzoeken/reference

Pesach would start that evening, at sunset, and this day would be the same day on the western calendar, but the next day on the Hebrew calendar. Where rhe Western calendar has its cut-off point for days at midnight, the Hebrew calendar has this at sunset. So for example, if Nissan 15, the first day of **Unleavened Bread** was on a Wednesday, the 15th started at sunset. Thus, all day long it would be the 14th until sunset. 
Next to that there are differences in this cut-off point depending on whether one was from the north or south of Israel. The northern Israelites used Roman conventions #todo/opzoeken where the day started at either 6AM or 12AM. 

Here, Matthew is perhaps uses two meanings of the word "day" interchangeably, namely that of the Jewish calendar day—when he wrote "**the first day of Unleavened Bread**"—and the western meaning of day. It could not be that he actually meant the next weekday (in our example this would be Thursday) because it was compulsory that the Pesach meal was eaten after sunset at the *start* of the 15th. #tags/apologetics/history #historicity
