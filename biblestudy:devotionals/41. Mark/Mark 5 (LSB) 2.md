> **28** For she was saying, “If I just touch His garments, I will 1be saved*from this*.”

Her motivation was to be healed, but Jesus immediately claimed her salvation. Perhaps she had the Gospel earlier and was already saved. Her inner faith, however, was now shown but her out acts. True faith is always shown by acts and so now it is *shown* that she is saved. All of this is aside from her healing, for even if she had not been healed, she still acted out of faith. Her healing was a matter of grace, not a result of her acts. 

> **29** And ==immediately== the flow of her blood was dried up; and she knew within her body that she had been healed of her aaffliction. **30** And immediately Jesus, ==perceiving== in Himself that athe power*proceeding* from Him had gone forth, turned around in the crowd and was saying, “Who touched My garments?” 

Of course, Jesus already knew what would happen even before it happened and before He **perceived** it.

> **37** And He allowed no one to accompany Him, except aPeter and James and John the brother of James. **38** And they came to the house of the asynagogue official; and He saw a commotion, and *people* loudly crying and wailing. **39** And entering in, He said to them, “Why are you making a commotion and crying? The child has not died, but is asleep.” **40** And they *began* laughing at Him. But putting them all out, He took along the child’s father and mother and His own companions, and entered*the room* where the child was. **41** And taking the child by the hand, He said to her, “Talitha kum!” (which translated means, “Little girl, aI say to you, arise!”). 

Why in Hebrew (or Aramaic)? Mark wanted to cite this as literally as possible. Most likely, when Mark heard of this story from Peter, Peter also mentioned this part in Aramaic instead of in Hebrew, even though the two of them most likely spoke in Hebrew. So Peter too, for some reason, cited Jesus' literal words. Cf. the part on "Abba", which Jesus cried out, which is not translated. 

> **42** And immediately the little girl stood up and *began to* walk, for she was twelve years old. And immediately they were completely astounded. **43** And He agave them strict orders that no one should know about this, and He said that *some ==food==* should be given to her to eat.

The food was not necessarily for her because she was hungry, although she would have been if she had been ill to the point of death, probably not having eaten for a long while. It was also for others to see that she was not a ghost and a product of their imagination.
