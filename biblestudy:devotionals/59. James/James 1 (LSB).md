> **James 1:5**   But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all generously and without reproach, and it will be given to him. **6** But he must ask in faith, doubting nothing, for the one who doubts is like the surf of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind. **7** For that man ought not to expect that he will receive anything from the Lord, **8** *being* a double-minded man, unstable in all his ways.

> **James 1:9**   But the brother of humble circumstances is to boast in his high position; **10** and the rich man *is to boast* in his humiliation, because like flowering grass he will pass away. **11** For the sun rises with a scorching heat and withers the grass; and its flower falls off and the beauty of its appearance is destroyed; so too the rich man in the midst of his pursuits will fade away.

We are not supposed to boast in temporary things that will fade away, also these riches are not part of the man himself, but are merely his possession. When a rich man is humble on the other hand, this trait is part of his being—his character, actually, yet something that he *is able* to bring to the next world. Then what about **the brother of humble circumstances**, is his position not temporarily and only a matter of a *lack of possessions*? No, his lack is indeed only material, but his **high position**—as James calls it—refers to the fact that he needs *character* to live through his circumstances #questions/language/english/spelling doorleven? Niet survive. One who is poor, needs perseverance and hope to make ends meet. He needs to be joyful in the worst of circumstances and generous though he has little to nothing. Just like the poor widow who gave two lepta[^1] (Luke 21:2)—joyfully, might I add—out of her generosity and reverence for God. It is easy for the **rich** man to have and show all the characteristics of the fruit of the Holy Spirit (Galatians 5:22), for with money it is easy to love, to have joy, peace, patience and kindness. But the poor man is in a higher position than the rich one, and though he is lacking materially, his perseverance (v. 12) will **perfect** his **work** so that he will ultimately be **lacking in nothing** (v. 2). #church/material/sermon

> **James 1:12**   Blessed is a man who perseveres under trial; for once he has been approved, he will receive the crown of life which *the Lord* has promised to those who love Him.

The man is blessed because if he had never undergone a **trial** he could never have been approved. 
**Example.** I know a few people who managed to get quite far in life but never finished their education. Some quit high school, while others went a bit further but still quit their higher education at university. Now they are intelligent enough to be able to have finished these, but what they are missing is the experience of hardship it required to give up a life of fun as a teenager—in the first case—and to spend many hours on intensive research and writing—in the second case—knowing that it is only a little thing they are sacrifice now in order to gain something better later. This experience—to me at least—is priceless.

> **13** Let no one say when he is tempted, “I am being tempted by God”; for God cannot be tempted by evil, and He Himself does not tempt anyone.

The question might are here why the statement that **God cannot be tempted by evil** is added here, as the reverse was never claimed. This becomes clear when one knows the meaning of the word "temptation". A temptation is an act to distract someone from his work in order to make his *fail*. The Oxford Dictionary even says that is to do "something wrong". God cannot and *will* not ever stimulate anyone to do wrong things. He will *allow* people to do wrong things, because of their own freedom of choice. 
Now, when anyone says that he is **tempted by God**, he implies that God wants him to do something wrong, which would mean that God himself has been tempted by evil.

> **14** But each one is tempted when he is carried away and enticed by his own lust.

Quite often this is the case, we are **carried away** and forget about what is important, namely that we are Christians, children of God and that we need to live a holy life. Then, in those very moments, temptations are strongest. 
**Example.** Suppose a man is carried away by his work. He is working, he is busy and he has lots of things on his mind. Now suppose a beautiful woman comes by to seduce him. He is not easily tempted by this woman, because he is still attached to his work. But now suppose he is finished from work and has let all of his responsibilities at his workplace, then if the woman comes by, he is much more susceptible to temptation. 
In the same way we should stay attached to the truth to living a holy life. As soon as we give in to our own **lust** and get carried away by it, then temptation will come. 

> **15** Then when lust has conceived, it gives birth to sin, and when sin is fully matured, it brings forth death. **16** Do not be deceived, my beloved brothers.

There is a very fine line between a "wrong" act and a "sinful" act. I am not speaking theologically here—for in Christian doctrine every wrong act is sin—but I am speaking philosophically. Suppose there is a certain threshold of a wrong act after which this is not simply a wrong act anymore that a sin, that is the *maturation* of that sin and from that point on it brings death to the person who committed this sin.
James does not want us to **be deceived** in thinking that **sin is fully matured** when one has *committed* the act—no, a mere thought can also be—and usually is—a *fully matured* sin.

> **18** In the exercise of His will He brought us forth by the word of truth, so that we would be a kind of first fruits among His creatures.

Among all the **creatures** that God has created man has become the first fruits, the only creature able to have a special relationship with God. But within all man only some are first fruits and will inherit His Kingdom. These are the creatures who have been **brought [...] forth by the word of truth**, only those are born from the truth and therefore know it and recognize it.

> ### Doers of the Word
> **James 1:19**   Know *this*, my beloved brothers. But everyone must be quick to hear, slow to speak *and* slow to anger; **20** for the anger of man does not achieve the righteousness of God. #biblestudy/memorization

It is obvious that a good listener makes for better conversations. If everyone were **quick to hear [and] slow to speak**, that is not interrupt the other and wait for the other to explain himself, all conversations would not end in anger. 
The **anger of man does not achieve the righteousness of God**, this in itself says more than enough, but to be sure the message comes across: what is meant here is that whatever man does to achieve righteousness, it will not be so when he had anger when doing so. The killing of a man may be righteousness, but not when it is committed in anger, likewise for the punishing of children. When punishing children, especially when giving them physical punishments, parents must always be aware that they do this out of love and the child needs to *perceive* it this way too!

> **21** Therefore, laying aside all filthiness and *all* that remains of wickedness, in gentleness receive the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.

In the same way, when we **receive the implanted word** it is of no use when we are filthy and wicked. It is like eating medicine to cure us, but we receive it with dirty hands so that all the medicine becomes spoiled and worthless when we touch it. We need to lay aside our filthiness and our **remains of wickedness**, for yes, even though we have become believers, we still have some of this wickedness in us. 

> **22** But become doers of the word, and not merely hearers who delude themselves. **23** For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man who looks at his natural face in a mirror; **24** for *once* he looked at himself and has gone away, he immediately forgot what kind of person he was.

This concept of hearing but not acting on what one hears exists in all cultures and in many forms. In English there is for example the saying, *practice what you preach* or something like *in one ear, out the other*. #questions/language/english/spelling The sayings are only judging one's behavior, but not explicitly saying that one is lazy or dumb for not actually taking the advice or that which was taught. These verses, however, go further and judge that these persons are those **who delude themselves**. 
It is like someone who wants to lose weight and signs up for course, pays for it, and then does nothing with the tips and tricks he received. Then, the next week he signs up for another course to do everything all over again. He is not deluding, or cheating, anyone but himself.

> **25** But one who looks intently at the perfect law, the *law* of freedom, and abides by it, not having become a forgetful hearer but a doer of the work, this man will be blessed in what he does.

The blessing over this man is beyond the blessing over the acts he does according to the Law but it is in **[*everything*] he does**. In the example above the blessing of this person would not only take form in succesfully losing weight, but he would also be blessed in his relations and in his finance. Now this does not make any sense of course, because the losing of weight is but one part of his live—his health—although there is an argument to make that becoming more healthy leads to extra benefits. But **the perfect law** encompasses *all areas of life*. Simply keeping the Ten Commandments will already positively affect one's *whole life*, let alone the rest, that is the specifics of the Law.
What must one do then? Three things, he must **[look] intently [, abide] by it [and do] the work**. Looking *intently* means that he must not *every aspect* of the Law, not forgetting one detail, especially not deliberately. God is merciful, but He is not a God Who allows us to pick and choose from His Law and He has written the Law down for us so that we will not overlook these things. This is one problem the many churches today have—they only keep part of the Law.
