# James 2 (LSB)

> ### The Sin of Favoritism
> **James 2:1**   My brothers, do not hold your faith in our glorious Lord Jesus Christ with *an attitude of* personal favoritism. **2** For if a man comes into your assembly with a gold ring and dressed in bright clothes, and there also comes in a poor man in dirty clothes, **3** and you pay special attention to the one who is wearing the bright clothes, and say, “You sit here in a good place,” and you say to the poor man, “You stand over there, or sit down by my footstool,” **4** have you not made distinctions among yourselves, and become judges with evil thoughts? 

James referred to hypocrites at the end of the previous chapter by specifically yet briefly mentioning one of their traits, and now continues with another trait which he dives deeper into. 
The sin of partiality is just like the comparison of the tongue with the rudder, though it seems small, it has an enormous effect. Partiality shows the inner heart of a person. If someone prioritizes another person because of his wealth or status, it only shows the amount of respect he has towards this wealth or status, and the lack of respect he has towards the person himself. It also shows his desire to have these things. 
Indeed when ministers in church treat important guests with an exaggerated amount of respect and reverence, it means they want to be treated this way as well. 
Now James is not saying of course that if one wants to be treated this way he should withhold himself from showing this to anyone. No, he is saying that one should not have these desires at all (v. 5). 

> **5** Listen, my beloved brothers: did not God choose the poor of this world *to be* rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom which He promised to those who love Him? 

There are some whom the Lord has chosen to have more faith in Him. Some might think this is unfair of God. They have more faith and therefore have accomplished more in life for the Kingdom. Of course these people are wrong. God judges with the right measuring stick (#todo/opzoeken/reference ) and always in a righteous way and according to the gifts he has given.
Aside from the category of people such as George Mueller and Hudson Taylor who accomplished great things through faith, there is also a category of people who have great faith but whose accomplishments are not very visible. This is the category of poor people. Their faith is greater than that of the average person, but their "accomplishments" are merely to bring enough food on the table every day, to trust in God when one loses his job and stay honest when one has run out of money. Most of the people that God has blessed with a great amount of faith belong to this group. Everyone wants to have more faith, to heal illnesses and to be relieved of one's problems, but few to none are willing to take on the life of **the poor man**. They do not realize, however, that the Israelites went out of Egypt and saw miracles every day. They had to walk miles through loose sands while laden with heavy burdens perhaps, but they saw the miracle of God's pillar of fire (Shekinah) heating them and the cloud covering them every day and night. For forty years they walked on sandals and not one sandal strap was broken in all these years. 
But in order to see this miracle, one had to walk in the desert for forty years. Analogously, no one wants to get sick in order to see God's healing power, and those who are already sick want to see it, but only to be relieved from it, not because they want to see His power.

> **6** But you have dishonored the poor man. Is it not the rich who oppress you and they themselves drag you into court? **7** Do they not blaspheme the good name by which you have been called?

It is a strange thing when people love that thing which caused them their poverty. In the movie *Slumdog Millionair* this phenomenon #questions/language/english/spelling was shown throughout the movie where a crime boss who drove with his expensive car through the slums, was respected by all, even though he was part of the reason why they were in the slums. One of the protagonists eventually even started working for this boss so he could be part of this gang. Not only this person, but people in general see the control and power these rich and mighty ones have over other people and they long for this themselves. Having this power, however, is not a beneficial thing but rather puts one in a position of daily temptation. We need to run from this temptation like Joseph did (Genesis  #todo/opzoeken/reference ). If Joseph had stayed in Potifar's house, even if he did not run away and did not sleep with his mistress, perhaps one day he would have succumbed to the temptation of Potifar's wife. Give away this power when possible and give away these riches when they are not needed. 

> **James 2:8**   If, however, you are fulfilling the ==royal law== according to the Scripture, “YOU SHALL LOVE YOUR NEIGHBOR AS YOURSELF,” you are doing well. **9** But if you show partiality, you are committing sin, being convicted by the law as transgressors. **10** For whoever keeps the whole law and yet stumbles in one *point*, he has become guilty of all.

James' point here is that no one is able to uphold the Law, which is summarized in the **royal law**. The sin of **partiality** is not even stated in the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20) but it is present in its *spirit*, for the last six out of the ten commandments are about loving one's **neighbor as yourself**. This point leads us to **being convicted by the law as transgressors**. His next point is that we then should **speak and act as those who are to judged**, and not as if we have not sinned (v. 12). 

> **11** For He who said, “DO NOT COMMIT ADULTERY,” also said, “DO NOT MURDER.” Now if you do not commit adultery, but murder, you have become a transgressor of the law. **12** So speak and so act as those who are to be judged by *the* law of freedom. 

Christ has set us free in His Law. This might sound contradictory to the statement that we are no longer under the Law of Moses, but it is not. We are indeed not under the Law when it comes to salvation. Our salvation is dependent on Jesus' work alone, which He completed at the cross and His Resurrection. Those who believe this and accept Jesus as their Savior and Lord are those who will be saved. 
Still, the Law of Moses is one of **freedom**. Every law that is different from the Law of Moses restricts man only more–even laws that remove restrictions, such as abortion laws that allow women not to kill, but to murder the baby in their womb. Every act in life has consequences, either to oneself or to one's neighbors. The more negative consequences, the more restrictions on freedom there is and the less freedom remains. The Law of Moses is the law that optimizes freedom for all in this world. 
Of course people argue that more freedom is possible, but in this world with imperfect people, unfortunately this is not. 

> **13** For judgment *will be* merciless to one who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment.

The seriousness of the sin *in question* #questions/language/english/spelling (i.e. this particular sin for which a person is judged) is only *part* of the reason this person is judged and condemned, for the **transgressor of the law** will be judged not only according to the seriousness of *this sin*, but also according to how *hypocritical* and how *merciful* one has been. Verse 12 therefore tells us, no, it rather *commands* us no to be hypocritical and thus **to speak and act as those who are to be judged**—for that is who we are—and speaking with this attitude is always with  a more humble attitude. Also, we must not forget that we are judged by the **law of freedom** (not by another law), for it is Christ Who has set us *free* and this freedom comes *through His **law***.

> ### Faith and Works
> **James 2:14**   What use is it, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but he has no works? Can that faith save him? **15** If a brother or sister is without clothing and in need of daily food, **16** and one of you says to them, “Go in peace, be warmed and be filled,” and yet you do not give them what is necessary for *their* body, what use is that? **17** Even so faith, if it has no works, is dead by itself.

Though this part is clear, as well as its implication, namely that one cannot say he has faith if he has no works, the practical implementation is difficult. Currently there is Romanian acquaintance of mine—a brother who works in construction but does not have a job due to a hernia, but also cannot find a suited job in his country—who regularly and, I believe, genuinely asks me for small amounts of money, just enough to buy his family groceries. Though there is nothing wrong with that in itself, there are a few objections and reasons for me not to give. 
1. Dependence on me, instead of on his local church. Though there is no church nearby him, there is one further away, maybe one or two hours by public transport. It is most likely orthodox and chances are slim the people there are willing to help him, but it the place where he *needs to* congregate. 
2. My own family whom I need to feed. At certain moment I am lacking myself and I need to prioritize those whom God has put me in charge to take care of.
3. Debts. This is a debt of such a size that I am not able to pay this off within a year or even two years. Is it wise to give to others in need, while I have my own duty to pay off these debts (Romans 13:8)? Not paying these will also influence my family's welfare, because it will induce interest and fines.

> **James 2:18**   But someone will say, “You have faith; and I have works. Show me your faith without the works, and I will show you my faith by my works.”

It is through the works that faith is shown. James is (perhaps) mocking the other person and challenging him to show his **faith without the works** which is impossible.

> **19** You believe that God is one. You do well; the demons also believe, and shudder. **20** But are you willing to recognize, you foolish fellow, that faith without works is useless?

The verb "believe" does not resemble the noun "faith" in its letters, but every English-speaking person would know these words are related together. As a matter of fact, in Greek these two words share the same root and this is very clear. James says this person might have *faith* (Greek: *pistis* (πίστις)), and that he *believes* (Greek: *pisteo* (πιστεύω)), but that demons also believe, an argument #questions/language/english/spelling Latin *ad ....*? Not only is it ridiculous, also the demons **shudder**, which means they are even *afraid* of God, but still they do not do any works. So one who does not do works, as for his works he can be considered just as bad as demons.  

> **21** Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered up Isaac his son on the altar? **22** You see that faith was working with his works, and as a result of the works, faith was perfected.

There is a theory that says that "faith" is translated incorrectly and should be a word that contains *faith and works*, just like the Dutch has the word "geloofsgehoorzaamheid". If that were true, these parts would be difficult to interpret, because faith and works are clearly separated here. Of course, it should be clear to the Christian that those are two sides of the same coin—they cannot be separated from each other. One's faith will cause him to act on. Suppose someone believes he will win the lottery—he is certain of it. Then he will definitely buy a ticket. The only reason he would not, is if he did not *want* to win it. Conversely, a person who acts, does so because he wants something done and also because he has at least *some* faith that it will happen. For example, even one who cleans the house, does so because he believes it will be clean, even if the house is a great mess that seems impossible to clean.

This people would hardly call "faith", but by definition it is. People tend to apply a certain standard to things before they give it a name. Beauty, e.g. is assigned to those with a minimum amount of it, and so it is with faith. All things can be **perfected**, however, and Abraham's faith was perfected here *through **his works***, through the ultimate test that tests one's faith. 
As a matter of fact, all traits of the character and of the gifts of the Holy Spirit (Galatians 5:22) are tested *and refined* through one's works, such as perseverance, righteousness, love, joy and peace. 

> **23** And the Scripture was fulfilled which says, “AND ABRAHAM BELIEVED GOD, AND IT WAS COUNTED TO HIM AS RIGHTEOUSNESS,” and he was called the friend of God. **24** You see that a man is justified by works and not by faith alone. **25** And in the same way, was not Rahab the harlot also justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way? **26** For just as the body without *the* spirit is dead, so also faith without works is dead.
