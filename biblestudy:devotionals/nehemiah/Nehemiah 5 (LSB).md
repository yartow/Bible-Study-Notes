### Each Usury Is Forsaken
> **Neh. 5:1**   Then athere was a great outcry of the people and of their wives against their bJewish brothers. 

One explanation for the description of three groups is that the need for food and the **great outcry** was among *almost everyone*. It is like when one explains the situation as "Some were eating dry bread, others were licking up the rests of their plates with their tongues, and there were even ones who picked up rests from the trash bins." It is clear then that the famine was widespread. 

The description is as if wives are not part of the people, however, this should be read as that not only them, who represented **the people** were having **a great outcry** but now even **their wives** as well because of the famine, who were usually silent and let their husbands take the word. This was, of course, because the usury was on an unprecedented scale, which was most definitely uncommon in Jewish history. 
The Israelites had been slaves during their stay in Egypt and oppressed many times and in many places in the period of the Judges. Even during Saul's reign the people were afraid of the Philistines. Later, during the kings many cities were in some way oppressed by the Assyrians, for example because their own king paid tributes of high value to their kings and perhaps then taxed the people to refill his treasury. Lastly and most recently, they were enslaved by the Babylonians and with a little bit more freedom the Persians as well.
Now the Israelites are not the only people who have been enslaved many times. Many Chinese peoples have been enslaved for thousands of years by the Huns and Mongols, but also by their own "kind". Even though the Chinese consist mainly of one kind of people—the Han Chinese—China #tags/country/china was divided among many kingdoms, just like Greece was, and they all conquered and enslaved each other to expand their kingdoms. In Africa, too, many African *tribes*[^1] were enslaved but it was only until the 18th (or 17th #todo/opzoeken) Century that they were enslaved by Western nations. Before the colonization African nations enslaved each other. #tags/history/slavery #tags/country/african
Now of course, genetically speaking the difference between one African nation or tribe and a neighboring one is just a big as between the Israelites and their neighbors, but the difference is that the Israelites worshipped their own god and had a culture that was very distinct from their neighboring countries, while African tribes worshipped the same gods as their neighboring tribes.  
So whereas in the rest of the world nations would enslave their own people, Israel had never done this or at least allowed this legally and the Bible explicitly forbade it, e.g. by specific laws for slaves and fellow Israelites, such as the lending of money to brothers, where the charging of interest was not allowed.

> **2** Now there were those who were saying, “We, *with* our sons and our daughters, are many; therefore let us aget grain that we may eat and live.” 

There were three groups of people, described here in three consecutive verses in order from richest to poorest. The fourth group is described in v.7, which are the nobles and the officials. The first group had to buy grain, while the second group had real estate to mortgage, but the third group was beyond that stage (perhaps because they had less real estate or because they had been borrowing for a longer time, for example since the time of Zerubbabel or Ezra) and had to even sell themselves and their children as slaves only to survive.

Yet behold, bwe are forcing our sons and our daughters to be slaves, and some of our daughters are forced into subjugation, and we have no power in our hands*to help*, and our fields and vineyards belong to others.”

Perhaps the people had **had no power in [their] hands to help** rebuild wall, because they had to obey their masters, or they had to work on their own fields, but could not even enjoy the profits thereof. For both of these reasons Nehemiah was right to be **very angry**. 

Amidst the increasing threats and increasing fatigue of the men, as well as losing half the workforce because of the former reason (Nehemiah 4:21) another problem arises, namely that of usury. Nehemiah is dealing with many problems at the same time. #tags/leadership 

The **subjugation** spoken of here probably refers to another kind of enslavement, perhaps as sex slaves or perhaps they were being forced to marry men, since specifically and only **daughters** are meant. 

> **Neh. 5:6**   Then I was very aangry when I had heard their outcry and these words. **7** I consulted within my own heart and contended with the nobles and the officials and said to them, “aYou are exacting usury, each from his brother!” Therefore, I held a great assembly against them. 

Nehemiah did not consult with others first but **consulted with [his] own heart**.  This was a personal meeting he, and he *alone*, had with the nobles in the first instance. Probably the nobles did not repent at first and **therefore**, only when the progress stalled, he held **a great assembly against them** (cf. Matthew 18:15-17). In v.8 Nehemiah is not alone anymore but speaks in the first person plural form and where he had brought it to a more public arena, namely the assembly, although it is not sure whether this was still behind closed doors with a small committee or more public than that. #tags/leadership

They are so proud that they think they are above the law and that they can use and abuse people in any way they want.

> **10** “And likewise I, my brothers, and my young men are lending them money and grain. 

If *young men* actually refers to servants or slaves (cf. Ruth 2:8), but in a Jewish sense, like one who sells himself to a master voluntarily just like anyone would accept a fixed contract at a company, then even those servants who do not have much money show their love for their brethren and their lovingkindness[^2]. This fact alone is a disgrace for the nobles and the officials. However, Nehemiah did not say this to shame them. He already shamed them in the previous paragraph and silenced them. That was his first purpose, to make them regret. What he was to say now, was meant to change their future ways, by showing the example *himself*, for he too lent money to his poor brothers (aside from the fact that he did not even use money from the people's taxes to the king but paid everything out of his own pocket (it is not sure if the people knew about this and could have possibly praised him for this)) and the example of his poor servants. 
So the difference between the two arguments in these verses is that his first argument does not contain a clause of what they *should* do, while his second argument does, and is therefore ab exemplary argument.

However, Nehemiah was not allowed by the Law of God to do these things without good reason. The people *had taken* money to buy grain. Even if the nobles were to give back all of the people's property, the people still had to pay back all of the money they had borrowed, albeit without interest.

### Nehemiah as Governor
> **Neh. 5:14**   Moreover, from the day that I was put in command to be their governor in the land of Judah, from athe twentieth year to the bthirty-second year of King Artaxerxes, *for* twelve years, neither I nor my 1relatives have eaten the governor’s food*allowance*. 

Nehemiah now explains that he did not only order the nobles and officials to not take more from the people than what was needed, but that he too held by this law, even though he was a governor and received a **governor's food allowance**. 

> **15** But the former governors who were before me 1laid heavy burdens on the people and took from them bread and wine besides forty 2shekels of silver; even their young men exerted*their* power over the people. But I did not do so abecause of the fear of God. **16** I also took hold of the work on this wall; we did not buy any land, and all my young men were gathered there for the work.

The **young men**, which were slaves or servants, would rise in power because of the governor's power. In the same way Joseph and Daniel were also—literally and figuratively—"young men", for they were servants and former slaves (Daniel was never named as such though) but wielded almost the same power as their masters.

It could be that he received these forty **shekels of silver** as payment for his work—his salary so to speak—and that he paid the food from his own salary. Or it could be that these forty shekels were his **food allowance**. Either way, he did not use that to which he had a right. 
**Example.** I know of the example of a director who moved to another company and not only got a very high salary, but also a very luxurious lease car. Now the car is considered as a gift by the tax revenue service #questions/language/english/spelling IRS? but the tax was even paid for by the company. This is like Nehemiah's situation. He probably received a high salary as a governor, and he could enjoy the more abundant feast meals (every ten days) which were paid for by the government. But aside from this, he also had a food allowance. The extra bread and wine his predecessors took from the villagers was illegal of course. Nehemiah decided, however, not to take *any* of this, just like David refused to accept the land of the threshing floor for the temple #todo/opzoeken/reference  and Abraham refused to accept the grave for his family for free.

> **17** Moreover, a*there were* at my table 150 Jews and officials, besides those who came to us from the nations that were around us. **18** Now athat which was prepared for each day was one ox*and* six choice sheep, also birds were prepared for me; and once in ten days all sorts of wine*were furnished* in abundance. Yet for all this bI did not require the governor’s food*allowance*, because the slavery was heavy on this people. **19** aRemember me, O my God, for good, *according to* all that I have done for this people.

These **150 Jews and officials** were probably fixed staff while the other people **from the nations** were temporary visiting officials and diplomats. 
It is not certain what the amount of animals and wine where nor whether this is much or not, but obviously Nehemiah mentions this to show that he did not abuse his power as governor. He also shows transparency by mentioning that he even received wine and that **in abundance**, but he paid everything from his own pocket. 

As Christians we should have the same generosity and attitude as Nehemiah concerning the stewardship of our money. Thriftiness is a virtue in our own *personal* realm, concerning spending and saving, but *generosity* is in the realm of our fellowmen. We should not take what we do not need, when others are in need, and we should give generously to others, when we are not in need. 
We see this same pattern throughout the New Testament, in Jesus' teachings and in that of His followers, so it is not a coincidence that the Old Testament men of God adhere to the same principle (because we know that Jesus is also the author of the Old Testament). Jesus said for instance that "it is more blessed to give than to receive" (Acts 20:35) and that we need to love one another more than we love ourselves (John 13:34).
