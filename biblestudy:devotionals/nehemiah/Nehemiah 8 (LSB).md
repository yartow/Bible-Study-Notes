# Nehemiah 8 (LSB)
> ### Ezra Reads the Book of the Law
> **Neh. 8:1**   And all the people gathered as one man at the square which was in front of the Water Gate, and they said to Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses which Yahweh had commanded to Israel. **2** Then Ezra the priest brought the law before the assembly of men, women, and all who *could* understand when listening, on the first day of the seventh month. **3** And he read from it before the square which was in front of the Water Gate from early morning until midday, in the presence of men and women, those who could understand; and all the ears of the people were attentive to the book of the law.

This was not a mere reading but a praise and an instruction or study of the Bible (vv.6,8) and perhaps even a worship with music. Realize that this was a very long time, considering the fact that all of this took place in the heat of the sun, even though it was before the sun rose to its highest point, but temperatures still got to at least 25 degrees before noon. Secondly, we see that the people were standing all the time (v.7).

> **4** Ezra the scribe stood on a wooden podium which they had made for the purpose.

It was not common that such a platform was used. Jesus sat when He taught #todo/opzoeken/reference  and in synagogues— these days and perhaps in the past as well, and as far as I know—the rabbi taught from a pulpit that was on the same level as the congregation, not elevated as in Catholic and in some Protestant churches. The Catholic Church may have copied this from the Romans.

> And beside him stood Mattithiah, Shema, Anaiah, Uriah, Hilkiah, and Maaseiah on his right hand; and Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, Hashum, Hashbaddanah, Zechariah, *and* Meshullam on his left hand.

**Meshullam.** It is not certain which Meshullam this is, but most likely the chief man mentioned in Ezra 8:16, the son of Berechiah. See also notes on #todo/opzoeken/reference The fact he was standing there does not indicate he was a man of honor in the eyes of God, but that he was *held* in high esteem by the people, so much so that nobles were sworn by oath to the father-in-law (Tobiah) of the one married to his daughter (Nehemiah 6:18). 


> **5** And Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people for he was above all the people; and when he opened it, all the people stood up. **6** Then Ezra blessed Yahweh the great God. And all the people answered, “Amen, Amen!” while lifting up their hands; then they bowed low and worshiped Yahweh with *their* faces to the ground.

The worship was with their faces to the ground. What a difference this is with today's worship where people raise their hands and faces to heaven. We still see this difference clearly between Reformed Christians where they do not have this practice (for example during music) and the more progressive Christians who do. 

> **7** Also Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah, Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, Pelaiah, the Levites, were providing understanding of the law to the people while the people *stood* in their place.

The Levites had more roles than simply the caretaking of the Temple. They were instructed and paid in order to learn. Some ultra-orthodox Jews in Israel nowadays (perhaps only the rabbis), that is those with the beards and black clothes, also have some kind of tax exemption status or receive some kind of welfare in order to study and teach from the Old Testament and the commentaries #todo/opzoeken hoe heten die ook alweer? 
What would it be like if a whole tribe was set apart to do these things? The Christian "people" are not split into different tribes with different blessings. Judah was blessed because from their tribe the kings would come and also the Messiah, the Levites had the Levites and the Priests, and other tribes had other blessings although not all of these are that specific or clear. But Christians get all the blessings and each of us is a priest himself and therefore must sanctify himself, that is set himself apart for God (see 1 Peter 2:5-9). 
Is there not the problem then that if everyone is responsible no one acts responsible? Who will teach then? The answer is that in the first place God has given some the gift of teaching or preaching, and those will be in charge of the teaching. However, the difference between Christians and Jews is that anyone could become a teacher, when God speaks through that person. The Essenes and Pharisees and scribes (who were often Pharisees) for example were also teaching, which was allowed and not only meant for the Levites to do. But the Levites did feel the *responsibility* to teach. Christians, on the other hand, must *all* feel this responsibility to teach and next to that also have the right to offer sacrifices that are acceptable in God's eyes. The offers are, however, not material but those of the heart. 

> **8** They read from the book, from the law of God, explaining and giving insight, and they provided understanding of the reading.



> ### This Day Is Holy
> **Neh. 8:9**   Then Nehemiah, who was the governor, and Ezra the priest *and* scribe, and the Levites, who provided the people with understanding, said to all the people, “This day is holy to Yahweh your God; do not mourn or weep.” For all the people were weeping when they heard the words of the law. **10** Then he said to them, “Go, eat of the fat, drink of the sweet, and send portions to him who has nothing prepared; for this day is holy to our Lord. Do not be grieved, for the joy of Yahweh is your strength.”

On these holy days when we celebrate things, we should not forget those who have **nothing prepared**. The best meals are those we can share with others. 

> **11** So the Levites quieted all the people, saying, “Be still, for the day is holy; do not be grieved.” **12** Then all the people went away to eat, to drink, to send portions, and to celebrate with great gladness, because they understood the words which had been made known to them.

> ### Feast of Booths Restored
> **Neh. 8:13**   Then on the second day the heads of fathers’ *households* of all the people, the priests and the Levites were gathered to Ezra the scribe that they might gain insight into the words of the law. **14** They found written in the law how Yahweh had commanded by the hand of Moses that the sons of Israel should live in booths during the feast of the seventh month, **15** and that they should make the report heard and make a proclamation *of it* pass throughout all their cities and in Jerusalem, saying, “Go out to the hills, and bring olive branches and wild olive branches, myrtle branches, palm branches, and branches of *other* leafy trees, to make booths, as it is written.” **16** So the people went out and brought *them* and made booths for themselves, each on his roof, and in their courts and in the courts of the house of God, and in the square at the Water Gate and in the square at the Gate of Ephraim. **17** The entire assembly of those who had returned from the captivity made booths and lived in the booths. The sons of Israel had indeed not done so from the days of Joshua the son of Nun to that day. And there was exceedingly great gladness. **18** And he read from the book of the law of God daily, from the first day to the last day. And they celebrated the feast seven days, and on the eighth day *there was* a solemn assembly according to the *legal* judgment.
