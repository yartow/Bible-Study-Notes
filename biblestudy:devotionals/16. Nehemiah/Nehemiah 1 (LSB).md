# Nehemiah 1 (LSB)
> **Neh. 1:1**   The words of aNehemiah the son of Hacaliah.

### Nehemiah Prays for the Sons of Israel
>   Now it happened in bthe month Chislev, c*in* the twentieth year—and I was in dSusa the 1capitol— **2** that aHanani, one of my brothers, and 1some men from Judah came; and I asked them concerning the Jews who had escaped and remain from the captivity, and about Jerusalem. **3** They said to me, “The remnant there in the aprovince who remain from the captivity are in great calamity and breproach, and cthe wall of Jerusalem is broken down and dits gates are burned with fire.”

Why would Nehemiah speak of **escaped** Jews? Did the groups of the First and Second Return from the Exile not freely and voluntarily migrate to Israel? It seems as if he is speaking of the small group who remained in Israel after the three Exiles to Babylon. However, v.3 then speaks of those **who remain from the captivity** which sound much like the latter group plus the first two groups who had returned. 
What they say next is that this group is **in great calamity and reproach**, the latter meaning either a reproach from God for their sin—and much sin was going on at that time, even though Ezra was leading the group there—or a reproach by the people of the land, as Nehemiah would experience himself a few months later. 
Note that what is written in the chapters on the events of Ezra (chapters 7-10) comprise exactly one year only, starting from the first of the first month of the seventh year of the reign of Artaxerxes. What happened in the twelve years that we do not know, but we do know that even in the first year people were already sinning and giving their sons and daughters in marriage to pagans (see notes on [[Ezra 10 (LSB)]] v.44). Also, it seems quite clear from Nehemiah 8 that the people did not know the Law. This sounds strange, for a scribe and most likely (high) priest, Ezra, had been appointed as their leader during those years. On the other hand, Ezra was so busy with building up society and possibly warding off enemies as well. #todo/opzoeken Nothing specific about Ezra's enemies is written here, but it is very likely that they were present. Ezra 4:4-5 writes that these were present *until* the days of Darius, which was the predecessor of Artaxerxes. Continuing in v.7 of that same chapter is the opposition against Nehemiah rebuilding the city walls. Nothing is said of any opposition against Ezra, perhaps because what he built was relatively smaller. However, the impact Ezra had must have been bigger, because he commanded a sizable (#todo/opzoeken ) part of the population to put away their wives and children. This must have caused some opposition to say the least. 

> **Neh. 1:4**   Now it happened that when I heard these words, aI sat down and wept and mourned for days; and I was fasting and praying before bthe God of heaven. 

The word **beseech** is used mostly in the Bible by those who were bold enough to seek the Lord. It is also translated as "o [God]" sometimes. In every reference this boldness comes from one's innocence, just like Nehemiah asks God in Nehemiah 13 to remember him, or the crew on which ship Jonah resided (Jonah 1:14) who asked God to forgive them preemptively #questions/language/english/spelling or: in advance, for throwing Jonah off their ship. There is one exception, and that is when Joseph's brothers besought him (Genesis 50:17) they knew very well they were innocent, and still they dared to beseech him, for one reason only, because they asked for forgiveness. 
No one is ever innocent or clean of sins, but if anyone ever is, it is because he has been *cleansed* of his sins. This is why we can approach God now, because we know our status in Christ. Our status is that we are *hidden with Christ in God* (Colossians 3:3). When God hears us, He sees Christ, for we are hidden with Him. 

> **5** I said, “I beseech You, O 1Yahweh, the God of heaven, athe great and fearsome God, bwho keeps the covenant and lovingkindness for those who love Him and keep His commandments, 

Does God keep the covenant only for those who love Him and keep His commandments? We know that two covenants were made with Abraham. Ezra (who wrote this) does not specify which covenant this concerns, but he also he did not add the word "only", although that could be implied from it intuitively. One thing is for sure, however, **for those who love him and keep his commandments**, God certainly keeps His covenant. Those who do not are at risk of God forsaking them. Why should anyone take this risk?

> **6** alet Your ear now be attentive and Your eyes open to hear the prayer of Your slave which I am praying before You today, day and night, on behalf of the sons of Israel Your slaves, bconfessing the sins of the sons of Israel which we have sinned against You; cI and my father’s house have sinned. 

Note the number of dimensions in which Nehemiah prays to deepen his request and his affliction. 
* Nehemiah asks for God's **ear [...] and eyes to hear [his] prayer**
* he calls himself **[God's] slave**
* he prays **days and night**
* he does not pray for himself, but for **the sons of Israel**, who are also **[His] slaves**
* he confesses **the sins of the sons of Israel**
* he confesses that they **have sinned against [*God*]**
* he confesses his own sin and that of his **father's house**.

Of course Nehemiah has sinned, but not particularly in this respect. We was not even born when the city was put on fire by Babylon, but he acknowledges that it is a cumulative sin of the whole people. Regardless of one's innocence, people in the (Near) Middle-Eastern community know that the demise and punishment of a people applies to every individual, but so do rewards.

> **7** “aWe have worked*in* utter destruction against You and have not kept the commandments, nor the statutes, nor the judgments bwhich You commanded Your 1servant Moses. 

Again, Nehemiah goes in-depth and admits the depth of their sin. Three things he mentions here, the first two being the Law of Moses and everything else written in the Bible, but what does Nehemiah mean by not having **kept [...] the judgments**? This is the first time I have read such an expression. It means that the Israelites were not only to *hold* the **commandments** themselves, but also to *execute judgment* on the people. This they had obviously not done with all the commandments that the Israelites had broken. 

This verse refers to [[Deuteronomy 30 (LSB)/Yahweh Will Take You Back|Deuteronomy 30:10,15]] (among others #todo/opzoeken/reference ) which has these exact same three words—commandment, statutes and judgments. The difference between the commandments and **statutes** has not been explained explicitly in the Bible #todo/opzoeken , but it could be the difference between what is explained in Deuteronomy 5-12 which start with the Ten Commands, and the section of Deuteronomy 12-26 which start with a description of the statutes, but see also [[Deuteronomy 12 (LSB)/Yahweh’s Chosen Place of Worship|notes there]].
