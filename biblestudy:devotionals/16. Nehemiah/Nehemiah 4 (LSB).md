# Nehemiah 4 (LSB)

> ### Sanballat and Tobiah Mock the Jews’ Work
> **Neh. 4:1**   Now it happened that when aSanballat heard that we were rebuilding the wall, he became angry and very vexed and mocked the Jews.

Note that there is a progress in the restoration of the walls and at the same time in the anger and hatred of Sanballat and Tobiah as well. Here they only **heard that [the Jews] were rebuilding the wall**, and so they **became angry and very vexed and mocked the Jews**. In vv.6-7 the wall was already to half its height and the breaches began to be closed, and they became *very angry* and started to *join together*, *fight* against Jerusalem and *cause disturbance*. In Nehemiah 6 the wall was rebuilt and no breach remained in it, and Nehemiah's enemies began a plan to harm him, and when that did not work, they slandered him and accused him of rebellion against the king and even hired fellow Jews and prophets (Nehemiah 6:10-14).
Note also that the number of enemies is increasing with every event. Whereas in the beginning it was only Sanballat with Tobiah **near him** (v.3), they were joined in later events by the Arabs, Ashdodites, Ammonites and then the rest of their enemies. 

> **2** He spoke in the presence of his brothers and athe 1wealthy *men* of Samaria and said, “What are these feeble Jews doing? Are they going to restore *it* for themselves? Can they offer sacrifices? Can they complete *it* in a day? Nehemiah's adversaries hated the Jews from the beginning. The reason for this is still not clear, but they mocked the Jews and stirred up the **wealthy men of Samaria** (translated as "army in the ESV). This is the beginning of the planting of a false image of the Jews in the heads of the people. It is the same thing the German Nazis did before the Second World War #tags/history/war/world_two , through mocking caricatures in newspapers and other media of the Jews' supposed lies, cheats, and of their wealth and greed in an impoverished economy, so that the people would be imbued an inherent hatred for the Jews, which would be the ultimate soil for the start of the persecution and eradication of Jews. 
Like Hitler #tags/people#, Sanballat and Tobiah were perhaps genius strategists and had planned all this from the beginning. We also see that Tobiah will work his way up and even get a room inside the temple as his living quarters—a feat that has never been done before. 

Despite the fact that the Jews knew Sanballat and Tobiah were despicable, a few high ranking Jews still let their daughters marry Tobiah and his son (Nehemiah 6:18) or let their son marry Sanballat's daughter (Nehemiah 13:28). Now marrying a bad person's child is not necessarily a sin for even Tobiah's son could have been a God-fearing man—although in this case all were Gentiles—.
It could also be that my whole interpretation on these high ranking persons was wrong. In my notes on Nehemiah and Ezra 10 #todo/opzoeken/reference I assumed they were good and that their advice was not selfish. If we assume both of these men are bad, then the whole interpretation about the advice given to Ezra (Ezra 10) concerning the sending away of the foreign women would be wrong. Moreover, it would even be hypocritical because he himself had married a foreign woman. 

Another thing to note here is that those who side on Sanballat and Tobiah's side also side with their views on the wall. They all believe the wall should not be built, they do not care about the restoration of their city and the protection from invaders. They do not see their responsibility in maintain God's property and gift to their people. Also, it clearly shows that they do not care about their people because they allow them to be mocked and jeered at. 

> **Neh. 4:4**   aHear, O our God, for we are despised! bReturn their reproach on their own heads and give them up for plunder in a land of captivity. **5** Do not 1aforgive their iniquity and let not their sin be blotted out before You, for they have 2vexed the builders.

Nehemiah is portrayed by David Pawson as one who is aggressive and expressive in his emotions, as compared to Ezra (see notes on [[Nehemiah 13 (LSB)/You Shall Not Marry Foreigners|Nehemiah 13:25]]) but his self-constraint is on a higher level than most of us. Given the fact that the Jews had had so many chances and the plethora of blessings and miracles God had given them, anyone would have raged and that in an even more uncontrolled way than Nehemiah did. 

The request Nehemiah makes here of God is a genuine call for help and not an expression of rage. As governor Nehemiah was in a position of power and could have had everyone arrested who opposed him and hence indirectly opposed the King as well, especially when they tried to harm him. He chose not to exercise his authority and not even to threat with this. 
Though there are many leaders in the Bible, for good reasons Nehemiah is most often #todo/opzoeken  chosen as the exemplary leader and used in seminars and courses on leadership, such as the #todo/opzoeken  iets met John... even though other leaders faced hardships in the same way or even worse than he, such as Jeremiah who was scolded, persecuted and eventually thrown in an underground prison, or Moses who led a group over two million people, who did not know the Law, in the worst territory ever, namely in the desert. 
It is not that Nehemiah *is* the best leader, but perhaps it is that his leadership has been documented best.

> **Neh. 4:7**   1Now it happened that when Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites, and the Ashdodites heard that the 2repair of the walls of Jerusalem went on, *and* that the *places* broken down began to be closed, they were very angry. **8** All of them ajoined together to come *and* fight against Jerusalem and to cause a disturbance in it. **9** But we prayed to our God, and because of them we astood a guard against them day and night.

Again, we see Nehemiah going for the spiritual way. Instead of using his physical means to go against his adversaries, he knows what wisdom is, he realizes his battle is a spiritual one and therefore only the Spirit can resolve in this. 
He also does not forget his duties, which is to stand guard. God will protect, but we have our responsibilities as well. This should not be interpreted as that things come about through *our* work, and it also not the case that we are helping God, for God does not need man's help. No, it is God's work and His plan and He wants to establish His plan through us. All He asks of us is that we *allow* ourselves to be used for His purpose. 
Now the word *allow* might let some think that they have worked, but there are different kinds of actions, one that deserve merit and those that do not. It is only the former category that can be deemed as *work*, and *allowing* and *receiving* are not in that category. 
It is the same discussion with the same answer concerning the problem of whether the Christian's salvation is obtained by faith or by works plus faith. The answer is the first one, of course, but to be more exact, it is *faith plus acceptance or reception*, and acceptance and reception both are not work. So we *do* something indeed, but it is not work. In theological terms, salvation is obtained by synergy, because it is God and the believer who *act* together (yet only God works) and not monorgy where the only actor is God. #tags/bible/calvinism/works

> **Neh. 4:11**   Our adversaries said, “They will not know or see until we come among them, kill them, and put a stop to the work.”

It is not clear whether this is cited by Judah or said by Nehemiah. Either way, it was a real threat and confirmed by the Jews who lived near the adversaries. 

> **12** Now it happened when the Jews who lived near them came and said to us ten times, “1They will come up against us from every place where you may turn,” **13** that I had *men* stand in the lowest parts of the space behind the wall, the 1exposed places, and I ahad the people stand by families with their swords, spears, and bows. **14** Then I saw *their fear*. And I arose and said to the nobles, the officials, and the rest of the people: “aDo not fear them; remember the Lord who is great and fearsome, and bfight for your brothers, your sons, your daughters, your wives, and your houses.”

On top of a lack of physical strength in v.10 where the **strength ... is failing**, they also face social oppression through the **adversaries**' threats to **kill them** at any time. This threat was confirmed by the Jews—I am assuming they meant well—but **their fear** overcame them. So now they were under physical, social and mental pressure. God has to go all the way before people are willing to surrender to Him. Even though the people humbly acknowledged they were **unable to rebuild the wall** this was merely the *physical* part they acknowledged. This is just like someone acknowledging he does not have the money to finish a project or the physical strength and mental prowess. Although that is an acknowledgement of one's shortcomings or weakness, it is far from a personal and full-fledged humbling of oneself, let alone an affliction, which is what the Lord requires #todo/opzoeken or is pleased with. 

Nehemiah addressed the second problem, which is the same as the of v.7, by letting the men stand **guard against them day and night**. This is perhaps what tired them. After the more explicit threats of v.10 Nehemiah imposed more actions and raised the security level even higher. Now the men had to guard even **the lowest parts of the space behind the wall**. I am not sure what this exactly means, but either there was no guard at all before v.7 or there was limited security, such as during the night, when no was working. Now, however, guards were even placed the bottom of the wall, while people were working on the top.
This is better understood when taking a look at the current-day walls of Jerusalem, which are not much different than those of Nehemiah. The city has expanded and perhaps some of the walls have been destroyed and built up elsewhere, but overall the structure and height and breadth would have been the same. The walls were on average [twelve meters high](https://www.stgeorgehoteljerusalem.com/en/the-walls-of-jerusalem) and most likely a scaffolding of some type or roped structure #questions/language/english/spelling zo'n ding waarmee glazen worden gewassen in gebouwen allowed the men to work on the top half of the walls since the bottom half was already finished (v.6). If everyone was working at the top, this of course meant that intruders could easily enter and sabotage the work, hence—I would suppose—there were no guards placed on the inside of the building, where "the lowest parts" probably meant ground level. 
Nehemiah also addressed the third problem of fear and encouraged them to do two things (1) **remember the Lord** and (2) **fight for your [family and possession]**. It is the *focus* of any action which gives us the strength and endurance to persist. #tags/leadership
**Example.** *The Daniel Plan* is a Christian book #tags/books/health and health program, designed by Rick Warren #tags/people/christian, Daniel Amen and Mark Hyman, which goes further than the conventional factors for losing weight and other health-related objectives. Their claim is that most programs do not work because these forget about three other factors—namely *friends, focus and faith*—aside from the two known ones, *food* and *fitness*. Without the right focus—which should be on God—one will lose his motivation or not have the right motivation. If one focuses on losing weight so he can wear those trousers, as soon as he fits those trousers, his focus is gone. Even a focus on living healthy is not right way to live. One might focus too much on living healthy and not have any focus on the costs of time and money, and forget about his focus on the mission or his church ministry.

Those two focuses are the same as that of the Ten Commands and subsequently Jesus' summary thereof to love the Lord and one's fellowmen. Here, however, it is not only loving the Lord, but remembering how **great and fearsome** He is, and it is not merely loving one's family—for they already did—but remember how much they cared for them and realizing it was *them* they were fighting for. For this reason as well, Nehemiah **had the people stand by families with their [weapons]**, so that the builders would not be burdened with the care for their family. 
The same principle is used throughout the rest of the Bible. Nehemiah did not want the Levites to be burdened with the care for their families and work in the temple, so the people had to provide for them (Nehemiah 10:37-38, 11:20, 12:45), which they did not do (Nehemiah 13:10). Paul as well instructed the Church (of Corinth, amongst others) to pay the wages of workers of the Gospel (1 Corinthians 9:9, 1 Timothy 5:18). Again, there needs to be *focus*, and one cannot focus on one thing while worrying about another thing. #church/materials/sermon
This is the lesson for every Christian. The lesson for the leader here is that if he wants his people to obtain his objective, he needs to provide them with the conditions that *allow* them to focus. 

> ### Doing the Work and Holding a Weapon
> **Neh. 4:15**   Now it happened that when our enemies heard that it was known to us, and that aGod had thwarted their counsel, then all of us returned to the wall, each one to his work. **16** And it happened that from that day on, half of my young men carried on the work while half of them took hold of the spears, the shields, the bows, and the breastplates; and the commanders *were* behind the whole house of Judah. 


The security seems to be excessive and a great sacrifice of the progress on the rebuilding of the walls, for now Nehemiah would lose half of his labor force and even more, for the laborers were carrying armor themselves as well. It seems especially costly because no attacks have occurred since then and the enemies' advantage of surprise tde was already gone. However, it is exactly *because* of this *visible and excessive* security that the enemies did not come and did not try to disrupt the Jews again. They were in fact discouraged by everything they saw. 
Whereas the Jews were discouraged by the threats and news of planned attacks and physical fatigue and problems with construction, Nehemiah knew he could use the same tactic to discourage his enemies. He could show them that the Jews were aware of those threats and that these had no effect on them, that the enemies' planned attacks would tde stuiteren op, their well-prepared men and security guards and construction continued as usual, the men being even more encouraged now that they knew whom (or Whom) they were fighting for. 
This is also a way of psychological warfare, which God has used before when [[Gideon]] attacked 18.000 Philistines #todo/opzoeken/reference with a group of only 300 people. He scared them when they were half-asleep, in the dark, with a lot sound by banging on pots and crashing them, suddenly creating bright lights through unhiding the covered lamps, and through the simultaneous shouts (and trumpets? tdd) making their enemies think as if they were with many. 
God does not shy away from using those tactics even though He could have destroyed His enemies with one word or command to His angels. However, He grants us, His people, to share in the victory, not to share in the honor—for all honor and glory are due Him—but to share in *seeing and partaking* in His glory.
To *see* glory is almost to partake in it. Everyone who shared in the fight with the Philistines could have boasted that he had fought alongside Gideon and that God turned the enemies into into a frenzy #todo/opzoeken and killing spree amongst themselves. But everyone would also know that they themselves did not do anything, they hardly #todo/opzoeken  killed anyone, they did not come up with the plan nor did they exhaust themselves. It was God Who did all the work. They saw God's glory and that alone was sufficient for them to *feel* (or partake) in this glory, for everyone of God enjoys to see His glory. Who would not enjoy that feeling of seeing their hero in real life? In this case it is the hero who enjoys the attention of his fans, but his fans enjoy the presence or sight of their heroes. 

> **17** Those who were rebuilding the wall and those who carried burdens took *their* load with one hand doing the work and the other holding a weapon. **18** As for the builders, each *wore* his sword girded at his side as he built, while 1the trumpeter *stood* near me. 


The effort and cost of preparing for the worst is very small compared to the outcome it has when the worst actually happens. In all areas of life, people take precautions through the purchase of insurances, following trainings on First Aid and the installations and periodic checks of defibrillators (AEDs, actually) and fire extinguishers. In most cases these are never used. My own colleague is a reserve tdd fireman who has been carrying a beeper tdd with him for twenty years, and a few months ago was the first time he was called for service—and that "only" because a horse had fallen into a greppel tde. 
Still, whenever the worst happens this could cost the lives of people. Even if it is only one life that is saved, all of the aforementioned precautions are worth it, because life is immeasurably worth more than everything of this world, or in other words, money.
In this specific case, if the enemies were to attack and Nehemiah would not have his trumpeter with him, all of the work—the work on the walls, but also the migration from Persia to Jerusalem, for many Jews most likely would have been killed in the ensuing attack—would have been for nothing.

The **young man** is synonymous to the servant some have.
