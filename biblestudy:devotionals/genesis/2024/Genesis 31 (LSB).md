# Genesis 31 (LSB)
> ### Jacob Leaves While Laban is Gone
> **Gen. 31:1**   Then [^1]Jacob heard the words of Laban’s sons, saying, “Jacob has taken away all that belonged to our father, and from what belonged to our father he has made all this [^2]wealth.” **[^2]** And Jacob saw the face of Laban, and behold, it was not *friendly* toward him as formerly. **[^3]** Then Yahweh said to Jacob, “[^a]Return to the land of your fathers and to your kin, and [^b]I will be with you.” **[^4]** So Jacob sent and called Rachel and Leah to his flock in the field, **[^5]** and he said to them, “[^a]I see your father’s face, that it is not *friendly* toward me as formerly, but [^b]the God of my father has been with me. **[^6]** “[^a]You also know that I have served your father with all my power. **[^7]** “Yet your father has [^a]cheated me and [^b]changed my wages ten times; however, [^c]God did not allow him to harm me. **[^8]** “If [^a]he spoke thus, ‘The speckled shall be your wages,’ then all the flock bore speckled; and if he spoke thus, ‘The striped shall be your wages,’ then all the flock bore striped. **[^9]** “Thus God has [^a]delivered your father’s livestock and given *them* to me. **[^10]** “Now it happened at the time when the flock were [^1]mating that I lifted up my eyes and saw in a dream, and behold, the male goats which were [^2]mating *were* striped, speckled, and mottled. **[^11]** “Then [^a]the angel of God said to me in the dream, ‘Jacob,’ and I said, ‘Here I am.’ **[^12]** “He said, ‘Lift up now your eyes and see *that* all the male goats which are [^1]mating are striped, speckled, and mottled; for [^a]I have seen all that Laban has been doing to you. **[^13]** ‘I am [^a]the God *of* Bethel, where you [^b]anointed a pillar, where you made a vow to Me; now arise, [^1]leave this land, and [^c]return to the land of your kin.’” **[^14]** Then Rachel and Leah said to him, “Do we still have any portion or inheritance in our father’s house? **[^15]** “Are we not counted by him as foreigners? For [^a]he has sold us and has also [^1]entirely consumed [^2]our purchase price. **[^16]** “Surely all the riches which God has delivered *over to us* from our father belong to us and our children; now then, do whatever God has said to you.”

> ### Laban Pursues Jacob
> **Gen. 31:22**   Then it was told to Laban on the third day that Jacob had fled; **[^23]** so he took his [^1]relatives with him and pursued him *a distance of* seven days’ journey, and he overtook him in the hill country of Gilead. **[^24]** [^a]And God came to Laban the Aramean in a [^b]dream of the night and said to him, “[^1c]Beware lest you speak to Jacob either good or bad.”

Laban was not do anything to Jacob. Now there seems to be no wrong in doing a good thing, but it is wrong when the good thing is done to persuade someone to do the wrong thing. If Laban had offered on the spot to give Jacob his own house and his actual earned wages and all kinds of other promises, it would still be wrong, for Jacob was meant to go back to Canaan and Laban was not supposed to stop him. (cf. Genesis 24:50) [[Genesis 24 (LSB)]]

> **Gen. 31:25**   So Laban caught up with Jacob. Now Jacob had pitched his tent in the hill country, and Laban with his [^1]relatives camped in the hill country of Gilead. **[^26]** Then Laban said to Jacob, “What have you done [^1]by deceiving me and carrying away my daughters like captives of the sword? **[^27]** “Why did you flee secretly and [^1]deceive me and not tell me—so that I might have sent you away with gladness and with songs, with [^a]tambourine and with [^b]lyre— **[^28]** and not allow me [^a]to kiss my sons and my daughters? Now you have acted foolishly. **[^29]** “It is in my hand to do evil against you, but [^a]the God of your father spoke to me last night, saying, ‘[^1b]Beware of speaking either good or evil to Jacob.’ **[^30]** “So now you have indeed gone away because you longed greatly for your father’s house; *but* why did you steal [^a]my gods?” **[^31]** Then Jacob answered and said to Laban, “Because I was afraid, because I said, ‘Lest you take your daughters from me by force.’ **[^32]** “[^a]The one with whom you find your gods shall not live; in the presence of our [^1]relatives recognize what is yours [^2]among my belongings and take *it* for yourself.” But Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen them.

> **Gen. 31:33**   So Laban went into Jacob’s tent and into Leah’s tent and into the tent of the two maidservants, but he did not find *them*. Then he went out of Leah’s tent and entered Rachel’s tent. **[^34]** Now Rachel had taken the [^1]household idols and put them in the camel’s saddle, and she sat on them. And Laban felt through all the tent but did not find *them*. **[^35]** And she said to her father, “Let not my lord be angry that I cannot [^a]rise before you, for the manner of women is upon me.” So he searched but did not find the [^1b]household idols.

> **Gen. 31:36**   Then Jacob became angry and contended with Laban; and Jacob answered and said to Laban, “What is my transgression? What is my sin that you have hotly pursued me? **[^37]** “Though you have felt through all my goods, what have you found of all your household goods? Place *it* here before my [^1]relatives and your [^2]relatives, that they may decide between us two. **[^38]** “These twenty years I *have been* with you; your ewes and your female goats have not miscarried, nor have I eaten the rams of your flocks. **[^39]** “That which was torn *of beasts* I did not bring to you; I bore the loss of it myself. You required it of my hand *whether* stolen by day or stolen by night. **[^40]** “*Thus* I was: by day the [^1]heat consumed me and the frost by night, and my sleep fled from my eyes. **[^41]** “These twenty years I have been in your house; [^a]I served you fourteen years for your two daughters and six years for your flock, and you [^b]changed my wages ten times. **[^42]** “If [^a]the God of my father, the God of Abraham, and the dread of Isaac, had not been for me, surely now you would have sent me away empty. [^b]God has seen my affliction and the toil of my hands, so He [^c]rendered *the* decision last night.”

The **dread of Isaac** is translated as "fear of Isaac" in the ESV. It is not Jacob's own God, but his father's, and not his own fear of God, but that of Isaac, which protected him. His father had prayed for him and due to his obedience God was merciful towards Jacob. 

> ### The Covenant at Mizpah
> **Gen. 31:43**   Then Laban answered and said to Jacob, “The daughters are my daughters, and the children are my children, and [^a]the flocks are my flocks, and all that you see is mine. But what can I do this day to these daughters of mine or to their children whom they have borne?

Even after Jacob's speech in which explained in details what Laban had done and how Jacob had honestly accumulated his wealth, and how Laban was unjust to Jacob by changing his wages, he still thinks that all of these are his. He even thought his daughters were still his to keep, even though his daughters were sold by *himself* to Jacob for the fourteen years of work. The daughters themselves even said they were sold by him (v. 15). Furthermore, in what sense would the children be his? Only if they were slaves did the children belong to the owner, and indeed, Laban treated everyone as slaves, for he required Jacob to work without payment, which worked for the first fourteen years only. 
Later on, his own sluggishness #questions/language/english/spelling luiheid, caused him to have nothing left (v. 15), for he had consumed it all and had no inheritance left for his daughters.
