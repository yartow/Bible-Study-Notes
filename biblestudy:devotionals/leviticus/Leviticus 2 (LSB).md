# Leviticus 2 (LSB)
#biblestudy/devotionals/leviticus

> ### The Statutes of Grain Offerings
> **Lev. 2:1**   ‘Now when anyone brings near a grain offering as an offering to Yahweh, his offering shall be of fine flour, and he shall pour oil on it and put frankincense on it. **2** ‘He shall then bring it to Aaron’s sons the priests; and he shall take from it his handful of its fine flour and of its oil with all of its frankincense. And the priest shall offer *it* up in smoke *as* its memorial portion on the altar, an offering by fire of a soothing aroma to Yahweh. **3** ‘And the remainder of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons: a thing most holy of the offerings to Yahweh by fire.

The **remainder of the grain offering** would also contain **frankincense**. Would it still be edible then? #biblestudy/questions/leviticus

> **Lev. 2:4**   ‘Now when you bring near an offering of a grain offering baked in an oven, *it shall be* unleavened cakes of fine flour mixed with oil or unleavened wafers spread with oil. **5** ‘And if your offering is a grain offering *made* on the griddle, *it shall be* of fine flour, unleavened, mixed with oil; **6** you shall break it into bits and pour oil on it; it is a grain offering. **7** ‘Now if your offering is a grain offering *made* in a pan, it shall be made of fine flour with oil. **8** ‘And you will bring in the grain offering which is made of these things to Yahweh, and it shall be brought near to the priest, and he shall bring it to the altar. **9** ‘The priest then shall raise up from the grain offering its ==memorial portion==, and he shall offer *it* up in smoke on the altar *as* an offering by fire of a soothing aroma to Yahweh. **10** ‘And the remainder of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons: a thing most holy of the offerings to Yahweh by fire.

Apart from the distinction in the sacrifices of different animals, there is even a distinction in the *way* grain was *prepared*. God shows His order here and the importance of this. 

> **Lev. 2:11**   ‘No grain offering, which you bring near to Yahweh, shall be made with ==leaven==, for you shall not offer up in smoke any leaven or any ==honey== as an offering by fire to Yahweh.

It is not explained why, but **honey** and grain products that included **leaven** could be offered as **first fruits**, but not burned as such. #biblestudy/questions/leviticus

> **12** ‘As an offering of first fruits you shall bring them near to Yahweh, but they shall not ascend for a soothing aroma on the altar. **13** ‘Every grain offering of yours, moreover, you shall season with salt, so that the salt of the covenant of your God shall not be lacking from your grain offering; with all your offerings you shall bring salt near.

Again, there is no explanation why there has to be salt. Of course everything would taste better with salt, this would not apply to meat because that tastes well by itself. 

> **Lev. 2:14**   ‘Also if you bring near a grain offering of ==early ripened things== to Yahweh, you shall bring near fresh heads of grain roasted in the fire, grits of new growth, for the grain offering of your early ripened things. **15** ‘You shall then put oil on it and place frankincense on it; it is a grain offering. **16** ‘And the priest shall offer up in smoke its memorial portion, part of its grits and its oil with all its frankincense as an offering by fire to Yahweh.

Grain could be offered multiple times, when it was **early ripened**, as the first fruits of the harvest, and even later as fine flour. 
The mixture of **oil** and roasted **grain** give a very pleasant fragrance. 
**Example.** In Rotterdam there used to be a factory that produced Quaker oatmeal, everytime I drove by that place, my palette would tingle because of the smell of roasted oats. Some supermarkets or malls even put the fragrance of roasted grain, the same fragrance as what one smells in bakeries, in their airconditioning, so that people will get hungry and buy more food. 

The smell of **frankincense** is not something common in the West, but in (Near- or Middle-)Eastern products it is present in many household products, such as soap, perfume and wooden products which have been oiled with a mixture of oil and frankincense. To the Israelites the burning of this mixture would have provided a pleasant fragrance. 
The image of the Tabernacle and the Temple that people have should be pleasant one, so God created this connotation of pleasing smells with the thanksgiving to God. 
The smell of fat and burning meat are pleasant to most people as well. Despite the fact that these could have been *sin* offerings—which one would not expect to have a pleasant smell—the offering itself needed to be a pleasing smell, because God needed to be pleased when He symbolically smelled the fragrant. #biblestudy/discovery
