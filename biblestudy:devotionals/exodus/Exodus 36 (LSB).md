# Exodus 36 (LSB)
#biblestudy/devotionals/exodus

> **Ex. 36:1**   “Now Bezalel and Oholiab and every person wise at heart, to whom Yahweh has given wisdom and discernment to ==know how to do== all the work of the service of *constructing* the sanctuary, shall do in accordance with all that Yahweh has commanded.”

The wisdom mentioned here is not the kind of wisdom as propagated through Solomon. Solomon had *wisdom*, that is, the skill to find solutions and to discern between truth and falsehood. What is meant by **wisdom** here refers to the skills needed **to do all the work of the service of constructing the sanctuary**. In other words, the skills and talents of carpentry, other works of art and perhaps the skills of leadership and management as well that are needed to perform these tasks.

> ### Making the Tabernacle
> **Ex. 36:2**   Then Moses called Bezalel and Oholiab and every person wise at heart to whom Yahweh had given wisdom, everyone whose heart ==stirred== him, to come to the work to do it. 

God selected **every person wise at heard [and] everyone whose heart stirred him** for the work on His tabernacle. Not everyone was allowed to work on His holy tabernacle. Oftentimes we see that churches allow anyone to serve, people who lack either or both **wisdom** and **discernment**, but also the holiness and devotion required. Bezalel and Oholiab were filled with the Spirit of God (35:31) which shows a devotion that is necessary before one can work for God.

> **3** And they received from Moses the entire contribution which the sons of Israel had brought to do the work in the service of the sanctuary. ==And they still *continued* bringing to him freewill offerings every morning.==

The second **they** does not refer to Bezalel and Oholiab, but to the Israelites. When something is done for the Lord and truly out of one's heart, this is shown by its consistent character. The people **continued bringing [...] freewill offerings *every* morning** (emphasis added), not because they were asked to, but because they *wanted* to, because their heart was **stirred**.

Ramsay helped the restaurant gain traction again, changed the menu and the tools they were using, but emphasized one advise, namely that he success of the business depended on the relationship within the family. However, not long after Ramsay had left, the owner reverted to his old ways, because he did not genuinely care about his mother. 

> **4** And all the wise men who were doing all the work of the sanctuary came, each from the work which he was doing, **5** and they said to Moses, “The people are bringing much more than enough for the service of the work which Yahweh commanded *us* to do.” **6** So Moses commanded and a proclamation was passed throughout the camp, saying, “Let no man or woman any longer do the work for the contributions of the sanctuary.” Thus the people were restrained from bringing *any more*. **7** Indeed, the material they had was sufficient and more than enough for all the work, to do it.

Note that the period of constructing and fabricating #questions/language/english/spelling right word? all of the artefacts could have taken months up to even—although unlikely—years. During this whole period there must have been an internal economy within the "community"[^1], where the ones with livestock could sell dairy or meat, and those who had less could work for others in exchange for those goods or with silver or gold pieces. Perhaps Bezalel and Oholiab were paid for their full-time work on the construction of the tabernacle, but it is uncertain if others, such as the women weaving the material, were compensated for their work as well. There are a few reasons to assume that this was the case. First, there were more than enough contributions for the tabernacle and in all wisdom Bezalel and Oholiab could have decided to use the money wisely for workers, which would be reasonable. Second, the ones who helped did so voluntarily and their hearts were stirred, meaning that they were motivated to do this for God, not for the money. This motivation would grant them more trust, meaning that it is more likely for these workers to receive their pay if it is known that they did not do it for the money. The motivation could not be seen with the eye, of course, but this is where the discernment of Bezalel and Oholiab came in handy #questions/language/english/spelling . Lastly, it was God Who stirred up the hearts and put His wisdom in these people, and it was God Who wanted to use these specific people, so God would surely provide for these, even if the contributions did not exceed what was needed. 
In short, this small economy seems like an utopian situation, where there is always enough food and work for everyone. The amount of money neither increased nor decreased, except when silver and gold were used for the tabernacle (and for the creation of the calf as well), so there was practically no inflation during these forty years. However, if assuming that the amount of livestock grew proportionally with the amount of people, then when a person received the same income but had more children over time, he could provide for his family with the same income. In other words, there was likely deflating economy. See the following example for an explanation of how deflation and inflation work within a closed economy.
**Example.** A farmer sells one cow to a family of three for one shekel. After a few years the family grows to nine people, and the farmer, through God's provision, has three times the amount of cows. The farmer does not make more money if all farmers' livestock have increased proportionally as well. The price of cows will only grow when demand becomes higher. As a matter of fact, if the number of cows becomes too high, and nobody wants them, then the price of cows will even drop. ~~In this case, however, I am assuming that the number of cows grows proportionally with the number of people, so demand stays the same.~~ However, if the price stays the same, the father of this family can still only buy one cow, but now has to share this with his family which has tripled in size. If this happens with every farmer he will have an #questions/language/english/spelling (not "abundance") too many cows which nobody wants, because they are too expensive. In other words, demand decreases and the price of cows goes down as well, up until the level that the father can now buy his cows for a third of the price, so he can now buy three times as much. 
Of course the farmer will have more costs and needs to hire more people to attend for the tripled number of cows. But this same principle applies to all industries and the farmer will thus spend less money on food and salaries, so that he can sell his products for less as well. 
All of this happens within a closed economy and one can see that this will only work if the amount of people and livestock grow proportionally. Only God can control this. Therefore, the economy in a country will never be perfect if God if the nation has rejected God. #tags/politics/economics

> **Ex. 36:8**   And all those wise at heart among those who were doing the work made the tabernacle with ten curtains; of fine twisted linen and blue and purple and scarlet *material*, with cherubim, the work of a skillful designer, Bezalel made them. **9** The length of each curtain was twenty-eight cubits and the width of each curtain four cubits; all the curtains had the same measurements. **10** He joined five curtains to one another, and *the other* five curtains he joined to one another. **11** He made loops of blue on the edge of the outermost curtain in the first set; he did likewise on the edge of the curtain that was outermost in the second set. **12** He made fifty loops in the one curtain, and he made fifty loops on the edge of the curtain that was in the second set; the loops were opposite each other. **13** He made fifty clasps of gold and joined the curtains to one another with the clasps, so the tabernacle was one unit.

There is a lot to be said about all the different details of artefacts of the tabernacle. Many things are symbolic—such as the number *five*, which indicates *grace*[^3]—but there are many instructions for practical reasons as well. One example of this is that **five curtains [were joined] to one another**. If all ten curtains were joined together this would be much heavier to carry, to spread out over the tabernacle, and the chances of tears would be higher. This way, there is a symmetric five curtains on the one and on the other side, with *fifty*—which is *five* times *ten* (the number of human completeness or perfection). Symbolically this means the through grace human perfection is obtained. 
The explanation of the first curtain is then as follows. In the tabernacle petition was made for the forgiveness of Israel's sin, which later would be forgiveness for the sin of all of mankind, though not through the temporary High Priest, but through the Eternal High Priest, Jesus Christ, and not in the temporary and physical Temple, but in our bodies which are the Temple (cf. Hebrews 10). Through grace God would grant this forgiveness as an act of mercy (note the difference between *grace* and *mercy*, see also notes on ... #todo/nogaftemaken nog ergens dit verschil opschrijven). The forgiveness God grants is humanly complete. If God says He has forgotten our sin, it is truly forgotten (cf. Micah 7:9), not like people who say they have forgiven, but not forgotten things. However, even though God's forgiveness is complete, man has not changed. That is why the completeness goes only as far as I know is humanly possible. God's perfection would be symbolized by the number *seven*, but here *ten* is used for this reason. #biblestudy/discovery

> **Ex. 36:14**   Then he made curtains of goats’ *hair* for a tent over the tabernacle; he made eleven curtains in all. **15** The length of each curtain *was* thirty cubits and four cubits the width of each curtain; the eleven curtains had the same measurements. **16** He joined five curtains by themselves and *the other* six curtains by themselves. **17** Moreover, he made fifty loops on the edge of the curtain that was outermost in the *first* set, and he made fifty loops on the edge of the curtain *that was outermost in* the second set. **18** He made fifty clasps of bronze to join the tent together so that it would be a unit.

The second **covering** was made of **goats' hair**. The practical reasons pertaining to a comfortable temperature in the Holy are mentioned in the footnote[^2]. An eleventh curtain was needed to fully cover the first ten curtains.
For our purpose the symbolic meanings are more important here. There were **eleven curtains**, where *eleven* is the number representing chaos[^3], in the sense that sin is disorder, and holiness is order. The curtain of goats' hair was not as smooth as the **fine twisted linen**, and the color was not as #questions/language/english/word niet monochroom maar dat het vlak is, egaal, nor as beautiful, as blue, purple and scarlet. The contrast between these are symbolic for the contrast between the holiness of Jesus and the sin of man. This is also the reason why the clasps were made of bronze as opposed to the golden clasps in the first cover. #biblestudy/discovery

> **19** He made a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red and a covering of porpoise skins above.

The third and fourth layers were not curtains, but (according to artist impressions' drawings I have seen) were patches placed on top of the second layer, to absorb the heat, deflect radiation, but not completely seal like the tabernacle, to provide some air flow. Perhaps this was another reason why the first and second coverings had a gap between the fifth and sixth curtain, which were not sewn but *clasped*. #biblestudy/discovery

> **Ex. 36:20**   Then he made the boards for the tabernacle of acacia wood, standing *upright*. **21** Ten cubits *was* the length of each board and one and a half cubits the width of each board. **22** *There were* two tenons for each board, fitted to one another; thus he did for all the boards of the tabernacle. **23** And he made the boards for the tabernacle: twenty boards for the south side; **24** and he made forty bases of silver under the twenty boards; two bases under one board for its two tenons and two bases under another board for its two tenons. **25** Then for the second side of the tabernacle, on the north side, he made twenty boards, **26** and their forty bases of silver; two bases under one board and two bases under another board. **27** For the rear of the tabernacle, to the west, he made six boards. **28** And he made two boards for the corners of the tabernacle at the rear. **29** And they were separated beneath, but were together at their completion at the top, at the first ring; thus he did with both of them for the two corners. **30** And there were eight boards with their bases of silver, sixteen bases, two bases under every board.

The Holy of Holies is a perfect cube, each side being ten cubits. The number of boards on the west side is not **six** in total, but **eight**, because **two [extra] boards** were made **for the corners**, preventing the whole western wall from falling backwards **to the west**. The number eight has a symbolic meaning which is harder to explain, but it is the first cube, i.e. $2^3$, and it is often connected to a new start or to life[^3]. 
The width of these **six boards** was nine cubits, but because of the thickness of the two boards on the **north** and **south side** of half a cubit (not mentioned, but assumed) this made the Holy of Holies exactly ten cubits on the outside (and nine cubits on the inside).

> **Ex. 36:31**   Then he made bars of acacia wood, five for the boards of one side of the tabernacle, **32** and five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the tabernacle for the rear *side* to the west. **33** And he made the middle bar to pass through in the center of the boards from ==end to end==. **34** He also overlaid the boards with gold and made their rings of gold *as* holders for the bars and overlaid the bars with gold.

Despite the covering of gold, the inside was made of the highest quality wood. Artefacts for God are always of the highest quality both on the inside as well as on the outside. 

> **Ex. 36:35**   Moreover, he made the veil of blue and purple and scarlet *material* and fine twisted linen; he made it with cherubim, the work of a skillful designer. **36** And he made four pillars of acacia for it and overlaid them with gold, with their hooks of gold; and he cast four bases of silver for them. **37** And he made a screen for the doorway of the tent, of blue and purple and scarlet *material* and fine twisted linen, the work of a weaver; **38** and *he made* its five pillars with their hooks, and he overlaid their tops and their bands with gold; but their five bases were of bronze.

Here the distinction between the Holy and anything outside of it is clearly seen. What was attached to the Holy—the boards as well as **the pillars** for **the veil**—has **bases of silver**, but what was at a distance—that is, the **screen** in front of the entrance at the east, which was not directy against the tent—had **bases [...] of bronze**.
