> ### Willing Hearts Contribute to Yahweh
> 
> **3** “You shall not kindle a fire in any of your *places of* habitation on the sabbath day.”

Fire served as source of light and heat, which do not imply that work is done, but these *could* indicate it. In normal weather conditions a fire would not be needed in Israel when people were going to sleep after sunset anyway, and the remaining light would be sufficient to do the last work needed before sleep.
Another explanation is that oil lamps were allowed to be used, but that these should be kindled before the start of the Sabbath, that is, on Friday at sunset. If one forgot, that would be his own responsibility for not having prepared well. The Sabbath was not to be taken lightly. Nothing is said about cooking if the fire was already on, and personally, I do not think that God is so specific that at sunset exactly (if that time were known and generally agreed on, which it was probably not[^1]) one had to drop everything. The main point was that people finished their work *before* sunset—not delay it to the final moment *at* sunset—so that they could *rest*. #biblestudy/discovery
As for cooking, generally speaking this is not a hobby for housewives who already do this every day, three times a day, but if, say, the husband wants to cook on Fridays as a way of his own rest, this might not be considered as work. However, do note that this is my personal opinion. 


> **Ex. 35:4**   And Moses spoke to all the congregation of the sons of Israel, saying, “This is the thing which Yahweh has commanded, saying, **5** ‘Take from among you a contribution to Yahweh; whoever is of a ==willing heart==, let him bring it as a contribution to Yahweh: gold, silver, and bronze, **6** and blue, purple, and scarlet *material*, fine linen, goats’ *hair*, **7** and rams’ skins dyed red, and porpoise skins, and acacia wood, **8** and oil for lighting, and spices for the anointing oil, and for the fragrant incense, **9** and onyx stones and setting stones for the ephod and for the breastpiece.
> 
> **Ex. 35:10**   ‘And let everyone ==wise at heart== among you come and make all that Yahweh has commanded: **11** the tabernacle, its tent and its covering, its clasps and its boards, its bars, its pillars, and its bases; **12** the ark and its poles, the mercy seat, and the curtain of the screen; **13** the table and its poles, and all its utensils, and the bread of the Presence; **14** the lampstand also for the light and its utensils and its lamps and the oil for the light; **15** and the altar of incense and its poles, and the anointing oil and the fragrant incense, and the screen for the doorway at the entrance of the tabernacle; **16** the altar of burnt offering with its bronze grating, its poles, and all its utensils, the laver and its stand; **17** the hangings of the court, its pillars and its bases, and the screen for the gate of the court; **18** the pegs of the tabernacle and the pegs of the court and their cords; **19** the woven garments for ministering in the holy place, the holy garments for Aaron the priest and the garments of his sons, to minister as priests.’”

Giving to the Lord and serving Him. The Lord is very specific *what* materials He wants, and the quality thereof, for His Tabernacle, and *what* has to be built or made with these. He is also very specific about *who* is to work with these materials. He is even specific about *how* the people should give these materials, for only those could offer to the Tabernacle **whose heart stirred him and whose spirit who was willing** (v. 21). The ones who make the objects must be **wise**—which in this case is not referring to the wisdom that Solomon is speaking of, but to the intelligence and skillfullness of artisanal work, that is, of among others carpentry, #questions/language/english/word metaal smelterijk, -bewerking, smith?  and weavery ?? The perfume and oils too had to be made with special recipes and by experts. Nothing in God's Tabernacle (and Temple, as would be explained later) was to be less than perfect, according to human standards. 
This in itself, would be an almost—or perhaps, literally—impossible thing to do. For example, the High Priest had to sacrifice a bull #todo/opzoeken/bible for the purification of himself first, so that he would be pure enough to sacrifice the bull for the people, but what if the priest had a sinful thought or committed a sinful action in these few minutes between the first and second sacrifice? About the materials built for God, these would never be perfect, for the building and measuring instruments were not perfect, and weights, angles and lengths would never be perfectly measured according to God's standards. Still, God accepted the sacrifices and the effort of the people, the willingness (albeit not a hundred percent pure) and the objects the people made. This, too, is a foreshadow of God's mercy towards His people. He accepts our prayers—even though these are not perfect—and moreover He accepts us as His children, even though we are far from perfect. But as long as we are covered with the blood of Jesus, He accepts us, in the same way as the High Priest was accepted through the blood of the bull.


> ### Contributions Brought by the People
> **Ex. 35:20**   Then all the congregation of the sons of Israel went out from Moses’ presence. **21** And everyone whose heart stirred him and everyone whose spirit was ==willing== came *and* brought the contribution to Yahweh for the work of the tent of meeting and for all its service and for the holy garments. **22** Then all whose hearts were ==willing==, both men and women, came *and* brought brooches and earrings and signet rings and bracelets, all articles of gold; so *did* every man who waved it as a wave offering of gold to Yahweh. **23** And every man, who had in his possession blue and purple and scarlet *material* and fine linen and goats’ *hair* and rams’ skins dyed red and porpoise skins, brought them. **24** Everyone who could raise up a contribution of silver and bronze brought the contribution to Yahweh; and every man who had in his possession acacia wood for any work of the service brought it.

Vv. 21, 22 say that **[only those] whose heart stirred him and everyone whose spirit was willing came and brought the contribution to Yahweh**, but v. 23 mentions **every man**. Most likely we can infer from this that God wanted—and *required*—that all gifts were brought willingly. Then, *within* this group of willing people, **every man, who had in his possession blue and purple [...] brought them**. Leaving the fact aside that there must have been some men who did this for outer appearances, this verse is literally speaking of every man who possessed these things and who was willing. This is very likely possible because the materials mentioned here was very expensive. Now I cannot explain how it was possible for any Israelite slave to have such expensive materials in his possession, other than that that was given him by the Egyptians, but let us suppose that there were actually rich and poor Israelites. For a rich person it was not a very big gift to donate his blue, purple and other materials. In this day the phrase would have been something like, "Every man who owned a Ferrari, donated his MacBook and iPhone." The same applies to those who had acacia wood in their possession. On the other hand, however, the same is not said about those who had precious metals in their possession. There it says that only those **who could raise up a contribution of silver and bronze** donated these. The people who possessed these were not necessarily rich, and they used these precious metals as a form of currency. Therefore, they only gave what they could *raise up*. To the rich people, however, the blue and purple were merely materials for clothes or furniture, which did not contribute to their #questions/language/english/word onderhoud, inkomen. 
If we suppose all Israelites were poor, because they had been slaves, but some had been given these precious cloths, then perhaps we can speculate that this gift was given freely as well, because it was not theirs to begin with.

> **25** All the women wise at heart spun with their hands and brought what they had spun, *in* blue and purple *and* scarlet *material* and *in* fine linen. **26** And all the women whose heart stirred with wisdom spun the goats’ *hair*.

There seems to be a progression from the most to the least important things in the Law in these verses and also a regression in one's willingness. I am not really sure what the difference is between v. 21 and v. 22. Perhaps the latter refers to only the objects mentioned there, while the former includes every contribution mentioned Exodus 25:3-7. In any case, the former required a stirred **heart** and a willing **spirit**, while the latter required objects "only" from those **whose hearts were willing**. 
Then, less valuable materials (v. 23) are mentioned such as the skins. The silver and bronze, and wood, in v. 24 were not necessarily worth less than the animal skinsbut perhaps the phrase "**raise up a contribution**" means something different from the phrase "**brought the contribution**" in v. 21. In present day terms raising up funds for e.g. a presidential nominee, means one has done the effort to raise others' money for this cause, but not one's own, although one has put significan effort and time in it. 
The last two verses clearly show a degression from where **women** did not bring materials, but their own efforts, and **spun** costly material and then in v. 26 less valuable **goats' hair**. 
Of course, none of this is to indicate the efforts are worth little, and in some cases the effort counts more than the result. But in this context—if my interpretation is right—Moses wrote this in this order for a specific reason. #biblestudy/discovery

> **Ex. 35:30**   Then Moses said to the sons of Israel, “See, Yahweh has called by name Bezalel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. **31** ==“And He has filled him with the Spirit of God, in wisdom, in discernment, and in knowledge, and in all craftsmanship;== **32** to devise designs for working in gold and in silver and in bronze, **33** and in the cutting of stones for settings and in the carving of wood, so as to do *well* in every work of thoughtful design.

The **Spirit of God** is also the One Who gives practical skills (cf. the gifts of the Holy Spirit #todo/opzoeken/reference 1 Corinthians?), but aside from the *ability* God also puts *willingness* in man's, indicated by v. 34 where God has put teaching in Bezalel and Oholiab's **heart**, which is something distinctively different from v. 35, which is not speakingn willingness but of intellectual skill. 

> **34** “He also has put in his heart to ==teach==, both he and Oholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan. **35** “He has filled them with wisdom *in their* heart to do every work of an engraver and of a designer and of an embroiderer, in blue and in purple *and* in scarlet *material* and in fine linen, and of a weaver, as those who do every work and make designs.

It seems that God did not only *choose* Bezalel and Oholiab, but that He had also *prepared* them with the **wisdom**, the (physical) capacity and the training needed on a *very broad range* of arts and craftmanship. I do not know the exact meanings of the difference between wisdom, discernment and knowledge, but I would say that the **wisdom** mentioned here is different from the wisdom often attributed to Solomon (see notes above). The wisdom here refers to intellectual and practical skill in craftmanship. The term used when referring to Solom, refers to knowing what do and say, at the right time and to the right person, or in other words, having the right discernment. Do note, though, that in the Bible this word is used with a slightly different interpretation, for Solomon was deemed to have wisdom (#todo/opzoeken/reference Proverbs) but he did not know nor do the right things. As a matter of fact, there is one Proverb where there is another condition next to wisdom, meaning that knowing it is no so much wisdom as it is about doing it as well.

Next, there was **discernment** and that interacts, of course, with wisdom and knowledge. Spiritual discernment was very important here, because no object in the tabernacle, including the tabernacle as a whole itself, should have resemblance with other religions. Of course there is always the aspect of sanctity and where certain people are allowed to enter certain places, but as a whole the worship in the tabernacle differs so much from every other form of worship in any other religion. Next to that, the whole foundation of God's plan of salvation can be found in the Tabernacle. God's grace—showed through the number *five*—appears at many places in its construction, as well as His other characteristics and plans. His holiness and the fact that nothing in and of the tabernacle may be worshipped, and that offering is very limited on only two places—the Altar of Incense and the Bronze Altar #todo/opzoeken/bible name—shows that God is so holy, that He cannot even be simply approached to offer something to, regardless of one's intentions, on the punishment of death. 

It is almost impossible to think that two people would have so much **knowledge, in all craftsmanship**. The mastery of hammering gold is completely different from smelting metals, let alone gold and other precious metals. The cutting of stones is again a completely different profession, and I have not even mentioned the treatment and dying of skins, the weaving of materials and the design of all of those. In today's terms these Bezalel would have had at least a degree in Mechanical Engineering and multiple degrees in #questions/language/english/spelling edelsmit, arts, woodworking, [stone cutting] there are so many education of which I do not even know the name! 
But aside from that **both** of them also had the talent and **heart** to **teach**, so that the work was not done only by them but could be sped up with others, and so that the wisdom, knowledge and discernment would not be lost but be passed on to the next generations.
