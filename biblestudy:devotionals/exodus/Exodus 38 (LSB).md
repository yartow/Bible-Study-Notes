> **11** For the north side *there were* one hundred cubits; their twenty pillars and their twenty bases *were* of bronze, the hooks of the pillars and their bands *were* of silver. **12** For the west side *there were* hangings of fifty cubits *with* their ten pillars and their ten bases; the hooks of the pillars and their bands *were* of silver.

Again, the **west side** and **east side** indicated grace, symbolized through the number **fifty**. 

> **13** For the east side toward the sunrise fifty cubits. **14** The hangings for the *one* side *of the gate were* fifteen cubits, *with* their three pillars and their three bases, **15** and so for the other side. On both sides of the gate of the court *were* hangings of fifteen cubits, *with* their three pillars and their three bases.

The front gate, at the **east side** needed to have an entrance. Without compromising on the breadth on this side, which needed to be equal to the opposite side, which was **fifty cubits**, this was solved by splitting the east side into three parts—two of **fifteen cubits** and the **gate of the court** being **twenty cubits**. On artists' impressions the gate was placed a few meters to the west or east of the other **hangings**. 
These hangings consisted of an extra pillar. Normally for every five cubits there would be one pillar and an extra *one* for the endpoint. Now, because there were three parts, there were three endpoints. So fifty cubits normally has eleven pillars, and one would expect the two sides of the gate to have four pillars, which are needed to span the fifteen cubits. However, for both sides, the first and last of the four pillars would not count, because these were part of the south and north sides, respectively. Hence, there were now two times **three pillars** for the hangings on **both sides of the gate** plus five pillars for the gate itself. 
The number *eleven*, which stands for chaos, would not be beautiful number, but in this way there are two times the number representing the Triune God and one time His grace.
