> ### Making the Ark of the Testimony
> **Ex. 37:1**   And Bezalel made the ark of acacia wood; its length was two and a half cubits, and its width one and a half cubits, and its height one and a half cubits; **2** and he overlaid it with pure gold inside and out, and made a gold molding for it all around.

The Ark of the Testimony had to be perfect. The number representing perfection is $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt(5) \approx 1.618$ which is also called *the Golden Ratio*[^1], and the ratio of the Ark was $2.5/1.5 \approx 1.667$. Of course square roots, fractions and decimals had not been invented yet and could not be calculated exactly, but it should be clear that God was approximating the Golden Ratio with the means available to man at the time. 

> **3** He cast four rings of gold for it on its four feet; even two rings on one side of it, and two rings on the other side of it. **4** He made poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with gold. **5** He put the poles into the rings on the sides of the ark to carry it. **6** He made a mercy seat of pure gold, two and a half cubits long and one and a half cubits wide. **7** He made two cherubim of gold; he made them of hammered work at the two ends of the mercy seat; **8** one cherub at the one end and one cherub at the other end; from *one piece* he made the mercy seat *with* the cherubim at its two ends. **9** The cherubim had *their* wings spread upward, covering the mercy seat with their wings, with their faces toward each other; the faces of the cherubim were toward the mercy seat.

The mercy seat was not to be idolized, it was not even to be seen by the eyes of man. The regular people were not allowed to come before it, but only the High Priest and only twice a year upon God's designation. For this reason the mercy seat was covered with the **[cherubim's] wings**. The cherubim themselves #todo/opzoeken or other types of angels? did not even look at God, but covered their own face with their wings #todo/opzoeken/reference (Revelation). Even though in Revelation the cherubim would be literally facing God Himself in Heaven if they did not cover their faces, here in Tabernacle the mercy seat would be empty and God would only *figuratively* be sitting there. Still, even the figurative or spiritual presence of God is sufficiently holy that it needs to be covered up. In this case, the Holy of Holies was so holy that no could enter it even without His actual presence and only His *spiritual* presence. At the time when God #todo/opzoeken  (or God's spirit?) was in the Tabernacle, even Moses was not able to stay in the Tabernacle, but everyone had to leave the whole Tent of Meeting, that is, leave the Holy as well.

> **18** There were six branches going out of its sides; three branches of the lampstand from the one side of it and three branches of the lampstand from the other side of it; **19** three cups shaped like almond *blossoms*, a bulb and a flower in one branch, and three cups shaped like almond *blossoms*, a bulb and a flower in the other branch—so for the six branches coming out of the lampstand. **20** In the lampstand *there were* four cups shaped like almond *blossoms*, its bulbs and its flowers; **21** and a bulb was under the *first* pair of branches *coming* out of it, and a bulb under the *second* pair of branches *coming* out of it, and a bulb under the *third* pair of branches *coming* out of it, for the six branches coming out of the lampstand. **22** Their bulbs and their branches were *of one piece* with it; the whole of it *was* a single hammered work of pure gold. **23** He made its seven lamps with its tongs and its trays of pure gold. **24** He made it and all its utensils from a talent of pure gold.

There is so much beauty in the design of the lampstand in several fields. First of all in the numerical field. Even though there were **six branches**, and six is not a good number. It is the one less than the number of godly perfect, *seven*, and symbolizes the devil in Revelation (#todo/opzoeken/reference  666). But because of the *trunk* itself, which also carries a lamp, it seems like there are seven branches, and indeed there were **seven lamps**. As for the number of artefacts on the branches, there were **three cups** on both sides, so again six in total, but the trunk itself had **four cups**, making the total *ten*. *Four* being the number of the Earth, indicating that this *menorah*, that is, the **lampstand**, is not a part of Heaven, but rather something on Earth with which God is honored. The number *ten*, which indicates human perfection, symbolizes the fullness and perfection of this lampstand. Nothing may be changed, not in its appearance, nor in the rituals. This is the way that God should be honored in the Tabernacle. 
It should be noted that there is no mention of the *hanukkah* in the Bible, the nine-armed candelabra (or eight-armed with a trunk). If by any chance this was permitted by God to be made, the twelve almond blossoms it would then have (four on the trunk and eight on its branches) would stand for the people of Israel. 

Then there is the spiritual beauty. The reason why there are almost blossoms, is because almonds are the first trees in Israel to bloom. Even more so, almond trees even grows leafs before they grow their blossoms, unlike other trees. The spiritual meaning hereof of this is referred to in Matthew ... by Jesus Himself #todo/opzoeken/reference (although not explained there). The meaning of this is that being a child of God should be *clearly visible* to everyone. Just like when in the desert climate of Israel everything is dry and colorless, except for the almond tree, which most definitely should stand out among all other tree, so the child of God should stand out in the secular world, and all peoples should see that they are different, because their God is different. #todo/opzoeken/reference Moses' call to God that the Israelites be distinguished from all other peoples, and Jesus' call to His disciples that they should be like a lamp in the darkness for everyone *to see*. 

Lastly, there is the aesthetic beauty of the lampstand. It was made of gold, probably polished to a mirror finish, with perfect symmetry in the lampstand and its branches. The almond blossoms and buds were probably not placed symmetrically, but were a piece of art in themselves.
