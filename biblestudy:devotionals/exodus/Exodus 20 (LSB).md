#### Essay. The Sabbath as guide to physical spiritual, financial, relational and mental health
The Sabbath is not only a day of rest for our bodies, it is first of all a day set aside for Yahweh. Advantageous side-effects then are restoration for the body, mind and soul. By not working on this day, one also puts his trust in God as keeper of the family. The financial responsibility of the man to take care of his family *financially* is lifted up for one day, but his spiritual responsibility is not. Every day of the week he needs to teach and live out the Word of God, and the way to do that is by meeting other believers on the Sabbath, to share bread and wine in order to remember Who God is and what He has done for us (and for Christians this includes His death and resurrection), to commune and receive the Word from elders and those given the gift of teaching—at least *one day* in the week. 
Of course communing is not possible if everyone does it on different days, and there is a synergetic effect in seeing this done by the whole community. 
Take for example Jews and Christians in an atheistic society. Christians (I hope) are happy when they meet other Christians. It is rare to meet authentic and radical Christians these days. Though there are hundreds of millions on Earth, there might only be a few in one's local community. There is a constant pressure of the world to do things in the way of the world, a temptation—if you will—but seeing another believer doing the right things as well *empowers* one another. These temptations are present in many forms, such as the practice of not (fully) reporting one's sales or income to the IRS, and dressing and making up oneself like others do. 
**Example.** I grew up in the restaurant business and restaurant owner I know, does not fully report all of his sales. Some of these restaurant owners became Christians later, but I still have my doubt as to whether they then started doing business with the fear of God. I do not own a restaurant myself, but I would set it as a high priority to report everything, not only as much as possible out of principle and out of obedience to God, but also to show my trust in Him to care of me and my family—and if the business should fail anyway, it would be because of His will.

The element of *financial health* starts here, by putting our trust in Him on this *single day* in the week. But at the same time believers can encourage others *spiritually* by being this kind of testimony and light in the dark times. 

> **Ex. 20:8**   	“Remember the sabbath day, to ==keep== it holy.

The word **keep** here (in Greek) has the same meaning as in the Threefold Blessing (Numbers 6:24–26) even though the original is written in Hebrew. In today's English "to keep" would mean "to hold on to and not give away". But the Greek and Hebrew meaning of this is to save and preserve it, to protect it from damage. When food is *kept*, for example, this means more than merely "not giving it away". It also means that it must be kept in a cool and dry place, so that mold and other things cannot get to it.
For Christians this means that **the sabbath day** is more than just a ritual that must be kept through time, it must be protected as well. That is the definition of **holy**—that is, *to be set aside*—no other things than the things of God may enter into the Sabbath day. It is not a time for worries, but for pleasure, to please God and one's fellowmen and oneself, and relaxation. It is not meant for work, although "work" can be defined in different ways.

#### Essay. Sacrificing according to God's standards
On the other hand God asks that nothing that is presented to Him is profaned by human work. God does not delight in diamonds, for example, as the worth of diamonds is only determined by its market value and its demand, which is controlled. Even gold and silver are intrinsically worth nothing other more than what the market determines. 
**Example.** In one of the Scrooge McDuck's comics #tags/books/comics Uncle Scrooge gives different tribal people a bead or a bottle cap in exchange for other "valuable" materials. Because tribal people had never seen these items they value these highly and because these are unique and extremely rare in their world, the value goes up and is exchanged for ever more "regular" products such as animal and natural products. 

Whatever we make for God would be like pebbles and beads or bottle caps for Him. It would be an utter insult and worthless garbage, which would spoil whatever He already created. Imagine a bottle cap—despite having been mined (for the iron ore) molten and treated and hardened into steel, and where the plastic inside the cap was made through an elaborate process from crude oil which had been pumped up with huge fracking machines, built with the knowledge obtained through centuries of scientific research in mathematics and physical (mechanical and electrical) and chemical engineering and more—in the middle of a beautiful forest, a desert or ocean. Regardless of how much effort man has put in the creation of this bottle cap or in bringing this cap all the way to this deserted place, the cap spoils the view! The cap—or even the most advanced nanotechnological quantum chip—cannot compare to the beauty of nature and it does not belong in this *realm*. A technological feat of man would fit nicely in an exposition together with other inventions of man, but not in nature.
