# 1 Peter 1 (LSB)
**1Pet. 1:1**   Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ,

### A Living Hope
> To those who reside as exiles, scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia, 

The **exiles** is sometimes translated as the *diaspora*, which is a formal term for the scattering of the Jews, perhaps most specifically referring to the diaspora caused by the Assyrians, but later on also used as a general term for all the Jews scattered by for example the Babylonians, Persians and later on the Romans for which the Jews were fleeing from in 60-70 AD. I am excluding the Christian scattering (of Acts 3) here. (See also notes on 1 Peter 2:10.)

> who are chosen **2** according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, by the sanctifying work of the Spirit, to the obedience of Jesus Christ and the ==sprinkling== of His blood: May grace and peace be multiplied to you.

If we interpret this verse as: 
*To those who are chosen* 
* *according to the foreknowledge of God the Father,* 
* *by the sanctifying work of the Spirit,* 
* *to the obedience of Jesus Christ and* 
* *to the sprinkling of His blood:* 
the interpretation becomes difficult at the second and fourth statements. The first one does not need any explanation. It is the Father Who knew who was going to be chosen. #todo write here something about the Father's foreknowledge vs. His decision to choose vs. people's free will. 
The second interpretation becomes difficult because if we say that it is the Spirit Who sanctifies the person *so that* he will live in a better way so that he is ultimately chosen by God to be His child, then we are left with the question: was it the Spirit Who caused the person's salvation or the person's own free will? The answer is simple: it is the person who must *want* to be saved and want to convert and live a sanctified life. It is then the Holy Spirit Who helps him at obtaining this sanctified life. 
The third statement is that those who are chosen are chosen because Jesus Christ *demanded* this, and thus He must be obeyed. 
The fourth statement says in fact that those who are chosen are chosen to sprinkle Jesus' blood. As Christians we are priests as well who are allowed in the inner sanctuary and we are allowed to sprinkle the blood of the Lamb on the altar, so that it is cleansed from our own sin. I am not saying that we can cleanse other people's sins—just like the priests of the Old Testament could not do that—but we merely fulfill a role as mediator between God and other people. Jesus is the real Mediator between man and God the Father, but we pray for other people here on Earth as well.

> **4** to *obtain* an inheritance incorruptible and undefiled and ~unfading~, having been kept in heaven for you, 

The difference between the three adjectives #questions/language/english/spelling predicate, is 
* **incorruptible** means that nothing can decrease the value of this inheritance, 
* **undefiled** means that nothing can decrease the purity of this inheritance, and 
* **unfading** means that regardless of time, the prospect of the obtaining of this inheritance will not become less. 
More concretely, the inheritance waiting for us in heaven, that is salvation leading to eternal life in a relationship with God is #todo .... and ..... and the pleasure to be with God will never become less, but always grow. It is not like a child who enjoys his new toys but eventually becomes bored with them, because the glory and joy of his toys are fading away.

> **1Pet. 1:13**   Therefore, having girded your minds for action, being sober *in spirit*, fix your hope completely on the grace to be brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ.

Girding was like #questions/language/english/spelling je mouwen opstropen. It was the process of tightening one's belt and putting everything one had in his hands and tuck it in/under #questions/language/english/spelling  his belt, so that one's hands were free and ready to do the work. Another interpretation is that one puts the things he will need under his belt, just like a carpenter does with his tools. Everything said in the verses above was part of the **[girding of the] minds**. What then is the action we are to expect? It is the continuous call to **be holy [...] in all [...] conduct**. So, in other words, this meant that everything that one had on his mind needed to be put away, because the full power and concentration of the mind was (and still is) to be set on the process of sanctification.
The second part of the verse deals with the expected difficulties of this process. Again, **being sober** is required, as in not being under the influence of anything. In the context of substances this could be alcohol or drugs. But our figurative hands need to be clear, and our literal minds need to be clear as well, without worries or thoughts that lead us away from our purpose. 
Perhaps you will think that it is impossible to do away with the worries, but that is why Peter tells us to **fix our hope completely on the grace to be brought to you**. Our hope is in the Lord and He will help us through. He will give His **grace** with which we will be able to be with Him. Note that this grace is only given **at the revelation of Jesus Christ**. Peter is not speaking of the daily grace we receive from Him.

> **18** knowing that you were not redeemed with corruptible things like silver or gold from your futile conduct inherited from your forefathers,

**[F]utile conduct [is] inherited from your forefathers**, although this does not give us an excuses to behave this way. Many of the kings mentioned in 1 and 2 Chronicles were born from immoral fathers who committed idolatry with many fake gods. All of them were not excused for their behavior, and some of them showed that is was possible to escape from their father's bad behavior and go against it. 

> **19** but with precious blood, as of a lamb unblemished and spotless, *the blood* of Christ. **20** He was foreknown before the foundation of the world, but appeared in these last times for the sake of you **21** who through Him are believers in God, who raised Him from the dead and gave Him glory, so that your faith and hope are in God.

Again, Peter makes it clear that "everything" was done **for the sake of you**. What was the purpose of all this then? It is **so that your faith and hope are in God**. In the end, all that matters, is that God—that is, the Father and the Son, but the Holy Spirit as well—obtain glory. 

> **1Pet. 1:22**   Since you have in obedience to the truth purified your souls for a love of the brothers without hypocrisy, fervently love one another from the heart, **23** for you have been born again not of corruptible seed but incorruptible, *that is*, through the living and enduring word of God. **24** For, 
> 	“ALL FLESH IS LIKE GRASS,

> 	AND THE FLOWER FALLS OFF,
> **25** 	BUT THE WORD OF THE LORD ENDURES FOREVER.”

>  And this is the word which was proclaimed to you as good news.

Peter is clearly distiguishing here between the flesh and the spirit, as he will be doing in 1 Peter 4 as well, but here he is using the words **heart** and **word of God**. Our actions, which include our love for **one another** should be **from the heart** and not from the flesh, for the love from the flesh, as beautiful as it may be (like the flower of grass) will **wither [and] fall off**. The love seen here on Earth can take great forms of unselfish and merciful love, but all of it withers in the light of eternity. It is only the **Word of the Lord [that] endures forever** and so we need to base our love on this as well.
