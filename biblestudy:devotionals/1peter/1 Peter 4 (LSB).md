> ### Keep Fervent in Your Love
> **1Pet. 4:1**   Therefore, since Christ has suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves also with the same purpose—because he who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin— 

This does not mean that the Christian will never sin anymore. The full meaning becomes clear when we understand the **[suffering] in the flesh** means. The flesh is the opposite of the spirit, and it everything that is part of our current being that is not godly[^1]. Every  part of our being that longs to sin—for example the part of our thoughts that are evil—is part of our flesh. Whenever we have negative and sinful thoughts, it is the flesh of our old self that still clings to our new body. With our spiritual part we cannot sin anymore, because it has been renewed and made righteous, but our old self, that is our flesh, can still sin.
When Christians then suffer in the flesh, meaning that they lose some of their flesh, they cannot suffer anymore. For example, who can have pain in their hands when they lose their arms? So when we suffer in our flesh, there is no more temptation nor any sin. The reason Christians still sin on this Earth is because the flesh is still present and has not suffered completely. 

> **2** so as to no longer live the rest of the time in the flesh for the lusts of men, but for the will of God.

When we do not suffer in the flesh, we will stay sensitive to the **lusts of men** but we should become sensitive to that of God. 0

> **3** For the time already past is sufficient *for you* to have worked out the desire of the Gentiles, having pursued a course of sensuality, lusts, drunkenness, carousing, drinking parties, and abominable idolatries. **4** In *all* this, they are surprised that you do not run with *them* into the same excesses of dissipation, maligning *you*, 

Each Christian has lived in the world for a certain amount of time, that is before he became a believer. That time was **sufficient [...] to have worked out the desires of the Gentiles** or in our case, the unbelievers, which are the things mentioned in the verse, or in other words a path that leads to a destination far from God. Though the world is **surprised that [the Christian does] not run with them**, because they are having the time of their life—the Christian should be surprised when he sees other Christians participating in these sinful acts. Is not the reason why a person wanted to become a follower of Christ to be *with* Christ? Is that not the definition of being a *follower*? Jesus has gone where the Christian cannot follow (#todo/opzoeken/reference ) but the Christian lives this life of suffering because he wants to be where Jesus is, and to do so he must follow this path. Now if anyone willingly strays from this path, why would he go through all the trouble to act like those of the path? For example, there are people who want to live like the world does, listen to worldly music, watch worldly movies, hang out with people of world, wear their clothes and even spend their time and money like they do, but they still want to go to church and do some things in the moral area right. This is, of course, because the Holy Spirit is still holding on to these people. These things could be traditions as well, but another reason is that the doing of those "good" things eases their conscience. At least they will not feel bad because they have not stolen or hurt anyone. But, in fact, they *have* hurt someone—God.

> **5** but they will give account to Him who is ready to judge the living and the dead. 

The world can live as it wants, but ultimately it has not followed Jesus and for that it needs to **give account to Him**.

> **6** For to this the gospel has been proclaimed even to those who are *now* dead, so that though they were judged in the flesh as men, they live in the spirit according to *the will of* God.

The audience in v. 5 is not the same as that of v. 6 and has shifted here to not only a Christian audience, but to the Christians who have died already. Though these Christians will be judged as well (or perhaps already have, since time works differently in Heaven), they will only be **judged in the flesh**, because their spirit had already died (cf. Galatians 2:20) and the punishment due to their flesh has been paid for by Jesus Christ. 

> **1Pet. 4:7**   The end of all things is at hand; 

Four commands are listed below that the Christian should do because **the end of all things is at hand**. In the end-times #tags/theology/eschatology Christians need to put away the strife between them and get closer, removing the boundaries of denominations and needless rituals and traditions. The four commands Peter gives, are (1) to be of sound thinking and sober spirit, (2) to keep fervent in love, (3) be hospitable and (4) to serve one another with the gift he received from God. 

> therefore, be of sound thinking and sober *spirit* for the purpose of prayer.

Note that the **purpose [is] prayer**. Prayer is the one thing that brings us most into contact with God, His feelings and His will. It is in the darkest periods—and literally so for the persecuted Christians in prisons with no lights—where Christians pray to God and can  still see the light and are fed, if you will, by His power, despite the fact they often times literally have no food at all. This verse explains the power of their prayers, namely because of **sound thinking and [a] sober spirit**. In Communist prisons like where Richard Wurmbrand was imprisoned, or the one in which Adoniram Judson #tags/people/christian/martyrs/missionaries was, one is made sober with ample time to think. This is exactly the problem in Western society and why prayer, and with it the power of the Spirit, is declining, namely because Christians spend less time on (sound) thinking and their spirit is filled up all the time. Without these (a good) prayer is impossible. 

**Prayer.** Lord, help me to keep my thinking sound, remove all hatred, violence and anger from my thoughts and all longing for—or worries due to—materials and the needs of this world. Remove all my passions for everything that is on this world and replace them with a burning heart for that which is part of your will and purpose. Make my spirit sober, even if it requires to be sober from all access to those things that feed the fleshly body, such as food, social contact and pleasure. Allow my flesh to suffer, so that I can come closer to you, knowing that this is painful, but that this is also the only way to get closer to You. 
Amen.

"Stay healthy and take care of your body, for health prevents a multitude of illnesses." This allows me to understand the meaning of this verse better. Which sins does love not cover then? True love can cover for all of these shortcomings and is more than sufficient. What can drive love away is unrighteousness, for love is from God and God loves righteousness, and so it must hate unrighteousness and injustice. If a person then is unrighteous, the Christian can and should still love this person, but it cannot cover his sin, unless the person repents. This applies to every sin, except the sin against the Holy Spirit. Every sin is covered and forgiven when the person genuinely repents of it.

The third command is to be hospitable. The first two were related to spiritual matters—to pray and to love one another—by why is hospitality brought up here? These differences are addressed in an *unhospitable* manner which make the other feel uncomfortable. Hospitability goes further than hosting a meal at one's house, although that is also necessary for good relations. In the end-times this refers to letting other Christians stay in their own house, because there will be many who will flee their own country due to persecution, but also to let the person feel welcome at this place which is not his home. This is the only way for Christians to work together against the powers of this world. 

> **10** As each one has received a gift, employ it in serving one another as good stewards of the manifold grace of God—

Every Christian needs to **employ** his **gift**, it is not meant to stay idle and be unused, nor is it meant to serve and enrich oneself, but rather it is meant to serve **one another**. 
What does it mean to be **stewards of [...] grace**? I can understand the concept of being being a steward of a house or even of a person. Parents are, for example, stewards of their children. The child has been delivered into their care for a number of years and they have to show accountability for it in the end. But how can one be a steward of grace? Grace is not something that can change, like a house. A house can become messy and dirty when one does not take care of it, but this is not so with grace. In the phrase "good stewards of the manifold grace of God", the prepositional phrase "of the manifold grace of God" does not indicate the domain or responsibility over which the stewards have control, as in "a steward of a house." Instead, it functions attributively, describing the nature or quality of the stewardship. In this sense, "grace" serves to characterize the kind of stewardship being exercised.
This is analogous to the phrase "an angel of light", where "of light" does not signify the realm in which the angel operates, as in "an angel of death", but rather serves to qualify or describe the angel's essence or attributes. Similarly, the "grace" in the original sentence characterizes the stewardship as being marked by, or derived from, the manifold grace of God, rather than defining an object or domain under stewardship.
The **manifold grace of God** refers to the grace which He has poured out on us in so many ways, first of all through the gift of His Son and the accompanying possibility to be with Him in Heaven for eternity. Secondly in all the things we have received on Earth, from nature and the technology found within in, all the way even to computers, which are—in fact—nothing else but implementations of that which is found in nature[^2].

> ### Sharing the Suffering of Christ
> **1Pet. 4:12**   Beloved, do not be surprised at the fiery trial among you, which comes upon you for your testing, as though some strange thing were happening to you.

The Christian is promised only a few things for sure on this Earth, and one of those is that he will be tested and tried, the devil will attack him because he does not belong to this world (cf. 1 John 3:1). Jesus also promised that the Son of Man would not have a place to sleep or even have a rock for a pillow.

> **13** But to the degree you are sharing the sufferings of Christ, keep on rejoicing, so that also at the revelation of His glory you may rejoice with exultation.

The **rejoicing** of the Christian should be **to the degree He is sharing in the sufferings of Christ**. This principle is seen throughout the New Testament, for example in the Beatitudes (Matthew 5) and in Jesus' parables and stories, such as that of Lazarus and the Rich Man. God's principle of *those who are last will be first* is shown throughout the whole Bible—He protects the weak (the poor, the blind, the lame, the orphans and the widows) and His chosen ones have almost always been the youngest ones or those who were mistreated (e.g. Jakob, Joseph, Hannah, David). 
The secret of how one gets to know God is *through suffering*. #biblestudy/discovery/godshiddenplans It is that simple. Jesus already revealed this secret when saying that for the rich person to enter the Kingdom of God, is just as hard as for a camel to go through eye of a needle. #todo/opzoeken/reference Why would God have created a world where a rule like this governs? Throughout life we see that certain principles always apply. Good conquers evil. Justice will prevail. Long-term benefits are always better than short-term benefits. Perseverance leads to building character. God created these principles because He is *the embodiment* of those principles. Even though there was no evil in the beginning, nor injustice or even time—before the creation of time, so the term "long-term" did not even apply then—this is who God *is*. Now it is not that suffering itself is something that God likes, for when He created this world there was no suffering at all. However, when control of this world was given to the Satan, he had power over everything on this Earth—provided that God allowed it—and, spiritually seen, over all flesh. But *suffering is outside of his power*! 
Let me illustrate this. The Satan controls this world through our weak points, which is our own will. When someone is hungry, he can be controlled with food. If he is afraid of something, he can be controlled with that. Every form of control works with what the person wants. This is why Buddhists believe that when one does not want, he will not suffer, but it is impossible to not want anything on this Earth, so their theory cannot be put into practice. Furthermore, if one were to live this way, he would never have joy in his life, other than becoming a Buddha in the afterlife, but that would not even be a certainty, and in practice we do not see this joy in Buddhists. #tags/religion/buddhism
The Satan uses our flesh to exert his control. From a young age on, we have been given over to our selfish needs. In the West the Satan uses the world's materials to control our lives' paths to the career we want, the person we want to marry or the person we want to become, one with fame, wealth or power, or all three. In other parts of the countries, few become wealthy, but they do so by suppressing the poor, such as in Brazil, who then become vengeful. Satan then uses their wrath to lead them into criminality.  No matter in what situation, *everyone's* ways are controlled by the Satan at some point in time. When one suffers, there are only two outcomes—the person rejoices or his flesh takes control and exerts its needs. #questions/language/english/spelling  In the latter case, the Satan now has a hold, but in the former case, the Satan does not and can only cause the person more suffering in hope of getting a hold. 
And so it is with the Christian, when he rejoices in suffering, he is not under the control of the Satan. The Christian may be blessed by the wealth of the world, but as long as it is not his longing, the Satan has no control over him. When the Christian is held by Satan, his view of God is clear. That is why it so often that the poor and weak come to Christ, in Jesus' times, but clearly shown in the revivals of the past centuries as well. Another phenomenon is that during the persecution of Christians and of the people, such as in Communism #tags/ideology/communism, Christianity does not shrink but actually *grows*.

> **15** Make sure that none of you suffers as a murderer, or thief, or evildoer, or a troublesome meddler; **16** but if *anyone suffers* as a Christian, he is not to be put to shame, but is to glorify God in this name.

This speaks for itself, but at the same time every Christian is guilty of sin. Perhaps it is just I, but I cannot help but see the Christian's suffering as a consequence of his sin. Even though many Christians have not killed or sinned otherwise, they suffer as a murderer or as one of the things mentioned in this verse. Joseph, for example, did not adulterate, but of course he was sinful, for he was a man. The Christian should **glorify God** because suffering is a sign that the judgment has begun, and if it has begun with us, this means that we are part of the house of God, because the judgment **[begins] with the house of God**. > **18** AND IF IT IS WITH DIFFICULTY THAT THE RIGHTEOUS IS SAVED, WHAT WILL BECOME OF THE GODLESS MAN AND THE SINNER? **19** Therefore, those also who suffer according to the will of God must entrust their souls to a faithful Creator in doing good.
