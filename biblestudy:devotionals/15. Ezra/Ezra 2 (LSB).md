This is the last of three mentions of **Barzillai the Gileadite** who appeared first in 2 Samuel 17:27. Even though this first reference to him was very short, where a number of people brought David and his men to eat—although the list of food was elaborate—it was Barzillai who was mentioned two more times later on in the Bible, and not the others. Barzillai was also mentioned for his humility, where David thanked him and rewarded him by letting him live with him in Jerusalem, but he declined instead (2 Samuel 19:31ff). 
Despite being a Gileadite and the fact that these people entered Israel through deception (#todo/opzoeken/reference Joshua ...) and remained servants throughout their stay, Barzillai accepted David as his own and true king and was truly faithful to him all the way to his death and even during David's temporary defeat by his son Absolom.
In the same way this is reflected by the Christian life and how all Christians entered the kingdom. Everyone who calls himself Christian has sinned against God. He might not have tried to deceive God, but he still sinned against Him and deceived *himself* instead by thinking he was God. 
However, it was by the grace of Joshua (cf. Yeshua) that the Gileadites could stay in the land, and it is by the grace of Yeshua that we could enter and stay His kingdom. It is as servants that the Gileadites entered the land—and not as sons or even as citizens—and it is as servants that we have entered God's house.
