### Ezra Sends for Levites
> **Ezra 8:15**   Now I gathered them at athe river that runs to Ahava, where we camped for three days. And I 1observed the people and the priests, but I bdid not find any Levites there.

It seems that none of the Levites who had been exiled chose to return to Israel. Perhaps this was due to the fact that they only had small cities of their own instead of their own region like the other eleven tribes and had to much work to do in the temple, even though they were paid for it by the people and not everyone had physical labor, but some were musicians or had other jobs. It was possible, however, and it had happened before that the Levites were not paid for their work and hence returned to work their lands, which happened in Israel before Ezra's leadership. It would be unlikely that this information had reached the ears of the Levites in Persia, but either way, there could be many other reasons for them not to return.

### God’s Protection Sought
> **Ezra 8:21**   Then I called for aa fast there at bthe river of Ahava, that we might ==chumble== ourselves before our God to ==seek== from Him a 1direct journey for us, our little ones, and all our possessions. 

The key to seeking God is to **humble ourselves**. It is not the **fast** itself—that is the deprivation of food and other things—that brings us closer to God, but the realization that God is greater—that is, He is *above* us—and that we have accomplished—that is, that which is *below* us—is only there because God accomplished it *through* us (cf. Philippians 2:13), which is the definition of fasting. In other words,  God accomplished everything above and below and around us. Realizing this will make us humble. Only the *pure in heart* will see Him (cf. Matthew 5:8), and humility is a condition to this purity. #todo/opzoeken/reference als die bestaat. And only those who **seek** God can see Him, and only those who can see Him can *find* God.
Note that Ezra sought **a direct [or safe] journey** for three targets, themselves, the **little ones, and all [of their] possessions**. There is no shame in praying for oneself and for one's possessions. Most of these possessions were material meant for the house of God, but some of it was their own, such as clothing and money which they needed to build a new living with in the land of Israel. Ezra was not holding on to these possessions and deeming the importance of it higher than the material value. He knows that God can accomplish everything without our money—just like God let Artaxerxes provide for everything for their journey, so God could also provide for their needed supplies in the land itself—but at the same time he knows that he can also ask for the "regular way". The regular way is that people work and make money and with this money buy food. We can ask for God to provide a job, and that we will have fair wages accompanying this job. We are allowed to ask for our daily bread (cf. Matthew 6:11), and we are allowed to ask for protection of that with which we buy our daily bread, such as the protection of our bodies, of our possessions—which are actually God's possessions and merely under our *stewardship*. #church/material/sermon

> **22** For I was ashamed to ask from the king for a military force and horsemen to help us against the enemy on the way, because we had said to the king, “aThe ==hand of our God== is upon all those who seek Him, for *their* ==good==, but bHis strength and His anger are against all those who cforsake Him.” **23** So we fasted and sought our God concerning this*matter*, and He 1awas ==moved== by our entreaty.

When we use the expression **the hand of [(our)] God** we usually mean this for **good**, but in fact God's hand is on everything. Pray that God shows you His goodness, because if it is not His goodness or mercy it will be His wrath and justice that are shown to you. One way or another man will eventually see God's **strength and His anger**, but will either have these **for their good** or have these **against** them.
And so for this reason it is good to humble ourselves, for God will stand in the way of the proud #todo/opzoeken/reference but He will lift up those who are humble. Again, the emphases here are on humbling oneself and seeking God—humbling ourselves for our iniquities, and seeking Him to have his *good* hand on us. ==One should not forget about these when fasting.==
God **was moved by [the] entreaty** of the Jews. God *sees* people's hearts and *hears* their prayers. Why do we not pray more, if we know God hears us? Why do we not fast more often if we know that God is moved by it? Have we forgotten that He is going to give us everything we ask for? Perhaps it is these requirements that hamper us from asking more of God, for when we ask Him, we will be reminded by those ourselves. 

> **Ezra 8:24**   Then I 1set apart twelve of the leading priests, aSherebiah, Hashabiah, and with them ten of their brothers; **25** and I aweighed out to them bthe silver, the gold, and the utensils, the contribution for the house of our God which the king and chis counselors and his princes and all Israel present*there* had offered. **26** aThus I ==weighed== into their hands 1650 talents of silver, and silver utensils*worth* 100 talents, *and* 100 gold talents, 

Artaxerxes was willing to give from his own treasury up to 100 talents of silver and an unspecified amount of gold. It is not certain how much Ezra accepted from the treasury, but even if he had received the full amount of 100 talents, these 1750 talents of silver far exceed that number, which means that the people in the province of Babylon—so merely in one province—had donated generously. Just like during the Exodus from Egypt, the Israelites were loaded with precious metals before their departure but this time it was out of a freewill offering (see v.28 and Ezra 7:15-16).

> **27** and 20 gold bowls*worth* 1,000 1darics, and two utensils of fine shiny bronze, precious as gold. **28** Then I said to them, “aYou are ==holy== to Yahweh, and the butensils are holy; and the silver and the gold are a freewill offering to Yahweh, the God of your fathers. 

These priests and even their brothers were considered holy to Ezra. Perhaps it was because of their Aaronite lineage or due to a ritual cleansing. Personally I think that they were holy and sanctified because of this important task they had. As an example, a diplomat does not obtain his diplomatic status because of his works—even though he will have been selected because of his previous work—but it is only when the task as diplomat has been given to him that he obtains his diplomatic immunity. In the same way these priests, just like any man, were not holy to begin with, but it is only through a divine ordination that they become holy. 
Christians, too, have been ordained as priests (1 Peter 2:5-9) and we are holy because we have been sanctified, which means we have been set apart. Sanctification is still taking place because as humans we will never be completely pure, but the fact that we have been set apart has been done once for all.
