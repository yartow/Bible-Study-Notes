# Ezra 1 (LSB)
> Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia—in order to complete the word of Yahweh from the mouth of Jeremiah—Yahweh stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he had a proclamation pass throughout all his kingdom, and also put it in writing, 

Whereas many may think that the Israelites were blessed with a king as good as Cyrus—and indeed they were blessed—it was Cyrus who was actually blessed, for it was he who was blessed by getting to know God in the very first year of his reign, so that all his future years would be blessed as well. 
How great of a blessing it is to know the Lord and how much greater it is to have known the Lord ==sooner==! There are many Christians who have written great books or songs where they worship the Lord God Almighty, but all of them would have wished that they had known the Lord sooner. John Newton #tags/people/christian for example, had committed a great deal of atrocities towards slaves and although it was only through the realization of the vastness of his transgressions that he saw the *amazing grace* through which he was saved—on which he based his eponymous song—he, just like any other Christian, by *repentance*, longed for holiness and purification of all unrighteousness (1 John 1:9). 

The historian Josephus #tags/people —but this was also according to Jewish tradition—wrote that it was Daniel who told Cyrus of Isaiah's prophecy (Isaiah 44:28) by the time he came to power in Babylon in 539 BC (he had already come to power in the countries around it) and that as a consequence he converted and proclaimed these verses.

> saying: “Thus says Cyrus king of Persia, ‘Yahweh, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and He has appointed me to build Him a house in Jerusalem, which is in Judah.
> Ezra 1:1-2 LSB

Cyrus does not explicitly state that Yahweh is his god, but he does acknowledge Him at least as the God of heaven and—possibly—the greatest god as well, and the One Who has **given [him] all the kingdoms**. In a *very specific* sense it is not bad to have other gods beside Yahweh as long as Yahweh's stays the biggest God whom one obeys. This would not contradict the First Commandment in Exodus 20:3 for if any *so-called "god"* ever demanded anything from his followers which would go against God's commands, the latter's command would overrule the former anyway. It would be like obeying the authorities, such the government and leaders of an organization, but never disregarding God's commands (cf. Romans 13:1-7). By definition, however, these authorities and these "gods" or idols would not be "gods" because a god is something one places as first priority in his life. The difference between a god and a government, however, is that a government does not ask of you to worship him and an idol and god does ask worship, praise and sacrifices. However, when one does not take heed of these activities because they are against God's commandments, *theoretically*—but I could be wrong here—this would be allowed. It needs to be noted that without these activities these others gods would not even be gods anymore for there would not even be any other activities. 
As far as I know, in practice, these are the only activities idolaters, such as Buddhists, do aside from living by Buddhist law, such as eating vegetarian for the sake of not killing more souls of reincarnated beings. One who believes in God, however, would not and could not believe in reincarnation because of the Biblical worldview of the afterlife. But if he were uneducated about this, merely *believing in reincarnation* would not be a sin.  
That being said, I do think that Cyrus worshipped other gods and had statues of these or artwork in other forms, and hence did not obey God's commands and thus violated the First and Second Commandment (Exodus 20:3:4). 

>  **3** ‘Whoever there is among you of all His people, may his God be with him! Let him go up to Jerusalem which is in Judah and rebuild the house of Yahweh, the God of Israel; aHe is the God who is in Jerusalem.

It is not that Cyrus limited God to a designated place—such as other religions did to either locations or phenomena, e.g. the Greeks and Romans considered Hades or Pluto the god of the underworld, Aphrodite or Venus the goddes of love and Ares or Mars the god of war, respectively—but he is rather emphasizing that God had *chosen* Jerusalem to be His city and that His house would be in **Jerusalem which is in Judah** and nowhere else. 
Cyrus did not disrespect God by building Him a house in Persia, no matter how beautiful or fertile that place would be. God does not care about wordly fertility. He is the One Who makes places desolate or fertile by a single word from His mouth or even with a mere thought. 
Making suggestions to God to change His mind because one thinks he knows better—no matter how well intended—always turn out to be detrimental. Peter rebuked Jesus for going His way to the cross and allowed the Satan to enter him (#todo/opzoeken/reference ); Uzzah (2 Samuel 6:6) tried to help God by holding the ark which was about the fall and was struck dead "for his irreverence". Even though both of their intentions may have been deemed to be good *by their own standards*, both Peter and Uzzah were being irreverent. Moses and Abraham, on the other hand—the latter even negotiating with God concerning the fate of this nephew Lot #todo/opzoeken/reference —were almost begging God and with the utmost reverence requesting God to change his mind, which is a whole other way of approaching God. Actually, they were not changing God's mind *per se* but rather asking *according to God's will*, for it is God's will that people are saved. 
We do not know whether Cyrus had proposed any request to **rebuild the house of Yahweh** in Persia, but it seems as if Cyrus showed instant obedience without negotiating and diminishing any of God's wishes nor showing any resistance—by returning all the vessels of the house of the Lord—but at the same time doing more than was asked, such as the decreeing the command in v.4.
