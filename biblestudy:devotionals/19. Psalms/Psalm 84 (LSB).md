> How lovely are Your dwelling places, O Yahweh of hosts! My soul has longed and even fainted for the courts of Yahweh; My heart and my flesh sing for joy to the living God. 

I do not know of any magnificent stories like that of Michaelangelo's #questions/language/dutch/spelling statue of David, which, upon revealing, shocked the women in the audience to that degree that some fainted and others exposed their bare breasts in lust and awe for the beauty of that statue. 
This is an example of pure idolatry by an idolatrous people, of course. Perhaps these stories are mere myths, but they could very well be true. Their behavior would not so much be explained by the beauty of that statue as much as that the people were used to idolizing statues. It was also not uncommon in Greek culture or religion to have sexual intercourse with statues, even in plain sight on the town square, as an offering to a god. #todo/opzoeken/history to a people with that kind of moral standard and which idolizes statues, while promoting these to gods, it is not a strange thing at all to bare one's chest. 
The stories of the temple and the sight thereof are, fortunately, of a much lesser degree, and more realistic. The temple itself is not idolized, regardless of its beauty, but seen as the holiest of places. Unlike mosques, where everyone has to take off their shoes and wash their hand and feet because of the holy presence of God, this was not required, yet what was required was a holy and circumcised heart. This in itself is also a big difference with other religions. In movies the leader of mosques know about the whereabouts of terrorists and sometimes even hide them. This has not been made up out of nothing. 
Granted, churches have traditionally been safe havens for criminals as well, but only so that the criminal would be granted due process instead of execution. 

Contrasting the sexually immoral behavior of the women in the time of Michelangelo, the sons of Korah's expression is a holy one, to worship by means of singing out of gratitude and awe and not for the building itself but for the presence of God. 

> Even the bird has found a home, And the swallow a nest for herself, where she sets her young, At Your altars, O Yahweh of hosts, My King and my God. 

Literally, the bird has his home under the roof of the temple just like almost every other building has birds such as swallows or pigeons under its roof. Still literally speaking, (other) birds are also placed on the altar for the offering. No matter how cruel that may sound, the birds have still found their home, for what else could be a better home for them, and where else should *we* be but on the altar of God? #church/material/sermon

> How blessed are those who dwell in Your house! They are ever praising You. Selah.

Even though God's physical house is not in our midst anymore, for His temple has been destroyed, we—Christians—know that we are not in His absence but in His presence, for we are His temple and His Spirit resides in us. 
When David wrote his psalms, he had never been *in* God's house, for no one was allowed in the temple except the priests and levites. The sons of Korah, who wrote this specific psalm, might have been in there because they were Levites. That is also why v.10 speaks of the temple's courts, which were the places around the temple, for the people were only allowed to view the temple from a distance. In the third and inner most court the people—the men—could see the whole temple directly, albeit it only the outside of it, but nevertheless all of it and even walk around it. In the second and first courts, on the other hand, the people's view was mostly blocked by the walls in front of them and they could only see the top part of the temple. In Herod's temple a balcony was placed for the women so that they were able to enter the third court and had an even better view of the temple, but still they were tens of meters away from it.
Can you imagine that someone would say he had rather be on this court, viewing, and never touching God's temple, and only one day in his whole lifetime, than anywhere else—let us say the best place in the world—for a thousand days? This shows the power of the glory of God. 
Moses had seen this as well. He was not near God at all. God was in His presence and was not even showing His back to him—that did not happen until later. #todo/opzoeken/reference In the beginning God did not show anything of Himself but was *only in his presence* and yet Moses' face was so bright that everyone kept staring at it. 
It is as if one person standing behind a veil meets another person, such as in a confessional room #questions/language/english/spelling biechtkamer in Catholic churches, and after the person leaves the room, anyone else would know he had been there. How would this be possible? Perhaps a very strong scent would pass on to the other, merely by being in that person's presence. 
An example that we understand slightly better is when people come back from conference, fully filled with the Holy Spirit. They have been even *more* in the presence of God. The presence of God is not only binary, i.e. he is present or not, but it is also qualitative, as in that God can be *more* present, show more of Himself and where the person sees and hears more of God, which is the case with prophets such as Abraham, Moses and Samuel. 

> How blessed is the man whose strength is in You, In whose heart are the highways to Zion! Passing through the valley of Baca they make it a spring; The early rain also wraps it up with blessings. They go from strength to strength, Each one of them appears before God in Zion. ==tweede zin nog niet behandeld==

In this verse as well the proximity near God is desired. People are called **blessed** when they are going to God's city of **Zion**. They aren not even in the city itself and already they are blessed. They are even blessed when they pass through the dry #todo/opzoeken **valley of Baca**. Even if they were at the edge of city, it would still be long walk to the temple mount (depending on the roads they had, about 45 minutes) and they would not even be *in* the temple yet, but now they are far away from even the city, and yet they are blessed. They are even called blessed in the very first line here, merely by having God's strength in them, no matter how far away they are. The highways to Zion were very long and extended all the way to China in the East and Morocco in the West, and to Europe through Syria in the North and all the way down to East Africa (Ethiopia and further #todo/opzoeken ?). See also notes on [Isaiah 29:8](bear://x-callback-url/open-note?id=FA328286-7D5E-4F04-9AE1-E1E25DC9B1F2-13631-0000E508D93D61DC). But there were also places where there was no highway to Zion. Even there, however, one could be blessed and still be in the presence of God, by having God's **strength** in him. 

These people, pilgrims one might say, on the way to Zion, are so much blessed by their purpose and with the presence of God that they make the valley of Baca a spring. Perhaps God literally made it a spring, when they passed through it. They had joy because they were looking forward for the feast at the temple. 
Christians who feel the joy of the Lord will express this as well. A friend of mine, let us call him Michael, who was saved years ago, still enjoyed the presence of the Lord so much that he was quite frequently singing. I did not know this at first, but one day another friend of mine told me that Michael was singing on the way to their weekly evangalization. I asked him why he was singing and if there was a reason for that, but my friend simply told me that Michael was "always" singing. 
Another example is of when I was working with my friend Wim, who had recently lost the tips of the fingers on his right hand during an accident at work, and now needed help with finishing his work to pave tiles in someone's garden. While he was working, he also was singing to the Lord. There was no sign of sadness or regret concerning his situation. 

**Example.** How my wife and I, when we visit empty restaurant, they often become filled afterwards. 
But even if this is not meant literally, it is known that Christians through their God bring joy to places so dark where human and earthly joy cannot reach. Take for example the lives of Saint Patrick and Richard Wurmbrand. #todo/nogaftemaken
