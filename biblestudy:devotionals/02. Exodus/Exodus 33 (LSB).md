> ### Moses and the Tent of Meeting
> **Ex. 33:1**   Then Yahweh spoke to Moses, “Go! Go up from here, you and the people whom you have brought up from the land of Egypt, to the land of which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, saying, ‘To your seed I will give it.’ **2** “And I will send an angel before you, and I will drive out the Canaanite, the Amorite, the Hittite, the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite. **3** “*Go up* to a land flowing with milk and honey; for I will not go up in your midst because you are a stiff-necked people, lest I consume you on the way.”
> 
> **Ex. 33:4**   Then the people heard this sad word and went into mourning; and none of them put on his ornaments.

It is a good sign that the people went into mourning. The Ninevites, too, when hearing God's warning through the prophet Jonah, dressed in sackcloth and put ashes on their heards and fasted and through this showed true repentance and delayed God's judgment on them (Jonah 4 #todo/opzoeken/reference ). If the Israelites had been joyful because of the fulfillment of this promise of going into the Holy Land, it would have shown that they only cared about the promised *land* and not about the *presence of God*, which is the actual promise. This promise will ultimately be fulfilled after the end-time and on the New Heaven and Earth (see Revelation 21:3). #tags/theology/eschatology/newworld

> **5** So Yahweh said to Moses, “Say to the sons of Israel, ‘You are a stiff-necked people; should I go up in your midst for one moment, I would consume you. So now, put off your ornaments from you, that I may know what I shall do with you.’”

The reason Yahweh did not **go up in [their] midst** was because He would have consumed them. This was a legitimate warning, but the people still insisted that He went with them. Eventually Yahweh did go up and many more were consumed due to their lack of faith in defeating the inhabitants of Canaan (#todo/opzoeken/reference Numbers ...) and their constant mumbling and complaining (Numbers 11:1). 
If the God had not gone up in the Israelites' midst, the Israelites would not have been consumed directly by God's wrath, but two things could have happened: the Israelites could have changed their ways and they would have been accepted by God, just like pagans can enter the kingdom of God. Or, they could have continued in their own ways and still be destroyed by God, but perhaps in a different way, not by *a consumption by God*, on a big scale event, such as by the plague where many thousands died on a single day, or by an Assyrian or Babylonian captivity, but perhaps in a slower way, through extinction, never to be heard of nor known by future generations. > **Ex. 33:7**   Now Moses used to take the tent and pitch it outside the camp, a good distance from the camp, and he called it the tent of meeting. And everyone who sought Yahweh would go out to the tent of meeting which was outside the camp.

This is not the Tabernacle, which is also called the **tent of meeting**. To distinguish the two, I will use capital initials, i.e. Tent of Meeting, when referring to the Tabernacle. 

>  **8** And it happened whenever Moses went out to the tent, that all the people would arise and stand, each at the entrance of his tent, and gaze after Moses until he entered the tent. **9** And it happened whenever Moses entered the tent, that the pillar of cloud would descend and stand at the entrance of the tent; and Yahweh would speak with Moses.

This does not mean that God was only present where Moses was. God is, of course, omnipresent at all times, but He indicated His presence more clearly near Moses, so that the  people could be certain that his authority was given by God. 

> **10** And all the people would see the pillar of cloud standing at the entrance of the tent. And all the people would arise and worship, each at the entrance of his tent. **11** Thus Yahweh used to speak to Moses face to face, just as a man speaks to his friend. Then Moses would return to the camp, and his attendant Joshua the son of Nun, a young man, would not depart from the tent.

Joshua is a great example of a godly man. From his young age on he already followed Moses and stood up courageously against the people. Here, he does not follow Moses but stayed behind at **the tent**, for following God is even more important than following any man of God. 

> ### Moses and the Glory of Yahweh
> **Ex. 33:12**   Then Moses said to Yahweh, “See, You say to me, ‘Bring up this people!’ But You Yourself have not let me know whom You will send with me. Moreover, You have said, ‘I have known you by name, and you have also found favor in My sight.’

The first question Moses had, was *whom* he needed to bring up to Israel. At the same time though, Moses argues that if he were to bring only *part* of the people, because the rest was stubborn and deserved to die in the desert, God would leave His own people behind. 
God answers only Moses' second question (in v.13) and does not say anything concerning his first question. It is not for Moses to know whom God selects or not, and it was also not the time for Moses to know. A short time later, after the spying on Canaan (see Numbers ... #todo/opzoeken/reference  ) the Israelites would sign their own death warrant. They would judge and banish themselves to a forty-year residence in the desert without any hope of entering the Holy Land. But right now, it was not for Moses to know the answer to his question nor what would happen in the future. 
Notice that God did not even have to say that Moses did not or should not ask. He can simply ignore a question.  #church/material/sermon A preacher once told me that God always answers a question and his answer is either a *yes* or a *no*, or *wait*. In this case it was a *wait* but God did not *say*, "Wait." We also should not expect from God that He will always answer all of our questions—we cannot even expect a "mail delivery notification"—we have no right to require that of Him. *If* He answers us, it is pure grace. Note, however, that God does answer Moses' question in a different way in v. 14, where he answers, "**My presence shall go with you**", implying that all that Moses *needs to* know is that God's presence is with him. 

> **13** “So now, I pray You, if I have found favor in Your sight, let me know Your ways that I may know You, so that I may find favor in Your sight. See also, that this nation is Your people.” **14** And He said, “My presence shall go *with you*, and I will give you rest.” 

Moses' request here is to **know [His] ways [so] that [He] may know** God. It is through God's *ways* that man gets to know Him. Indeed, this applies to man—a man can only be truly known through his ways (although man is able to present himself as better than he truly is). However, what Moses really desires, is to *know God personally*, and he thinks that by knowing His ways he will get to know God. Isaiah writes, however, that God's ways are higher than man's, so even when we look at how God works—even when looking in hindsight—we often still do not understand why God did something, nor understand or see His goodness in each situation. 
Moses' *ultimate* request is to **find favor in [God's] sight**. When one loves another, his goal is to please the other and let everything in his sight or surroundings be pleasing to him. Suppose a man loves a woman and wants her to see the most pleasant things of the world. He would then remove all the withered flowers and ugly paintings, for these would not be pleasing to her. In the same way, Moses wants to be pleasing to God and he knows he can only be that if he *knows* Him better and knows what pleases Him, for if he does not know God, he cannot know what to change or remove from God's sight. #biblestudy/discovery

> **15** Then he said to Him, “If Your presence does not go *with us*, do not lead us up from here. **16** “Indeed, how then can it be known that I have found favor in Your sight, I and Your people? Is it not by Your going with us, so that we, I and Your people, may be distinguished from all the *other* people who are upon the face of the earth?”

Moses' main argument to pursuade Yahweh to go with him and the people is that other peoples have to be able to *distinguish* the Israelites from them. This distinction is the main driver of God's commandments, not for the sake of being different, but to let God be made known to the world. The world cannot see God, so the God's people must show this to the world. This is the meaning of "Be holy, for I am holy". The world cannot see the holiness of God, and therefore the Israelites need to be holy, so that the world can differentiate between what is holy and what is not. In short, if the Israelites hold by the commandments, the world will see rituals and ceremonies that do not occur in any other religion or culture in the world, and they will see a blessing that no other people has been blessed with. 
Unfortunately, we have seen in history that even when the Israelites were abiding by the commandments and winning wars and growing in numbers, having beautiful daughters (e.g. Esther and (daughter of Jacob and other references #todo/opzoeken/reference ) and very talented sons (e.g. Joseph and Daniel), the people became jealous of the Israelites instead of revering God and worshipping him instead. #biblestudy/discovery

> **Ex. 33:17**   Then Yahweh said to Moses, “I will also do this thing of which you have spoken; for you have found favor in My sight, and I have known you by name.” 

Yaweh did not have to choose to go with the Israelites for He could have distinguished Himself through another people—a less stiff-necked people—and be represented by these people. However, God did this for Moses, because he had **found favor in [God's] sight**. So in other words, God chose to be represented by a people who would represent Him with less dignity and holiness than He deserved. #biblestudy/discovery 

> **18** Then Moses said, “==I pray You, show me Your glory!==” **19** And He said, “I Myself will make all My goodness pass before you, and I will proclaim the name of Yahweh before you; and I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and I will show compassion on whom I will show compassion.”

His *Name*—which I have written here with a capital *N*—is proclaimed in Exodus 34:6–7 and this proclamation is more than just saying out loud His name. His Name involves everything Who He *is* and *has done*, it is almost like but even more than one's reputation. Yahweh answered Moses' request by *showing* His **goodness**. God does not only **proclaim the name of Yahweh**, but also shows its worth, in this case by being **gracious** and showing **compassion**. His grace is shown here by giving Moses what he asked for—which Moses did not deserve—and His compassion is shown by letting Moses live despite the fact that *more of His presence* is shown to Moses. See also notes on vv. 20–23. #biblestudy/discovery

> **20** But He said, “You cannot see My face, for no man can see Me and live!” **21** Then Yahweh said, “Behold, there is a place by Me, and you shall stand *there* on the rock; **22** and it will come about, while My glory is passing by, that I will put you in the cleft of the rock and cover you with My hand until I have passed by. **23** “Then I will remove My hand, and you shall see My back, but My face shall not be seen.”

When we think about it, God is so holy that the sight of not only His **face**, but His **back** and even His **hand** would destroy a person. If we take the sun, for example, it is so deadly that even at a great distance its radiation would kill us on Earth if there were not an atmosphere that protects us. Would God's presence then not be much more deadly? Regardless of whether we see Him or not, merely His presence alone, even from outer space, would be sufficient to kill us. Therefore, what is signified by these verses is that God shows only certain parts of Himself (to us), and even those parts that He shows are shown with a mercy that keeps us alive. #biblestudy/discovery
