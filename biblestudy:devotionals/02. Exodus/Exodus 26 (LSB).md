> ### Instructions for Making the Tabernacle

![](Exodus%2026%20%28LSB%29/03-the-4-covering.gif)![](Exodus%2026%20%28LSB%29/%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%9B%D7%9F-%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%92-1411074854.jpg)![](Exodus%2026%20%28LSB%29/b71fe738199a66672d091b4eb6c2f9a0.jpg)

> **Ex. 26:1**   “Moreover you shall make the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twisted linen and blue and purple and scarlet *material*; you shall make them with cherubim, the work of a skillful designer.

This art could be seen from the inside of the Holy Place. This seems counterintuitive. Why would God create the most beautiful art, made of the most precious materials, and place this in a place where no one could see this, except for a small group of people—because only the Levites and Priests were allowed on the insideof the Holy Place—while placing on the outside (vv. 7,14) the least attractive animal skins which were visible to everyone? This also answers the question a friend of mine, who left the faith, concerning why God does not reveal Himself to the world (see also the same question Judas asked Jesus in John 14:22): God does reveal Himself, but only to those who seek Him, who love Him and do His Word.

> **2** “The length of each curtain shall be twenty-eight cubits, and the width of each curtain four cubits; all the curtains shall have the same measurements. **3** “Five curtains shall be joined to one another, and *the other* five curtains *shall be* joined to one another. 

It can clearly be seen that God is a god of order. He "likes" symmetry, repetition and precision and therefore has extensively detailed almost everything concerning the tabernacle. The two parts of five curtains—the number "five" indicating grace again, which we will see again in the fifty clasps, which is five times ten—are perhaps also split up to make it more practical for the curtain to be carried. Each curtain would have been about nine meters by six meters and would have weighed a lot. Even if there were carts—although I do not expect carts with wheels to have been practical in the desert sand—it would still be more practical when lifting these curtains in and out of the carts and to put these on top of the Holy Place. 

> **4** “And you shall make loops of blue on the edge of the outermost curtain in the one set, and likewise you shall make *them* on the edge of the curtain that is outermost in the second set. **5** “You shall make fifty loops in the one curtain, and you shall make fifty loops on the edge of the curtain that is in the second set; the loops shall be opposite each other. **6** “You shall make fifty clasps of gold, and you shall join the curtains to one another with the clasps so that the tabernacle will be a unit.

The number fifty is five times ten, where ten is human perfection. Grace, indicated by the number five, is given through the Holy Place, where offers are made for God, but it is only perfect in human terms, for it must be offered continuously and will never end until the day we die. That is, until there is better Holy Place, which is Jesus Christ. The Lord God the Almighty and Jesus Christ *are* the Temple (see Revelation 21:22). No description of any tent covering is given there, but if there were one, it might have thirty-five clasps of gold, because seven is the godly number of perfection. 

> **Ex. 26:7**   “Then you shall make curtains of goats’ *hair* for a tent over the tabernacle; you shall make eleven curtains in all. 
> **8** “The length of each curtain *shall be* thirty cubits, and the width of each curtain four cubits; the eleven curtains shall have the same measurements. **9** “And you shall join five curtains by themselves and the *other* six curtains by themselves, and you shall double over the sixth curtain at the front of the tent.

**Eleven** is the number of disorder, because after perfection (e.g. in Eden) disorder began through man, and this curtain is divided into two smaller parts made up of five and **six** parts, where the number six is the number of man. #biblestudy/numerology

> **10** “You shall make fifty loops on the edge of the curtain that is outermost in the one set, and fifty loops on the edge of the curtain *that is outermost in* the second set.
> **Ex. 26:11**   “You shall make fifty clasps of bronze, and you shall put the clasps into the loops and join the tent together so that it will be one unit.

Interestingly, there is no degression from gold to silver to bronze.

> **12** “The overlapping part that is left over in the curtains of the tent, the half curtain that is left over, shall lap over the back of the tabernacle. **13** “The cubit on one side and the cubit on the other, of what is left over in the length of the curtains of the tent, shall lap over the sides of the tabernacle on one side and on the other to cover it.

The first curtain was 28 cubits **in the length**—because the curtain was made up of long strips—while the height and width of the Holy Place were both ten cubits—and was one cubit short of touching the ground. This means that in order to cover the left and right side walls and the top, thirty cubits were needed. This second curtain was thirty cubits long and therefore fully covered the first curtain by an extra cubit on the sides. This is known by the author who described this as **the cubit on one side and [...] on the other, of what is left over**. 
The same principle applied for the back part of the tent. The first curtain did fully cover the back of the tent, as the Holy Place was thirty cubits long, and the curtain was forty cubits *wide* (remember that the first curtain's length is thirty and its width forty cubits). The second curtain, however, was forty-four cubits wide and fully covered the first one.

> **14** “You shall make a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red and a covering of porpoise skins above.

Even though the sizes of these skins are not given, it is clear that the curtains below need to be fully covered. The picture above shows the meaning of each curtain, which I will not go further into, but the red rams' skin indicates the blood that must flow to atone for our sins, and of course this needs to cover everything. 

The word **porpoise** is translated as *badger* in some Bible versions, but in fact it is a kind of porpoise which is now almost extinct that lives in the Red Sea (dooga? #todo/opzoeken ). It is conceivable that the materials needed would be in possession or present in the vicinity.

> ### The Boards of the Tabernacle
![](Exodus%2026%20%28LSB%29/d6599c8865e2ae14b9e15e0100d49ae6-3054577168.jpg)

> **Ex. 26:15**   “Then you shall make the boards for the tabernacle of acacia wood, standing upright. 

A great amount of precious acacia wood was needed for this part of the Tabernacle. See notes on [[Exodus 25 (LSB)|Exodus 25:2]]. 

> **16** “Ten cubits *shall be* the length of each board, and one and a half cubits the width of each board. **17** “*There shall be* two tenons for each board, fitted to one another; thus you shall do for all the boards of the tabernacle. **18** “You shall make the boards for the tabernacle: twenty boards for the south side. **19** “You shall make forty bases of silver under the twenty boards, two bases under one board for its two tenons, and two bases under another board for its two tenons;

I do not understand the reason why two **bases** are used. The base would seem to be more stable when it is heavy and long. One reason these bases where kept short, is probably because the ground in the desert was not flat. A longer base will then influence the stability of the wall. Another reason might be the significance of the number forty (ten times four), which is the number for a period. It is also the number of years the Israelites resided in the desert. #biblestudy/questions/exodus

![](Exodus%2026%20%28LSB%29/Board-and-Socket-157x300.jpg)

> **20** and for the second side of the tabernacle, on the north side, twenty boards, **21** and their forty bases of silver; two bases under one board and two bases under another board. **22** “For the rear of the tabernacle, to the west, you shall make six boards. 

Though the rear of the tabernacle only contained six boards, making up nine cubits instead of ten, the exterior width was still ten cubits because the boards on the north and south were each half a cubit thick. God had even thought of that, when indicating the thickness of the boards! 

> **23** “You shall make two boards for the corners of the tabernacle at the rear. **24** “They shall be separated beneath, but together at their completion at its top, at the first ring; thus it shall be with both of them: they shall form the two corners. **25** “There shall be eight boards with their bases of silver, sixteen bases; two bases under one board and two bases under another board.

![](Exodus%2026%20%28LSB%29/Rear%20Boards%20and%20rings.png)

The two boards in the **rear** corners were to make the structure more stable and were put at a slanted position so that the rear wall would not topple. It is not mentioned how the rear wall was connected to the side walls or how the boards were placed over the bars. One [explanation](https://thepatternonline.org/the-structure) is that the two extra boards at the rear, the bars and rings were on the *outside* of the tabernacle such as on the image below. In this way, the slanted boards would push the rear wall *in*. Another possibility is that the rings and bars were both on the in- and outside. Since everything was overlaid with gold, however, these were items that belonged on the inside of the Holy Place. ~~No gold was present on the outside~~ #todo/nogaftemaken , except at the front, where the pillars were covered with gold. However, anything on the outside was covered by the first and second curtain, so that makes it possible again. 
Another question is how the bars were placed such that they would not be in the way of the pillars. The image above provides a possible answer and a special base needed to be constructed for it, where the right side of the image depicts the west side or the outside of the tabernacle.
Note that on both impressions of the tabernacle below and above the number of rings is not correct. There are shown respectively three and two rings and bars, while there should be five on each side.

> **Ex. 26:26**   “Then you shall make bars of acacia wood, five for the boards of one side of the tabernacle, **27** and five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the side of the tabernacle for the rear *side* to the west. **28** “The middle bar in the center of the boards shall pass through from end to end. **29** “You shall overlay the boards with gold and make their rings of gold *as* holders for the bars; and you shall overlay the bars with gold.

The bars went through the rings and prevented the boards to move sideways. It is not clear how the **middle bar [passed] through from end to end**. It could have meant that this only applied for each wall and not that there was a middle bar that was bent in the corners, albeit with some kind of joint. 

> **30** “Then you shall erect the tabernacle according to its plan which you have been shown in the mountain.

Much thought was given to the structure and stability of the tabernacle as well, despite the fact that it was mobile. Even if the tabernacle was placed there only for a short time, a few weeks #todo/opzoeken ever happened? during this period it was still to be considered as the holiest place on Earth, and a shamble and sloppy erected building was not sufficiently holy as a place to bring glory to God. 

> **Ex. 26:31**   “You shall make a veil of blue and purple and scarlet *material* and fine twisted linen; it shall be made with cherubim, the work of a skillful designer. **32** “You shall hang it on four pillars of acacia overlaid with gold, their hooks *also being of* gold, on four bases of silver.

Logically, the bases of the pillars leading to the Holiest of Holies would be made of gold, given that the bases of the pillars of the [[Exodus 27 (LSB)/The Court of the Tabernacle|Court of the Tabernacle]] were made of bronze, and that of the Holy Place were made of silver. But, as always, we do not always understand God's logic. #biblestudy/questions/exodus

> **33** “You shall hang up the veil under the clasps, and you shall bring in the ark of the testimony there within the veil; and the veil shall separate for you the holy place and the Holy of Holies.

The difference between between this veil and the screen at the front, aside from its function, is that this had ornaments of **cherubim** on it. I think this depicted the place where God was and that it was therefore surrounded and protected by cherubim, just like the gate to Eden, which also was at the east (Genesis 3:24). Note that there were four pillars and three visible parts of the veil—though being one part—indicating the Trinity, which is also what the number three stands for.

Also note that it is nearly impossible to tear the veil because it hangs loose on rings the slide over a bar.

> **35** “You shall set the table outside the veil and the lampstand opposite the table on the side of the tabernacle toward the south; and you shall put the table on the north side.

There were only two objects in the Holy Place, on the left side the **lampstand** and on the right side the **table** of Showbread. Because of the screen and the four curtains on top of the tabernacle it would nearly be impossible for any light from outside to enter. The only light visible would be that of the lampstand. This emphasizes the holiness of that place even more. No light—or anything else for that matter—from the world was allowed to enter the Holy Place and in the Holiest of Holies it would even be completely dark, but no one would be there anyway, except for one person for one day a year. 
As Christians our temple—our body in the first place, but actually everything else as well, our mind, heart and soul—must be clean and nothing from the world may enter it. Only the light of God may shine in our hearts. In practice this is hard to do if we are commanded to live *in* the world and evangelize *to* the world. 

Note also that Jesus said that He is the light and that He is the bread, the only two objects in the Holy Place. In the Ark in the Holiest of Holies there were only three objects: the budding almond branch of Aaron which indicates the priesthood, the golden jar with manna and the two tablets with on it the Law of Moses (or just the Ten Commandment #todo/opzoeken )(Hebrews 9:3). Jesus has replaced this priesthood by being the ultimate and forever living High Priest, He replaced the manna that needed to be replenished every  day by being the Living Word (and doing God's Word is bread #todo/opzoeken/reference ) and He fulfilled the Law. In fact, Jesus has replaced the whole Temple (Revelation 21:22). 

> **Ex. 26:36**   “You shall make a screen for the doorway of the tent of blue and purple and scarlet *material* and fine twisted linen, the work of a weaver. **37** “You shall make five pillars of acacia for the screen and overlay them with gold, their hooks *also being of* gold; and you shall cast five bases of bronze for them.

There were **five pillars** and behind it was one screen, although it looked like four separate screens. The number four indicates that of man, and indeed man is only allowed to enter the Holy Place because of grace (indicated by the *five* pillars). 
The **bases of bronze** of course indicate that the Court of the Men was less holy than what was in the Holy Place. The Holy Place itself had bases of silver, and the Holiest of Holies also has bases of silver, because it is adjacent to the Holy Place. There were no gold bases. Everything that touches anything outside of that place, becomes less holy. 
The logic does not completely follow suit, because then the silver bases of the walls of the Holy Place should have been made of bronze, because they were adjacent to the Court of Israel. #biblestudy/questions/exodus
