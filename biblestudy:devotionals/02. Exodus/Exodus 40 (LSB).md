> **Ex. 40:17**   Now it happened, ==in the first month of the second year, on the first *day* of the month==, the tabernacle was erected. 

Not coincidentally, the creation of all artefacts took about one year and the tabernacle was erected on *exactly* the start of the new year. The start of their calendar was a few days or weeks after they had left Egypt, or by definition it was the day or night (#todo/opzoeken ) that they left Egypt #todo/opzoeken/reference. God understands man and knows that he is forgetful, and has created feasts on days that are easy to remember. Most feasts are also on a new or full moon. > **18** Moses erected the tabernacle and laid its bases and set up its boards and inserted its bars and erected its pillars. **19** He spread the tent over the tabernacle and placed the covering of the tent on top of it, just as Yahweh had commanded Moses. 

Everything was done with the utmost care by Moses, **exactly as Yahweh had commanded Moses**. I can imagine that Moses inspected every part of the tabernacle during and after construction and was present during the creation of all the artefacts, because everything had to be—almost literally—perfect, in as far this was possible. Now the placement of all the freely standing objects, such as the table and the lampstand, there was no exact instruction written as to where these should stand, but Moses had seen it (perhaps in a vision) and had received instructions for this. So he was the only one who knew where everything needed to be placed *exactly*.

The specific order in which Moses put everything has soteriological (that is, it has to do with salvation) relevance #tags/theology/soteriology . Most noticeably, **the altar of burnt offering** is outside of the tent. This is not only for practical reasons (so that the air inside the tent stays clean), but also because the sacrifice needs to be made before one enters the tent. The remarkable thing is why **the laver** is needed *before* the sacrifice is offered. It would seem more logical that after the killing and burning of the offer, one would need to wash himself of all the blood, char and fat before being able to enter the Tent. 
The answer is that the one who sacrifices the offer is unclean himself and therefore needs to cleanse himself so that he will not make the offer unclean. Only the cleanest and best of animals can be sacrificed and only the cleanest people, in this case priests, can sacrifice these, which means that this job required a blameless character. 
The blood may seem dirty, and biologically seen it contains a lot of bacteria, but spiritually it is clean and cleanses every unclean thing, which is why it is sprinkled on the most important artefacts #todo/opzoeken/reference which exactly? The altars, the Ark and tabernacle on the inside are bloody, and the altar outside is sprinkled again with every new burnt or guilt offering. As far as I know this is never washed off, so the images shown of artist impressions with shiny bronze altars and horns is incorrect. Rather, the gruesome image of a blood-stained tabernacle should show the countless amount of blood from sacrificed animals that had been poured on it, indicating the vast greatness of the sin of the people. #biblestudy/discovery 

> **31** From it ==Moses== and Aaron and his sons washed their hands and their feet.

Despite the fact that Moses was not a priest, he could enter the Holy Place, do all the things mentioned above and washed his hands with the water from the laver. Remember that Moses was before the Law. He was not above the Law, but he was, by appointment of God, the one to give the Law and thus—by definition—*before* the Law.
There would be circular reasoning if there was a law to appoint a leader to set up the rules. For who would be the one to give this first law then? So that is why Moses was appointed, before the existence of the Law, so that he could pass on the Law to the Israelites. 
A valid question is whether Moses could also enter the Holy Place or even the Holies of Holies—which even the High Priest was not allowed to do except for one day a year—*after* **the glory of Yahweh [had] filled the tabernacle**. Moses had been in the presence of God on Mount Horeb, so "close" even that his face shone. Of course the only reason he did not die was because God *allowed* Moses to see (part of) Him and protected him (cf. Exodus 24:11, 33:22-23).

> **32** When they entered the tent of meeting, and when they approached the altar, they washed, just as Yahweh had commanded Moses. **33** He erected the court all around the tabernacle and the altar, and he put up the screen for the gateway of the court. Thus Moses finished the work.

Note that this, most likely, needed to happen *every time* the tabernacle was moved. Moses was responsible for the work and needed to check whether everything was set up correctly. 

> ### The Glory of Yahweh
> **Ex. 40:34**   Then the cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of Yahweh filled the tabernacle. **35** And Moses was not able to enter the tent of meeting because the cloud had dwelt on it, and the glory of Yahweh filled the tabernacle.

It was from this moment on that all the artefacts in the tabernacle were sanctified. They were to remain sanctified by the ongoing offerings with blood and incense, but initially it was through God's presence, the "touch"—if you will—of God's hand that sanctified everything. 
In the same way it is God's "touch" with His breath of life that gave life to a formerly unliving thing and sanctified it, to be meant for Him alone. Everything that is not used for God, must and will be destroyed. So it is with every vessel (see Leviticus #todo/opzoeken/reference ), if it is made unclean it must be destroyed or burned in order to cleanse it. Every vessel that could not be burned (that is, everything not made of metal) was to be destroyed. And so man, that is we, have been unfaithful to God and have been made unclean, and therefore we need to be cleansed by fire. But because man cannot be cleansed by fire, the only thing that remains for us is to be destroyed, for if man is burned, there is nothing left of him to be sanctified. No, there is one other way, if there were a man who could be burned, until all of his impurities were gone and he would be completely clean, then he would sanctified and be accepted by God again. No human person is able to undergo this burning, but there is One, Jesus Christ, Who underwent this suffering, who took upon Himself all the sins of man—no sin *entered* His body, but it was all on the outside—and suffered the scorching yet purifying fire for many hours. All of the impurities have been removed now. #church/material/sermon
