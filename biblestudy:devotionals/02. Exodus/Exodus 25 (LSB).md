# Exodus 25 (LSB)
> ### Contributions for the Sanctuary
> **Ex. 25:1**   Then Yahweh spoke to Moses, saying, **2** “Speak with the sons of Israel so that they take a contribution for Me; from every man whose heart is willing you shall take My contribution. **3** “And this is the contribution which you shall take from them: gold, silver, and bronze, **4** blue, purple, and scarlet *material*, fine linen, goat *hair*, **5** rams’ skins dyed red, porpoise skins, acacia wood, **6** oil for lighting, spices for the anointing oil and for the fragrant incense, **7** onyx stones, and setting stones for the ephod and for the breastpiece. **8** “And let them make a sanctuary for Me, that I may dwell among them. **9** “According to all that I am going to show you, *as* the pattern of the tabernacle and the pattern of all its furniture, just so you shall make *it*.

A voluntary **contribution** was asked for the building of the tabernacle, and fortunately there was literally more than enough (Exodus 36:7). God does specifically mention the materials He requires—not *any* contribution is accepted, but only those of the highest quality. 
One question that is easily answered is where all this material came from, which is from Egypt and probably partially gifted by the Egyptians upon their leave. Another question which is more difficult to answer is how much there was of each material. With two million Israelites, say five hundred thousand families, there would have been enough material to build the Tabernacle, however, would there have been enough oil to light the menorahs for forty years on straight? A conservative estimate would amount to forty thousand litres needed, which is less than 100 milliliters of oil per family. 
Where the wood came from, on the other hand, is even more difficult to answer. Where did all the wood come from to cook with and sacrifice? And where did the acacia wood come from to build the accessories #questions/language/english/spelling of the Tabernacle? #biblestudy/questions/exodus Though acacia trees grow in the desert of the Sinai peninsula, so far I have found only small trees and not those sufficient to provide the size of boards mentioned in the text.

> ### The Ark of the Testimony
> **Ex. 25:10**   “And they shall make an ark of acacia wood ==two and a half cubits long, and one and a half cubits wide==, and one and a half cubits high. 

This is approximately the the Golden ratio. #todo/nogaftemaken 

> **11** “You shall overlay it with pure gold, inside and out you shall overlay it, and you shall make a gold molding around it. **12** “You shall cast four gold rings for it and fasten them on its four feet, and two rings shall be on one side of it and two rings on the other side of it. **13** “You shall make poles of acacia wood and overlay them with gold. **14** “You shall put the poles into the rings on the sides of the ark, to carry the ark with them. **15** “The poles shall remain in the rings of the ark; they shall not be removed from it. 

Apparently the poles were removed before David brought back the Ark from the Philistines #todo/opzoeken/reference because when he brought these back, the Ark was put on a cart. If the poles had still been inside the rings, David surely would not have neglected to use these. 
This is the reason why we need to pay attention to small details that God commands us. Though the "sin" of neglecting this rule of keeping the **poles [...] in the rings of the ark** at all times is small, the consequences are *big* when one forgets it and not only can, but already *have* led to death. 
God created these rules to *remind* us. In the rest of this and the next books of the Torah we will see similar rules where the purpose is not always clear, especially the ceremonial laws and even more especially for us, who are reading these rules 3,500 years after they were written. But we know there *is* a reason, even if we do not understand it, for even though the Law does not *give* life, we do know that the words of God contain life (cf. e.g. John 6:63, 8:51). 


> **16** “You shall put into the ark the testimony which I shall give you.

The ark was not to be touched, and everything inside was to be untouched as well. The covenant included the Ten Commandments and the budding almond branch, indicating that Aaron's line was the priestly line. However, since we know that the ark has been allowed by God to be stolen #todo/opzoeken/reference by the Romans or Babylonians? In the list of items that Xerxes returned to the Temple there was no ark (cf. Ezra #todo/opzoeken/reference ), and since we know that the priestly line has also stopped, for God has changed the priesthood to one that has Jesus Christ as the first, only and eternal High Priest, this loss of the ark could symbolize this, but would it also symbolize the replacement of the Ten Commandments? This would seem strange. In a sense, yes, for God has always said that whoever loves Him will keep His words (or commandments) and He and the Son will make their home with this person and Jesus will manifest Himself to him (John 14:22,23). This description is similar to God's description of His covenant with the people of Israel—as mentioned to the patriarch Abraham (#todo/opzoeken/reference )—and with the Gentiles in the next world (cf. Revelation 21:3). The covenant is the same, but the priesthood has been replaced with a better priesthood and the Law has been replaced with the eternal Word that dwells in our hearts (cf. Colossians 3:16-17). 

> **Ex. 25:17**   “You shall make a ==mercy seat== of pure gold, two and a half cubits long and one and a half cubits wide. **18** “You shall make two cherubim of gold; make them of hammered work at the two ends of the mercy seat. **19** “Make one cherub at one end and one cherub at the other end; from *one piece* you shall make the mercy seat *with* the cherubim at its two ends. **20** “The cherubim shall have *their* wings spread upward, covering the mercy seat with their wings, with their faces toward one another; the faces of the cherubim are to be toward the mercy seat. **21** “You shall put the mercy seat on top of the ark, and in the ark you shall put the testimony which I will give to you. **22** “==There I will meet with you==; and from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubim which are upon the ark of the testimony, I will speak to you about all which I will command you for the sons of Israel.

> ### Bread of the Presence
> **Ex. 25:23**   “You shall make a table of acacia wood, two cubits long and one cubit wide and one and a half cubits high. **24** “You shall overlay it with pure gold and make a gold border around it. **25** “You shall make for it a rim of a handbreadth around *it*; and you shall make a gold border for the rim around it. 

It seems most logical that the *cubit* used here was the *short cubit*, because the [royal cubit](https://answersingenesis.org/noahs-ark/how-long-was-the-original-cubit/) was the short cubit plus a handbreadth, which is approximately 20 inches. Including this handbreadth as the rims, the ratio approaches the Golden Ratio again. The exact sizes of the short, long and royal cubit are unknown, as well as the handbreadth, but in the picture below we can see that using the conventional measurements we approach the Golden Ratio. In black the size of the table is drawn with the perfect ratio, which does not differ much visibly.

> **26** “You shall make four gold rings for it and put rings on the four corners which are on its four feet. **27** “The rings shall be close to the rim as holders for the poles to carry the table. **28** “You shall make the poles of acacia wood and overlay them with gold, so that with them the table may be carried. **29** “You shall make its dishes and its pans and its jars and its offering bowls with which to pour drink offerings; you shall make them of pure gold. **30** “You shall set the bread of the Presence on the table before Me at all times.

The bread was never to run out and always had to be replaced by fresh bread. When David and his arrived at the Temple and asked for food and weapons, he ate the old bread, which had just been replaced by new bread (
[[1 Samuel 21 (LSB)/David Takes Consecrated Bread|notes on 1 Samuel 21:6]]). 

[Biblical Toolbelt](https://www.biblicaltoolbelt.com/l/the-table-of-showbread-exodus25/) has two designs  of the Table of Showbread, but failed to incorporate one important fact, namely the weight of the bread. In the impression below the bread is formed as rectangular prisms, placed in two rows of six each. Seeing the table was about 50 centimeters wide, this would mean that the bread was really dense, which is unlikely. Given our current type of bread, which is about 0.5 kg per loaf, the consecrated bread would have been about twice as long, twice as wide and twice as high as our current loafs. So the second impression below of Model B makes more sense.

> ### The Golden Lampstand
> **Ex. 25:31**   “Then you shall make a lampstand of pure gold. The lampstand, its base, and its shaft are to be made of hammered work; its cups, its bulbs, and its flowers shall be of *the same piece*. **32** “Six branches shall go out from its sides; three branches of the lampstand from its one side and three branches of the lampstand from its other side. **33** “Three cups *shall be* shaped like almond *blossoms* in the one branch, a bulb and a flower, and three cups shaped like almond *blossoms* in the other branch, a bulb and a flower—so for the six branches going out from the lampstand; **34** and in the lampstand four cups shaped like almond *blossoms*, its bulbs and its flowers. **35** “And a bulb shall be under the *first* pair of branches *coming* out of it, and a bulb under the *second* pair of branches *coming* out of it, and a bulb under the *third* pair of branches *coming* out of it, for the six branches coming out of the lampstand. **36** “Their bulbs and their branches shall be of *the same piece*; all of it shall be one piece of hammered work of pure gold. **37** “Then you shall make its lamps seven *in number*; and they shall mount its lamps so as to shed light on the space in front of it. **38** “And its tongs and their trays *shall be* of pure gold. **39** “It shall be made from a talent of pure gold, with all these utensils. **40** “And see that you make *them* after the pattern for them, which was shown to you on the mountain.

The difficulty with interpreting this passage is that there are a few details not specified. Perhaps God has deemed these as less important and given these to be filled in creatively. There are, for example, different impressons on the Internet showing what the lampstand could have looked like, for example there is where the trays are not at the same height. Here is an impression by https://www.biblicaltoolbelt.com/art-temple-tabernacle-series/. It notes that the dimensions are not given, but merely that it has to **be made from a talent of pure gold**, which is approximately 35 kilograms or about 1.5 liters. This means the dimensions vary depending on how thin the gold is hammered or how heavy the base is, if weight of the base is even counted. I am assuming that no other materials are allowed in the lampstands, like the Ark, which is made of wood, but covered with hammered gold.
