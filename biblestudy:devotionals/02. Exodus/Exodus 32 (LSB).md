> ### The Golden Calf
> **Ex. 32:1**   Then the people saw that Moses delayed to come down from the mountain. So the people assembled about Aaron and said to him, “Arise, make us gods who will go before us; as for this Moses, the man who brought us up from the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him.” **2** And Aaron said to them, “==Tear== off the gold rings which are in the ears of your wives, your sons, and your daughters, and bring *them* to me.”

Both the ASV and the LSB use a better translation of root word, which means to break off something, while the NIV and the ESV simply write "take off". There is perhaps a deeper meaning to this. 

> **3** Then all the people tore off the gold rings which were in their ears and brought *them* to Aaron. **4** And he took *this* from their hand and ==fashioned== it with a graving tool and made it into a molten calf; and they said, “These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt.”

Aaron's explanation (v. 24) deliberately misses important information that would negatively portray him. 

> **5** And Aaron looked and built an altar before it; and Aaron made a proclamation and said, “Tomorrow *shall be* a feast to Yahweh.” **6** So the next day they rose early and offered burnt offerings and brought peace offerings; and the people sat down to eat and to drink and rose up to play.

The Law and the Ten Commandments had not been given yet at this point, so were the people to blame for requesting this representation of God? They did not want to follow other gods. The answer is that they are guilty of many sins. They rebelled against Aaron. This is speculation, but Aaron may have said words to appease them, but the people would not listen and required to see God anyway. 
The people should have known that God *did not want* to show Himself, for He had hid Himself in the clouds. The people should also have known not to rebel against their leaders #todo/opzoeken/reference . Furthermore, God is never unjust. He would not punish the people for something they did not know. Perhaps all of these rules had been written in the Book of the Covenant, which they *had* received from Moses already.

> **Ex. 32:7**   Then Yahweh spoke to Moses, “Go! Go down at once, for your people, whom you brought up from the land of Egypt, have corrupted *themselves*.

In His anger God now refers to Israel as **your people, whom your brought up**, while in Exodus 20:2 He give this honor to Himself. 

> **8** “They have quickly turned aside from the way which I commanded them. They have made for themselves a molten calf and have worshiped it and have sacrificed to it and said, ‘These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt!’”

Had there been a rule not to make images before Moses had come down with the Law and the Commandments? #todo/opzoeken  #biblestudy/questions/exodus

> **9** And Yahweh said to Moses, “I have seen this people, and behold, they are a stiff-necked people. **10** “Now then let Me alone, that My anger may burn against them and that I may consume them; and I will make you a great nation.”

I am not sure whether this was a test to see Moses' loyalty or pride. I think either answer of Moses would have been right. God was right to let His **anger [...] burn against them** and in consuming them, and Moses would have been to let God do what is right or to entreat God and bring Him more glory. His arguments (v. 12) are valid, but God has done—and still does—things that incite the anger of the nations (Psalm 2:2). Currently He has led Israel in a war to retrieve the more than 150 Israeli hostages by Hamas, thereby killing tens of thousands of terrorists #tags/history/war/israel/2023 —things he had similarly done through the likes of Abraham, Deborah, Simson and other mighty men, which have always been perceived by Christians as a showing of God's control and power. Even the people of Canaan lost courage (Joshua 2:9-11), hundreds of years after the happenings of these events. Yet, "Christians" have now descended to such a level that they are of the opinion that the war should stop, that the hostages must stay in the possession of Hamas, that they believe Hamas will return the hostages as soon as Israel stops the attacks and they pity the Palestines and believe their lies concerning the number of casualties, for example that there are almost a hundred thousand civilian casualties, or that of all casualties ninety percent are women and children. And as for the lies, this is only the tip of the iceberg, including the fact that there has never been a nation (i.e. people), state or country named Palestine. 
All of this is from the "Christians"! How much more so from the world. Only a handful of nations in the UN, about five, publicly supported Israel, and except for the United States, the other countries were so small one might not have even heard of them. 
Though we should always think about what other people will say about our God, like Moses did (v. 12, but this is not the main reason why Moses entreated Yahweh, see notes on v. 14.), we should also be glad when God finally shows His wrath against the nations and makes them fear Him again. 

> **Ex. 32:11**   Then Moses entreated the favor of Yahweh his God and said, “O Yahweh, why does Your anger burn against Your people whom You have brought out of the land of Egypt with great power and with a strong hand? **12** “Why should the Egyptians speak, saying, ‘With evil *intent* He brought them out to kill them in the mountains and to consume them from the face of the earth’? Turn from Your burning anger and relent concerning *doing* harm to Your people. **13** “Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, Your servants to whom You swore by Yourself, and You said to them, ‘I will multiply your seed as the stars of the heavens, and all this land of which I have spoken I will give to your seed, and they shall inherit *it* forever.’” **14** So Yahweh relented concerning the harm which He said He would do to His people.

The main reason why **Moses entreated the favor of Yahweh** is not because of God's reputation, but because of his *promise* to give **all this land** to Abraham's **seed**. In the case of the Israel-Palestine War this is very much the reason why the war is fought, namely because other countries want to remove the Israelites from their land and kill them, not only those living in Israel, but also elsewhere in the world. So in this case it is only right to ask God not to relent and pour out His wrath on every person Who is against His people and His promise. 
Some of the Christians who are against the war are so because they think this promise (Genesis 12) has been lost due to the disobedience of the Israelites, but they fail to see that this covenant consists of four parts, some of which are conditional to Israel's obedience to God's commandments, but others are *not*. > **Ex. 32:15**   Then Moses turned and went down from the mountain. And the two tablets of the testimony were in his hand, tablets which were written on both sides; they were written on one *side* and the other.

It is unknown what was written exactly on the tablets. It would have contained at least the Ten Commandment of Exodus 20, but possibly also the laws in the chapters after that regardless of the amount of text it contained. God could have written on any size of the tablet and in any font size. According to some sources it is the Book of the Covenant that contained these chapters detailing the law.

> **16** Now the tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God engraved on the tablets. **17** Then Joshua heard the sound of the people as they shouted. And he said to Moses, “There is a sound of war in the camp.” **18** But he said,

> **Ex. 32:19**   Now it happened, as soon as Moses came near the camp, that he saw the calf and *the* dancing; and Moses’ anger burned, and he threw the tablets from his hands and shattered them at the foot of the mountain. **20** Then he took the calf which they had made and burned *it* with fire and ground it to powder and scattered it over the surface of the water and made the sons of Israel drink *it*.

Most likely not everyone participated in the worship of the **calf**, there may have been people like Joshua and Caleb. 
This process of burning gold until it is molten and grinding it to powder must have taken hours. All this time the people of Israel let Moses do this. Despite their ecstasy  (v. 25) they still feared God and were still aware of Moses' authority.

I do not want to fill in too much, but Moses killed a man in rage, and he **shattered** the **tablets**. Perhaps this act of letting the people **drink** the water with the gold in it was an act of rage, rather than something symbolic or something commanded by God. Although the shattering of the stone tablets—which would have created some **powder**—is similar to the grinding of the gold into powder. Instead of letting the people ingest the powder of the tablets, however, he let them ingest what they wanted themselves, namely the golden statue. #church/material/sermon

> **Ex. 32:21**   Then Moses said to Aaron, “What did this people do to you, that you have brought *such* great sin upon them?” **22** And Aaron said, “Do not let the anger of my lord burn; you know the people yourself, that they are prone to evil.

Though Moses was Aaron's younger brother, he still called him **my lord**, indicating the respect he had for him and the literally by God ordained position.

> **23** “Indeed they said to me, ‘Make gods for us who will go before us; for this Moses, the man who brought us up from the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him.’

Instead of asking the right question, such as when Moses would come back, whether  he was still alive or whether  God could give a sign, they asked for **gods** (plural), and seemingly not even the Lord Yahweh. He it was Who would **go before** them, using these exact same words. Still, this is an excuse, their longing for other gods, for a method of worship as they were used to in Egypt—which they most likely participated in—is hidden behind their question of whether Moses was still alive.

This would likely also answer the question why the Israelites had been slaved in Egypt for four centuries, namely due to their participation in the worship of Egyptian  gods. They seemed to have no trouble worshiping a *calf* in this passage, which was also one of the animals worship in the Egyptian religion. No one remarked why God would like one of His own creations. Rather yet, they asked Aaron to **[m]ake gods**, as if a god can be made. 

> **24** “And I said to them, ‘Whoever has any gold, let them tear it off.’ So they gave *it* to me, and I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf.”

> **Ex. 32:25**   Now Moses saw that the people were out of control—for Aaron had let them get out of control to be a ==derision== among their enemies— **26** so Moses stood in the gate of the camp and said, “Whoever is for Yahweh, *come* to me!” And all the sons of Levi gathered together to him. 

It seems strange that only the Levites seem to be **for Yahweh**.

If this were literally understood, then there were about one thousand Levites who each killed three men. > **31** Then Moses returned to Yahweh and said, “Alas, this people has committed a great sin, and they have made gods of gold for themselves. **32** “But now, if You will forgive their sin—but if not, please blot me out from Your book which You have written!” **33** And Yahweh said to Moses, “Whoever has sinned against Me, I will blot him out of My book.

Moses is most likely referring to the book of life (#todo/opzoeken/reference Revelation). He is willing to throw away his own salvation along with that of the people of Israel. God does not work that way though—He does not listen to threats or even to #questions/language/english/spelling oprecht, heartfelt? pleas—and does not answer Moses' question (v. 32), but merely states His principles: he who sinned against God, He will blot out of the book, and he who did not sin against God will stay in the book. So in other words, God does not listen to Moses. He will still blot out the sinful Israelites out of His book and He will *not* remove Moses from His book. God does not listen to man in this way, He always does what is right, for man—like in this case Moses—can request for things that are not correct.
