# Galatians 5 (LSB)
> ### CHRIST SET US FREE
> 
> **Gal. 5:1**   It was for freedom that Christ set us free. Therefore, stand firm and do not be subject again to a yoke of slavery.

Of course we are under the yoke of Christ (cf. Matthew 11:29-30) and He is our lord (Romans 10:9), so there is still some form of bondage, but every Christian knows this is the lightest and most pleasing form of bondage in the world. It cannot even be called bondage or **slavery**, despite Paul calling himself "a slave for Christ" (#todo/opzoeken/reference ). But the **freedom** Christians enjoy in Christ, that is the purpose for which He has **set us free** from the bondage of the world. 

> **Gal. 5:2**   Behold I, Paul, say to you that if you receive circumcision, Christ will be of no benefit to you. **3** And I testify again to every man who receives circumcision, that he is under obligation to keep the whole Law. **4** You have been severed from Christ, you who are being justified by law; you have fallen from grace!

It is not the physical act of circumcision perse, but the spiritual act of it, that symbolizes severance **from Christ**. It is like when a woman throws her wedding ring away, or when women in general threw away—or refused to wear—their bras during the Emancipation in the sixties #tags/history/emancipation to show their new (*morally*) liberal positions. They refused to submit themselves (cf. Ephesians 5:22), that was the message they were carrying out—and unfortunately it was not a mere message or threat. #biblestudy/discovery

> **5** For we through the Spirit, by faith, are eagerly waiting for the hope of righteousness.

The "**for**" seems to be out of place here. If it is placed directly after v. 4, it would say, "You have fallen from grace, for we [...] are eagerly waiting [...]", this would imply that their severance with Christ is related to our eager in waiting. It would be more logical to put this after v. 3, which would then render the verses as, "Christ will be of no benefit to you [...] and every man [...] is under obligation to keep the whole Law [...] for we [...] are eagerly waiting [...]". This still sounds off. It is best to realize that these #questions/language/english/spelling prepositions? cannot be translated easily into English unless the meaning is changed with it as well. Paul perhaps means "but" here, to show the contrast between those who trusted in the circumcision—to circumvent punishment by the Jews (cf. Galatians 6:12)—and an *easier* way to live out Christianity, and those who betted everything on their **faith**, being able to do nothing except for **waiting for the hope of righteousness**. 

> **6** For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision means anything, but faith working through love.

Faith means everything, and a "special form" of faith, namely **faith working through love**. There are many kinds of faith. Those who worship idols also have faith, but it is not the kind that is expressed in love. People of religions with idols and animism such as Hindus and the tribes in the north of Vietnam are not known for their hospitality. Also the tribes in Ireland where Saint Patrick spread the Gospel were cruel against anyone who defied their idols or gods. Clearly, their faith in their gods was not great either. The leader who defied Saint Patrick and wanted him burned, was afraid to step in the fire himself, while Saint Patrick trusted in God. Hindus are afraid to anger their gods and fear that these will retaliate and kill them or their children. This is why they are afraid of foreign influence and proselytizing people, especially Christians. They do not have any faith in that their own gods can defend themselves.
This is why their faith is one without works of love. Their is some belief and some commitment, but it is not out of love for their gods and therefore there is no true loyalty. This is what John means with "there is no fear in love". Idolators are filled with fear and there depleted of love. They cannot give any love, because they do not have it, and they desperately desire it at the same time, even though they do not know this nor admit this when they know it. 
In Christian faith the only thing that matters is faith working through love. That is, in other words, the only thing that can give certainty of one's destination. 
Circumcision and the knowledge and even abiding of a plethora of rules will not provide any certainty of one's position in Heaven. Not even the works of love. It is faith, expressed in works of love. Only that.

> **Gal. 5:7**   You were running well; who hindered you from obeying the truth? **8** This persuasion *is* not from Him who calls you. **9** A little leaven leavens the whole lump.

Paul is not actually asking who the false teachers are. Perhaps he knows already, or perhaps he does not care. All that matters is that these persons brought in these lies in little bits, and these were enough to leaven **the whole lump**. He is warning at the same time to be extremely careful, even for small deviations from the truth. 
**Example.** Coincidentally I just received an invitation of a church to join them in watching *the Lord of the Rings* trilogy #tags/movies. I would not disagree with the view of the author, J.R.R. Tolkien #tags/people/authors/christian, nor with the story of the book itself, but the movie has skipped many of the deeper aspects of the spiritual world and inserted more superficial elements which are not important to the story. This movie could in a sense contain a little bit of leaven. Perhaps the movie is completely clean[^1] and contains not even a trace of falsehood, but it might lead to others thinking that watching Hollywood movies is a safe thing, as long as the author of the original story is Christian, which would eventually lead to the viewing of many disturbing movies. 
We need to be careful and not base our judgment on precedents. Just because a certain activity was allowed in the church, this does not mean a follow-up should be accepted without criticism. 

> **10** I have confidence in you in the Lord that you will adopt no other view. But the one who is disturbing you will bear his judgment, whoever he is. 

Regardless of who they are, Paul is confident (and hopeful) that those **disturbing** the members of Church of Galatia **will bear [God's] judgment**. v. 12 shows Paul's intent and *wish* for their harm (see notes there). 

> **11** But I, brothers, if I still preach circumcision, why am I still persecuted? Then the stumbling block of the cross would have been abolished.

It is obvious that Paul was *not* preaching **circumcision**, for otherwise the Jews would have left him alone. This **stumbling block of the cross** is the fact that one *cannot* do anything to be saved. When a person asks, "What can I do to be saved?", "nothing" is a more difficult answer to accept than something, because people want to feel like they have contributed to something, or they believe that nothing is for free and that there is a catch; or they still feel guilty about their sins, because they have not atoned for it nor *felt* this atonement in the form of pain or effort. This is unnatural to man, and this exactly is the stumbling block of the cross. The Jews were unable to accept this "free gift" and therefore they continued preaching circumcision and the Law as a means to obtain salvation. 

> **12** I wish that those who are upsetting you would even mutilate themselves.

This is one of the expressions that cause Christians on the far right to doubt whether to take this verse and Paul's aggressive words seriously, because they fear this goes against God's creed to love one another and be *kind*. The same people who "accept" the Psalms as canonical, have problems accepting psalms where God's hatred for His enemies is shown and where He is called up to avenge and punish the wicked and proud (e.g. Psalm 94:1-3, 139:19-22). They sing from the Psalms, but only because it is a command (Colossians 3:16-17), but do not *read* from it as, thinking that they are not allowed to pray these words to God Himself, even though psalmists such as David do.

> **Gal. 5:13**   For you were called to freedom, brothers; only *do* not *turn* your freedom into an opportunity for the flesh, but through love serve one another. **14** For the whole Law is fulfilled in one word, in this: “YOU SHALL LOVE YOUR NEIGHBOR AS YOURSELF.” 

Paul segues into the topic of freedom as a contrast to the circumcision he mentioned before and warns that their freedom through not being under the Law anymore, does not mean that the reciprocal love for God and one's neighbors—that was commanded in the Law of Moses (e.g. Deuteronomy 5:6ff, 6:5)—should not be part of our lives anymore.

> **15** But if you bite and devour one another, beware that you are not consumed by one another.

What beautiful use of irony! How can one **bite and devour another** and yet be **consumed by one another**? *Physically* this is not possible[^4] but this consummation happens *spiritually*. This is actually a holistic and general principle in life. One may want to have control, for example, and therefore resort to a power grab and become boss of the underworld, but most likely this person will be consumed by this power and become addicted to it. 
**Examples.** Pablo Escobar, Scarface and even Jafar in the Disney animation #tags/movies/animation *Aladdin*. #todo/nogaftemaken Take for example the character of Jafar, who wanted to become powerful and once he obtained the lamp of the genie, he wished to become sultan, and then the most powerful wizard, and last, to become the powerful genie. This last wish was his demise, for  as a genie he was now chained by his own lamp. 
Another example of this is the person who was looking for the crystal skull in the movie #tags/movies *Indiana Jones and the Crystal Skull* and was obsessed with obtaining all the knowledge in the world, but was eventually consumed by this knowledge. #biblestudy/discovery

> ### WALK BY THE SPIRIT
> 
> **Gal. 5:16**   But I say, walk by the Spirit and you will not carry out the desire of the flesh. **17** For the flesh sets its desire against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh; for these are in opposition to one another, so that you do not do the things that you want. 

There are always forces at work in the spiritual realm, where the flesh always works **against the Spirit**. There is the question of course whether the devil, whom the flesh obeys, plays "4D-chess", meaning that he can seduce a person to let him do a good thing, which eventually leads to a bad thing. Partially, the answer is *yes*. Many—definitely not all—people of the Left #tags/politics/left are driven by their good intentions, and the devil tells them that something is not fair or good, such as poverty and young single mothers. However, they are blinded themselves by their own decision to overlook important facts, such as that many of the poor refuse to work and cannot keep jobs because of a lack of discipline. Next to that, most single mothers are that because they did not choose to marry before having sex and did not obey their own mothers or their conscience. 
Now there might be some arguments the Left makes which are true, such as that people in these social-economic circumstances often do not receive the teachings that other classes do. But then the answer is simply that that is what should be done about their situation. Poor and lower class people should be *educated*, instead of given more food coupons and free abortions #tags/politics/abortion. #tags/sex/marriage/before
It is the Left's own lack of discipline, that is their *flesh*, that pushes them to these wrong resolutions. They know, for example, that if they educate young men and women to not have sex before marriage, this wisdom must apply to themselves as well, and their flesh does not want to stop doing so. Another thing the government should do, is stop promoting sex. In all products, from perfumes to even cars and clothes and music, sex is prominently shown, because "sex sells". And so the flesh of those manufacturers that craves for money does not want to educate young people about sex in the right way and the devil leads them right into his hands. 

Paul's advise is simple, **walk by the Spirit**. Live a holy, law-abiding life and everything in you will resist the flesh, and the conflict that you feel within you is an indication that something is wrong. In your head and in your heart you will know what is good and bad, because the Spirit will teach you. (1 John 2:27, 4:13).

> **18** But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not under the Law. **19** Now the deeds of the flesh are evident, which are: sexual immorality, impurity, sensuality, **20** idolatry, sorcery, enmities, strife, jealousy, outbursts of anger, selfish ambition, dissensions, factions, **21** envying, drunkenness, carousing, and things like these, of which I forewarn you, just as I have forewarned you, that those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom of God.

This list is not meant to indicate what the worst sins are of the flesh, but it is purely to show the **evident deeds of the flesh**. Wherever a person goes and sees these deeds in abundance, it is clear that not the Spirit, but the flesh reigns. 
**Example.** I happen to be in Prague at the time of writing. Though this city is far from wretched and godless cities like Amsterdam and San Francisco, for it is clean here and relatively quiet—even at nights—it has civilized people and little criminality (due the low numbers of immigrants), but the deeds of the flesh are still evident when one looks around casually. **[S]exual immorality, impurity [and] sensuality** are easily found and in many forms available such as gambling, though not as abundant and explicit as in Amsterdam where everyone—even children—is exposed. An example of **idolatry [and] sorcery** was found in a book store, to which a whole wall was dedicated. Whereas other categories such as cooking and psychology each had their own shelves, esoterism and other pagan and idolatrous subjects literally had about ten shelves. The popularity of sorcery was also found in the toys department where *Harry Potter* #tags/movies had its own glass display cabinet prominently placed in the center. 
So, even though this city does not seem drenched with satanism, the *lack of Christiany* is apparent through its deeds of the flesh and it is clearly *on the road* to hell. 
One might say that I am wrong and that the city could be on its way to heaven instead. Their presumption then is that when an object cannot be judged by a fleeting impression, but rather has to be viewed over a longer period of time. Then—they argue—when looking at the country's history, one can see that it has come a far way from Communism, and it is doing relatively well. However, this is not how Christianity works. When a person shows little or much of the fleshly deeds, he is led by his flesh. But when a person shows little or much of the **fruit of the Spirit**, he is **led by the Holy Spirit**. The road from being an unbeliever to becoming a believe does not follow a linear path where one, for example, stops smoking less and less, starting from a whole pack of cigarettes (containing twenty) a day, to nineteen and so on until he gets to zero and only then starts doing good things. No, the deeds of the flesh stop immediately and the fruit of the Spirit show up immediately as well. Christians are far from perfect, especially at the beginning of their walk with Christ, but their change *is immediately visible*—so much so that Muslims who converted to Christianity, who were trying to hide their newfound belief and did not speak of it, were found out by their family simply because of their Christ-like behavior and inner joy, which they could not hide. Those who want to stop smoking, and those who have the discipline and power to do so, will stop immediately, but *every* Christian at the very least *wants*—or *should* want—to stop. 
So if there were multiple lines for each of the deeds of the flesh, ranging from ten to zero (from left to right) there would also be lines for each of the characteristics of the fruit of the Spirit in the opposite direction. At whatever position the believer was *before* he came to Christ, he will jump immediately to the middle as soon as he repents and receives the Holy Spirit. Instead of being all the way to the left on most deeds in the old situation, in the new situation he would rather be in the middle with a few back-drops to the left, but mostly growing towards the right side. > **22** But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, **23** gentleness, self-control. Against such things there is no law. 

When reading v. 18 together with v. 22 (skipping vv. 19-21) it can be seen that the Christian who is **led by the Spirit** will produce the good things mentioned here, for which a law, or *the* Law, is not needed.
**Example.** In a church where people do good things and want to do good things, there are no laws written for what the members can and cannot do. #todo/nogaftemaken 
This is what it means not to be under the Law. It does not mean that Christians can sin because they are above the Law, which they are not, but it means that Christians will not do anything that falls under the Law. The Christians is spiritually seen in a new realm where he does his best to live a life like Jesus. An example that explains this better is when there is a child who is stubborn and disobedient. He lives in a realm where he must be taught what is right and live according to rules. There is police who constantly watches his every move, to punish him whenever he does wrong. All of this is to teach the child what is good and bad. The child, however, has no will to live a good life. He just lives his life in order to stay out of trouble. 
But when the child changes and *wants* to become good, then he enters a new realm. Now, there will not be punishments anymore because we know his mistakes were not intentionally. He can be taught with a softer hand #questions/language/english/spelling#. He might not know the Law completely yet, but he is ready to go into the other house where security is less strict, where he is (in this sense) not under the Law, but where he can practice bringing out his fruit.

> **24** Now those who belong to Christ Jesus crucified the flesh with its passions and desires.

Every Christian fails and sins, but no Christian ever wants to go back to the former position he was in. It is like with a former drug addict: He might be tempted to use drugs and even use some sometimes, but if you were to ask him if he wanted to go back to that position of being an addict, he would not want to of course. In the same way, Christians can still sin and long for some things *sometimes* that belong to the world, their former life, but *no Christian* wants to go back to that position where they were slaves of the Satan (cf. Matthew 6:24-26). If he does, this means that he cannot feel the difference between the freedom he has in Christ right now and the bondage he had when serving the Satan. #biblestudy/discovery
In this way Christian have **crucified the flesh**, the source of the **passions and desires**. And now some of this source is still clinging to the newborn Christian, but that will soon fade away, just like the smell of a dirty jacket that sticks to one's skin will soon fade away, as long as the jacket is not worn anymore.  

> **Gal. 5:25**   If we live by the Spirit, let us also walk in step with the Spirit. **26** Let us not become *those* with vain glory, challenging one another, envying one another.

As the whole Bible points out, Christians need to give their *all* to God. What is confessed with the mouth, must also be *believed* with the heart, and *done* with one's hands. Spiritually seen, if the life of a Christian has been renewed and the Holy Spirit has entered his life, then he must also **walk in step with the Spirit**, meaning that he must change his ways, from those who lived in the world, to one who belongs to Heaven.
