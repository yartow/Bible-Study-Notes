# Galatians 3 (LSB)
> ### The Righteous Shall Live by Faith
> **Gal. 3:1**   O foolish Galatians, who bewitched you, before whose eyes Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed *as* crucified? **2** This is the only thing I want to learn from you: did you receive the Spirit by the works of the Law, or by hearing with faith? **3** Are you so foolish? Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being perfected by the flesh? **4** Did you suffer so many things for nothing—if indeed it was for nothing? **5** So then, does He who provides you with the Spirit and works miracles among you, do it by the works of the Law, or by hearing with faith?

Not only is the use of rhetorical questions abundant here, but there is also an undertone of sarcasm. 
v. 2. All believers know intuitively that it is impossible to receive the Spirit by abiding by the Law. It is not a certain level of holiness and righteousness one obtains and with that the Holy Spirit as well as **miracles** from God (v. 5). Strangely, though, this is believed in Buddhism #tags/religion/buddhism. It is believed there that when one lives the life one *should* live according to the first Buddha, Siddhartha Gautama, without any longings or lust for *anything*, not even for food, love or family, then he will not suffer anymore. Extending this principle will lead to enlightenment or, in other words, becoming a Buddha. I am not sure whether Gautama became a Buddha while he lived here on Earth, or after his death, but the main point I am trying to make is that he obtained all these powers of the Buddha, simply by living according to a certain law[^1]. 
v. 3 adds to this thought. It is foolish to think that anything heavenly can be perfected by something Earthly, or something spiritually can be perfected by something fleshly. These are not only two different and separate realms, but the latter (i.e. Earth and the flesh) are also of infinitely lower quality and can never be compared with the former. 
v. 4. Paul tells his readers that their suffering was not for nothing, but that it could be for nothing if their faith is not enough. An unbeliever, for example, suffers for nothing. Believers who suffer, however, suffer for the Kingdom of God, which is never for nothing. This is why Paul adds his parenthesis "**if indeed it was for nothing"**.

> **Gal. 3:6**   Just as Abraham BELIEVED GOD AND IT WAS COUNTED TO HIM AS RIGHTEOUSNESS, 

See [[Genesis 15 (LSB)|notes on Genesis 15:6]]. 

> **7** so know that those who are of faith, those are sons of Abraham. 

Not only Abraham's descendents–the Israelites and later on the Jews (which are the remaining Israelites who returned from Persia)—but everyone who is **of faith** is a son of Abraham. The author is not disregarding the promise made to the Israelites, but rather emphasizing that the promise is made to everyone **of faith**. 

> **8** And the Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, proclaimed the gospel beforehand to Abraham, *saying*, “ALL THE NATIONS WILL BE BLESSED IN YOU.” **9** So then those who are of faith are blessed with Abraham, the believer.

The blessing given to Abraham—confirmed in Genesis 17:6-8—was not completely meant for Abraham's descendents. For example, the promise that nations would come from him and kings would go forth from him was only meant for Abraham. However, the covenant and the land was meant for his *seed*. With his "seed" was not meant his descendants, but those who are in Christ, for Abraham had many sons, but not all his descendants shared in this blessing. Even out of Isaac and later on Jacob, there were many who did not share in the blessing, most notably those who after the Exodus from Egypt were disobedient and died in the desert—those who were killed by snakes, the plague or by their brothers by the command of the Lord after the committed their idolatrous practices. Similar events happened to those who did enter the land but continued to disobey God. They were not cut off from the land directly, but after God had run out of patience, they were. So, in short, this blessing is not for Abraham's descendents, but for anyone who *obeyed the Lord*, or in other words, **those who are of faith**. This is repeated in v. 16 where it is emphasized that "seed" refers not to Abraham's descendents but to Christ, and by extrapolation we know that those who are *in* Christ, i.e. Christians, also share in this blessing. This is what it means when it is said the **all nations will be blessed**, for everyone from every nation can come to Christ. 

> **Gal. 3:10**   For as many as are of the works of the Law are under a curse, for it is written, “CURSED IS EVERYONE WHO DOES NOT ABIDE BY ALL THINGS WRITTEN IN THE BOOK OF THE LAW, TO DO THEM.” **11** Now that no one is justified by the Law before God is evident, for “THE RIGHTEOUS SHALL LIVE BY FAITH.”

The whole part above states that no one is righteous (cf. Rom 3:10) and therefore everyone is **cursed**, even when someone abides by the whole Law except for one little thing. Still, v. 11 speaks of **the righteous**, by whom Paul means the people who are far more righteous than others, like Job and Noah, but still not completely righteous. Even those "righteous" people *must* **live by faith**, for that is the only way to be saved. Another interpretation—perhaps even better—is that the quote in v. 11 means that *those who want to be righteous* must live by faith. As a matter of fact, there are different interpretations for each of the three times verse is quoted from Habbakuk 2:4. See [[Habbakuk 2 (LSB)/The Righteous Will Live by His Faith|notes there]].

> **12** However, the Law is not of faith; rather, “HE WHO DOES THEM SHALL LIVE BY THEM.”

Paul is not saying that Christians should not abide by the Law. That would be contradictory to all of Scripture (cf. 1 John 1:6, 2:3-6,27-29, among others). His contention here is that He who wants to achieve anything by the Law, must live *under* it as well and abide by *everything* under the Law or otherwise be cursed. 

> **13** Christ redeemed us from the curse of the Law, having become a curse for us—for it is written, “CURSED IS EVERYONE WHO HANGS ON A TREE”— **14** in order that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we would receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.

This is something that is difficult for many Christians to understand. How can Christ, who is holy, be cursed other than that he has become unholy, even though this was done for our sake? We need to understand that Christ has never become unholy and has taken upon Himself the curse of being unholy, yet He has not taken the unholiness itself upon Him. It is like sacrificing a baby for your sins, but this does not mean that baby is sinful because of this.

> ### The Promise by Faith
> **Gal. 3:15**   Brothers, I speak in human terms: even though it is *only* a man’s covenant, yet when it has been ratified, no one sets it aside or adds conditions to it. 

Paul did not have the availability of quotes, otherwise he would have used 'it is only a "man's" covenant'. He meant that "even" a covenant between two men is holy, because it happens under heaven, and promise made by man should be kept in the same way as a promise made by God. Likewise, we could say, "Even if it is only a promise between children", this would not make the promise any less valid though (aside from the fact that children could be unaware of the impact of a promise they made). 
Here, however, the promise was *between man and God*, and it was God who promised it. So there is all the more reason that this promise is kept.

> **16** Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed. He does not say, “And to seeds,” as *referring* to many, but *rather* to one, “And TO YOUR SEED,” that is, Christ. **17** And what I am saying is this: the Law, which came 430 years later, does not invalidate a covenant previously ratified by God, so as to abolish the promise. **18** For if the inheritance is by law, it is no longer by promise, but God has granted it to Abraham through promise.

Paul's main point here is that the covenant with Abraham, a **promise** that is, exists *next* to Law. Humanly speaking there could be a contradiction between the two in which case one of the two overrules the other, but this is never the case if God made both. In the same way, the *New Covenant*, also called the *New Testament* (not the second part of the Bible, which has the same name, see 1 Corinthians 11:25 in the KJV), also does not **invalidate** the covenant God made with Abraham nor does it replace the Law. 
The promises in the Abrahamic Covenant where told to Abraham and to **Christ** (Whom is meant by "**your seed**"), even though the blessings within this covenant and the land and the people as a nation were for Abraham's descendents. Because this promise was also made to Christ, Who was born 1,500 years after the Law, it means that the promise of the Abrahamic Covenant remained valid from its inception, about 2000 B.C., until 30 A.D., regardless of other promises or covenants made by God.  

> **Gal. 3:19**   Why the Law then? It was added because of trespasses, having been ordained through angels by the hand of a mediator, until the seed would come to whom the promise had been made. **20** Now a mediator is not for one *person only*, whereas God is one.

The Law is was a temporary measure **until the seed**, which is Jesus, **would come**. 
The part about the mediator is probably mentioned because a mediator usually works between two *parties*. Here it is emphasized that **God is one**, and no reason is given as to why He used a **mediator**. From other parts in the Bible we know that if God spoke, people would be scared, if he showed His face, people would die who saw it. This, and the fact that He is a King and should not do these things Himself but let his **angels** do this work, are enough reason for Him to use a mediator.

> **21** Is the Law then contrary to the promises of God? May it never be! 

Of course the Law is not **contrary to the promises of God**. The Law was never meant to impart life, *not even temporarily*, it was only there to let people know about the trespasses so that they would get to know God more and abide by Him. Even Psalm 119, which speaks praiseworthy words about the Law, does not imply that the Law gives life. Even if it said that, it only meant that abiding by the Law lets us live life the way it should be. 
To some, the Law does not prevent people from breaking it, but actually incites them to do so. Just like Adam who ate from the tree, because he knew he was not allowed to eat from it. So to some a Law tempts people into breaking it, while for others, who want to do good, it helps them to keep away from the boundaries. 

> For if a law had been given which was able to impart life, then righteousness would indeed be by law. 

Make no mistake, there is not law (with a small letter "l") **able to impart life**. How did Jesus then obtain righteousness, if there was no Savior to impute righteousness on Him? Did Jesus not obtain this by the Law? No. The difference is that Jesus abided by the Law completely. It would even be better to say that the Law abides completely by Jesus, for He wrote the Law according to His own being. For example, Jesus would never lie and therefore He wrote in the Law that no one should lie. Regardless of that, though, He did abide by the Law and never sinned and did not *obtain* life but rather, He never *lost* it. Man, on the other hand, *lost* life from the very moment he sinned for the first time. Within the Law (capital "L") there is no single law that says that the person who has lost his life, will obtain life gain when doing so and so. The Law only provides measures to *sin less*. Doing good will help the believer to store up treasures in Heaven, but this does not compensate for any sins done.

> **22** But the Scripture has shut up everyone under sin, so that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe.

**Shut up** refers here to the encapsulation of **everyone under sin**, not that their mouths have been shut. Even though Scripture was only given to some people at certain times, sin was present nonetheless (cf. Romans 5:13). Adam had received some instructions which he passed on to his children. If all children received and remembered all of Adam's instructions—which would mean that Noah and his family received all of these as well—and if Noah passed on all the instructions he had received from God after the Flood to all seven people present at the time, this means that the whole world would remember the instructions God had given. From the time when Moses received the Law with more instructions, however, the world could say that they had no knowledge of Scripture, but still, that did not excuse them  from keeping at least the laws written on their hearts (cf. Romans 2:15). 
All of this was done so that the promise of future redemption **might be given to those who believe**. This could be interpreted as if God had taken away all ability of man to work for their salvation so that man could only achieve salvation by the means that God set for man. Even if that were the case, God would be righteous in doing so. For it would be more righteous (and fair) to let everyone start with the same baseline, for otherwise those who are better at studying would have a better chance of understanding the Law and (perhaps) keeping it as well, to just name an example. But in this way, the judgment is more fair, for belief is something that everyone has, and is only diminished through on one's own stubbornness and the measure in which one closes his heart. #biblestudy/discovery
This might not be the true reason God chose this way, but even if He did, it can be justified. That is, however, that man was never able to work for his own salvation. That is something that is intuitively understandable when imagining someone who saves himself from drowning. The person drowns because he cannot swim or does not have the energy anymore to stay afloat. There is no way he can save himself. Others might be able to be save him for a while, but they too will eventually drown. #church/material/sermon

Note that this example clearly explains the difference between "saving" and "salvaging". Even though the person may have been *saved*—or better said, kept safe for the time being—he has not been salvaged. Salvation (the noun form of *salvaging*) occurs only when one is put on dry land. 

> **Gal. 3:23**   But before faith came, we were held in custody under the Law, being shut up for the coming faith to be revealed. **24** Therefore the Law has become our tutor unto Christ, so that we may be justified by faith. **25** But now that faith has come, we are no longer under a tutor. 

For each believer **faith came** at a different time. This is not about the moment of Jesus' incarnation, but about the moment the person believed in and accepted the saving grace of Jesus Christ and Him as Lord. Before that time, it was **the Law** that functioned as **a tutor** to teach us about Christ. The Law in fact *teaches* believers about what is moral and why it is so. Everyone who reads what the Jews call *the Law*, i.e. the Pentateuch (the first five books of the Bible)—but of course the actual Law of God contains many more laws and encompasses the whole Old Testament—will grasp the logic of the Law, as well as the righteousness, justice and mercy woven into it. The Law *points* to the character of God—of Who He *is*, and thus also to the exactly similar character of Jesus Christ—and to some (i.e. to those who come to faith) the Law also *leads* to a recognition of the fallibility of man's character and the fact that only faith in Jesus Christ can save. #biblestudy/discovery
Does this mean then that as soon as people come to faith they know everything about the Law? No, believers will continue being taught by the Law, yet not as tutees but as a **sons of God**. 

> **26** For you are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus. **27** For all of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ. **28** There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free man, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus. **29** And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham’s seed, heirs according to promise.
