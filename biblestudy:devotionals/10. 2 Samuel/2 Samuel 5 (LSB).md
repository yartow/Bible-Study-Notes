### David Anointed King over All Israel
> **2Sam. 5:1**   Then all the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, “Behold, we are your bone and your flesh. **2** “Previously, when Saul was king over us, you were the one who led out and brought in Israel; and Yahweh said to you, ‘You will shepherd My people Israel, and you will be a ruler over Israel.’” **3** So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and King David cut a covenant with them in Hebron before Yahweh; then they anointed David king over Israel. **4** Now David was thirty years old when he became king, *and* he reigned forty years. **5** At Hebron he reigned over Judah seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty-three years over all Israel and Judah.
> 
> **2Sam. 5:6**   Then the king and his men went to Jerusalem against the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land, and they said to David, “You shall not come in here, but the blind and lame will turn you away”; thinking, “David cannot enter here.” **7** Nevertheless, David captured the fortress of Zion, that is the city of David. **8** And David said on that day, “Whoever would strike down the Jebusites, let him reach the lame and the blind, who are hated by David’s soul, through the water tunnel.” Therefore they say, “The blind or the lame shall not come into the house.” **9** Then David lived in the fortress and called it the city of David. And David built all around from the Millo and inward. **10** And David became greater and greater, and Yahweh, the God of hosts, was with him.

Note. Other translations have instead of v.6b: "...unless you remove the blind and the lame". 

The saying about the blind and lame originated from the Jebusites' mocking of David. There are several possibilities why they said this. Of course, part of the answer is already given , which is that **David cannot enter** the city, but that is either because David is too weak, so weak that even blind and lame can defeat David, or because the city is fortified and strategically positioned in such a way that even blind and lame can protect the city. In fact the latter is very much true, for the city of Jerusalem lies on a hill, and to attack it, one must climb up the hill and fight against enemies who are on the top of the walls at the same time. It is surprising how the Assyrians and the Babylonians managed to capture Jerusalem. The Ottomans destroyed the wall with by then present technology such as catapults #todo/opzoeken  or that special kind of sling (who invented it?). 
As a side note, David used a smart tactic to enter the city. In present day Jerusalem it is possible to visit the water tunnel of the Jebusite. As a matter of fact, when one wants to enter Hezekiah's Tunnel from the City of David, he will pass the vertical shaft of the Jebusites. 
Jerusalem's main water source is the Gihon spring, located at the east side of Jerusalem, where the citadel is located as in the picture below. As a matter of fact, the citadel was built specifically for this purpose, namely so that the spring would be protected from those who would lay the city under siege. Because the Gihon spring water lay lower than the city level, there was a vertical shaft from within the city downwards to the spring, so that people could obtain water at any time (see second picture). 
The Jebusites did not realize, however, that that was also an entrance into the city. How exactly David (or his men) entered the tunnel is not known, for the main entrance was surrounded by the citadel and if men were positioned on the citadel they would have seen all the movements of David's men.

The theologic and christological percussions of the Jebusite saying it that it has become spiritually true. There were blind and lame people in Jerusalem, even though David said that these **would not come into the house** (which could mean the city or into the house of God), Jerusalem was also a spiritual symbol of God's kingdom and *spiritually* blind and lame people could not enter the Kingdom of God. Jesus, however, *physically* healed the blind and lame and in so doing He spread a spiritual message that through Him, even the spiritually blind and lame will be able to see and walk and enter the Kingdom. Those who stayed blind and lame because they did not want to be healed by Jesus would and still are not able to have a relationship with God.

> ### Children Born to David in Jerusalem
> **2Sam. 5:13**   And David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem, after he came from Hebron; and more sons and daughters were born to David. **14** Now these are the names of those who were born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon, **15** Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia, **16** Elishama, Eliada and Eliphelet.

*MacSBNnkjv* notes that just like in 2 Samuel 2:10-3:5 the exact same pattern occurs here as well, where David relocates and establishes his reign from a new location, taking more wives and bearing children. Another way of seeing this is that God grants and blesses. David abuses this, however, by taking more wives. God "grants" this for the time being and blesses the wives by giving them children. 
Unlike his forefathers, the patriarchs, he did not have one wife he preferred. In the case of Abraham this is somewhat less clear, for it is not certain whether he had multiple wives simultaneously or consecutively and that "wives" should be translated "concubines". But both he, Isaac and Jacob, most definitely loved only one wife and would have gone with one wife if things had turned out differently. 
Aside from this flaw in David's character we see that David sins two more times according to the pattern above, namely the blessing from God and the abuse thereof. The first one is where he has relative peace—although his army is fighting on the battlefield—and then seizes someone else' wife. As a result he loses two people, his good friend (perhaps) and loyal "general" #todo/opzoeken  Uriah, and his newborn son. The second one is where David ponders on the greatness of his army and becomes haughty and wants to size it. Here he loses many people #todo/opzoeken 15k? due to illness. 
As a result of the first two sins he lost two sons #todo/opzoeken/reference names, and later on, he lost the respect of his oldest #questions/language/english/spelling eldest? son, who would be killed by Solomon #todo/opzoeken/reference after David passed away. 
Though this is meant as a warning to all believers not to forget God when ironically we have been blessed *by* Him, the same thing still happens frequently. How often we do not get spiritually drunk after celebrating a victory?
