# 2 Samuel 13 (LSB)
> ### Amnon Violates Tamar
> **2Sam. 13:1**   Now it happened afterwards that Absalom the son of David had a beautiful sister whose name was Tamar, and Amnon the son of David loved her. 

There are different story arcs throughout the Bible and throughout Samuel and Kings, of which the events could run simultaneously—such as the parallel story arcs of Israel and Judah in 1 and 2 Kings— but are told sequentially. The arc in [[2 Samuel 24 (LSB)]] is another example of that, although it is actually too short to be called an arc. The arc of Absalom's betrayal is one that starts here, with the events of Amnon's violation of Tamar, but just like in the previous arc, it was not the actual precursor but rather David's sin—in this case, his lack of action—that started the course of events. 

> **2** And Amnon was so distressed because of his sister Tamar that he made himself ill, for she was a virgin. So it was hard in Amnon’s sight to do anything to her. 

This sentence probably requires a different order in order to be interpreted correctly by English-speaking readers, as often is the case with the Hebrew and Greek languages and in many of the sentences in the Bible. A (perhaps) better translation would be #composed/bible/2samuel

*"And Amnon was so distressed because of his sister Tamar that he made himself ill. However, it was hard in Amnon’s sight to do anything to her, for she was a virgin."* 

> **3** But Amnon had a friend whose name was Jonadab, the son of Shimeah, David’s brother; and Jonadab was a very shrewd man. **4** And he said to him, “O son of the king, why are you so depressed morning after morning? Will you not tell me?” Then Amnon said to him, “I am in love with Tamar, the sister of my brother Absalom.” **5** Jonadab then said to him, “Lie down on your bed and pretend to be ill; and your father will come to see you, and you will say to him, ‘Please let my sister Tamar come and give me *some* food to eat, and let her prepare the food in my sight, that I may see *it* and eat from her hand.’”

Being shrewd makes one creative in thinking of plans to accomplish one's goals. Jonadab was not only shrewd but also so wicked that he did not even condemn Absolom for being **in love with Tamar**, his half-sister! It is as if Absolom would say he wanted to sleep with an animal or a young child, and Jonadab would still have thought of a way for him to accomplish it. People like those, even though they do commit the sins themselves, are just as bad as the perpetrators themselves. > **6** So Amnon lay down and pretended to be ill; and the king came to see him, and Amnon said to the king, “Please let my sister Tamar come and make me a couple of cakes in my sight, that I may eat from her hand.”

Perhaps David could not be blamed for allowing Tamar to get close to Amnon, even though it was his responsibility as a father to protect his daughters. It was for this reason that no man was allowed near the virgin daughters of the king. We can imagine that what Amnon was about to do did not even cross David's mind. However, we also know that David had only just "recovered" from the events in the previous chapter and might have had a lot of things on his mind or lost the sensitivity of the Holy Spirit. Then again, we also know God allowed these things to take place because of David's sin. Still, though, the fact that David's senses did not give him any warning about such a strange request, namely that his son wanted to see her **make [...] cakes in [his] sight [and] eat from her hand**, shows that David was lacking at least some sensitivity. Also, he could have called a doctor to check his body. 

> **2Sam. 13:7**   Then David sent to the house for Tamar, saying, “Go now to your brother Amnon’s house, and prepare food for him.” **8** So Tamar went to her brother Amnon’s house, and he was lying down. And she took dough, kneaded *it*, made cakes in his sight, and baked the cakes. **9** And she took the pan and dished *them* out before him, but he refused to eat. And Amnon said, “Have everyone go out from me.” So everyone went out from him. **10** Then Amnon said to Tamar, “Bring the food into the bedroom, that I may eat from your hand.” So Tamar took the cakes which she had made and brought them into the bedroom to her brother Amnon.

All of this is already showing some weird behavior. Perhaps some of it was customary in that culture, but the commanding way in which Amnon said to Tamar to **bring the food [and to] eat from [her] hand** is really peculiar and would cause suspicion. 

> **11** Then she brought *them* near to him to eat, but he took hold of her and said to her, “Come, lie with me, my sister.” **12** But she said to him, “No, my brother, do not violate me, for such a thing is not done in Israel; do not do this disgraceful thing! **13** “As for me, where could I get rid of my reproach? And as for you, you will be like one of the wicked fools in Israel. So now, please speak to the king, for he will not withhold me from you.”

> **14** But he was not willing to listen to her voice. Now he was stronger than she, so he violated her and lay with her.

This indicates that Amnon was already raised at commanding his servants, to have his way and to sleep with women before he was married—or perhaps he was already married and still took Tamar—for even a young man would normally not do a thing like this for his first time of having sex.

Imagine the struggle with which Tamar resisted Amnon, but the strength with which he withheld her and **violated her**. How much love is there when a man sees a young woman crying out of fear and pain and how much hatred must he have towards her to still continue his unlawful and wicked actions, even up to the point that he is still aroused?

> **2Sam. 13:15**   Then Amnon hated her with a very great hatred; for the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her. 

It is important to notice that the **hatred** was present even before the act. Every man (and sometimes women as well) who lusts after a woman that is not his wife and does not want to marry this woman, hates her. If you think about it, the man wants to enjoy her body and then leave her, letting the woman think he loves her, but then abandon her. This is not only true for one night stands, but for any relation, even serious ones. A couple might have even had a relationship for years, but when the man leaves the woman (or vice versa), he leaves her behind with mixed up emotions and a body that has not been kept exclusive for her future husband[^1]. Who would use something from someone and take it away and leave her with nothing. This person must hate this person in order to do something like that. 
Suppose a man lives together with a woman for years and uses up all of the money in her bank account and then leaves her. He can say that that was not his intention when he became her partner, but it also never was his intention to stay with her, and *if* the account would run dry, that he would work so he could provide for her. The same goes for any man who dates a woman and lusts after her, regardless of his intention.

If Amnon had merely **called his young man** and the latter had heard him calling, that would mean that the doors were not in such way made sound proof that he would not have heard her screaming, unless Amnon had covered her mouth during the act of violence.

> **19** Then Tamar put ashes on her head and tore her long-sleeved garment which *was* on her; and she put her hand on her head and went away, crying aloud as she went.

How deeply had Tamar fallen because of Amnon. She was a virgin and daughter of the king and could be given in marriage to one of the royal men or another man of high position or perhaps anyone she had chosen herself. > ### Absalom Strikes Down Amnon
> **24** And Absalom came to the king and said, “Behold now, your servant has sheepshearers; please let the king and his servants go with your servant.” 

Perhaps the reason the **sheepshearers** are mentioned is because Absalom wanted to hold a feast for his servants or because it was some kind of festive day meant for sheepshearers, such as those for the harvest. 

> **25** But the king said to Absalom, “No, my son, we should not all go, for we will be burdensome to you.” Although he urged him, he was not willing to go, but blessed him. **26** Then Absalom said, “If not, please let my brother Amnon go with us.” And the king said to him, “Why should he go with you?” **27** But Absalom urged him, so he sent Amnon and all the king’s sons with him.

The king showed his doubts, but ultimately relented. He knew there was something between the two brothers for a big matter as what happened two years ago, would not resolve itself on its own. An invitation to a feast would also not resolve the matter, for before feasting, repentance was needed, of which nothing is said about Amnon.

> **2Sam. 13:34**   Then Absalom fled. And the young man who was the watchman raised his eyes and looked, and behold, many people were coming from the road behind him by the side of the mountain. **35** And Jonadab said to the king, “Behold, the king’s sons have come; according to your servant’s word, so it happened.”

The shrewdness of Jonadab is subtly seen here. If he had been guessing, he happened to have spoken the truth and now says something like, "You see, I was not lying" in order to gain the trust of the king. 
The fact that he uses a (standard) phrase to humble himself, such as **your servant**, does not imply actual deference. It is just like when Absalom prostrated himself (2 Samuel 14:33) but had already planned to take over the throne. 
Jonadab had also deliberately never told David that it was his idea that opened up a way for Amnon to rape his sister. This is also the last time of any reference to Jonadab in the Bible. As in Proverbs #todo/opzoeken/reference the evil ones are short-lived. 

> **36** As soon as he had finished speaking, behold, the king’s sons came and lifted their voices and wept; and also the king and all his servants wept very bitterly.

There are some who become enraged after hearing news like this and there are others who break down and cry. David was of the second type, and it happened again when Absalom died, being unable to lead the people for a while (2 Samuel 18:33ff). 

> **2Sam. 13:37**   Now Absalom had fled and went to Talmai the son of Ammihud, the king of Geshur. 

This is written in an ambiguous way, but it is **Talmai**, Absalom's grandfather by his mother's side, who was **the king of Geshur**, not **Ammihud**.

> And *David* mourned for his son every day. **38** So Absalom had fled and gone to Geshur, and was there three years.

Despite the evil Amnon had done, David still loved his son and mourned for him about **three years**. This not only shows his weakness as a father to instill justice in his children—for his children did not receive the right example from David's actions—but it also shows his strength, for he truly loved his children *despite* their evil, just like God loves us despite our evil. 

> **39** *And the heart of* King David was consumed with going out to Absalom; for he was comforted concerning Amnon, since he had died.

As soon as David **was comforted** he started missing Absalom again. Again, David's weaknesses and mistakes as a father are shown again here, for he either should have apprehended Absalom for taking matters into his own hand, or commended him like God commended Phinehas (Numbers 25:7-13) and admitted his own mistake (cf. 1 Samuel 3:18) in failing to apprehend Amnon and bring him to justice and bring justice to Tamar.
