# 2 Samuel 7 (LSB)
> ### The Ark of God Inhabits a Tent
> **2Sam. 7:1**   Now it happened when the king inhabited his house, and Yahweh had given him rest on every side from all his enemies, **2** that the king said to Nathan the prophet, “See now, I inhabit a house of cedar, but the ark of God inhabits *tent* curtains.” **3** So Nathan said to the king, “Go, do all that is in your heart, for Yahweh is with you.”
> 
> **2Sam. 7:4**   Now it happened in the same night, that the word of Yahweh came to Nathan, saying,

In this whole conversation God does not even rebuke Nathan for falsely speaking on behalf of God. Of course Nathan did not do this on purpose, he merely did not see the danger or incorrectness of his blessing. 
Assuming the conversation in v. 5ff actually went as written and that no parts were left out[^1] this points not only to God's mercy and forgiveness but also to His #questions/language/english/spelling daadkracht and focus on the important points. 

> **5** “Go and say to My servant David, ‘Thus says Yahweh, “Are you the one who would build Me a house to inhabit? **6** “For I have not inhabited a house since the day I brought up the sons of Israel from Egypt, even to this day; but I have been going about in a tent, even in a tabernacle.

Before Moses' day God did not even inhabit a tent. It is logical, though, that David had this thought. David knew that God wanted to be with His people and perhaps thought that due to inconvenience it was more practical to have a smaller mobile tent[^2]. Now that the promised land and the promised city #todo/opzoeken are there references to Jerusalem before this that David could know of? were in possession by the Israelites it was time for God to have a fixed dwelling place.  

> **7** “Wherever I have gone about with all the sons of Israel, did I speak a word with one of the tribes of Israel, which I commanded to shepherd My people Israel, saying, ‘Why have you not built Me a house of cedar?’”’ 

God reasons that if He had wanted a house, He would have asked for it. God is righteous here in that He would not blame anyone who did not know of God's wishes. He does not say, "If you did not know, why did you not ask?" Just like the hard master in the parable in Matthew 25:24ff rightfully punished his slave because the slave *knew* about the master's character. 

> ### David’s House Will Be Established Forever
> **8** “So now, thus you shall say to My servant David, ‘Thus says Yahweh of hosts, “I Myself took you from the pasture, from following the sheep, to be ruler over My people Israel. **9** “And I have been with you wherever you have gone and have cut off all your enemies from before you; and I will make you a great name, like the name of the great men who are on the earth. **10** “And I will appoint a place for My people Israel and will plant them, that they may dwell in their own place and not be disturbed again; and the unrighteous will not afflict them any more as formerly, **11** even from the day that I commanded judges *to be* over My people Israel; and I will give you rest from all your enemies. Yahweh also declares to you that Yahweh will make a house for you. 

Three things are promised to David here: 
1. God will make David a great name
2. God will appoint for the people of Israel a peaceful place
3. God will make a house for him
All three are promises that are fulfilled both in the world as well as spiritually. > ### David Magnifies Yahweh’s Name
> **2Sam. 7:18**   Then David the king went in and sat before Yahweh, and he said, “Who am I, O Lord Yahweh, and what is my house, that You have brought me this far? **19** “And yet this was a small thing in Your eyes, O Lord Yahweh, for You have spoken also of the house of Your slave concerning the distant future. And this is the law of man, O Lord Yahweh. **20** “And again what more can David say to You? And You know Your slave, O Lord Yahweh! **21** “For the sake of Your word, and according to Your own heart, You have done all this greatness to let Your slave know. **22** “For this reason You are great, O Lord Yahweh; for there is none like You, and there is no God besides You, according to all that we have heard with our ears. **23** “And what one nation on the earth is like Your people Israel, whom God went to redeem for Himself as a people and to make a name for Himself, and to do a great thing for You and awesome things for Your land, before Your people whom You have redeemed for Yourself from Egypt, *from* nations and their gods? **24** “Yet You have established for Yourself Your people Israel as Your own people forever, and You, O Yahweh, have become their God. **25** “So now, O Yahweh God, the word that You have spoken concerning Your slave and concerning his house, establish *it* forever, and do as You have spoken, **26** that Your name may be magnified forever, by saying, ‘Yahweh of hosts is God over Israel’; and the house of Your servant David shall be established before You. **27** “For You, O Yahweh of hosts, the God of Israel, have revealed in the hearing of Your slave, saying, ‘I will build you a house’; therefore Your slave has found *courage* in his heart to pray this prayer to You. **28** “So now, O Lord Yahweh, You are God, and Your words are truth, and You have promised this good thing to Your slave. **29** “So now, be pleased and bless the house of Your slave, that it may be forever before You. For You, O Lord Yahweh, have spoken; and with Your blessing may the house of Your slave be blessed forever.”

[^1]: It is unknown who the author is but it may have been Samuel or the same author as 1,2 Kings and Ezra-Nehemiah. 1,2 Kings have many parts that are not fully covered, such as David's response to Michal's despise of his behavior, which are expanded on in 1,2 Chronicles. Next to that, Ezra, as well as the author or Esther, left out certain things possibly because those would negatively portray the Persian King. It could have been that the author of this book had left out some of the conversation between God and Nathan because it would have negatively portrayed Nathan but also because it would deviate from the main point. 
[^2]: Note that both root words for [tent](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h168/kjv/wlc/0-1/) (Heb: אֹהֶל (*'ōhel*)) and [tabernacle](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h4908/kjv/wlc/0-1/) (Heb: מִשְׁכָּן(*miškān*)) in this verse are usually translated as "tabernacle" which means "dwelling place", so it is really difficult to translate this verse correctly. The meaning could come across better if—as an example—it were substituted with "house" and "home", rendering it as: "All these years I have not had a home, and not even a house to live in." #church/material/sermon
