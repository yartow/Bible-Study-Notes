> **Col. 1:3**   We give thanks to God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, praying always for you,

Especially for those of a young faith prayers must be made, because they need all the support from Christians and because they are most prone to Satan's attacks. The same goes for those who are on the verge of conversion.

> **4** since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and the love which you have for all the saints, **5** because of the hope laid up for you in heaven, of which you previously heard in the word of truth, the gospel

No one knows about this **hope** unless it **heard [in] the gospel**. Surely, one can say he *knows* that Christians have hope and long for Heaven and to be with God, but that does not mean that they actually understand this hope—if they did, they would have become believers. True understanding of this hope changes life, because it shows the person that there is hope, even for them, to be saved—at least for those who *desire* to be saved, for not everyone *wants* to have this hope.

> **6** which has come to you, just as in all the world also it is constantly bearing fruit and multiplying, just as *it has been doing* in you also since the day you heard and understood the grace of God in truth;

The Gospel is **constantly bearing fruit and multiplying** both on a grand scale of the world as well as on an individual level, both in the realm of this world and in the spiritual one. The Gospel is **the word of truth** and truth itself has the property of rising above the surface, just like oil in water eventually separates and floats to the surface, even if it has been shaken and mixed with the water. Aside from that the truth opens eyes and is easily multiplied by the spread of it.
A characteristic of this world is that it constantly wants to cover up the truth. So in other words, when lots of attention is paid to promote something, such as climate change and so-called gender equality and support for Palestine, this is because the world is trying to cover up the truth or divert the people's attention to something else. C.S. Lewis #tags/people/authors/christian wrote about these methods of the devil in his book #tags/books/christian *The Screwtape Letters*.

> **7** just as you learned *it* from Epaphras, our beloved fellow slave, who is a faithful servant of Christ on our behalf, **8** who also informed us of your love in the Spirit.

Perhaps Epaphras was an actual slave. This actually shows us that Christianity was really popular among the poor people because it provided hope and a community, both spiritual and practical, to rise up out of poverty.

> **Col. 1:9**   For this reason also, since the day we heard, we have not ceased to pray for you and to ask that you may be filled with the full knowledge of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding, **10** so that you may walk in a manner worthy of the Lord, to please *Him* in all respects, bearing fruit in every good work and multiplying in the full knowledge of God; **11** being strengthened with all power, according to His glorious might, for the attaining of all steadfastness and patience; joyously **12** giving thanks to the Father, who has qualified us to share in the inheritance of the saints in light.

The above sums up a perfect and *exclusive* list (meaning that there is not a single item on this list that can be scrapped, not meaning that there is no overlap between two or more items) of everything a Christian should long for and **pray for** with regards to his spiritual growth. 
1. **full knowledge of His will.** The believer must know God's will, both His revealed and unrevealed will. This can also be known by having knowledge of God (point 5).
2. **spiritual wisdom and understanding.** Discernment between what is good and bad—or between good and best—is needed for the believer to walk in the right way (point 3). *Understanding* is not given to everyone in the same measure. Some parts in the Bible are difficult to understand and almost impossible without a good teacher. Even if one has this, it is hard to memorize these explanations. Most simply, this could be paraphrased as intellect. This is why some are convinced by Christian apologetics concerning topics such as Creation and the Age of the Earth, and once did understand why this is true, but cannot explain these arguments themselves to others. Some topics, such as divorce #tags/divorce, Once Saved Always Saved, free will and #biblestudy/theology/calvinism/osas and salvation by grace alone, are disputed, often because the topic is too difficult to understand and one or both parties does not understand the matter. I know one person, for example, who said he came to the conclusion by himself—by researching the Bible, not through external sources—that Israel is no longer God's people #tags/country/israel. This person probably did not know he was influenced by his teachers and had subconscious assertions and assumptions, and perhaps he did not know how to properly use exegesis.
3. **walk in a manner worthy of the Lord.** If it is known what the Lord wants and what the believer should do as instructed by Scripture (#todo/opzoeken/reference the Scripture is one's teacher), then he knows in theory what to do, but should still do this in practice, for which he needs the Holy Spirit for strength and will, and to be reminded constantly of this. 
4. **bearing fruit in every good work.** The main emphasis here is on *good works*, which refer to the helping of the stranger, the poor, weak and imprisoned (see Matthew 25:35-36).
5. **full knowledge of God.** This is the actual definition of theology. Knowing God *Himself* is the most important thing. Theology these days only refer to knowing *about* God, therefore it is possible to even have *atheist* theologians, such as Bart Ehrman #tags/people/critics/formerchristians. In my opinion this part refers to knowing everything that is revealed about God through Scripture, while the first part refers to everything that is personally revealed to one—which cannot contradict each other.
6. **steadfastness.** One of the hardest things for Christians is to remain steadfast. There are too many places to quote from, but James 1:12, for example, speaks of remaining steadfast under trial, for the Christian is constantly under trial. The true believer will not give up and knows that His savior is coming, not only the savior of His soul for the next world, but also the savior of all of his problems in this.
7. **patience.** Oftentimes *patience* is mixed up with *steadfastness*. The difference between these two is that patience refers to the ability to stay emotionally calm, while steadfastness refers to the long-term waiting for something to happen. 
8. **joyously.** Thanks to all of the above, the believer has something to look forward to, which gives that joy that Christians are known for. Meeting God personally in Heaven is the ultimate reward, but here on Earth we already get to see, hear and feel glimpses of Him. This can be compared with the joy that a person has when he receives a letter from his lover, even though she is not with him.
9. **giving thanks.** God has given everything to man for free, and after the people destroyed everything God had given—nature, freedom, safety, health, *freedom from sin*, etc.—God gave Himself to restore all these things to man. There is nothing that man could not be grateful about towards God, for there is nothing—absolutely nothing—that God did not make or give (Colossians 1:16ff).

> ### The Firstborn of All Creation
> **Col. 1:15**   Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.
> **16** 	For in Him all things were created, *both* in the heavens and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities—all things have been created through Him and for Him. #biblestudy/memorization

This is a summary of everything that is written below in vv. 17–20, but it is also a continuation of v. 15. Jesus is the **image of the invisible God** and even though we are also created in God's image and born of God, Jesus is different for He is the **firstborn**, shown by the fact that **all things haven been created through Him and for Him**.
Not that this does not say that Jesus was the first creation within all of creation. No, Jesus was *born* out of God, unlike creation, which was created out of nothing (*ex nihilo*). Again, He is not part of creation, but He is the *master* of creation, phrased here as **the firstborn of all creation**.

It cannot be emphasized enough that **in Him all things were created**, where *all* encompasses more than just what the eye can see. Even the comcepts of **thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities** and—to go further—that of marriage were all *created*. We see for example that in the animal kingdom there is no such thing as marriage. Also, there may be a strong animal such as bear or a lion, but there is no ruler or authority in the animal "kingdom". As a matter of fact, for this reason the word "*king*dom" is not even appropriate. 
In fact, every concept one can think of, as long as it is good, it was created by Jesus. The world cannot exist without the concepts, such as the concept of good and bad. Therefore **in Him all things hold together**. This goes even more so, and *literally*, for the physical laws of nature. 

> **Col. 1:17**   And He is before all things, 
> 	And in Him all things hold together.
> **18** 	And He is the head of the body, the church; 
> 	Who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, so that He Himself will come to have first place in everything.
> **19** 	For in Him all the fullness *of God* was pleased to dwell,
> **20** 	And through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, having made peace through the blood of His cross—through Him—whether things on earth or things in heaven.

On the spiritual field **all things hold together [...] in Him** as well. **He is the head of the body, the church**, like the head or end (although He is at the beginning as well, for He is the **firstborn of all creation** (and also the **firstborn from the dead**)) of a thread with many smaller threads. Without this head the thread would unravel, but with this head, all threads not only stay together, but also come closer to each other as they approach Jesus. 
Now it may sound as if #todo/nogaftemaken the firstborn of the dead is still later than the beginning. But again, the firstborn does not only literally mean just that, but also that he is the master of all those who are reborn. He is the one Who did it first and did it best, and even though His rebirth was 4600 years after the beginning of Creation, He is outside of time, so this argument does not apply. Therefore He is both at the beginning and at the end, as seen from a human perspective. Therefore, when the Pharisees asked Him how it could be that He 
Jesus said He was even before Abraham. Abraham was a reborn person—He had God's Spirit and is now in Heaven, although rebirth was a little bit different conceptually before Jesus' Resurrection—but Jesus was reborn out of death even before Him. This does not make sense in human perspective, but understand that Jesus is always first.

Back to the example of the threads being held together. #todo/nogaftemaken iets beter opschrijven
All spiritual principles *only* hold together **in Him**. For example, this could be a discussion about whether something is good or bad, such as climate change, abortion or euthanasia, but this only has value in the presence of existing *moral values*. If there is no good or bad, there can be no discussion about this either. Now God is the creator of moral value—for He set the standard, and without a standard there would exist no discussion about any of this—and He has written these standards in the heart of man (cf. Romans 2:15). But the same applies not only to moral standards, but to *all* standards and all principles, whether this concerns the standard of beauty or any other standard or principle—all principles only hold together *in Christ*.

> **Col. 1:24**   Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake, and I fill up what is lacking of Christ’s afflictions in my flesh, on behalf of His body, which is the church,

Just like in v. 4—where the hope is not literally *laid* in Heaven, but the hope is *located here* and is *in* Heaven (not referring to the location, but to that in which we put our hope)—and v. 6—where it is not literally the Gospel that bears fruit and multiplies, but it is the *people* who carry the Gospel that bear fruit—so here too Paul's writing should not be taken too literally. It is not that Christ is **lacking [any] afflictions**, as if Christ had not suffered enough on the cross and Paul needs to suffer a bit more because of what Christ lacked in suffering. However, symbolically, it can be said that all Christians suffer as an extension of Christ's suffering. Jesus already warned His disciples that they would treat His disciples worse than Him. Also, the suffering of Christians has not reached the measure of God (#todo/opzoeken/reference  Genesis, where God tells Abraham of the suffering of the Jews and the sins of the Ammorites). So it is true that Christian still need to suffer a bit until Christ returns. 

> **25** of which I was made a minister according to the stewardship from God given to me for you, so that I might fully carry out *the preaching of* the word of God, **26** *that is*, the mystery which has been hidden from the *past* ages and generations, but has now been manifested to His saints, **27** to whom God willed to make known what is the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

Paul was **made a minister** to preach **the word of God**. It is not clear whether the word of God is this **mystery** which he speaks of or that it is something in that, which Paul is now preaching. Of course, the latter is true, for Paul has spoken of this mystery, which is that God bring salvation *to* the Jews *through* the Gentiles, who would be converted *by* the Christians, which started with a small group of Jews, because larger part of the Jews rejected Christ. This story is so complicated that no author could have thought of this. #biblestudy/discovery/godshiddenplans However, the former is true as well, because this mystery is interwoven in all of the word of God.

> **28** Him we proclaim, admonishing every man and teaching every man with all wisdom, so that we may present every man complete in Christ. **29** For this purpose I also labor, striving according to His working, which He works in me in power.

Even if one *knows* this mystery, this does not mean he knows **the riches of the glory of this mystery**. The value of this is hidden and known only to the **saints**. These riches are, of course, **Christ in [us]**. When Christ is **[made] known** this is on a whole other level of intimacy than *knowing about* Christ. 

> which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

According to Colossians 2:2 **Christ Himself [is] God's mystery**. However, in the notes above we read that this mystery is the way in which God has reached out to the Jews and Gentiles. The explanation for this supposed contradiction #biblestudy/apologetics/bible/contradictions is that Christ is the *way* through which God established His plan.

> **28** Him we proclaim, admonishing every man and teaching every man with all wisdom, so that we may present every man complete in Christ.

The spread of the Gospel goes together with **admonishing [...] and teaching**. The ultimate goal is to **present every man**, of course, this includes women as well, but Paul's goal here—as the teacher of teachers—is the men, so that they can teach other men *and women* and so that the women can teach the children.

> **29** For this purpose I also labor, striving according to His working, which He works in me in power.

This mission is one of hard work, even though God **works in [Paul] in power**, this still required Paul's intense **labor**—even aside from all his suffering and persecution and imprisonment. All Christians are required to work no less than Paul did. Despite the fact that none have the gifts of the twelve (or thirteen) apostles of Christ, each must work with the gifts he is given. Even a woman who does not have a job, but is a housewife, must work just as hard to bring her children to Christ and to support her husband in fighting the battles outside of his home.
