# Colossians 3 (LSB)

> ### Put On the New Man
> **Col. 3:1**   Therefore, if you have been raised up with Christ, keep seeking the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. **2** Set your mind on the things above, not on the things that are on earth. **3** For you died and your life has been hidden with Christ in God. **4** When Christ, who is our life, is manifested, then you also will be manifested with Him in glory.

There are many consequences of being in Christ. First of all, when He died, the believer died as well. This is difficult to understand, because He died 2,000 years ago, but when a person accepts Christ and enters *into* Christ, he immediately dies spiritually—because he enters into the dead body of Christ—but is immediately raised back to life again as well—because the body of Christ was raised as well.
The second consequence is mentioned in the previous chapter and relates to that which man falls under when he is still alive, that is, the elementary principles, and specifically, authority, rituals and commandments of a certain kind. These, too, are not applicable anymore when one is dead. 
The third consequence is mentioned here, which is the "location" where the believer is. If he is in Christ, and Christ is in Heaven **above**, then the believer is there as well. Why then should he look down to **the things that are on Earth**? It makes no sense for someone who has moved on to a better place to look back at things that others have that he does not have himself. For example, suppose a man becomes rich and has a house and servants who prepare him the best meals. He realizes that in the village that he left the people there eat something that is worth far less. Why should he occupy himself with what *they* eat while he has much better food? This would not make sense. 

> **Col. 3:5**   Therefore, consider the members of your earthly body as dead to sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and greed, which is idolatry.

In order to understand this verse better, consider the members of your earthly body as being able to have different *states*, such as *dead* or *alive*. For example, some viruses that are harmful for people do not have any effect on dogs, such as the flu. So to this particular kind of flu virus man is *alive* and the dog is *dead*. Now to all of the things mentioned in this verse the Christian needs to be *dead*. It should not have any effect on the Christian.
**Example.** Now practice this can already be seen to some extent. There was one time when a part of my molar had broken off. My wife told me of a dentist friend who could fix it for a lower price. So I went there and after he had done his work, I asked him how he was able to give me a discount. He said that he did this off the books. Immediately I said to him—as in a reflex, without even thinking about it—that I had rather pay the full price in that case. 
This is just one example of an area where the Christian can be completely dead, but the ultimate goal is that he is dead in all areas, which can take a lifetime or even more.

> **6** On account of these things, the wrath of God is coming upon the sons of disobedience, 

The **sons of disobedience** are all those who disobey God. God's **wrath** has come to Earth many times in the form of diseases, acts of nature and other disasters. All these have come as a result of disobedience to the things mentioned in the previous verse. 
**Example.** Take for example **greed**. In the movie *The Birds* #tags/movies by Alfred Hitchcock #tags/people/director birds are suddenly killing people, either conscious or not, but in the end it is explained that this is caused by a rupture in natural processes due to man's influence on the environment. 
A similar example is given through the deaths of many due to sexually transmitted diseases (STDs). In one of the Ahmanson Lecture series[^1] hosted by Saddleback Church in 2011, Paige Patterson, mentor of Rick Warren, told of a discussion he had with someone on the plane. The argument of his fellow passenger was that God was not good, for He allowed there to be diseases in this world, such as AIDS. Patterson's counterargument was that those diseases are the wrath of God, and that it can be shown that if everyone was to commit to one partner in his life, STDs would be out of this world in twenty years.

> **7** and in them you also once walked, when you were living in them. 

Each Christian also **once walked** in those sins in a more or lesser way. Note, however, that Paul says that we **were living in them**. So no Christian can say that he merely *committed* one or more of these sins "every now and then". No, *all people* were **living in them**.

> **8** But now you also, lay them all aside: wrath, anger, malice, slander, *and* abusive speech from your mouth. 

All of the terms mentioned here are consequences of sins in v. 5. All of these can be traced back to one or more of these terms. Take for example **anger** and **wrath**, these could come from evil desires that one has towards a certain thing. If this thing cannot be obtained or taken from one, anger and wrath could rise to the surface. 

> **9** Do not lie to one another, since you put off the old man with its *evil* practices, 

This verse explains the latter term in v. 8. **[Abusive] speech** also includes lying.

> **10** and have put on the new man who is being renewed to a full knowledge according to the image of the One who created him—

The **new man** does not have **full knowledge** *yet* (see also notes on Colossians 2... #todo/opzoeken/reference  ) but he is **being renewed**. This means that the Christian is in the process of obtaining full knowledge. The Christian realizes much more after his conversion compared to before it, but it still takes time to know Christ even more. 

> **11** *a renewal* in which there is no *distinction between* Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian, slave, *and* freeman, but Christ is all and in all.

The Christian should never discriminate on the basis of ethnicity when it concerns other Christians, because **Christ is [...] in all** who have accepted Him. 
The main point of this verse, however, is not about treating others but that this renewal applies to all ethnicities. In practice it can be seen that each culture has its quintessential sins. In the Chinese culture for example parents hardly show their emotions to their children, gambling is a serious problem in many Chinese families, and idolatry is practiced through Buddhist and Taoist elements and many forms of superstition[^2]. When a Chinese person becomes a Christian it is difficult to let go of all of these old practices. So, regardless of one's background and cultural traits, people of all cultures will be renewed fully, eventually, and get rid of the most #questions/language/english/word hardnekkige resisting? traits.

> **Col. 3:12**   So, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, put on a heart of compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, *and* patience; **13** bearing with one another, and graciously forgiving each other, whoever has a complaint against anyone, just as the Lord graciously forgave you, so also should you. 

In multiple places in Scriptures this principle of reciprocity is given. This applies to forgiveness and all other good and bad things. Do not expect good things to happen to you, when you do bad things to others. Do unto others as you would do unto yourself. The most important one is that God does not forgive us our sins if we do not forgive others #todo/opzoeken/reference. 

> **14** Above all these things *put on* love, which is the perfect bond of unity.

Not being angry or having wrath, to name two examples, is not the command, for these are but symptoms of a lack of love. Of course anger and wrath can occur in a purer form without sin, for God had these as well #todo/opzoeken/reference, but leaving those exceptions aside, all of these are the result of a not loving the other. No sane person would use abusive speech and malice and slander to the one he loves.
There are many tips on how to improve relationships between people, but these will never cover all aspects. There will always be a situation where one will not have the experience or knowledge on how to address this. The only thing that covers everything is **love**.

> **15** And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body, and be thankful. 

The clearest trait of a Christian is the **peace of Christ**, which should be shown at all times—by *definition*. A Christian should have peace in calm times—everyone should have—and remain peaceful in unrest, because his hope is on Christ Who is the Rock #todo/opzoeken/reference . 
If any Christian is to speak of his *calling* and what that is, then here is the answer: he is called to **Christ**, but not only that, but to come to Him **in one body**. 
**Example.** In the movie #tags/movies *Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles* (the version with Megan Fox #tags/people/actors in it) the turtles think their mission (or calling) is to become stronger and ultimately defeat Shredder. Their master is nearly mortally wounded by Shredder and so they decide to avenge him, but they fail in doing so. In the end, they do succeed, but only when working as a team. So they discover that their calling was not to defeat the enemy, but to defeat the enemy *and* work together. 
In the same way the calling of a Christian is not to be in Christ, but to be in Christ **in one body**, that is, together with the other Christians. There are believers (perhaps they are actually Christians) who regularly have fits with other Christians due to differences in theology. This is like two Christians in one body, where one is trying to kick the other one out. Also, others—outside of the body—would not even *want* to enter this body anymore.

Thankfulness or **gratefulness** is mentioned three times between vv. 15–17, emphasizing the *need* for this in order to grow in Christ.

> ### Family and Work
> **Col. 3:18**   Wives, be subject to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord.

I do not think there is a specific reason why the wives are mentioned first here. It is not that wives should subject to their husbands first, and that *after* that the husbands will love their wives. Both need to start first, and when both are reluctant, it is the husband who needs to start first, just like Jesus did. He came to Earth and loved us before we loved Him (cf. 1 John 4:19). 
The wife should **be subject to [her] husband**, because she is the head of the family and of *her* as well. This is what she *chose* to be when she married him. Women are not inherently subject to anyone, like children are to their parents. Children did not have a choice in whether they wanted to have parents, unlike orphan children, who—sometimes—get to choose whether they want to be adopted by a certain family or not. But women who choose to get married, also choose the position that goes along with it. Just like someone who applies for the role of vice-present in a company chooses to be subject to the present, so the wife must know her role and act like it. Unfortunately, in this world—especially in the West—and in this day, many women do not know what they sign up for, because they do not know the roles in marriage—because did not have a good example from their own parents'—and because they never learned what the meaning is of marriage and how it symbolizes God's future marriage with Christians, but expect to be the head of the family as soon as they are married, or at least equal to the husband in decision making. Of course this can be negotiated before marriage, and some do and speak out their expectations. 
**Example.** I know of a very progressive couple who decided not to marry but have a #questions/language/english/word samenlevingscontract. Despite the lack of an official matrimony they still exchanged rings—without realizing the significance of this[^3]. However, the woman (for lack of a better word for "wife"—which is just "partner" or "girlfriend") required that the man take on her last name if she were to do that for him. Clearly, this woman did not understand that she was going to be adopted into *his* family and carry *his name* from now on. This should be an honor for the woman, but instead she found it a burden and—because of reasons of equality—wanted the man to share this burden with her. I do not blame the woman for her ignorance, as she grew up in the most progressive time in one of the most progressive countries in the world. Logic, however—and a little bit of research—would show that one finds an honorable husband and wants to belong to him, and if he is not honorable, she should not choose to marry him. #biblestudy/discovery
Even if a couple decides to change the roles of a traditional marriage and let both have equal or reversed positions, the marriage is doomed to fail and most definitely—not maybe, but definitely—not give glory to God, because marriage *reflects* God. Even if a post-modern marriage somehow works it will never give God glory, because God created marriage to show how *He* operates, namely by being the leader and decision-maker, by loving His bride, and washing and teaching His bride so that she will become beautiful in word and deed (cf. 1 Peter 3? #todo/opzoeken/reference ). This is the way in which God has a partnership with His bride and what works best. Any other way that somehow works, is purely by coincidence and is not the way God intended. 
So, if the wife has chosen her husband and her role in the new family structure, she *must* assume her role of subjection to the head of the family. If the wife did not choose her husband but was given in marriage to him, she still has a choice whether to make the marriage succeed or fail.

> **19** Husbands, love your wives and do not be embittered against them.

After long marriages it is not uncommon when the husband ignores his wife. Especially complaints and nagging irritate men in general. However, the husband should continue to **love [his wife]**, for only through love he will get the strength not to become **embittered**.
**Example.** Al Bundy in the TV-series #tags/movies/series *Married with Children* seems like a bitter middle-aged man who has children who are far from exemplary and who do not obey him, just like their mother. His love for his wife and children are gone and this is why he is embittered. This is a fictional comedy series but it depicts what real marriages become when men lose the love for their wives. 

> **20** Children, obey your parents in all things, for this is pleasing to the Lord. 

For children—who had no choice in their position nor in the choice of the their parents—their comfort is in God Who is pleased when children obey. On the other hand, the child usually does not know any better other than the way he is raised, until he is at a certain age or sees how his friends are raised differently. If he is taught well and learns that obedience to one's parents is crucial in order to be raised as a good Christian. 
Their other comfort—in the situation where the child does not enjoy the protection of his parents or where the parents do not take on their role correctly—is that eventually they will break loose from the parental yoke, so to speak.

> **21** Fathers, do not exasperate your children, so that they will not lose heart.

In this era this verse has less meaning than when Paul wrote this, for fathers these days—in the West—are too #questions/language/english/saying relaxed relinquish? And rarely beat their children. So there are hardly cases of exasperation in normal families. In countries outside of the West—which is most of the world, by the way—this still occurs on a frequent basis. What bothers me is if I am misunderstanding this verse, since my underlying assumption is that the Bible is written for all people of all times. Now of course there are certain parts that are only meant for some group of people at some point in time, but it should not be so that all of this chapter is generally for all times, except for this verse. 

> **Col. 3:22**   Slaves, in all things obey those who are your masters according to the flesh, not with eyeservice, as men-pleasers, but with integrity of heart, fearing the Lord.

The person who was born as slave, did not choose to become one, like the woman given in marriage and like the children in the previous verse. The same principle applies to him then as well. This "partnership" that he has with his master in this "company"—which could be a household or an actual company—can only succeed when the slave assumes the role he is given. In time  he will be promoted accordingly to higher positions with more authority and perhaps better rewards. But if the master fails to see so and does not reward the slave or punishes him unjustly, he himself will be punished by God.
For the slave who sold himself and did not have a choice when he did so, this is different from the woman who decided to marry a man because of his honorable character, for the slave was not (necessarily) in a position to choose his master. Still, he still had two options to choose from, to let his family starve or to sell himself for a number of years and endure the loss of his freedom, and if he chose the latter he had the choice to be treated well by his master or not. This of course applies to people who have regular jobs as well. Not everyone gets to choose the job he wants to have and most people need a job to survive. Still, when one chooses to work, as a Christian he is obliged to serve his master.

> **23** Whatever you do, do your work heartily, as for the Lord rather than for men, 

Too often I catch myself not working for my employer **as for the Lord**. This is a sin—there is no excuse for it. It would be like when a friend needs to leave for an urgent reason for an indefinite amount of time and asks me to take care of his child as I would take care of my own. Of course the child would have more feelings than my employer, but that is irrelevant.
