> ### Alive with Christ
> **Col. 2:1**   For I want you to understand how great a struggle I have on your behalf and for those who are at Laodicea, and for all those who have not seen my face in the flesh, **2** so that their hearts may be encouraged,

During fasting it is not humble to tell anyone about it, according to Matthew 6, but for struggles and persecutions, as Paul is experiencing, it is different. Struggles are not what the Christian chooses to undergo, and it is not only normal, but encouraged to tell other Christians about it so that they can show their love in relieving the Christian of his struggles. 
In this case, Paul is telling of his struggles **so that [the] hearts [of the Christians at Colossae and Laodicea] may be encouraged**, letting them know that he is struggling *for their sake*. He is spreading the Gospel so that they can hear it as well as those who have not seen his face yet. It is just like in certain stories where passive bystanders become active when they see another person fighting and risking his life for others. This kind of behavior encourages others to come into action. Paul's hope here, then, is to get those he addressed to come into action as well, through prayer, donations and through other acts of love.

> having been held together in love, even unto all the wealth of the full assurance of understanding, unto the full knowledge of God’s mystery, 

Paul's goal is not merely encouragement, for this too is for a greater purpose, namely **the full knowledge of God's mystery**. For the newborn Christian this literally takes a lifetime in order to obtain. On this way, however, Paul struggles for the sake of their unity, wanting them to be **held together in love** (cf. Philippians 1:27). He also speaks of "**even unto**", meaning that it takes a long time to get to—in this case—**the full assurance of understanding**. Understanding God is something that we will never obtain in this lifetime, but will in the next one, but what we do have is the *assurance* that someday we *will understand* (cf. 1 Corinthians 13:12). 
So in short, Paul wants us to be held together in love as long as it takes to be completely assured that we will someday understand God.

> *that is*, Christ *Himself*,

For the explanation of what this mystery is, see notes on Colossians 1:27.

> **3** in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. **4** I say this so that no one will delude you with persuasive argument. **5** For even though I am absent in body, nevertheless I am with you in spirit, rejoicing to see your good order and the stability of your faith in Christ.

Paul probably means here that **all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge** are **hidden** in Christ. Therefore there is no need to have any *special* person to *interpret* these treasures to you or reveal these prophecies. Sure, there is a need for teachers to explain the Word and to guide and disciple young Christians, and teachers can explain the cultural context, the root words and the exegesis, but all of this would be no special revelation or teaching. There is a clear line between a true teacher and a false one. > **Col. 2:6**   Therefore as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, *so* walk in Him, **7** having been firmly rooted and being built up in Him, and having been established in your faith—just as you were instructed—*and* abounding with thanksgiving.

This seems like a reference to the past, to one's *first love* (cf. Revelation 2:4). The way in which one as **received Christ Jesus** at the beginning of one's walk with Christ, continue in that way—that is, **having been firmly rooted and being built up in Him, and having been established in your faith [...], abounding with thanksgiving**. A tree will grow and extend its roots deeper as it grows, but there are storms which will make the tree shake and loosen its roots. In the same way the Christian will encounter spiritual storms in his life which will shake the foundation on which he is resting, which is Christ, but he can and should always go back to that former state of where he was firmly rooted. Thanksgiving is a big key to this. See also notes on Colossians 3:15–17.

> **Col. 2:8**   See to it that no one takes you captive through philosophy and empty deception, according to the tradition of men, according to the elementary principles of the world, and not according to Christ. 

There are three main ways (perhaps more) that Paul notes here through which one is **[taken] captive**. **[P]hilosophy and empty deception** is the first one. Philosophy, in fact, sort of empty. It is nothing but words and it can lead to all kinds of theories, such as Deism and Sophism. These are theories that do not take the proof into account, which Paul speaks of in v. 9. Philosophy, however, *can* lead one to faith as well, when one is pure and rational, such as happened in the life of C.S. Lewis #tags/people/authors/christian as described in his book #todo/opzoeken *Surprised by Joy* #tags/books/christian. Paul himself even used philosophy to convince the Corinthians (see #todo/opzoeken/reference 1 Corinthians ). 
The **tradition of men** is the second one on the list and this is often put on a higher place than faith. **Example.** I heard a sermon once by a Chinese pastor who was evangelizing under Chinese unbelievers. There was one person who was willing to accept Christ as His Savior, be it not that he could not let go of his tradition to honor his parents, which is one of the most important aspects of Chinese culture. This person apparently was taken **captive** by the **tradition of men**. This story ends on a good note, however. Just like Paul told the Colossians not to be taken captive by philosophy and used it himself to captivate the unbelievers of Corinth to believe in Christ, this Chinese pastor used the tradition of men to captivate this unbeliever. He told him that honoring one's parents is at a very high position in the Bible, so high that it is even in the Ten Commandments. Even more so, it is the first of the six commandments to love one's neighbor. And even more so, it is the only commandment that comes with a promise (see #todo/opzoeken/reference Exodus 20... and ). The only difference with the Chinese is that the Bible prohibits *worship* of parents and the veneration of those who have deceased for only God is to be worshipped.
Lastly, there are the **elementary principles of the world**, probably this is referring to natural science—i.e. physics, chemistry and biology. Evolution has replaced Creation and within this evolutionary framework all forms of human behavior, and differences between men and women, are explained by evolutionary psychology.

> **9** For in Him all the fullness of Deity dwells bodily, **10** and in Him you have been filled, who is the head over all rule and authority; **11** in whom you were also circumcised with a circumcision made without hands, in the removal of the body of the flesh, in the circumcision of Christ, **12** having been buried with Him in baptism, in which you were also raised up with Him through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead.

The ample use of figurative examples shows Paul's literal qualities. The **circumcision made without hands [...], the removal of the body of flesh [...], having been buried [and] raised up with Him** are all figurative, but the last things—that **God [...] raised Him from the dead**—is *literal*!

The first figure of speech is that we **have been filled**. Jesus is fully God, and we are like Him, so it as if we have been filled with Him. We are like a cup, partially filled with Jesus' spirit.
It is also as if we have been circumcised, but now not our foreskin is removed but our whole body of flesh. Everything pertaining to our old nature has been removed with our conversion to Christ.

> **13** And you being dead in your transgressions and the uncircumcision of your flesh, He made you alive with Him, having graciously forgiven us all our transgressions. 

Anyone being stripped of his flesh—**the uncircumcision of [the] flesh** would be **dead**. Anyone bearing these **transgressions** would also be dead. The part about transgressions could also be interpreted with an emphasis on being dead **in** transgressions, meaning that transgressions have no more effect on the person. However, this interpretation could not be applied to the second part of the sentence.

> **14** Having canceled out the certificate of debt consisting of decrees against us which was hostile to us, He also has taken it out of the way, having nailed it to the cross. **15** Having disarmed the rulers and authorities, He made a public display of them, having triumphed over them in Him.

Again, Paul uses a figure of speech. There is, of course, no such thing as an official **certificate of debt** and neither has Jesus literally **nailed [this] to the cross**, but it is so beautiful to know that Jesus has done just that in the *Heavenly* realm. For indeed, there is a certificate like this in Heaven—albeit a symbolic one—and the Father still has those certificates in possession of those who have not put their faith in Christ. 

> **Col. 2:16**   Therefore, no one is to judge you in food and drink, or in respect to a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath day— **17** things which are *only* a shadow of what is to come; but the substance belongs to Christ.

Is there a relationship between this **certificate of debt** and the rules concerning **food and drink** etc.? The fact that this certificate has been nailed to the cross, does not mean that Christians are free to sin and that there no more rules that apply to them. However, it does mean that the traditions do not apply to them anymore, because traditions are partially made by the **rulers and authorities**, and Jesus has **made a public display over them, having triumphed over them**. To be more precise, Jesus has triumphed over them **in Him**, that is, within Jesus there rulers and authorities do not have any power anymore, and since Christian are *in* Jesus, they do not need to abide by the rules they have set, concerning festivals and special days. Of course, rules concerning the order of a country and those that are in line with the Bible still apply (cf. Romans 11? #todo/opzoeken/reference ). #biblestudy/discovery

> **18** Let no one keep defrauding you of your prize by delighting in self-abasement and the worship of the angels, going into detail about *visions* he has seen, being puffed up for nothing by his fleshly mind, **19** and not holding fast to the head, from whom the entire body, being supplied and held together by the joints and ligaments, grows with a growth that is from God.

These are *two conditions* that Paul mentions, namely being **puffed up [...] by his fleshly mind, and not holding fast to the head**. There are those who **[go] into details about visions**, but the problems arise when this person does not hold fast to Christ. These persons see their vision as more important than that which Jesus said. Joseph Smith, for example, saw a vision (it is doubtful that that actually happened, but let us suppose so for the sake of argument) and wrote the instructions he received from it (which are the golden en silver books #todo/opzoeken )[^2] in a separate book that overrules the Bible. The whole problems with cults and sects like that is that they do not hold fast to Christ anymore, who is the head of the entire body, but rather hold fast to their leader and to his visions. #biblestudy/discovery

> **Col. 2:20**   If you have died with Christ to the elementary principles of the world, why, as if you were living in the world, do you submit yourself to decrees:

In the flesh Christ was part of **the elementary principles of the world**. Of course He was God as well, and therefore He broke with these every now and then. I am speaking of, among others, the physical principles—such as when He walked on water, and when He moved from the middle of the Sea of Galilee to the other side in a split second—but also "spiritual" ones, such as authority and obedience, which apply to all humans. Jesus Himself obeyed His parents and the authorities most of the time, but at certain times He did not and carried out the plan of His Father.

> **21** “Do not handle, nor taste, nor touch”? **22** Which deal with everything destined to perish with use, *which are* in accordance with the commands and teachings of men; **23** which are matters having, to be sure, a word of wisdom in self-made religion and self-abasement and severe treatment of the body, *but are* of no value against fleshly indulgence.

Paul is not speaking here of every human tradition, but of those that deal with restrictions and **self-abasement and severe treatment of the body**, *and* those which **deal with everything destined to perish**. These are, again, *two* restrictions that both need to apply. Fasting, for example, is a severe treatment for the body that causes the body to use itself as the food source since there is not external one. This is called autophagy and is actually good for the body because the weaker cells and unhealthy tissue get eaten up first, which leaves a cleaner remaining body. If fasting is done for the Kingdom and for one's spiritual growth, it is a good thing, but when it is done for any purpose here on Earth that will eventually perish, it is not something that Christians should hold to. The Sabbath could be an example of that. If the Sabbath is truly kept to bring one closer to God and create value for eternity, it is a good thing. But if it is done out of pure religious rituals then it falls under the category that Paul speaks of here.
