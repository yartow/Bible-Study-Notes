### Essay. Christians are allowed to receive money from the church. 
Not only should we **support** our brothers in Christ who have given their lives for Christ and work in the ministry[^1] because we are to pay them for their *services*, but also because we are **fellow workers** for Christ. The first point is that these brothers provide services to the church when they are with us. Some preach on Sundays and teach during the week. They provide their testimonies and more. Even in normal situations when Christians such as Nick Vujicic #tags/people/christian are requested to provide a testimony or teaching, they will ask for a speaking fee, if only to cover their traveling expenses[^2]. However, in the case that they do not preach to the congregation but only preach on the streets, they need a means of survival as well. Should Christians not have to pay for the service they are doing for the Kingdom of God?
The answer is *yes*, of course. Every church needs evangelists, people who *actively* spread the Gospel on the streets or in other forms. This is the task and mission of every local congregation, and it is also the preferred way to make one's local church grow. It brings new life to the church and along with it, new students of the Word and new gifts from the Holy Spirit to complement and serve the Body of Christ. 
One might think, "Other volunteers in the congregation do not get paid, so why should these missionaries?" The fact is that other workers in the church would get paid if they did it full-time, but in practice usually only one pastor works full-time. In some bigger churches though there are people who train others in music or Bible studies full-time and they also get paid, or they do not, because they already have an income through other sources. The principle is that they have the right to be supported by the church for their work. It is not wrong to receive money from the church for work that one does. 
I would like to add to that that many volunteers help out in church *next* to their daily job. So if one works 40 hours a week, and works another six hours to prepare for and teach at Sunday School, he does not get paid by the church for these extra hours. So regular volunteers work 48 hours a week, but receive a total income for 40 hours. In the same way—in my opinion—people who receive a full-time salary from the church should also work more than 40 hours. These 40 hours are compulsory and part of the contract, the extra hours are voluntary. This would be more fair to the other volunteers. So this comes down to a salary of `40/48 = 83.3%`. Salaries in churches usually are lower than that of commercial companies, perhaps for this reason.

John here asks Gaius to go a step further in being this good worker that he already is—and so should we aim for this at all times. Gaius is asked to give money, next to the care and love he has already given these brothers whom he did not even know. I do not know about Gaius' ministry, but I do know that most Christians in church are not workers in the ministry of Evangelization. This, then, is the way to participate in that ministry, by giving to those workers. Either give your time and effort, or give your money for the Gospel, so that unbelievers will hear the Word of God.
