Why is **nations** (Hebrew: *goy*) used in v.1 but here **peoples** (Hebrew: *am*)? #biblestudy/questions/rootwords It seems as if the meaning of *[goy](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h1471/kjv/wlc/0-1/)* is more like that of the literal meaning of "nations", while *[am](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/h5971/kjv/wlc/0-1/)* is more on the field of compatriots.
So verses 1 and 3 form a contrast, not only in this concept of nations and peoples, but also in the two words "driven" and "scattered". God has **driven** Israel among the *nations*, and **scattered** them to all the *peoples*. *Scattered* goes a step further than being driven away. #### Essay. A comparison between the Law in Deuteronomy and Micah
It is striking to see such a similarity between v.16 and Micah 6:8, in which the Law has been summarized in three commandments. What is asked of the Israelites—and of us as well—are to act out three verbs: love, walk and keep. These three verbs are almost exactly used in Micah, as follows (emphasis added): 

> but to do justice, and to *love* kindness,
> and to *walk* humbly with your God? (Micah 6:8 (ESV))

However, regardless of how similar these verses look, there is no one-to-one mapping between the two verses, for *loving kindness* is not the same as *loving Yahweh*, and *walking in His ways* is not the same as *walking humbly with God*. Even more so, that which v.16 is speaking of, i.e. the keeping of the Law, is dissected in different ways in Micah and in the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:3). What follows, is a concise elaboration on these three dissections and the distinctions between these.
***Jesus' bisection*** (#questions/language/english/spelling bipartition better word?) is the one most people know of, because Jesus actually *said* the Law could be *summarized* into two commandments (Mark 12:28-34). His is also the easiest to understand. In Jesus' summary He emphasizes the *object* to whom one keeps the commandments, which is either God or one's neighbor, denoted in the first four and last six commandments, respectively. The love for these two parties cannot be  seen as two separate things, however, for our neighbors are creatures of God and therefore one cannot *not love* them and *still love* God (cf. 1 John 4:8,21). The motivation for keeping the commandments is love, and more specifically, one's love *for God*.
Micah dissects the Law into a tripartition and does this firstly with an overall focus on the *implementation* of the Law, but secondly on the balance between justice and mercy (which is translated as *kindness* in the LSB). This balance is shown not only through the presence of these two words and as if these are offsetting each other, but also by the extra command "to walk humbly with your God", for it is humility that enacts our mercy. It might seem there is an imbalance here, because there two *soft commands* (mercy and humility) and there is only one *hard command* (justice), but this is so because the soft commands are what are need to be emphasized and practiced more[^2], also because these are more difficult. When we have experienced mercy ourselves, we can be merciful to others. Some, perhaps, will not, even when they have experienced it, like the slave in the parable *The Unforgiving Slave* (Matthew 18:21-35), but it is certain that those who do, *have experienced* it before. 
This balance between mercy and justice is also emphasized by the *lack* of a mention of any balance in ***Moses' tripartition*** here in v.16. But even though Moses does not *explicitly* mention any form of mercy, it is *implicitly* present in at least the first two commands, i.e. to **love Yahweh**  and to **walk in His ways**, for if one wants to walk in God's way, he must also be merciful in the way God is merciful, and if he loves God, he will also love the ones God created (cf. 1 John 5:1), and mercy is a part of that love (1 Corinthians 13:5,7).
I have not called this verse a *pentasection* (i.e. split in five) on purpose, because I still see that the third to fifth commands are grouped together. That is to say, the commandments of God need to be *done and kept* (1 John 5:2-3), done in the *right way* according to the statutes (Deuteronomy 12 and further) and there needs to *be acted upon* any activity that is not according to the commandments. 

Even though I have said much about these last three commands, ***Moses' tripartition***  #questions/language/english(or is trisection a better word, even if it does not exist?) actually puts an emphasis on loving God and wanting to be like Him. The first command is to **love Yahweh** and the second is to **walk in His ways** as a result of this love. Walking in God's ways refers to the way one should live—with humility, kindness and mercy. It were the Pharisees and possibly the Scribes as well who kept that last command (concerning the commandment, statutes and judgments) to the letter and Jesus even commended them for that (#todo/opzoeken/reference ), but it was the *second* command that they *lacked*, for they did not do all of these things *in His ways*—that is in the way God would have done it—nor did they walk, i.e. live out these commandments in their own lives, *in His way* and that is why Jesus called them hypocrites.
