# 1 Samuel 21 (LSB)
> ### David Takes Consecrated Bread
> **1Sam. 21:1**   Then David came to Nob to Ahimelech the priest; and Ahimelech came trembling to meet David and said to him, “Why are you alone, and no one is with you?” **2** And David said to Ahimelech the priest, “The king has commanded me with a matter and has said to me, ‘Let no one know anything about the matter on which I am sending you and with which I have commanded you; and I have directed the young men to a certain place.’ **3** “So now, what do you have on hand? Give five loaves of bread into my hand, or whatever can be found.” 

David had many men with him, about six hundred (1 Samuel 23:13), but he asked only for five loaves. The priest thought David was asking for **ordinary bread**, but of course that would never have been enough, so it is most likely David was referring to the consecrated bread. David must also have been aware of the fact that priests could eat the food sacrificed to God and that there could have been a lot. However, some types of sacrificed food were not allowed to be kept longer than one or two days (Leviticus 7:15-18, 22:30) depending on the type of offering. In this case, there happened to be no food at all except for the consecrated bread.
The consecrated **five loaves of bread** each contained 4.4 liters of flour (Leviticus 24:5), which would amount to about nine regular loaves of bread, or 45 loaves in total. Not even the local bakery would have this much at hand. Still, this would only amount to about one-and-a-half slice for each person. 

About the five loaves, I would not put any relation between the "five loaves" mentioned here and when [[Matthew 14 (LSB)/Jesus Feeds Five Thousand|Jesus fed the five thousand]]. The number "five" though does have significant meaning, for it represents grace. It was through grace and not coincidence (see v. 6) that there was this large amount of bread to at least feed the soldiers a little bit. #biblestudy/numerology 

> **4** And the priest answered David and said, “There is no ordinary bread on hand, but there is consecrated bread; if only the young men have kept themselves from women.” 

Though the priest knew that this bread was only meant for descendants of Aaron and no one else (Leviticus 22:10-13), he knew the spirit of the Law, just like Jesus did, and provided the food to anyone who really needed it, however, he still required that the men had not had intercourse with their wives (see Leviticus 22:4). 

> **5** And David answered the priest and said to him, “Surely women have been kept from us as previously when I set out and the vessels of the young men were holy, though it was an ordinary journey; how much more then today will their vessels be holy?” **6** So the priest gave him consecrated *bread*; for there was no bread there but the bread of the Presence which was removed from before Yahweh, in order to put hot bread *in its place* when it was taken away.

It was no coincidence that there was showbread on this very day. The text does not say that it was on this very day that **the bread of the Presence [...] was removed** but we know there was a great number of priests who could have eaten from it. The fact anything was left in itself already is a miracle.

> ### David Flees to Gath
> **1Sam. 21:10**   Then David arose and fled that day from Saul and went to Achish king of Gath. **11** But the servants of Achish said to him, “Is this not David the king of the land? Did they not sing of this one as they danced, saying, 
> 	‘Saul has struck his thousands, 
> 	And David his ten thousands’?”
> 
> **1Sam. 21:12**   And David took these words to heart and greatly feared Achish king of Gath. **13** So he disguised his sanity in their sight and acted insanely in their hands and scribbled on the doors of the gate and let his saliva run down into his beard. **14** Then Achish said to his servants, “Behold, you see the man behaving as a madman. Why do you bring him to me? **15** “Do I lack madmen, that you have brought this one to act the madman before me? Shall this one come into my house?”
