Probably Shimeah had such a bad character that the chronicler thought it not worthy to portray him in this list, which consists of a total of seven sons only. Note that in this case I have assumed that the name *Shimeah* is different from **Shimea**, although there are dictionaries (among which Strong's #questions/language/english/spelling is that called a dictionary?) that claim that these two writing variants are the same (they are different in Hebrew). Shimea is named in *Shammah* 1 Samuel 17:12, but there it is clear that these two are the same, because of the indicator that he is **the third**.

This is only one explanation though. The other, most commonly noted explanation of the difference between the seven sons in these verses and the *eight* sons in 1 Samuel 16:10 is that the unnamed eight son in this verse is a son that passed away at for example childbirth. This explanation, however, would not be correct, for Jesse passed on *seven* (living) sons before Samuel besides David #biblestudy/apologetics/bible/contradictions.
A third explanation is that the missing son had no children and that therefore it was not important to write him down in this lineage document[^3].
