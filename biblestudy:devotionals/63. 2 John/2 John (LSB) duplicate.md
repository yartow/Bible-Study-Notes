> ### Walk in Truth and Love
> **2 John 1**   The elder to the elect lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all who know the truth, **2** for the sake of the truth which abides in us and will be with us forever: **3** Grace, mercy, *and* peace will be with us, from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love.
> 
> **2 John 4**   I rejoiced greatly to find *some* of your children walking in truth, just as we received commandment from the Father. **5** Now I ask you, lady, not as though *I were* writing to you a new commandment, but the one which we have had from the beginning, that we love one another. **6** And this is love, that we walk according to His commandments. This is the commandment, just as you have heard from the beginning, that you should walk in it.

There is a circular reasoning here. The commandment is to **love another**, and love is to **walk according to His commandments**. #todo/nogaftemaken 

> **2 John 7**   For many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who do not confess Jesus Christ *as* coming in the flesh. This is the deceiver and the antichrist. **8** See to yourselves, that you do not lose what we accomplished, but that you may receive a full reward. **9** Anyone who goes too far and does not abide in the teaching of Christ, does not have God. The one who abides in the teaching, he has both the Father and the Son. 

**[T]oo far** means here that in the field of not **[abiding] in the teaching of Christ**. It is hard to see how "too far" would refer to "**not confessing Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh**" for that would not be something anyone can do partially, rather it is binary #questions/language/english/spelling#, that is one either confesses or he does not confess that Jesus Christ is God and has now incarnated in the flesh. 
Every teacher makes mistakes in his teachings and does not abide in Him on a daily basis. Christians make mistakes, but there are levels of sin which the Christian does, and there is the intention, frequency and seriousness of the sin that matter as well. It is hard to know where to draw the line, but the person who is led by the Spirit (1 John 2:27, Galatians 5:16,25) will know at the right time when a teacher **goes too far**.

> **10** ==If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching, do not receive him into *your* house, and do not give him a greeting, **11** for the one who gives him a greeting participates in his evil deeds.==

This verse refers to the teacher who has gone too far. At such as points, when the Christian realizes that no discussion is possible with the teacher because of his stubbornness or any other shortcoming to realize the errors in his teaching, the Christian, should let the elders take over this discussion. But when the teacher does not even listen to the elders, the church members should not try to discuss with him individually. The danger is that the church member will be led astray by the teacher. Rather and better yet, they should stay away from him and treat him as an unbeliever (cf. 1 Corinthians 5 #todo/opzoeken/reference ). 
Why should this teacher not be treated then? Assuming that this teacher is not evil socially, and does not have intentions to do, but is simply mistaken in his beliefs, why should we treat him so harshly so as to not even greet him when encountering him, for example, on the street? The greeting mentioned here is in the Ancient Near East context #tags/culture/ane#.
